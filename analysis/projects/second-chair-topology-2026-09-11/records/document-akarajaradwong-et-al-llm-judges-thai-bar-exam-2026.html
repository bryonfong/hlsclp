Corpus synthesis of this source: A Two-Phase Stability Study of LLM Judges and Bar Council Examiners on Thai Bar-Exam Free-Form Essays compares models with students as examinees and with human examiners as graders. It finds overlapping examinee performance but asymmetric grading: models largely reproduce the majority human interpretation where the rubric leaves room for disagreement. The authors caution that high grader agreement need not preserve legitimate interpretive diversity.

Corpus framing of this source: Thai bar-exam evaluation separates answer correctness from interpretive reasoning quality

Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied excerpt from A Two-Phase Stability Study of LLM Judges and Bar Council Examiners on Thai Bar-Exam Free-Form Essays explains the examination rubric, the study’s scoring procedure and the limits of interpreting its stability measurement. It supplies methodological reasoning rather than numerical stability findings.

Corpus source-specific finding

The rubric combines externally grounded correctness with interpretive reasoning quality.

Exact returned source excerpt

The regulation specifies an 8-tier ordinal scale crossing two axes: final-answer correctness against the gold answer (matches / differs), and reasoning quality (unusable / some / acceptable / good / very good).

Exact returned source excerpt

2.2 The deterministic and interpretive axes We label the two axes deterministic correctness (final-answer-matches-gold against the official gold answer issued by the examiner committee) and interpretive quality (the reasoning-quality scale, judged without a gold target).

Corpus source-specific finding

Scores do not uniquely identify tiers, so the study records both.

Exact returned source excerpt

First, the numeric score alone does not recover the tier: a score of 1 could be T2 (incorrect, somewhat acceptable reasoning) or T4 (correct, unusable reasoning); a score of 5 could be T5 or T6.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our LLM judges return both a tier number and a score within that tier’s range, mirroring the human two-step procedure (Appendix C).

Corpus source-specific finding

The rubric can reward good reasoning above a correct final answer.

Exact returned source excerpt

Second, the regulation overlaps and inverts: a correct final answer with unusable reasoning (T4: 0–1) scores strictly below an incorrect final answer with good reasoning (T3: 2–4), so the rubric is not a clean correctness split.

Corpus source-specific finding

The stability measurement concerns examiner calibration as well as formal rules.

Exact returned source excerpt

Third, the regulation prescribes tier categories but not the natural-language anchors that distinguish “somewhat acceptable” from “acceptable” reasoning.

Exact returned source excerpt

Examiners fill this calibration from their training and prior experience.
