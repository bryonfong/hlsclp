Corpus synthesis of this source: Liz Meira Miossi’s working paper reports a 38-month deployment in US immigration document production. Corpus framing of this source: Regulatory-document automation shifts the hard work to oversight, trust maintenance and evolving QA

Corpus synthesis of this source: Liz Meira Miossi’s 2026 working paper reports a 38-month field deployment in US immigration document production. It argues that reliable operation depends on combining automated checks with expert human judgment, countering complacency and revising quality rules after observed failures. The author presents preliminary, retrospective observations from one operator and domain, not controlled evidence of general effects.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study examines AI-assisted advocacy rather than automated adjudication.

Exact returned source excerpt

This paper reports findings from a longitudinal field deployment study spanning 38 months (March 2023 to May 2026), during which we designed, deployed, and iterated a multi-stage AI orchestration

Exact returned source excerpt

Single domain: All findings are derived from US immigration petition documents, predominantly EB2-NIW.

Corpus source-specific finding

Automated QA and expert review catch different failures, leaving humans responsible for the hardest judgments.

Exact returned source excerpt

The automated system handles factual consistency and formatting (Categories A and D).

Exact returned source excerpt

The human operator handles narrative coherence and regulatory compliance judgment (Categories B and C).

Corpus source-specific finding

Trust progressed from costly scrutiny to efficient selective review, then to complacency risk.

Exact returned source excerpt

Phase 1: Productive Distrust (Months 1 through 8, approximately cases 1 through 60) In the early period, the operator reviewed every output extensively.

Exact returned source excerpt

Revision time frequently exceeded the time that manual production would have required.

Corpus source-specific finding

Effective oversight requires deliberate safeguards against declining vigilance.

Exact returned source excerpt

Counter-Complacency Mechanisms In response to complacency episodes, we introduced deliberate mechanisms to resist excessive trust: Mandatory QA gates: the pipeline now includes binary go/no-go checkpoints where specific conditions must be met before processing continues.

Exact returned source excerpt

These gates operate independently of the operator's review and cannot be bypassed.

Corpus source-specific finding

QA rules evolved from production failures rather than a pre-deployment specification.
