Native topic findings

  1. AI productivity gains depend on the task, user and workflow—not access or model capability alone.
  2. Several studies find substantial task-level gains, often concentrated among weaker or less-experienced performers.
  3. Equalization is conditional: open-ended decisions and learning tasks can instead favor stronger users.
  4. Training and structured support can improve outcomes, but more usage is not itself evidence of productive use.
  5. Effective reliance requires recognizing both AI’s limitations and one’s own.
  6. Assisted performance and independent skill formation can move in different directions.
  7. AI can replace some collaborative functions without eliminating the value of human teams.
  8. Individual gains may carry collective costs in rework, creative diversity and expertise transmission.
  9. Adoption, time savings, financial returns and aggregate productivity require separate measurement.
  10. Narrower performance gaps do not establish better employment prospects or lower economic inequality.
