Open topic: EU governance: Simplification tests enforceable safeguards

Corpus framing of this source: Humans in the Loop: strengths and limits of the draft EU AI Act’s oversight requirements

Corpus synthesis of this source: Humans in the Loop credits the draft EU AI Act with embedding human oversight and systemic risk mitigation into AI design, but questions whether its requirements adequately equip overseers or legitimate contested decisions. The authors combine legal analysis with reported policy research and facial-recognition risks, and urge lawmakers to address governance gaps before finalising the law.

Corpus source-specific finding

The draft Act places human oversight within a broader risk-based regulatory framework.

Exact returned source excerpt

The draft AI Act, broadly speaking, creates obligations that apply to two sets of actors: AI “providers,” who build the systems, and AI users, who use them.

Exact returned source excerpt

The draft AI Act divides and regulates AI systems according to levels of risk, ranging from “unacceptable” systems that are banned to low-risk systems whose builders and users voluntarily self-regulate.

Nicholson Price II’s 2023 article argues that meaningful human oversight requires explicit roles, workable interfaces and appropriate accountability. Drawing on existing law, human-factors research and accident accounts, the authors challenge the assumption that adding a human automatically improves an automated system. Corpus framing of this source: Humans in the Loop: regulate the system, not merely the human reviewer

Corpus synthesis of this source: Rebecca Crootof, Margot E. Kaminski and W. Nicholson Price II’s March 2023 article combines cross-sector legal analysis, published accident accounts and human-factors research. It argues that human involvement needs explicit purposes, suitable working conditions and system-wide governance—not an assumption that human oversight automatically improves algorithmic decisions. The authors do not adopt a collective preference for human over machine decisionmaking.

Corpus source-specific finding
