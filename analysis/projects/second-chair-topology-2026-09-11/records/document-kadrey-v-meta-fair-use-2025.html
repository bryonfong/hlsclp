Corpus framing of this source: Kadrey v. Meta: a record-specific fair-use victory and a warning about market dilution

Corpus synthesis of this source: The court grants Meta partial summary judgment on thirteen authors’ training-related reproduction claims, while distinguishing that result from its prediction that better-supported claims of AI-driven market dilution could defeat fair use. Its contribution combines a four-factor analysis, a critique of competing legal analogies, and a strict separation between potential technological harm and evidence sufficient to decide this case.

Corpus framing of this source: Kadrey v. Meta: record-specific fair use ruling and market-dilution framework

Corpus synthesis of this source: Judge Vince Chhabria granted Meta partial summary judgment on fair use for reproducing thirteen authors’ books to train Llama, while stressing that the ruling rests on these plaintiffs’ arguments and evidence rather than a general authorization of unlicensed AI training. The order’s principal doctrinal contribution is its account of market dilution: LLMs may indirectly substitute for copyrighted works by enabling vast numbers of competing outputs, potentially outweighing even highly transformative use. The plaintiffs lost because they pursued unsuccessful regurgitation and training-licensing theories and supplied no meaningful evidence connecting Llama’s outputs to dilution of their own markets. The order predicts that stronger records will often produce different outcomes and that licensing, rather than stopping AI development, will likely follow where training is not fair use.
