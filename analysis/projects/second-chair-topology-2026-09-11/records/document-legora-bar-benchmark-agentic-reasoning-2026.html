Since high-importance misses are penalized heavily, a materially incomplete deliverable still scores like one.

SupportsLegal quality: Scoring methods encode different harms

Open topic: Legal quality: Scoring methods encode different harms

Corpus framing of this source: Legora BAR: weighted, lawyer-defined scoring of agentic legal work

Corpus synthesis of this source: Legora describes its benchmark methodology and argues that importance-weighted rubrics distinguish legal errors more meaningfully than all-or-nothing grading. The supplied material explains the procedure and offers a worked example, rather than comparative model results.

Corpus source-specific finding

Lawyers define the criteria; systems produce legal deliverables against them.

Exact returned source excerpt

01 — **A lawyer writes the checklist.** The must-haves for a great answer: the facts, the analysis, the conclusions, and the sources.

Exact returned source excerpt

02 — **The system does the task.** It works through the matter and produces the deliverable, the same way a lawyer would be asked to.

Corpus source-specific finding

Legora repeats each case three times and uses an LLM judge.

Exact returned source excerpt

Each case is run three consecutive times to ensure statistical relevance, and the output gets scored by an LLM-as-a-judge, which then gets compared to the corresponding rubrics.

Exact returned source excerpt

This procedure is repeated for all models.

Corpus source-specific finding

Legora argues that weighted scoring captures distinctions that binary grading hides.

Exact returned source excerpt

Some agent benchmarks grade all-or-nothing: a deliverable either satisfies every criterion or fails outright.

Exact returned source excerpt

All-or-nothing grading collapses every result into a single pass/fail and hides where systems actually differ, on the hard work where the differences matter most.

Corpus source-specific finding

The worked example illustrates how weighting rewards material correctness despite a minor omission.

Exact returned source excerpt

Worked example (Agreement review, Quality rubric score 90%): ✓ Identifies every material change-of-control provision — High ✓ Flags the termination rights correctly — High ✓ Explains the commercial impact of the key risks — Medium ✓ Proposes appropriate redline language — Low ✕ Notes the secondary filing deadline — Low This answer got the high-importance points right and missed only a minor one, so it scores well.

Exact returned source excerpt

Miss something marked high and the score falls much further than missing a low item.

Corpus synthesis of this source: Legora Research introduces its vendor-run Benchmark for Agentic Reasoning, explaining its production-system design, private case methodology, risk-sensitive scoring and reported model differences. Corpus source-specific finding

Legora argues that legal AI benchmarks should test the full deployed system, not the underlying model alone.

Exact returned source excerpt

Legora BAR evaluates AI models across a comprehensive set of end-to-end legal tasks drawn from real-world cases, purpose-built to track how Legora performs as both the underlying models and our own harness advance over time.

Exact returned source excerpt

Many legal benchmarks are designed as technical evaluations that run in synthetic environments with simplified harnesses built specifically for benchmarking.

Corpus source-specific finding

BAR covers substantial legal deliverables, including work requiring sustained senior judgment.

| | Long | A week or more, up to hundreds of hours | An end-to-end delivery that moves the whole matter forward: a full position paper, a complete pleading, a deal's document set.
