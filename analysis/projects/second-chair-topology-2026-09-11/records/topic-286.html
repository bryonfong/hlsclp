AI privilege: Legal subject matter is insufficient

Corpus synthesis: Shows chatbot exchanges require genuine counsel direction and confidentiality controls, with discovery treatment remaining unsettled.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Native topic findings

  1. AI use tests the boundaries of confidentiality and legal protection; legal subject matter alone does not make chatbot exchanges privileged.
  2. Heppner rejected protection for independently generated defense reports despite existing representation and later delivery to counsel.
  3. Contrasting discovery decisions leave the treatment of client AI exchanges unsettled.
  4. Enterprise contracts can strengthen confidentiality claims, but they do not establish privilege automatically.
  5. Recommended safeguards combine genuine counsel direction, documented client warnings and controls on sensitive workflows.
  6. Confidentiality controls do not replace verification and supervision of AI-assisted work.
  7. Mandatory AI-use disclosure creates a separate tension between court oversight and protection of legal workflows.
