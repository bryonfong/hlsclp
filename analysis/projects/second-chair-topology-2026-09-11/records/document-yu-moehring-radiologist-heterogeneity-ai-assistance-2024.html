Corpus synthesis of this source: Heterogeneity and predictors of the effects of AI assistance on radiologists examines individual changes in chest X-ray diagnostic accuracy with AI support. The authors find substantial variation, weak predictive value from experience and baseline skill, and an important association between AI accuracy and assisted performance. They recommend individualized deployment tests while qualifying what the experiment establishes about mechanisms and generalizability.

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Heterogeneity and predictors of the effects of AI assistance on radiologists

Corpus synthesis of this source: Yu and colleagues report a large-scale study of 140 radiologists performing 15 chest X-ray diagnostic tasks. The study’s principal contribution is to show that AI assistance affects radiologists heterogeneously, while conventional experience and baseline-skill measures poorly predict who benefits. AI error is more informative, particularly for calibration, but its relationship with outcomes varies by pathology and performance metric. The authors therefore advocate individualized, deployment-specific assessment rather than uniform allocation of AI assistance.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI assistance produces materially heterogeneous effects across radiologists.

Exact returned source excerpt

This large-scale study examined the heterogeneous effects of AI assistance on 140 radiologists across 15 chest X-ray diagnostic tasks and identified predictors of these effects.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our findings, based on a large-scale sample of 140 radiologists, highlight the existence of radiologist heterogeneity in treatment effects, which has substantial implications for both absolute and relative performance.

Corpus source-specific finding

Experience, specialization, prior AI use and baseline diagnostic error do not reliably identify who will benefit.

Exact returned source excerpt

To understand the predictive power of experience-based radiologist characteristics together, we built a combined characteristics linear regression model that used binary variables for years of experience (whether the radiologist had less than or equal to the median 6 years of experience), subspecialty in thoracic radiology (whether the radiologist specialized in thoracic radiology) and experience with AI tools (whether the radiologist had experience with AI tools) as independent variables and an intercept term to predict the mean treatment effect of each radiologist.

Exact returned source excerpt

No statistically significant difference was observed in treatment effect between subgroups on all pathologies aggregated ( P  > 0.05; Supplementary Table 3 ) or on individual pathologies ( P  > 0.05, Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P  > 0.05).
