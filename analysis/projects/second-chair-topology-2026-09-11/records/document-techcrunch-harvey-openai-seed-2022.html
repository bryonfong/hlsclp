# Harvey, Which Uses AI to Answer Legal Questions, Lands Cash from OpenAI *Kyle Wiggers — TechCrunch (2022)* # Harvey, which uses AI to answer legal questions, lands cash from OpenAI [Kyle Wiggers](https://techcrunch.com/author/kyle-wiggers/) 5:00 AM PST · November 23, 2022 [Harvey](https://harvey.ai/), a startup building what it describes as a “copilot for lawyers,” today emerged from stealth with $5 million in funding led by the OpenAI Startup Fund, the tranche through which OpenAI and its partners are investing in early-stage AI companies tackling major problems.

Exact returned source excerpt

Harvey was founded by Winston Weinberg, a former securities and antitrust litigator at law firm O’Melveny & Myers, and Gabriel Pereyra, previously a research scientist at DeepMind, Google Brain (another of Google’s AI groups) and Meta AI.

Corpus source-specific finding

Pereyra presents Harvey as lawyer augmentation rather than replacement.

Exact returned source excerpt

“Instead of manually editing legal documents or performing legal research, Harvey enables lawyers to describe the task they wish to accomplish in simple instructions and receive the generated result.

Exact returned source excerpt

To enable this, Harvey leverages large language models to both understand users’ intent and to generate the correct output.”

Corpus source-specific finding

Wiggers flags confidentiality and unreliable output; Harvey’s disclaimer requires attorney supervision.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus framing of this source: Harvey as a lawyer-supervised natural-language interface for legal work

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source presents Harvey as a beta legal-AI product that can answer and act on legal questions expressed in ordinary language. Harvey co-founder Gabriel Pereyra frames it as an augmentation tool intended to improve lawyers’ efficiency and work quality rather than replace them. The article separately highlights confidentiality, toxicity and fabricated-fact risks, and notes Harvey’s restriction to use under licensed-attorney supervision rather than as legal advice for nonlawyers.

Corpus source-specific finding

Harvey handles natural-language legal queries and drafting tasks.

Exact returned source excerpt

More concretely, Harvey can answer questions asked in natural language like, “Tell me what the differences are between an employee and independent contractor in the Fourth Circuit,” and “Tell me if this clause in a lease is in violation of California law, and if so, rewrite it so it is no longer in violation.” On first read, it almost seems as though Harvey could replace lawyers, generating legal arguments and filing drafts at a moment’s notice.

Pereyra positions Harvey as an intermediary that augments lawyers rather than replaces them.

Exact returned source excerpt

But Pereyra insists that this isn’t the case.

“We want Harvey to serve as an intermediary between tech and lawyer, as a natural language interface to the law,” he said.

Harvey provides a unified and intuitive interface for all legal workflows, allowing lawyers to describe tasks in plain English instead of using a suite of complex and specialized tools for niche tasks.” It’s powerful stuff in theory.
