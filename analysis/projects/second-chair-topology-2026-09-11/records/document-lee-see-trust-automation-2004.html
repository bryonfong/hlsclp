Corpus framing of this source: Trust in automation depends on interaction cues, self-confidence and operating context

Corpus synthesis of this source: Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance synthesises research on how people develop trust and decide whether to rely on automation. It emphasises affective cues and users’ confidence in their own abilities, recommends presenting information in ways compatible with multiple trust-forming processes, and cautions against transferring interpersonal trust concepts uncritically to automation.

Corpus framing of this source: Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance

Corpus synthesis of this source: Lee and See integrate multidisciplinary research into a dynamic model of human–automation trust. They define trust as a goal-oriented attitude under uncertainty and vulnerability, distinguish it from reliance behavior, explain appropriate trust through calibration, resolution and specificity, and model its development through context, information, cognition and feedback. They use laboratory, field, survey and neurological findings to derive tentative design, evaluation and training guidance, while identifying limits on extrapolation and substantial research gaps.

Corpus source-specific finding

Defines trust as an attitude that guides, but does not determine, reliance

Exact returned source excerpt

A simple deﬁnition of trust consistent with these considerations is the attitude that an agent will help achieve an individual’s goals in a situation characterized by uncertainty and vulnerability.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the context of trust and reliance, trust is an attitude and reliance is a behavior.
