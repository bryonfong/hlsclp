Corpus framing of this source: Economic theory of profit sharing, firm-specific capital and law-firm organisation

Corpus synthesis of this source: The article combines portfolio, agency and transaction-cost theory to explain why lawyers organise in firms and how partners divide profits. It argues that sharing can diversify lawyers’ human-capital risk and promote firm-wide value, but creates shirking, grabbing and leaving; productivity-based alternatives also recreate agency costs through incomplete measures and distorted incentives. It identifies stable client portfolios, client-specific knowledge, working relationships and reputational quality assurance as the principal firm-specific assets that constrain departures. It predicts that increasingly sophisticated in-house counsel, selective specialist procurement and internalisation of routine work will weaken those assets and pressure seniority-based sharing, while retaining qualifications about residual scale, scope and team advantages.
