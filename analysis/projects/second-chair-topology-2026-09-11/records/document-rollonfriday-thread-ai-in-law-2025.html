Open topic: Associate training: Automation threatens expertise reproduction

Corpus framing of this source: Anonymous lawyers debate AI’s effects on junior training, verification and firm economics

Corpus synthesis of this source: The RollOnFriday thread offers no unified position. Anonymous participants contrast poor and effective AI use, debate whether AI will reduce junior roles, stress the need for human verification, and speculate about profitability and distribution. One participant separately reproduces MS Copilot’s proposed model for adapting legal training; that AI-generated response is reported material, not necessarily the participant’s own view. The thread supplies practitioner anecdotes and predictions rather than systematic evidence.

Corpus framing of this source: AI in legal work and junior training: limits, adoption barriers and standardisation

Corpus synthesis of this source: Anonymous practitioners debate whether AI can absorb routine legal work and how that would affect training. The thread contrasts a Copilot-generated vision of earlier strategic development with posters’ concern that strategy depends on experience. Participants describe current legal AI as junior-level assistance requiring supervision, identify organisational and technical barriers to adoption, and favour standardisation or automation for repetitive documents. They also qualify demonstrations of generative AI outside law, distinguishing useful creative tooling from parity with professional human work.
