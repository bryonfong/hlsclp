Open topic: Justice gap: Fair resolution exceeds legal assistance

Corpus framing of this source: CRS documents unmet civil legal need and LSC funding constraints

Corpus synthesis of this source: Legal Services Corporation: Background and Funding (CRS Report RL34016) synthesises LSC studies, budget estimates and administrative records to describe persistent gaps in civil legal assistance, declining resources per eligible person, and the population and services LSC supports. The supplied material reports historical findings and policy changes rather than advancing an independent forecast or recommendation.

Corpus source-specific finding

LSC studies found substantial unmet need and a marked disparity in attorney availability.

Exact returned source excerpt

The LSC found that (1) 50% of potential clients requesting legal assistance were denied assistance because the LSC did not have the resources to help them; (2) at least 80% of persons who were eligible for LSC services did not get access to either a legal services attorney or a private attorney (pro bono or paid) when they need such assistance; and (3) there was one legal services attorney for every 6,415 low-income persons—the comparable number for the general population was one attorney for every 429 persons, a difference of 15 to 1.[10](#fn10) ## Funding When the LSC was first established, its initial goal was to provide all low-income people with at least "minimum access" to legal services, defined as the equivalent of one legal services attorney for every 5,000 poor persons.

Corpus framing of this source: LSC funding, unmet civil legal need and congressional controls

Corpus synthesis of this source: CRS documents the Legal Services Corporation’s delivery system, funding history and statutory constraints through December 2016. It reports LSC evidence of substantial unmet civil legal need and declining real resources, while distinguishing agency funding requests, competing congressional proposals and enacted appropriations. Its contribution is descriptive: recommendations and forecasts belong to LSC, administrations or congressional committees, not CRS.
