Corpus framing of this source: Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 26: disclosure, proportionality, e-discovery and discovery accountability

Corpus synthesis of this source: Rule 26 and its Advisory Committee Notes define federal civil discovery as access to relevant, nonprivileged information subject to proportionality, judicial control and protections for trial preparation. Across successive amendments, the source narrows and clarifies initial disclosure, integrates electronically stored information, establishes procedures for inaccessible sources and preservation, protects work product and expert communications, and uses planning, certification and sanctions to deter excessive or evasive discovery.

Corpus source-specific finding

Rule 26 frames discovery as fact-sharing rather than tactical combat and limits its scope to relevant, nonprivileged matter proportional to the case.

Exact returned source excerpt

The purpose of discovery is to provide a mechanism for making relevant information available to the litigants.

Exact returned source excerpt

Thus the spirit of the rules is violated when advocates attempt to use discovery tools as tactical weapons rather than to expose the facts and illuminate the issues by overuse of discovery or unnecessary use of defensive weapons or evasive responses.

Corpus source-specific finding

The amendments make proportionality a shared, case-specific responsibility and reinforce active judicial management.

Exact returned source excerpt

Restoring the proportionality calculation to Rule 26(b)(1) does not change the existing responsibilities of the court and the parties to consider proportionality, and the change does not place on the party seeking discovery the burden of addressing all proportionality considerations.

Exact returned source excerpt

Nor is the change intended to permit the opposing party to refuse discovery simply by making a boilerplate objection that it is not proportional.

Corpus source-specific finding

Initial disclosure covers material a party may use to support its claims or defenses, including electronically stored information, but not material it does not intend to use.

Exact returned source excerpt

(1) Initial Disclosure.* (A) *In General.* Except as exempted by Rule 26(a)(1)(B) or as otherwise stipulated or ordered by the court, a party must, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to the other parties: (i) the name and, if known, the address and telephone number of each individual likely to have discoverable information—along with the subjects of that information—that the disclosing party may use to support its claims or defenses, unless the use would be solely for impeachment; (ii) a copy—or a description by category and location—of all documents, electronically stored information, and tangible things that the disclosing party has in its possession, custody, or control and may use to support its claims or defenses, unless the use would be solely for impeachment; (iii) a computation of each category of damages claimed by the disclosing party—who must also make available for inspection and copying as under [Rule 34](/rules/frcp/rule_34) the documents or other evidentiary material, unless privileged or protected from disclosure, on which each computation is based, including materials bearing on the nature and extent of injuries suffered; and (iv) for inspection and copying as under [Rule 34](/rules/frcp/rule_34), any insurance agreement under which an insurance business may be liable to satisfy all or part of a possible judgment in the action or to indemnify or reimburse for payments made to satisfy the judgment.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rule 26(a)(1)(B) is amended to parallel Rule 34(a) by recognizing that a party must disclose electronically stored information as well as documents that it may use to support its claims or defenses.

Corpus source-specific finding

Specified proceedings may avoid initial disclosure, but the court retains case-management authority and the exemptions remain bounded.

Exact returned source excerpt

Specified categories of proceedings are excluded from initial disclosure under subdivision (a)(1)(E).

Exact returned source excerpt

In addition, the parties can stipulate to forgo disclosure, as was true before.

Corpus source-specific finding

Rule 26 treats electronically stored information broadly while recognising that accessibility and retrieval costs vary by system and source.

Exact returned source excerpt

The term “electronically stored information” has the same broad meaning in Rule 26(a)(1) as in Rule 34(a).

Exact returned source excerpt

Electronic storage systems often make it easier to locate and retrieve information.

Corpus source-specific finding

For electronically stored information claimed to be inaccessible, Rule 26 allocates burdens and permits focused testing, good-cause production and tailored conditions.

Exact returned source excerpt

On motion to compel discovery or for a protective order, the party from whom discovery is sought must show that the information is not reasonably accessible because of undue burden or cost.

Exact returned source excerpt

The requesting party has the burden of showing that its need for the discovery outweighs the burdens and costs of locating, retrieving, and producing the information.

Corpus source-specific finding

Early, informed discovery planning is the principal mechanism for reducing e-discovery uncertainty, expense and disputes.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rule 26(f) is amended to direct the parties to discuss discovery of electronically stored information during their discovery-planning conference.

Exact returned source excerpt

The rule focuses on “issues relating to disclosure or discovery of electronically stored information”; the discussion is not required in cases not involving electronic discovery, and the amendment imposes no additional requirements in those cases.

Corpus source-specific finding

Preservation planning must balance evidentiary needs against continued operations, and inaccessible status does not erase preservation duties.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rule 26(f) is also amended to direct the parties to discuss any issues regarding preservation of discoverable information during their conference as they develop a discovery plan.

Exact returned source excerpt

The volume and dynamic nature of electronically stored information may complicate preservation obligations.

Corpus source-specific finding

Privilege review poses heightened cost and delay in e-discovery, so the rule encourages advance agreements on handling privilege and post-production claims.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rule 26(f) is also amended to provide that the parties should discuss any issues relating to assertions of privilege or of protection as trial-preparation materials, including whether the parties can facilitate discovery by agreeing on procedures for asserting claims of privilege or protection after production and whether to ask the court to enter an order that includes any agreement the parties reach.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Committee has repeatedly been advised about the discovery difficulties that can result from efforts to guard against waiver of privilege and work-product protection.

Corpus source-specific finding

Trial-preparation protection requires more than relevance and safeguards both lawyer and nonlawyer preparatory work, especially mental impressions and legal theories.

Exact returned source excerpt

The rules are amended by eliminating the general requirement of “good cause” from Rule 34 but retaining a requirement of a special showing for trial preparation materials in this subdivision.

Exact returned source excerpt

The required showing is expressed, not in terms of “good cause” whose generality has tended to encourage confusion and controversy, but in terms of the elements of the special showing to be made: substantial need of the materials in the preparation of the case and inability without undue hardship to obtain the substantial equivalent of the materials by other means.

Corpus source-specific finding

The expert-discovery amendments protect drafts and specified attorney–expert communications while leaving expert opinions and their foundations open to examination.

Exact returned source excerpt

*Rule 26(b)(4)(B) is added to provide work-product protection under Rule 26(b)(3)(A) and (B) for drafts of expert reports or disclosures.

Exact returned source excerpt

It applies regardless of the form in which the draft is recorded, whether written, electronic, or otherwise.

Corpus source-specific finding

Rule 26(g) makes lawyers and unrepresented parties certify discovery conduct after a reasonable inquiry and uses sanctions to deter excess and evasion.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rule 26(g) imposes an affirmative duty to engage in pretrial discovery in a responsible manner that is consistent with the spirit and purposes of Rules 26 through 37.

Exact returned source excerpt

In addition, Rule 26(g) is designed to curb discovery abuse by explicitly encouraging the imposition of sanctions.

Corpus source-specific finding

The rule favours structured party planning and flexible court intervention over either unlimited discovery or rigid categorical limits.

Exact returned source excerpt

The revised rule directs that in all cases not exempted by local rule or special order the litigants must meet in person and plan for discovery.

Exact returned source excerpt

Following this meeting, the parties submit to the court their proposals for a discovery plan and can begin formal discovery.
