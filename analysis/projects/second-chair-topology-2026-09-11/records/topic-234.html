Native topic record

Executable rules: Formalisation conceals interpretive choices

Corpus synthesis: Contrasts computational demonstrations with legal understanding and converges on narrow, expert-informed, explainable support.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Legal institutions and professional practice: Substantive outcomes depend on context, capacity and judgment · Rule-based legal automation: Formalisation supports rather than replaces expert judgment

6 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 2 · Supports: 3 · Unclear: 1

Native topic findings

  1. The central dispute concerns whether executable legal rules capture expertise or provide only a bounded aid to legal work.
  2. Formalisation can conceal interpretive choices even when it successfully executes rules.
  3. The methodological disagreement concerns how legal understanding should guide computational design, not simply whether to use logic.
  4. Technical demonstrations and application examples do not settle the dispute over operational success.
  5. The practical convergence favours narrow, expert-informed support with substantive explanations and demonstrated user value.
  6. Predictions about growth and explanations of failure remain distinct from demonstrated outcomes.

Corpus framing of this source: Leith’s critique of legal expert systems: formalism, weak validation and neglected user needs

Corpus synthesis of this source: In The Rise and Fall of the Legal Expert System, Philip Leith revisits his early scepticism through a historical and jurisprudential critique. He attributes disappointing deployment to oversimplified models of law and logic, inadequate testing, poorly understood demand and funding incentives. He advocates technology that supports lawyers’ actual work, while distinguishing his retrospective judgments and counterfactual speculation from the views and deployment evidence he reports.
