Corpus framing of this source: UNISON bargaining guide on AI, workplace technology, monitoring and surveillance

Corpus synthesis of this source: UNISON presents workplace AI as a widely adopted, implementation-dependent technology that can augment workers, improve services and remove routine tasks, but can also displace exposed roles, intensify and deskill work, undermine autonomy, reproduce bias, generate false information and enable intrusive surveillance. It supports these claims with external research, public-sector and healthcare examples, and a reported union intervention, while arguing that safeguards and worker involvement shape whether benefits materialise.

Corpus framing of this source: UNISON bargaining framework for worker-centred AI and workplace technology

Corpus synthesis of this source: UNISON presents AI, algorithmic management, automation and surveillance as potentially beneficial but highly context-dependent workplace technologies. The guide combines legal analysis, research findings, union cases and model policies to argue for collective governance: legitimate and proportionate use, transparency, informed agreement, human review, recurring impact assessments, early trade-union involvement, protection of jobs and working conditions, and fair sharing of productivity gains. It also supports stronger statutory protections where existing UK law leaves gaps.

Corpus framing of this source: UNISON: negotiate workplace AI to protect job quality, rights and public services

Corpus synthesis of this source: UNISON’s September 2025 bargaining guide treats AI adoption as a workplace governance issue, not simply a productivity or headcount question. It combines reported research, polling and workplace examples with recommendations for collective agreements, transparency, human oversight, retraining and stronger employment protections. It recognises potential benefits but argues that implementation and worker involvement determine who gains and who bears the risks.
