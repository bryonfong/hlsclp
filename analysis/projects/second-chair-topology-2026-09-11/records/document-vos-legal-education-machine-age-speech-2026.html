Corpus framing of this source: Sir Geoffrey Vos on lawyers and legal education in the machine age

Corpus synthesis of this source: Vos argues that AI is already changing court participation, client demand and law-firm recruitment, while predicting that lawyers will retain roles requiring explanation, client guidance and advocacy before human judges. He recommends a comprehensive redesign of legal education around enduring doctrinal knowledge, technology-related ethics, data protection, data security and the detection of AI-generated fakes.

Corpus framing of this source: Sir Geoffrey Vos on justice, lawyers and legal education in the machine age

Corpus synthesis of this source: Vos predicts radical but not precisely foreseeable AI-driven changes to justice over the next 15–20 years. He expects machines to handle or shape routine decisions and legal information while humans retain responsibility for trust, explanation, legal development and consequential adjudication. He calls for lawyers and legal education to adapt accordingly, while flagging unresolved legal constraints.
