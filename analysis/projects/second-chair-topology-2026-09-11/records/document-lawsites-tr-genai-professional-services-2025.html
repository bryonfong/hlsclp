Open topic: Legal workflows: Immediate gains create competence risks

Corpus framing of this source: Thomson Reuters: Legal Gen AI Adoption Is Accelerating but Implementation Lags

Corpus synthesis of this source: The 2025 Thomson Reuters report characterises legal-sector generative AI as an accelerating but evolutionary development. It documents rising adoption and optimism, predicts that AI will become central to legal workflows, identifies established use cases, and highlights shortcomings in governance, training, ROI measurement, client visibility and skills-based recruitment. Robert Ambrogi contextualises its adoption findings with results from other industry surveys, while Thomson Reuters executive Raghu Ramanathan separately frames AI as augmenting rather than replacing legal expertise.

Corpus source-specific finding

Legal organisations report rising adoption and more positive sentiment toward generative AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

According to the report, 26% of legal organizations are now actively using gen AI, up from 14% in 2024.

Exact returned source excerpt

In 2024, hesitancy was the predominant reaction (35%), but in 2025, excitement (27%) and hopefulness (28%) have taken the lead, while hesitancy has dropped to 24%.

Corpus source-specific finding

Legal professionals principally use generative AI for document review, legal research and summarisation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Document review (77%), legal research (74%), and document summarization (74%) remain the top use cases for legal professionals.

Corpus source-specific finding

Respondents expect generative AI to become central to legal workflows, although relatively few firms consider it central today.

Exact returned source excerpt

While only 15% of law firm respondents say gen AI is currently central to their workflow, a striking 78% believe it will become central within the next five years.

Exact returned source excerpt
