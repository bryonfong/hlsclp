Corpus framing of this source: Katsoris: concrete damages and causation are essential to legal-malpractice claims

Corpus synthesis of this source: In Katsoris v. Bodnar & Milone, LLP, 186 A.D.3d 1504, 131 N.Y.S.3d 89 (2d Dep't 2020), the court applies established malpractice principles to allegations concerning a divorce settlement. It upholds dismissal because the plaintiff failed to plead ascertainable damages and factual support for a better outcome absent counsel's alleged negligence, and dismisses a duplicative fiduciary-duty claim.

Corpus source-specific finding

Malpractice requires a departure from ordinary professional competence that causes actual, ascertainable damages.

Exact returned source excerpt

"To state a cause of action to recover damages for legal malpractice, a plaintiff must allege: (1) that the attorney failed to exercise the ordinary reasonable skill and knowledge commonly possessed by a member of the legal profession; and (2) that the attorney's breach of the duty proximately caused the plaintiff actual and ascertainable damages" (_Dempster v Liotti_, [86 AD3d 169, 176](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5969148/dempster-v-liotti/#176) [internal quotation marks omitted]; _see Nomura Asset Capital Corp.

Exact returned source excerpt

As to the first prong, "[a]n attorney may be liable for ignorance of the rules of practice, for failure [*2]to comply with conditions precedent to suit, for neglect to prosecute or defend an action, or for failure to conduct adequate legal research" (_Conklin v Owen_, [72 AD3d 1006, 1007](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5939676/conklin-v-owen/#1007); _see Dempster v Liotti_, [86 AD3d at 176](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5969148/dempster-v-liotti/#176)).

Corpus source-specific finding

A settlement does not bar malpractice liability, but the plaintiff must establish causation.

Exact returned source excerpt

To establish causation in a legal malpractice action, "a plaintiff must show that he or she would have prevailed in the underlying action or would not have incurred any damages, but for the lawyer's negligence" (_Rudolf v Shayne, Dachs, Stanisci, Corker & Sauer_, [8 NY3d at 442](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2086789/rudolf-v-shayne-dachs-stanisci-corker-sauer/#442); _see Keness v Feldman, Kramer & Monaco, P.C._, [105 AD3d 812, 813](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6054088/keness-v-feldman-kramer-monaco-pc/#813)).

Corpus framing of this source: Katsoris v. Bodnar & Milone, LLP: Pleading Damages and Causation in Legal Malpractice

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Appellate Division applies New York legal-malpractice pleading standards to claims arising from counsel’s handling of a divorce and settlement. It explains the requirements for actual damages and but-for causation, recognizes protection for reasonable strategic choices, and concludes that the plaintiff’s generalized financial and settlement allegations lacked sufficient factual support.
