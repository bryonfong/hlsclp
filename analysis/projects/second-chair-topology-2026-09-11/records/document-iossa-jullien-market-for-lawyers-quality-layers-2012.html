Corpus framing of this source: Lawyer quality can improve welfare while certification distorts judicial decisions

Corpus synthesis of this source: Iossa and Jullien’s 2012 theoretical study distinguishes the benefits of lawyer quality from the effects of publicly certifying it. Their model links judges’ reputational concerns to favoritism toward certified lawyers and inefficient market allocation. The supplied excerpts report supporting literature and a model extension, rather than a new empirical test.

Corpus source-specific finding

The model treats lawyer quality as an informational advantage within strategic litigation.

Exact returned source excerpt

We show that reputational concerns generate a decision bias in favor of certified lawyers and that this causes misallocation of lawyers at the market equilibrium.

Corpus framing of this source: Lawyer quality, decision accuracy and social welfare

Corpus synthesis of this source: Iossa and Jullien present a theoretical model in which higher-quality, certified lawyers improve judicial accuracy and social welfare through superior interpretation of evidence. They define welfare by weighing correct decisions and contractual-enforcement benefits against appeal costs, then test the result against less stylised assumptions about lawyer quality and interpretive ability.

Corpus source-specific finding

Certified lawyers increase social welfare by improving decision accuracy.

Exact returned source excerpt

The social welfare is higher if there are certified lawyers than if all lawyers are of low quality.

Intuitively, the quality of lawyers is relevant, because it improves the accuracy of the decision by two mechanisms.
