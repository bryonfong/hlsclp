Corpus framing of this source: Expanding BigLaw Bench: diagnosing legal-model knowledge and framing sensitivity

Corpus synthesis of this source: Harvey presents BigLaw Bench (BLB) as a diagnostic framework for examining whether models have internalized legal rules, can recall information, perform legal tasks and resist sycophantic framing. It describes a prompt-variation experiment, explains how lawyer review informs model selection and guardrails, and cautions that BLB provides insights rather than a complete solution.

Corpus source-specific finding

Harvey uses individual BLB tasks as diagnostic canaries or bellwethers for model knowledge and task performance.

Exact returned source excerpt

This single BLB task serves as a canary for collecting additional data on any model’s internalization of legal rules.

Exact returned source excerpt

It also gives us the ability to mitigate this gap for our customers through the use of better aligned models or access to live data.

Corpus source-specific finding

BLB tests behavior against lawyer-defined standards, including whether models favor agreeable answers over correct ones.

Exact returned source excerpt

#### Testing Model Behavior Beyond understanding, BLB also allowed us to probe model behaviors by pre-aligning on what defines an accurate response.

Exact returned source excerpt

In 2025, there was a concern about model sycophancy: Models would aim to be pleasant rather than correct.

Corpus source-specific finding

Harvey tests framing sensitivity by holding the legal issue constant while changing the perspective expressed in the prompt.

Exact returned source excerpt

Instead of looking at how the models performed on this task, we looked at whether performance changed when we rephrased the BLB prompt.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the side letter example, we posed the question three different ways: (1) neutrally, (2) as the general partner hoping to find a loophole, and (3) as the MFN holder hoping to obtain the proposed benefit.

Harvey treats changes in reasoning as more informative than answer accuracy alone and uses lawyer review to identify sycophancy and design guardrails.
