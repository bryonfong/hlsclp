Corpus framing of this source: Generalist AI, legal-tech competition and regulatory change

Corpus synthesis of this source: Sean West argues that capable general-purpose LLMs challenge the differentiation and valuations of specialised legal-tech products. He distinguishes OpenAI’s apparent lack of focus on owning legal services from Microsoft’s opportunity to expand into adjacent enterprise legal markets. His central prediction is that adoption by lawyers and large technology companies will create constituencies and political momentum for rules permitting AI to perform more legal work, eventually including the practice of law. He cites observed reliance on LLMs in general terms but supplies no specific data, regulatory proposal or recommendation.

Corpus framing of this source: Sean West on AI, rule-of-law erosion and legal-market adaptation

Corpus synthesis of this source: West connects legal AI to a wider interaction among politics, technology and weakening rules. He argues that cheap, readily accessible AI will diffuse outside formal procurement, passively displace work, lower the cost of asserting claims and intensify regulatory arbitrage. He urges businesses and law firms to build situational awareness, prepare litigation-management systems, develop higher-value advisory work and handle workforce changes transparently.
