Corpus framing of this source: Artificial Intelligence and Human Legal Reasoning

Corpus synthesis of this source: Bednar, Cleveland, Erbsen and Schwarcz report a preregistered randomized controlled trial on whether AI-assisted legal synthesis affects later independent comprehension and application. They find large gains in synthesis quality, speed and productivity; no short-term comprehension loss; better subsequent application through stronger initial synthesis; and divergent effects when AI revises existing work, improving weaker memos but degrading stronger ones. They interpret the results as evidence that AI’s effects depend on task design, user expertise, verification time and cognitive conditions, while stressing limits on external and long-term inference.
