Native topic findings

  1. Platforms exercise private governance as they become infrastructure for economic and social life.
  2. Economic incentives and technical architecture jointly explain concentrated platform power.
  3. Platforms enable external innovation while retaining control over its conditions.
  4. Consumer benefits and efficient provision do not settle questions about collective costs, exclusion or discrimination.
  5. The accounts differ in explanatory emphasis and leave the future of platform dominance unsettled.
  6. The practical agenda centres on infrastructural accountability and sector-specific public-value choices.

The Platform Society: Public Values in a Connective World

Academia & research · Automation debate (2015-2022)

Corpus framing of this source: Platforms as infrastructures that organise society and contest public values

Corpus synthesis of this source: In The Platform Society: Public Values in a Connective World (2018), José van Dijck, Thomas Poell and Martijn de Waal develop a political-economic account of platforms as intertwined technical, commercial and governance structures. The supplied excerpts offer conceptual reasoning and illustrative cases, rather than quantified causal findings or forecasts. They distinguish promises of empowerment and disruption from their own argument that platforms reshape existing institutions, concentrate infrastructural power and provoke disputes over collective benefits.

Corpus source-specific finding

Platforms transform existing institutions rather than create a separate, revolutionary social order.

Exact returned source excerpt

Some management and business scholars have touted this development the “platform revolution” and focus on the positive economic effects of a technological development they hail as “innovative disruption” (Parker, Van Alstyne, Choudary 2016; Sundararajan 2016).

Exact returned source excerpt

Platforms, in our view, do not cause a revolution; instead, they are gradually infiltrating in, and converging with, the (offline, legacy) institutions and practices through which democratic societies are organized.

Corpus source-specific finding

A concentrated infrastructural core governs the wider platform ecosystem.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Western ecosystem is mostly operated by a handful of big tech companies (Alphabet-Google, Apple, Facebook, Amazon, and Microsoft) whose infrastructural services are central to the ecosystem’s overall design and the distribution of data flows.

Exact returned source excerpt

Most influential are the infrastructural platforms, many of them owned and operated by the Big Five; they form the heart of the ecosystem upon which many other platforms and apps can be built.

Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt

Now let us first look more closely at the elements that construct a single platform’s anatomy: a platform is fueled by data, automated and organized through algorithms and interfaces, formalized through ownership relations driven by business models, and governed through user agreements.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

Infrastructure studies meet platform studies in the age of Google and Facebook

Academia & research · Automation debate (2015-2022)

Corpus framing of this source: How platforms become infrastructures—and reorganize public services around private control

Corpus synthesis of this source: Plantin, Lagoze, Edwards and Sandvig combine infrastructure studies with platform studies to explain digital systems that both support everyday life and organize participation for corporate benefit. Their contribution rests on historical interpretation, a literature synthesis and cases of the Open Web, Facebook and Google. They acknowledge innovation and efficiency gains while examining lock-in, exclusion and the tension between public service and private control.

Corpus source-specific finding

Treat infrastructure and platform as complementary analytical lenses, not mutually exclusive categories.

Exact returned source excerpt

Through case studies of Google, Facebook, and the Open Web, we have shown that rather than distinguishing two entirely separate objects, these approaches instead offer different lenses on phenomena fundamental to everyday life in contemporary societies.

Exact returned source excerpt

We argue that the rise of digital technologies, in a neoliberal, political, and economic climate, has simultaneously facilitated a “platformization” of infrastructures and an “infrastructuralization” of platforms.

Corpus source-specific finding

The two research traditions reveal different mechanisms of dependency and power.

Exact returned source excerpt

Dependency stems from the qualities of ubiquity, reliability, and especially durability; major infrastructures such as railroads, telephone networks, Linnaean taxonomy, and the Internet endure over many decades, even centuries.

Exact returned source excerpt

Evolving from systems to networks to webs, infrastructures integrate many heterogeneous components by means of sociotechnical gateways.

Corpus framing of this source: Public utility thinking as a response to private platform power

Corpus synthesis of this source: The New Utilities argues that major internet platforms increasingly function as social and economic infrastructure. It proposes public utility thinking as a way to identify and address the private power this role creates, while acknowledging the benefits of consolidated platform control. The supplied passage offers a conceptual argument rather than empirical findings or a detailed regulatory programme.

Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt

Whether it is Google, Facebook, Amazon, Uber, or Airbnb, these internet giants all share a common structure.

Exact returned source excerpt

They operate online interfaces that function as marketplaces or clearinghouses-in short, as platforms-linking producers and consumers of goods, services, and information.

Exact returned source excerpt

The benefits of platforms depend on consolidated control-but platform power presents another form of problematic private power with outsized abilities to set the terms of exchange, influencing wages, prices, and standards, and extracting rents in the process.
