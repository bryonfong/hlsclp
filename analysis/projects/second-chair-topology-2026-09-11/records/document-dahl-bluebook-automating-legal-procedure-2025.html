Corpus framing of this source: Bluebook benchmarking reveals limits in procedural compliance and long-context learning

Corpus synthesis of this source: Bye-bye, Bluebook? Automating Legal Procedure with Large Language Models tests citation formatting as a measure of legal procedure-following distinct from substantive legal reasoning. It reports incomplete compliance across five flagship models and limited improvement when Gemini receives the underlying citation rules.

Corpus source-specific finding

The paper builds an expert-grounded benchmark to isolate procedural rule-following.

Exact returned source excerpt

We aggregate an original dataset of 866 challenging Bluebook formatting tasks, complete with ground-truth answers provided by experts.

Exact returned source excerpt

The underlying content and ground-truth answers for each task are sourced from two legal writing resources: the Interactive Citation Workbook for The Bluebook: A Uniform System of Citation (Norton, 2024) and Understanding and Mastering The Bluebook: A Guide for Students and Practitioners (Barris, 2020).

Corpus source-specific finding

The evaluation compares five models under zero-shot conditions, with explicit testing constraints.

Exact returned source excerpt

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Bye-bye, Bluebook? Automating Legal Procedure with Large Language Models

Corpus synthesis of this source: Matthew Dahl’s May 2025 working paper uses Bluebook citation formatting as a narrow benchmark of whether current general-purpose LLMs can follow long, intricate legal procedures. It finds that off-the-shelf models remain too unreliable to replace human citation diligence, identifies substantial task variation, memorization effects and occasionally consequential errors, and calls for cautious human-supervised use and broader procedural evaluation.

Corpus source-specific finding

The paper fills a procedural-evaluation gap and treats Bluebook compliance as a narrow proxy for rule-following in legal practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

Missing from the conversation has been analysis of whether LLMs can comply with the important formal requirements that typify the practice of law—procedural rules related to the structure, timing, and formatting of legal documents.

Exact returned source excerpt

In this paper, we begin to fill this gap by evaluating LLMs’ abilities to adhere to the requirements of one notoriously complicated source of American legal procedure—The Bluebook: A Uniform System of Citation (Columbia Law Review et al., 2020).222Available online at https://www.legalbluebook.com/.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study tests five flagship LLMs on an original, expert-grounded dataset and separately tests long-context rule provision.

Exact returned source excerpt

To evaluate whether large language models (LLMs) are able to adhere to the procedures of such a complicated system, we construct an original dataset of 866 Bluebook tasks and test flagship LLMs from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and DeepSeek.

Exact returned source excerpt

The underlying content and ground-truth answers for each task are sourced from two legal writing resources: the Interactive Citation Workbook for The Bluebook: A Uniform System of Citation (Norton, 2024) and Understanding and Mastering The Bluebook: A Guide for Students and Practitioners (Barris, 2020).

Corpus source-specific finding

Current off-the-shelf LLMs do not achieve reliable automatic Bluebook compliance.

Exact returned source excerpt

Overall, performance is mediocre, ranging from 74% average accuracy at the high end (GPT 4.1) to 69% average accuracy at the low end (Llama 3.1 405B).

Exact returned source excerpt

These results suggest that LLMs should not be used in a zero-shot setting to replace human Bluebook diligence.

Performance varies sharply by task, and some apparent case-law strength reflects memorization rather than rule application.

The models perform the best—but not perfectly—on case law tasks (blue); they perform much worse on enacted law (yellow) and other tasks (green).

Exact returned source excerpt

They are especially strong at generating volume, reporter, and page combinations (pooled average = 96%) and court and date abbreviations (pooled average = 90%).
