Open topic: AI filings: Disclosure rules divide otherwise similar courts

Corpus framing of this source: Federal Court of Canada: disclosure of AI-generated litigation materials

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Federal Court’s amended notice of 7 May 2024 establishes a disclosure regime for AI-generated litigation materials. It distinguishes AI co-authorship from advisory editing, explains exceptions and practical qualifications, and grounds disclosure in fairness and judicial integrity. It also commits the Court to consultation before using AI to make decisions or render judgments.

Corpus source-specific finding

Require a first-paragraph declaration identifying AI-generated content.

Exact returned source excerpt

Declaration for AI-Generated Content This Notice applies to all materials that are (i) submitted to the Court, and (ii) prepared for the purpose of litigation.

Exact returned source excerpt

This shall be done by a Declaration in the first paragraph stating that AI was used in preparing the document, either in its entirety or only for specifically identified paragraphs (the “Declaration”).

Corpus source-specific finding

Distinguish AI co-authorship from suggestions and non-generative automation.

Exact returned source excerpt

For clarity, a Declaration is required if content in the material was directly provided by AI, whether or not it was inserted from an external source like a web-based generative AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

A Declaration is required when the role AI plays in the preparation of materials for the purpose of litigation resembles that of a co-author.

Corpus framing of this source: Federal Court guidance on disclosure, caution and consultation for AI use

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Federal Court of Canada’s amended notice of 7 May 2024 requires disclosure of AI-generated content in litigation materials and sets out principles for its use. It grounds its approach in transparency, equal treatment and risk management, while acknowledging AI’s benefits. The supplied excerpts provide procedural guidance and institutional commitments, not empirical findings.

Corpus source-specific finding

Disclose AI-generated content in litigation filings through a first-paragraph declaration.

Exact returned source excerpt

This Notice requires counsel, parties, and interveners in legal proceedings at the Federal Court to make the Declaration, and to consider certain principles (the “Principles”), when using AI to prepare materials filed with the Court.

Exact returned source excerpt

This shall be done by a Declaration in the first paragraph stating that AI was used in preparing the document, either in its entirety or only for specifically identified paragraphs (the “Declaration”).

Corpus source-specific finding

The notice targets generated content, not every use of AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

Declaration for AI-Generated Content This Notice applies to all materials that are (i) submitted to the Court, and (ii) prepared for the purpose of litigation.

Exact returned source excerpt

It bears underscoring that this Notice only applies to content that was created or generated by AI.

Corpus source-specific finding

Equal treatment justifies applying explicit responsibilities to represented and self-represented participants.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Court recognizes that counsel have duties as Officers of the Court.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, these duties do not extend to individuals representing themselves.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI’s potential benefits coexist with risks that warrant caution and reliable legal sources.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Court recognizes both the risks and benefits of AI, and the potential for bias in AI programs, their underlying algorithms, and data sets.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Court also recognizes that counsel, parties, interveners and the administrative bodies whose decisions they may challenge may increasingly rely on - or be impacted by - AI.

Corpus source-specific finding

Counsel should consider human services when clients lack familiarity with AI or do not want it used.

Exact returned source excerpt

Further, the Court understands that there are both ethical and access to justice issues regarding a lawyer’s use of AI when their client may not be familiar with AI and its various applications.

Exact returned source excerpt

Before using AI in a proceeding, the Court encourages counsel to consider providing traditional, human services to clients if there is reason to believe a client may not be familiar with, or may not wish to use, AI.

Corpus source-specific finding

The Court commits to consultation before judicial AI decision-making and continued revision of its guidance.

Exact returned source excerpt

For its part, the Court will not use AI, and more specifically automated decision-making tools, to make its decisions or render its judgments, without first engaging in public consultation.

Exact returned source excerpt

This guidance has benefited from feedback received from various stakeholders.
