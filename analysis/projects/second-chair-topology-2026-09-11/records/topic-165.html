Native topic findings

  1. Organizational invention arises when practices crossing social domains reshape the networks that sustain them.
  2. Florentine partnerships illustrate a distinction between political origins and later social institutionalization.
  3. Biotechnology extends the argument from new firms to geographically concentrated institutional communities.
  4. The central disagreement concerns technological sufficiency and inevitable progress, not whether technology matters.
  5. Network reinforcement can sustain both institutional resilience and resistance to novelty; tipping conditions remain unresolved.
  6. The practical implication is to study changing relationships over time, not infer institutional transformation from adoption alone.

Transposition and Refunctionality: The Birth of Partnership Systems in Renaissance Florence

Academia & research · Foundations (pre-2015)

Corpus framing of this source: Florentine partnership systems as inventions of interconnected social networks

Corpus synthesis of this source: John F. Padgett explains the emergence of Florentine partnership systems through feedback among business, political and kinship networks. His historical account distinguishes organizational invention from incremental innovation, links partnership formation to post-Ciompi political mobilization, and traces its institutionalization through elite marriage and social class. He offers a mechanism-based explanation rather than a predictive theory or an account of inevitable modernization.
