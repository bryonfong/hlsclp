Corpus framing of this source: ABA Formal Opinion 471: Client-file surrender as a duty to protect former clients

Corpus synthesis of this source: The ABA Standing Committee’s July 2015 opinion updates its earlier guidance on file surrender under Model Rules 1.15 and 1.16(d). It sets a minimum end-product obligation, requires additional internal materials when needed to protect an unfinished matter, distinguishes broader jurisdictional requirements, and recommends practical file-management arrangements.

Corpus source-specific finding

The opinion defines an ethical minimum, not the full extent of a client’s legal rights.

Exact returned source excerpt

This opinion addresses the ethical duties of a lawyer pursuant to the ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct, when responding to a former client’s request for papers and property in the lawyer’s possession that are related to the representation.

Exact returned source excerpt

The opinion does not address a client’s property rights or other legal rights to these materials.

Corpus source-specific finding

Client protection governs the surrender obligation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Model Rule 1.15 provides that a lawyer must safeguard a client’s property and promptly deliver it to the client upon the client’s request.

Exact returned source excerpt

As noted above, Model Rule 1.16(d) requires a lawyer to take steps to the extent reasonably practicable to protect a client’s interest.

Corpus framing of this source: ABA Formal Opinion 471 on surrendering former-client files

Corpus synthesis of this source: The ABA Committee interprets lawyers’ duties under Model Rules 1.15 and 1.16(d) when a representation ends. It distinguishes client-facing end products from materials generated primarily for internal use, creates a protective exception for unfinished matters, describes competing jurisdictional approaches, addresses retention and duplicate-production qualifications, and recommends regular document sharing during the representation.

Corpus source-specific finding

Termination duty protects the former client’s interests

Exact returned source excerpt

35 Conclusion Upon the termination of a representation, a lawyer is required under Model Rules 1.15 and 1.16(d) to take steps to the extent reasonably practicable to protect a client’s interest, and such steps include surrendering to the former client papers and property to which the former client is entitled such as materials provided to the lawyer, legal documents filed or executed, and such other papers and properties identified in this opinion.

Exact returned source excerpt

This duty applies even when the lawyer believes the client’s discharge is unfair.

Corpus source-specific finding

Internal work product generally need not be surrendered after a completed matter

Exact returned source excerpt

A client is not entitled to papers and property that the lawyer generated for the lawyer’s own purpose in working on the client’s matter.

Exact returned source excerpt

In contrast, under these facts, it is unlikely that within the meaning of Rule 1.16(d), the client is entitled to papers or other property in the lawyer’s possession that the lawyer generated for internal use primarily for the lawyer’s own purpose in working on the municipality’s matters.

Corpus source-specific finding

Retention obligations are fact-specific and remain partly outside the opinion

Exact returned source excerpt

This opinion does not address a lawyer’s obligations to retain specific material relating to a representation in the first instance (whether in paper or electronic form).

Exact returned source excerpt

However, a lawyer’s duty under Rule 1.16(d) to “surrender papers and property to which the client is entitled” at the termination of a representation necessarily requires some consideration of this issue.
