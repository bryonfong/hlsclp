Corpus framing of this source: Strong 2021 finances masked rising talent costs and retention risks

Corpus synthesis of this source: The 2022 Report on the State of the Legal Market: A Challenging Road to Recovery, by Thomson Reuters Institute and Georgetown Law’s Center on Ethics and the Legal Profession, documents an uneven demand recovery, stronger pricing and collections, and continued profit growth. Using mainly data through November 2021, it argues that hiring costs and associate departures threatened that financial momentum and identifies talent management, hybrid work and operational flexibility as priorities for 2022.

Corpus synthesis of this source: Thomson Reuters Institute and Georgetown Law’s 2022 report describes a strong financial recovery alongside mounting workforce pressures. It cautions against using volatile 2020 results as a comparison baseline and identifies talent competition, retention, hybrid work and operational flexibility as priorities for 2022.

Corpus framing of this source: Post-pandemic law-firm recovery depends on rethinking talent, hybrid work and operations

Corpus synthesis of this source: The 2022 report presents a financially resilient but operationally unsettled U.S. legal market. Drawing on reported financial results from 171 firms and supplementary surveys and preliminary turnover analysis, it argues that talent retention, changing worker expectations, equitable hybrid work and continued operational flexibility will matter more than restoring pre-pandemic practices.

Corpus source-specific finding

The market recovered strongly in 2021, but growth was uneven and generated costly talent pressures.

Exact returned source excerpt

From a financial standpoint, demand growth continued to rise at a healthy pace, mirroring the trend seen in the second half of 2020.1 Firms were also able to maintain a fairly steady increase in working rates, while achieving very strong realization, again reflecting a pattern seen in 2020.

Exact returned source excerpt

Compared to 2019, however, demand growth — as shown with the numbers on the bottom of Figure 3 — was more modest, with only the corporate (including M&A) and real estate practices showing positive growth.

Corpus source-specific finding

Talent became a leading profitability risk despite large compensation increases.

Exact returned source excerpt

In order of priority, the risks included: (i) lawyer recruitment and retention; (ii) poaching of staff by competitors; and (iii) increasing associate salaries.

Exact returned source excerpt

These results were in stark contrast to the 2020 survey in which talent issues didn’t even make the top five risks threatening law firm profitability.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report questions a compensation-led talent strategy and argues that retention depends substantially on less tangible sources of attachment.

Exact returned source excerpt

In response to the fierce competition for talent, firms are spending huge amounts of money and putting their profits at increasing risk for fairly modest returns — at least if you consider the real costs of high levels of lawyer turnover.

Exact returned source excerpt

The intriguing question thus becomes, whether refocusing recruitment strategies to emphasize factors other than (or in addition to) compensation might not prove more productive?
