SupportsBusiness disruption: Incumbents must separate experiments credibly

Open topic: Business disruption: Incumbents must separate experiments credibly

Corpus framing of this source: Disruption as a market trajectory and a guide to incumbent strategy

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Christensen source defines disruption as a business-model-led movement from fringe markets into the mainstream, not an intrinsic property of technology. It uses minicomputers, retail, Uber and the iPhone to distinguish competitive paths and recommends protecting profitable core operations while developing separate disruptive businesses.

Corpus source-specific finding

Disruption describes an evolving market trajectory, not a product at a single moment.

Exact returned source excerpt

The term “disruptive innovation” is misleading when it is used to refer to a product or service at one fixed point, rather than to the evolution of that product or service over time.

Exact returned source excerpt

The first minicomputers were disruptive not merely because they were low-end upstarts when they appeared on the scene, nor because they were later heralded as superior to mainframes in many markets; they were disruptive by virtue of the path they followed from the fringe to the mainstream.

Corpus source-specific finding

Disruption need not produce rapid or complete substitution.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, more than 50 years after the first discount department store was opened, mainstream retail companies still operate their traditional department-store formats.

Exact returned source excerpt

Complete substitution, if it comes at all, may take decades, because the incremental profit from staying with the old model for one more year trumps proposals to write off the assets in one stroke.

Corpus source-specific finding

Managers should distinguish peripheral competitors, disruptive entrants and direct attacks on core customers.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, small competitors that nibble away at the periphery of your business very likely should be ignored—unless they are on a disruptive trajectory, in which case they are a potentially mortal threat.

Exact returned source excerpt

And both of these challenges are fundamentally different from efforts by competitors to woo your bread-and-butter customers.

Corpus source-specific finding

Similar platform models can follow different competitive paths.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, both Uber and Apple’s iPhone owe their success to a platform-based model: Uber digitally connects riders with drivers; the iPhone connects app developers with phone users.

Exact returned source excerpt

But Uber, true to its nature as a sustaining innovation, has focused on expanding its network and functionality in ways that make it better than traditional taxis.

Corpus source-specific finding

Incumbents should sustain profitable core businesses while pursuing disruption through separate operations.

Exact returned source excerpt

Incumbent companies do need to respond to disruption if it’s occurring, but they should not overreact by dismantling a still-profitable business.

Exact returned source excerpt

Instead, they should continue to strengthen relationships with core customers by investing in sustaining innovations.
OpposesDisruption theory: Historical examples weakly fit predictions

Open topic: Disruption theory: Historical examples weakly fit predictions

Corpus framing of this source: Disruptive innovation: a specific market trajectory, not a synonym for upheaval

Corpus synthesis of this source: Christensen, Raynor and McDonald’s 2015 restatement defines disruption through entrants’ movement from overlooked markets into the mainstream. It explains the incentives behind that movement, distinguishes disruption from sustaining innovation, and recommends a qualified dual response for incumbents. The authors claim empirical support while acknowledging limits to prediction and managerial prescriptions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Disruption begins in overlooked low-end or new markets and develops through mainstream adoption.

Exact returned source excerpt

*Low-end footholds* exist because incumbents typically try to provide their most profitable and demanding customers with ever-improving products and services, and they pay less attention to less-demanding customers.

Exact returned source excerpt

By contrast, new-market disruptions take hold in a completely new value network and appeal to customers who have previously gone without the product.

Corpus source-specific finding

Customer dependence and resource-allocation routines explain incumbent vulnerability; competition pushes entrants upmarket.

Exact returned source excerpt

Empirical findings showed that incumbents outperformed entrants in a sustaining innovation context but underperformed in a disruptive innovation context.

Exact returned source excerpt

Researchers then arrived at a second insight: Incumbents’ focus on their existing customers becomes institutionalized in internal processes that make it difficult for even senior managers to shift investment to disruptive innovations.

Corpus source-specific finding

Disruption describes a trajectory, not a product label or a guarantee of success.

Exact returned source excerpt

If we get sloppy with our labels or fail to integrate insights from subsequent research and experience into the original theory, then managers may end up using the wrong tools for their context, reducing their chances of success.

Exact returned source excerpt

The term “disruptive innovation” is misleading when it is used to refer to a product or service at one fixed point, rather than to the evolution of that product or service over time.

Corpus source-specific finding

Uber, the iPhone and care clinics show why market context and business models matter.

Exact returned source excerpt

Uber’s financial and strategic achievements do not qualify the company as genuinely disruptive—although the company is almost always described that way.

Exact returned source excerpt

But Uber, true to its nature as a sustaining innovation, has focused on expanding its network and functionality in ways that make it better than traditional taxis.

Corpus source-specific finding

Tesla illustrates the authors’ prediction that head-on entry provokes incumbent defense.

Exact returned source excerpt

The theory of disruption predicts that when an entrant tackles incumbent competitors head-on, offering better products or services, the incumbents will accelerate their innovations to defend their business.

Exact returned source excerpt

But its foothold is in the high end of the auto market (with customers willing to spend $70,000 or more on a car), and this segment is not uninteresting to incumbents.

Corpus source-specific finding

Incumbents should preserve profitable core operations while exploring disruption separately.

Exact returned source excerpt

Incumbent companies do need to respond to disruption if it’s occurring, but they should not overreact by dismantling a still-profitable business.

Exact returned source excerpt

Instead, they should continue to strengthen relationships with core customers by investing in sustaining innovations.

Corpus source-specific finding

Anomalies refine the theory, while variation in speed does not imply a different mechanism.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, we originally assumed that any disruptive innovation took root in the lowest tiers of an established market—yet sometimes new entrants seemed to be competing in entirely new markets.

Exact returned source excerpt

This led to the distinction we discussed earlier between low-end and new-market footholds.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors claim empirical predictive value without presenting a universal theory of business success.

Exact returned source excerpt

The key elements of that theory have been tested and validated through studies of many industries, including retail, computers, printing, motorcycles, cars, semiconductors, cardiovascular surgery, management education, financial services, management consulting, cameras, communications, and computer-aided design software.

Exact returned source excerpt

But there is cause for hope: Empirical tests show that using disruptive theory makes us measurably and significantly more accurate in our predictions of which fledgling businesses will succeed.

Corpus framing of this source: Christensen on the scope, mechanisms and management of disruptive innovation

Corpus synthesis of this source: Christensen presents disruption as a particular market trajectory and business-model pattern rather than a synonym for technological novelty or commercial success. He uses Netflix, Apple, Uber, Tesla and health-care delivery to distinguish disruptive from sustaining paths, explain incumbent responses and define the theory’s practical limits.

Corpus source-specific finding

Disruption describes a trajectory, not success itself.

Exact returned source excerpt

A third common mistake is to focus on the results achieved—to claim that a company is disruptive by virtue of its success.

Exact returned source excerpt

But success is not built into the definition of disruption: Not every disruptive path leads to a triumph, and not every triumphant newcomer follows a disruptive path.

Corpus source-specific finding

Disruptive and sustaining strategies can use similar technologies or platforms but produce different competitive trajectories.

Exact returned source excerpt

This creates a danger: Managers may mix and match behaviors that are very likely inconsistent with one another and thus unlikely to yield the hoped-for result.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, both Uber and Apple’s iPhone owe their success to a platform-based model: Uber digitally connects riders with drivers; the iPhone connects app developers with phone users.

Corpus source-specific finding

Apple’s iPhone moved from sustaining smartphone innovation to disruption of laptop-based internet access through a new business model.

Exact returned source excerpt

The product that Apple debuted in 2007 was a sustaining innovation in the smartphone market: It targeted the same customers coveted by incumbents, and its initial success is likely explained by product superiority.

Exact returned source excerpt

The iPhone’s subsequent growth is better explained by disruption—not of other smartphones but of the laptop as the primary access point to the internet.

Corpus source-specific finding

Slow movement from an initially marginal foothold into the mainstream explains why incumbents may rationally overlook disrupters at first.

Exact returned source excerpt

The fact that disruption can take time helps to explain why incumbents frequently overlook disrupters.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, when Netflix launched, in 1997, its initial service wasn’t appealing to most of Blockbuster’s customers, who rented movies (typically new releases) on impulse.

Corpus source-specific finding

Disrupters often use business models unlike incumbents’ models.

Exact returned source excerpt

Disrupters often build business models that are very different from those of incumbents.

Exact returned source excerpt

General practitioners operating out of their offices often rely on their years of experience and on test results to interpret patients’ symptoms, make diagnoses, and prescribe treatment.

Corpus source-specific finding

Head-on entrants invite incumbent investment and counterinnovation, so prominent high-end entrants should not automatically be labelled disruptive.

Exact returned source excerpt

Understanding what drives the rate of disruption is helpful for predicting outcomes, but it doesn’t alter the way disruptions should be managed.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rapid disruptions are not fundamentally different from any others; they don’t have different causal mechanisms and don’t require conceptually different responses.

Corpus source-specific finding

Incumbents should preserve a profitable core while placing disruptive opportunities in a separate, protected operation.

Exact returned source excerpt

The mantra “Disrupt or be disrupted” can misguide us.

Exact returned source excerpt

Incumbent companies do need to respond to disruption if it’s occurring, but they should not overreact by dismantling a still-profitable business.

Corpus source-specific finding

Christensen expressly limits both the reliability and scope of the theory’s managerial prescriptions.

Exact returned source excerpt

We are eager to keep expanding and refining the theory of disruptive innovation, and much work lies ahead.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, universally effective responses to disruptive threats remain elusive.
