Corpus framing of this source: A Dynamic Theory of Organizational Knowledge Creation

Corpus synthesis of this source: Nonaka develops a conceptual theory in which organizations amplify individually created knowledge through repeated interaction between tacit and explicit forms. The source distinguishes information from committed, action-related knowledge; describes socialization, externalization, combination and internalization as an interdependent spiral; explains how dialogue, metaphor, analogy, shared experience, experimentation and information redundancy support that spiral; and proposes middle-up-down management and the hypertext organization as practical implementations. Its support is principally theoretical reasoning and engagement with prior scholarship rather than reported empirical testing.

Organizational knowledge creation forms a dynamic spiral among four conversion modes rather than a simple sequential process.

Exact returned source excerpt

In this sense, the theory of organizational knowledge creation is at the same time a basic theory for building a truly “humanistic” knowledge society beyond the limitations of mere “economic rationality.” Organizations play a critical role in mobilizing tacit knowledge held by individuals and provide the forum for a “spiral of knowledge” creation through socialization, combination, externalization, and internalization.
