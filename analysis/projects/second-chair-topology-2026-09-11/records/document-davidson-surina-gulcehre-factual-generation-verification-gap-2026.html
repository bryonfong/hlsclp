Corpus framing of this source: The Future of Facts: Tracing the Factual Generation-Verification Gap

Corpus synthesis of this source: The paper defines and traces a factual generation–verification (GV) gap across knowledge acquisition, continual learning and updating. Controlled experiments across four open-model families and naturalistic tests of deployed Google and OpenAI models find that factual verification often emerges earlier and persists longer than generation, while distillation, reasoning effort and model scale alter the gap. The authors argue for separate, longitudinal evaluation of generation and verification, qualify the study’s scope and warn that these asymmetries may propagate through increasingly model-mediated information ecosystems.

Corpus source-specific finding

The paper treats factual generation and verification as distinct, measurable capabilities and contributes a controlled, life-cycle analysis rather than another frozen-checkpoint comparison.

Exact returned source excerpt

Existing work has largely treated generation and verification as a single capability, or has measured the gap only on frozen checkpoints; our contribution is to trace both capabilities jointly across the life cycle of a fact in a controlled setting.

Exact returned source excerpt

The distinction between factual recall (generation) and recognition (verification), e.g., remembering a name versus picking it from a list, has long been studied in cognitive science [43], where dual-process models posit related but partially distinct mechanisms for the two [44, 45, 46, 47].

Corpus source-specific finding

The controlled acquisition experiments find that verification can emerge before generation and that ordinary training loss does not reveal the gap.

Exact returned source excerpt

First, the gap persists whenever data exposure is insufficient: verification can be well above chance while generation is near zero.

Exact returned source excerpt

Second, the location of the gap is not visible in the loss curve: training loss can decrease monotonically through the regime where the gap opens, peaks, and closes, so the gap cannot be diagnosed by standard training metrics alone.

Corpus source-specific finding

Under continual learning, generation forgets more sharply than verification, reopening or widening factual GV-gaps.

Exact returned source excerpt

We continue training on unrelated factual data (T-REx subset), starting from {0, 3, 6, 12} epochs after acquisition.

Exact returned source excerpt

Generation accuracy collapses sharply under distributional shift, while verification degrades more gradually and stabilizes at a higher floor — a floor that is higher for larger models and that rises with longer pre-shift training.

Corpus source-specific finding

Fact updating can create a “multi-verse” state in which generation adopts the new answer but verification accepts both old and new answers.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our updating experiments reveal a related but distinct failure mode: when a fact is updated through continued fine-tuning, the model can enter a “multi-verse” regime in which both the old and new answers are simultaneously verified as correct while generative outputs successfully shift to the new answer.

Exact returned source excerpt

Models reject ranked-noise queries significantly less often than random-noise queries, with the gap peaking at k = ±1 and fading toward k = ±5 — a residual multi-verse pattern in frontier models.

Corpus source-specific finding

Naturalistic tests reproduce the controlled regimes and show that distillation generally widens the GV-gap and weakens verification utility.

Exact returned source excerpt

The three regimes from our controlled experiments surface in frontier models under natural coverage variation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Results for three model sizes: Large, Medium, and Small; (a) Distillation increases the raw GV-gap and decreases verification utility across both families.

Corpus source-specific finding

Additional reasoning effort does not close the factual GV-gap and affects the tested model families differently.

Exact returned source excerpt

For GPT 5.4, increased reasoning effort widens both the GV-gap and the verification bias across all scales, with UV (×) falling below the chance floor of 0.5 at medium and small scales.

Exact returned source excerpt

For Gemini 3, the effects are muted at large and medium and reverse at the small scale, where reasoning closes the UV (✓) − UV (×) bias by lowering UV (✓) rather than raising UV (×).

Refusal behavior varies by family, scale and dataset; some larger models calibrate abstention to difficulty, whereas smaller Gemini models never refuse.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

Our findings suggest that when it comes to factuality, verification and generation should be treated as distinct capabilities with different learning dynamics, rather than two facets of the same underlying competence.

Exact returned source excerpt
