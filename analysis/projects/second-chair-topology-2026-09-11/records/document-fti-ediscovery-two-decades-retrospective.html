Corpus framing of this source: In Two Decades, E-discovery Has Changed and Stayed the Same

Corpus synthesis of this source: The author argues that improved technology has shifted routine e-discovery work toward in-house teams and non-lawyer specialists, while complex matters and persistent technology-skill gaps sustain demand for outside expertise. The author expects e-discovery technology to keep evolving and recommends adaptable legal teams. The source states that these are the author’s views, not necessarily those of FTI Consulting or its affiliates.

Corpus source-specific finding

Technology has enabled legal departments to bring more e-discovery work in-house.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the past, many legal departments outsourced their preservation and collection work, but today’s technology capabilities have made it easier for in-house counsel to do that work and reserve outside counsel for second review and highly complex matters.

Corpus source-specific finding

E-discovery expertise increasingly extends beyond lawyers.

Exact returned source excerpt

Further, e-discovery acumen is no longer limited to lawyers.

Exact returned source excerpt

Many companies now rely on e-discovery practitioners—that may not be lawyers but bring legal process and technology expertise—to manage e-discovery tasks and program management.

Corpus source-specific finding

Substantive knowledge and external expertise remain essential.

Exact returned source excerpt

- **What Hasn’t:** In-depth knowledge of case law, changing regulations and emerging technology is still critical for e-discovery professionals to be effective.

Exact returned source excerpt

Keeping abreast of the current climate and being able to implement change in process and technology when needed will always be important.

Corpus source-specific finding

Changing litigation and technology require adaptable legal teams.

Exact returned source excerpt

Overall, the types of litigation e-discovery teams are facing today are very different from those we worked on historically.

Exact returned source excerpt

The types of technology available to support and streamline e-discovery will also continue to evolve.
