Impact measurement: Sample balance does not remove attrition

Corpus synthesis: Examines validity checks for AI experiments and labour surveys, distinguishing credible design from substantive productivity or employment findings.

Native topic findings

  1. This cluster concerns the credibility of AI-impact measurement, not evidence that AI raises productivity, narrows education gaps or changes employment.
  2. Experimental comparisons show broad baseline similarity, with individual imbalances that qualify the aggregate picture.
  3. Attrition checks address differential completion, but substantial non-completion remains an interpretive limit.
  4. Education classifications and labor-market differences constrain interpretations of education-based productivity gaps.
  5. The labor-market survey combines several selection and validation checks, each with a specific scope.
  6. Sampling changes can produce apparent demographic shifts, so comparisons across survey rounds require attention to occupational composition.
