Corpus framing of this source: Munich Re: transferring AI performance risk through insurance and warranties

Corpus synthesis of this source: Munich Re presents aiSure as insurance for AI providers and corporate adopters, arguing that everyday model failures create exposures that conventional insurance does not adequately address. Its commercial case combines adoption statistics, a distinction between vendor warranties and deployer losses, and named deployment examples rather than measured claims outcomes.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI insurance addresses recurring performance errors rather than only catastrophes or third-party damages.

Exact returned source excerpt

Unlike traditional casualty policies protecting against catastrophic loss or traditional liability covering third party damages claims, AI insurance, pioneered by Munich Re in 2018, is triggered by unexpected errors in the ongoing performance of AI models in daily business use.

Exact returned source excerpt

This makes it suited to covering high frequency, low severity events which in the aggregate can create significant economic loss or class action exposures that existing forms of insurance simply don’t address.

Corpus framing of this source: aiSure™: Insurance-Based Transfer of AI Performance and Liability Risk

Corpus synthesis of this source: Munich Re presents aiSure™ as a risk-transfer suite intended to reduce adoption barriers for AI vendors and corporate deployers. It argues that performance uncertainty, legal exposure and potentially uninsured financial losses inhibit AI investment, then describes warranties and insurance coverage designed to shift those risks. It also reports Mosaic Insurance’s use of a parametric-like claims structure as an example of the product in practice.
