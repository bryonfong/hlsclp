Corpus framing of this source: Structuring private equity investment in U.S. law firms while preserving lawyer independence

Corpus synthesis of this source: Sidley Austin’s March 2026 practitioner analysis explains two investment pathways, the contractual separation of legal practice from nonlegal operations, and lenders’ ethics-constrained underwriting and enforcement. It draws on regulatory examples and reported transaction practice, including Sidley’s experience representing transaction participants, rather than presenting a quantitative study or explicit market forecast.

Corpus source-specific finding

Transactions follow two core pathways, neither of which removes professional-independence constraints.

Exact returned source excerpt

Building on the pathways discussed in our prior Sidley Update, current transactions generally fall into one of two categories: (i) equity participation in law firms where expressly permitted by regulation and (ii) MSO or split-practice structures in jurisdictions that continue to prohibit nonlawyer ownership of the practice of law.

Exact returned source excerpt

Importantly, however, the ethical rules of nonpermissive jurisdictions generally prevent a law firm with nonlawyer ownership from operating outside of its home (permissive) jurisdiction — effectively limiting its growth prospects.

Corpus source-specific finding

The MSO model separates sponsor-owned business infrastructure from lawyer-owned legal practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

Under this approach, the law firm remains owned and controlled by licensed lawyers, while a sponsor-owned MSO owns and operates the nonlegal business platform that supports the firm.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rather than having an equity stake in the law firm directly, the sponsor will make an equity investment in the MSO and derive its economic return from the MSO’s revenues under a long-term management services agreement (MSA) and related contractual arrangements.

Corpus source-specific finding

MSO compensation should pay for genuine nonlegal services, not participation in legal fees.

Exact returned source excerpt

Its economic terms must be sufficiently robust to support sponsor underwriting while avoiding impermissible fee sharing or *de facto* nonlawyer control.

Exact returned source excerpt

Guiding Principle * The central objective is to demonstrate that the MSO is compensated for bona fide *nonlegal *services at fair market value, not for the provision of legal services or participation in legal fees, and that it performs its role without directing or controlling the professional judgment of the practicing lawyers.

Corpus source-specific finding

Rollover equity and carefully limited replacement rights support continuity without transferring legal judgment.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rollover Equity and Minority Interests * Rollover equity is frequently used to align incentives, bridge valuation gaps, and preserve operational continuity following a transaction.

Exact returned source excerpt

In many transactions, a portion of the selling lawyers roll value into the MSO platform (or, where permitted, into an ABS entity), allowing them to participate in postclosing upside while the law firm itself remains under lawyer ownership and control.

Corpus source-specific finding

Institutionalized, scalable practices can make more suitable investment targets.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, plaintiff-side personal injury firms often underwrite better than relationship-driven practices because - client demand is consistent and can be generated through scalable advertising and digital channels - intake and conversion metrics can be measured and optimized - case management can be standardized across large volumes of matters - revenue is less dependent on individual rainmakers or on relationships with individual lawyers Firms that already operate with centralized systems, disciplined data tracking, and institutional processes tend to integrate more effectively into sponsor-backed platforms.

Exact returned source excerpt

Even apart from ethics rules, law firms often present structural challenges for leveraged investment, including limits on enforceable noncompetes for lawyers, revenue tied to individual relationships, and practice-area-specific cash flow volatility (particularly in contingency-fee matters).

Corpus source-specific finding

Lenders prioritize enforceable MSO value and must plan remedies that preserve lawyer control.

Exact returned source excerpt

Core Credit Thesis * For MSO transactions, lenders typically focus on whether the value resides in the nonlawyer platform and whether that value can be collateralized and enforced without appearing to control the practice of law.

Exact returned source excerpt

This leads lenders to concentrate on MSO assets, contracts, and cash flows, as well as how the MSA captures such cash flows, rather than on the law firm itself.

Corpus framing of this source: Private Equity Law-Firm Transactions: MSO Architecture, Ethical Boundaries and Lender Protections

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source presents a practitioner account of how private equity investment in U.S. law firms is structured where direct nonlawyer ownership is restricted. It identifies direct investment where permitted and management-services-organization structures elsewhere, then explains how MSO economics, governance, collateral and enforcement rights seek to preserve lawyer control while supporting sponsor and lender underwriting. Its observations describe market practice and transaction-design reasoning rather than an empirical study or quantified forecast.

Corpus source-specific finding

The market uses direct investment where permitted and MSO structures where ownership restrictions apply.

Exact returned source excerpt

**Conclusion ** Private equity investment in law firms is no longer hypothetical, but it remains highly regulated and structurally complex.

Exact returned source excerpt

The market has converged around two core pathways — direct investment where permitted and MSO structures elsewhere — supplemented by a sophisticated toolkit of contractual and economic arrangements.

Corpus source-specific finding

The MSA must support the investment without creating impermissible fee sharing or nonlawyer control.

Exact returned source excerpt

MSO Economics and Design: Avoiding Fee-Sharing and Nonlawyer Control Concerns ** The MSA is the fulcrum of the MSO structure.

Exact returned source excerpt

Its economic terms must be sufficiently robust to support sponsor underwriting while avoiding impermissible fee sharing or *de facto* nonlawyer control.

Corpus source-specific finding

Transaction documents should expressly preserve professional independence and limit the MSO to administrative functions.

Exact returned source excerpt

Preserving Professional Independence * The carefully drafted MSA expressly delineates the parties’ roles, allocating responsibility to the law firm for supervision and direction of the legal and legal support services, including the employment and compensation of lawyers, client acceptance or termination, establishment of fee structures, determination of case strategy, and provision of client advice.

Exact returned source excerpt

The MSO’s role is limited to administrative services in support of the business of the firm, such as IT, cybersecurity, bookkeeping, HR administration, facilities and real estate, accounting, and marketing, and its access to client and financial data, trust accounts and client confidential information is appropriately limited.
