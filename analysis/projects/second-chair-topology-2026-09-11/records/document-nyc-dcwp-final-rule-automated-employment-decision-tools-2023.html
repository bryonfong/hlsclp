Open topic: Employment screening: Audits reveal bias without remedying it

Corpus framing of this source: NYC rules set audit, disclosure and notice requirements for automated employment decisions

Corpus synthesis of this source: DCWP’s 2023 adoption notice implements Local Law 144 through binding requirements governing automated employment decision tools. Its substantive contribution is regulatory: it defines covered reliance on automated outputs, auditor independence, audit-data options, public reporting and advance notice. The supplied examples illustrate compliance rather than report empirical findings or predict effects.

Corpus source-specific finding

Use depends on a recent bias audit, public audit information and notice.

Exact returned source excerpt

Local Law 144 of 2021 prohibits employers and employment agencies from using an automated employment decision tool unless the tool has been subject to a bias audit within one year of the use of the tool, information about the bias audit is publicly available, and certain notices have been provided to employees or job candidates.

Exact returned source excerpt

These rules establish that a bias audit of an AEDT must calculate the selection rate for each race/ethnicity and sex category that is required to be reported on to the U.S.

Open topic: Hiring audits: Disclosure cannot guarantee nondiscrimination

Corpus framing of this source: New York City’s AEDT rule specifies audit, disclosure and notice obligations

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Notice of Adoption of Final Rule: Automated Employment Decision Tools sets out binding compliance requirements for employers and employment agencies, rather than predictions about AI or empirical findings about discrimination. It defines covered uses, auditor independence, audit calculations and data requirements, public reporting, and individual notices. Its worked examples illustrate compliance; its account of public comments explains revisions without attributing specific positions to individual commenters.
