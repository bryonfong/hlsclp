Corpus framing of this source: Legal aid AI adoption and its perceived potential to narrow the justice gap

Corpus synthesis of this source: Bob Ambrogi’s September 2025 report presents survey findings on legal aid organizations’ AI adoption and practitioners’ optimism about access to justice. He interprets those findings as evidence of AI’s potential to democratize legal services, rather than reporting measured improvements in legal outcomes.

Corpus source-specific finding

The survey reports substantially higher AI adoption among legal aid organizations than across the broader profession.

Exact returned source excerpt

It found that 74% of legal aid organizations are already using AI in their work – significantly higher than the 37% adoption rate recently reported across the wider legal profession for generative AI tools.

Exact returned source excerpt

The survey, The AI Advantage: How Technology Can Help Bridge the Justice Gap, was conducted by Everlaw in partnership with the National Legal Aid & Defender Association (NLADA), Paladin, and LawSites.

Corpus source-specific finding

Respondents express strong optimism about AI’s ability to address unmet civil legal needs.

Exact returned source excerpt

### AI As Justice Gap Solution The study’s most striking finding centers on legal aid professionals’ optimism about AI’s potential impact: 88% of respondents believe AI can help address the access to justice gap to some extent, with 34% saying it can help “to a great extent.” This widespread belief in AI’s transformative potential comes against the backdrop of a stark justice crisis in America.

Exact returned source excerpt

According to the study, 92% of civil legal problems encountered by low-income Americans receive no or inadequate legal help, and the United States ranks just 107th out of 142 countries in affordability and accessibility of civil justice.

Ambrogi personally interprets the findings as signaling potential to democratize legal services.
