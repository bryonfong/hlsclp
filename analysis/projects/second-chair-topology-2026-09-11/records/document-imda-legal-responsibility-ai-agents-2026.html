Corpus framing of this source: Allocating civil liability for autonomous AI agents

Corpus synthesis of this source: Legal Responsibility for AI Agents (Discussion Paper) synthesises an IMDA-convened Singapore legal working group’s March–May 2026 discussions. Through legal reasoning and a hypothetical consumer deployment, rather than empirical findings, it examines attribution, negligence, contractual risk allocation and strict liability. It records disagreement over liability reform and identifies questions for further study rather than endorsing a settled regime.

Corpus source-specific finding

The paper locates accountability with people and organisations, not AI agents.

Exact returned source excerpt

This paper summarises the issues raised by the group in discussions between March and May 2026, including areas where views diverged.

Corpus framing of this source: Legal Responsibility for AI Agents: control-based allocation under existing private law

Corpus synthesis of this source: This 2026 IMDA discussion paper synthesises a Singapore legal working group’s analysis of civil responsibility when agentic AI causes harm. It argues that existing statutes, contract and tort can address many cases, but autonomy, multi-actor system design, information asymmetry and unpredictable behaviour create unusually difficult questions of attribution, proof and risk allocation. The paper explores rather than endorses fault-based and strict-liability regimes, records disagreements among members, and identifies areas for further study rather than making specific policy recommendations.

Corpus source-specific finding

The paper frames agentic AI liability as a private-law problem involving autonomous conduct and multiple responsible actors, not liability of the AI itself.
