Corpus framing of this source: Consulting partnerships aim to move enterprise AI beyond experimentation

Corpus synthesis of this source: David Adubiina’s Techloy report (2026), drawing on Wall Street Journal reporting and corporate surveys, describes OpenAI and Anthropic’s consulting partnerships as a response to limited enterprise AI scaling and reported financial benefits. It outlines implementation arrangements and a prospective banking application, rather than demonstrating that the partnerships have delivered results.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI developers are recruiting consulting firms to help corporate clients integrate AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

According to a report by The Wall Street Journal, the companies have struck agreements with consulting firms including McKinsey, Boston Consulting Group, Accenture, Capgemini and Deloitte to help corporate clients integrate AI systems into their operations.

Exact returned source excerpt

The partnerships aim to address a widening gap between the promise of AI and how widely companies actually use it.

Corpus source-specific finding

Two surveys supply evidence of limited scaling and reported financial returns.

Exact returned source excerpt

A McKinsey survey of nearly 2,000 employees last summer found that about two-thirds said their organisations had not yet begun scaling AI across the enterprise.

Exact returned source excerpt

In a separate survey of nearly 4,500 chief executives conducted by PricewaterhouseCoopers, more than half reported seeing little or no financial benefit from AI so far.

Corpus source-specific finding

OpenAI combines technical deployment support with consultants’ expected strategy and process-redesign work.

Exact returned source excerpt

To close that gap, OpenAI has been working with consulting firms to help companies deploy its Frontier platform, which allows businesses to build and manage AI agents for specific tasks.

Exact returned source excerpt

The company’s team of roughly 70 forward-deployed engineers works directly with organisations to identify potential use cases and integrate the technology into real-world workflows, the Journal reported.

Corpus source-specific finding

A European bank illustrates use-case exploration rather than demonstrated deployment benefits.

Exact returned source excerpt

At a large European bank, OpenAI and one of its consulting partners evaluated several potential uses for Frontier, including applications related to credit-risk analysis and voice capabilities, Colin Jarvis, OpenAI’s global head of forward-deployed engineering, told the Journal.
