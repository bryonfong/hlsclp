Native topic record

Disruption theory: Historical examples weakly fit predictions

Corpus synthesis: Contrasts advocates’ refinement claims with critics’ concerns about retrospective adjustment and weak testing, especially in professional services.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Cross-domain institutional evidence: Innovation and market correction remain conditional on incentives, gatekeeping and measurement · Disruption theory: Weak historical fit makes business-model displacement conditional

8 distinct stance-bearing works · Opposes: 2 · Redirects: 1 · Supports: 4 · Unclear: 1

Native topic findings

  1. The central dispute concerns disruption as a specific causal theory, not whether industries change or newcomers sometimes win.
  2. The main quantitative critique finds weak conformity among the theory’s own historical examples, not a measured economy-wide failure rate.
  3. Advocates describe theoretical refinement and predictive value; critics see retrospective adjustment and insufficiently demanding tests.
  4. Alternative explanations challenge any simple equation between incumbent experience, inertia and failure.
  5. Professional-service applications identify plausible competitive pressures but do not establish inevitable legal-sector displacement.
  6. The practical debate favors context-sensitive responses over an unconditional imperative to disrupt.
  7. A separate boundary dispute asks whether commercial disruption provides an appropriate standard for public-serving institutions.
