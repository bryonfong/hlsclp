Corpus framing of this source: Rule-based legal expert systems: practical utility and limits of formalisation

Corpus synthesis of this source: Artificial Intelligence and Law: Stepping Stones to a Model of Legal Reasoning presents rule-based systems as useful but incomplete models of legal argument. Corpus source-specific finding

HYPO demonstrates how case-based reasoning can make precedent comparison and argument computationally explicit.

Exact returned source excerpt

"Case-based reasoning" is the process by which a program uses previous cases (e.g., prece- dents, experiences, problem-solving episodes, detailed solutions, plans) to solve a new case.

Exact returned source excerpt

There are two kinds of CBR: (1) "problem-solving CBR," in which the emphasis is on generating a solution to a new problem (case) by reusing and modifying old solutions; and (2) "interpretive CBR," in which the emphasis is on generating an interpretation of a new case by comparing and analogizing it with interpretations made in past cases.
