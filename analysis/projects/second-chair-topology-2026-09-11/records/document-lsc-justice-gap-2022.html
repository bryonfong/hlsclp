Corpus framing of this source: LSC’s 2022 Justice Gap Study: widespread unmet civil legal need and constrained legal-aid capacity

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Justice Gap: The Unmet Civil Legal Needs of Low-income Americans (2022) combines a nationally representative survey with legal-aid intake data to document unmet civil legal need. LSC attributes the shortfall to limited resources and barriers involving cost, knowledge and confidence in the legal system, identifies income and pandemic-related disparities, and explains important limits on its estimates.

LSC frames the justice gap as an access-to-justice crisis affecting basic needs.

Corpus framing of this source: The Justice Gap: The Unmet Civil Legal Needs of Low-income Americans (2022)

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Legal Services Corporation presents an empirical assessment of unmet civil legal need among low-income Americans. Using a nationally representative 2021 household survey, LSC intake data and supporting Census sources, it measures problem prevalence and impact, help-seeking, barriers, income disparities, pandemic effects and legal-aid capacity. It concludes that civil legal problems are widespread, assistance is rarely sought or sufficient, and available legal-aid resources cannot meet eligible demand.
