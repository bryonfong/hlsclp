Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Damien Riehl on lawyer value, pricing and competition in an AI-enabled legal market

Corpus synthesis of this source: Riehl argues that AI shifts legal value away from time-intensive production and toward proprietary judgment, trusted risk transfer, human advocacy and client relationships. He predicts that in-house legal departments will absorb routine work, forcing law firms to prove incremental value and move more revenue toward fixed or outcome-based fees. He expects substantial task automation even in relatively human practices, while identifying live advocacy, negotiation and sales as more durable. He supports the argument with the earlier adoption pattern of technology-assisted review, a reported Salesforce saving, his litigation experience and prospective examples involving agentic negotiation, automated dispute resolution and AI-first legal education. He qualifies these forecasts by retaining a role for billable hours, questioning whether efficiency will increase demand or merely reduce cost, and emphasizing institutional resistance to changing profitable models.
