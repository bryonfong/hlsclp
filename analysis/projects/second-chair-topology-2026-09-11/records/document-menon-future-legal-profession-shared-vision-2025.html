Corpus framing of this source: Superstar pay and quiet cutting signal a growing focus on short-term profit

Corpus synthesis of this source: The symposium opening address contrasts competition for highly paid star lawyers with the informal removal of junior lawyers, particularly in the United States. It cites reported employment practices and survey findings, interpreting both trends as signs of an increasing focus on short-term profit that it expects to intensify.

Corpus source-specific finding

Large firms compete aggressively for superstar lawyers.

Exact returned source excerpt

First, we have seen the rise of a special class of “superstar lawyers”, especially in the United States (or “**US**”).

Exact returned source excerpt

These are highly successful lawyers who are subject to aggressive bidding wars from large law firms, “bought out” from their employment contracts, and offered staggering high annual packages that have been compared to those of NBA superstars – in some instances to the tune of more than USD25 million.(7) 15.

Corpus source-specific finding

Billable-hour performance management can function as informal attrition.

Exact returned source excerpt

Second, we have seen a rise in the trend that has been described as “quiet cutting” or “silent sacking”, where young lawyers are *effectively* asked to leave their law firms without being formally told to do so.

Exact returned source excerpt

Again, this trend appears to be particularly prominent in the US and it has been attributed largely to the fixation on the billable hour requirement.(9) What was once intended to be a transparent technique for charging clients has in many US firms become the *primary means *to evaluate the performance of their associates.(9) When these associates fail to meet their expected billable hour requirements, some are placed on “performance improvement plans”, which have been described as “instruments of controlled attrition, allowing firms to reshape their talent pools without resorting to public layoffs or damaging their recruitment brands”.(10) Just to provide some numbers, a recent survey by an American legal magazine found that: (a) for every formal layoff in the US legal services sector, there are now three performance-based departures; (b) the use of formal performance improvement plans has more than doubled since 2021; and (c) nearly 70 per cent of lawyers who are placed on such plans will leave their firms within six months.(11) 16.
