Builds onWork redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Guest Post: We're having the wrong conversation about legal technology: A call to measure the AI metrics that matter

Corpus synthesis of this source: Rob Beard, Coherent’s chief legal officer, argues that in-house legal teams should assess AI by measurable customer value rather than vendor adoption, utilisation or investor-oriented metrics. He supports the argument with Coherent’s reported contract-review results and projected reductions in outside-counsel dependency and manual work, explains why provider and buyer incentives require different measures, predicts pressure on the billable-hour model, and urges CLOs to demand transparent evidence of gains, costs, timing and implementation setbacks.

Corpus source-specific finding

Beard treats the debate over Harvey’s user and utilisation figures as context rather than evidence of value for in-house legal departments. He acknowledges that retention and utilisation can indicate AI traction, but argues that seat counts, utilisation and revenue metrics principally serve vendors, investors and law firms and do not establish improvements for corporate legal teams.

Exact returned source excerpt

Last week, a debate erupted in the legal tech community, questioning whether Harvey, a leading AI vendor that sells primarily to law firms, had achieved some of its key metrics around number of users and software utilization (i.e., how many of the lawyers are using the AI tool).

Exact returned source excerpt

Harvey responded by claiming its metrics were better on these fronts than previously claimed.

Corpus source-specific finding

He proposes measuring AI by eliminated repetitive work, corresponding cost reductions, lower dependence on outside counsel, faster turnaround and improved legal-team performance.

Exact returned source excerpt

- How many hours of repetitive tasks did our use of AI eliminate (not hours tracked), and how is that reflected in our costs (the total bill, for outside counsel, and internal people cost that can now be redeployed to higher use, for internal teams)?

Exact returned source excerpt

- How has using AI reduced dependency on outside counsel (not how many seat licenses does the law firm have)?

Corpus source-specific finding

Coherent’s implementation strategy favours an AI provider designed specifically for in-house legal teams over off-the-shelf tools whose users must integrate them into existing workflows.

Exact returned source excerpt

But instead of buying off-the-shelf tools that we have to figure out how integrate into our workflows (and instead of measuring how many people use those tools), my team is following [McKinsey's advice to find partners](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/how-to-lead-through-the-ai-disruption) and we're partnering with an AI provider that builds its products and business model from the ground up, specifically to make in-house legal teams better, faster and smarter.

Exact returned source excerpt

Because they only work with in-house teams, we can hold them and ourselves accountable to metrics like cost savings from work that previously went to outside counsel and productivity gains that allow us to redeploy people costs to higher-value opportunities.
