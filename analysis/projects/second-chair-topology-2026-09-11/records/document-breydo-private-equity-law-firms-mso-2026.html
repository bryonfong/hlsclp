Builds onOutside capital: Permission does not create investable firms

Open topic: Outside capital: Permission does not create investable firms

Corpus framing of this source: Breydo: law-firm MSOs need governance safeguards before private equity expands

Corpus synthesis of this source: Lev E. Breydo argues that management services organizations offer outside investors a route into law-firm operations while leaving unresolved risks to professional independence. His 2026 post combines reported transactions, analogies with healthcare and accounting, and a proposed minimum governance framework; it warns of prospective risks rather than demonstrating investor control at the named law firms.

Corpus source-specific finding

Ownership restrictions and AI-related capital needs encourage split-entity financing.

Exact returned source excerpt

[ABA Model Rule 5.4](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_5_4_professional_independence_of_a_lawyer/) prohibits nonlawyer ownership of law firms—a restriction that also limits access to outside capital, constrains the recruitment of nonlawyer talent, and effectively locks firms into the partnership model regardless of size or complexity.

Exact returned source excerpt

As AI integration raises capital intensity across the sector, the pressure for alternative financing is growing rapidly.

Corpus source-specific finding

Reported transactions suggest investor interest extends across different law-firm segments.

Exact returned source excerpt

The deal used a management services organization, or MSO: a split-entity [workaround](https://www.ft.com/content/9f0033b5-8481-4d3f-8e1b-af6751a6b494?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in which attorneys keep ownership of the law firm while a PE-backed vehicle acquires the operating platform—technology, billing, marketing, HR, and everything else that isn’t “practicing law.” The buyer, Orion Legal, has broader ambitions: building a national consolidation platform for personal-injury practices, echoing the roll-up playbook PE has already run in healthcare and accounting.

Exact returned source excerpt

Cohen & Gresser, an elite boutique, is [reportedly](https://www.ft.com/content/9487e94f-fa82-4cea-aec3-ebc10ef44d43?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in PE negotiations, with its founder citing “over a decade” of preparation for institutional capital.

Corpus source-specific finding

Healthcare and accounting provide cautionary analogies, not findings about legal MSOs.

Exact returned source excerpt

The problem is that this structure rests on a fragile assumption: that “business operations” and “legal practice” can be clearly and durably separated.

Exact returned source excerpt

There, arrangements that started with clean governance and independent practice boards drifted toward de facto investor control over professional decisions—through staffing, scheduling, intake systems, and revenue optimization tools.

Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

He recommends a minimum governance framework before a crisis forces intervention.

Exact returned source excerpt

First, structural safeguards that limit MSOs to genuine support functions and give firms a real termination right if independence is compromised.

Exact returned source excerpt

Second, board composition requirements, including independent directors and a standing ethics committee.
