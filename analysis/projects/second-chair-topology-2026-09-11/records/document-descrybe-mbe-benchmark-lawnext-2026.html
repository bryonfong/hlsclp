Corpus framing of this source: Descrybe’s legal-reasoning benchmark reports an advantage over general-purpose models

Corpus synthesis of this source: LawSites/LawNext reports Descrybe’s vendor-run comparison of four AI systems on 200 bar-exam questions. It highlights DescrybeLM’s higher scores and the verification burden of persuasive but incorrect reasoning, while noting judge-model scoring and possible training-data exposure.

Corpus source-specific finding

DescrybeLM led the reported benchmark in answer accuracy and reasoning quality.

Exact returned source excerpt

| System | Correct answers out of 200 | Rubric-scored reasoning quality | |---|---|---| | DescrybeLM | 200 (100%) | 99.70% | | ChatGPT 5.2 | 187 (93.5%) | 93.41% | | Claude Opus 4.5 | 177 (88.5%) | 89.03% | | Gemini 3 Pro | 184 (92.0%) | 91.45% | *Rubric-scored reasoning quality reflects judge-model scoring; see Evaluation Approach within the white paper for methodology and limitations.

Exact returned source excerpt

Descrybe ran a controlled benchmark against ChatGPT 5.2, Claude Opus 4.5, and Gemini 3 Pro on 200 multistate bar exam questions.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study assessed legal reasoning under standardized conditions, with material methodological qualifications.

We know that vendor-produced benchmarks invite scrutiny — as they should, that's why we published our methodology and invite anyone to replicate it." > — Kara Peterson, Co-Founder and CEO of Descrybe ## What the benchmark showed All four systems were tested under standardized, no-external-web benchmark conditions using the NCBE MBE Complete Practice Exam (Questions 1–200, no exclusions), producing 800 separate evaluation runs with blinded scoring.

Exact returned source excerpt

The study measured not just whether each system chose the correct answer, but whether the legal reasoning behind it was sound: Did it identify the right rule?

Corpus source-specific finding

The article emphasizes confident legal errors as a burden on human verification.

Exact returned source excerpt

Among 52 incorrect outputs, 49 delivered assertive, well-structured reasoning that did not signal uncertainty — the failure mode that imposes the highest verification burden on legal practitioners.

Exact returned source excerpt

The dominant patterns were applying the wrong legal standard or misapplying the correct one, while the prose read like competent analysis.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source distinguishes recorded overconfidence flags from the broader problem of persuasive errors.

Exact returned source excerpt

The scoring log also revealed that two general-purpose models — Claude Opus 4.5 and Gemini 3 Pro — exhibited overconfident tone on correct outputs, not only incorrect ones.

Exact returned source excerpt

DescrybeLM and ChatGPT 5.2 each received zero overconfidence flags across all 200 outputs.

Corpus source-specific finding

The article questions cross-model checking as a substitute for initial accuracy.

Exact returned source excerpt

The study also found that cross-checking between general-purpose models is not a reliable substitute for getting the answer right the first time.

Exact returned source excerpt

Across 200 questions, 40 were missed by at least one model, 11 were missed by two or more, and only 1 was missed by all three — meaning errors were largely unpredictable and non-overlapping.

Corpus source-specific finding

Descrybe’s CEO advocates evidence-based tool selection and scrutiny of vendor benchmarks.
