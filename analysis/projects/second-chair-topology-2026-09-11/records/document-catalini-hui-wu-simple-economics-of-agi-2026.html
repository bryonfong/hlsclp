Corpus framing of this source: Some Simple Economics of AGI: verification as the constraint on agentic growth

Corpus synthesis of this source: The paper develops a reduced-form economic theory linking AI deployment, human expertise, alignment, liability and platform competition. It argues that execution can scale faster than trustworthy verification, creating incentives for privately profitable but socially risky deployment. It supports its mechanisms with published studies and reported examples, then recommends investment in human capability, independent verification, provenance and risk-bearing institutions. Its macroeconomic outcomes and market forecasts remain model-based predictions, not measured findings.

Corpus source-specific finding

The paper shifts the automation boundary from routine work to measurable work, and the growth bottleneck from execution to verification.

Exact returned source excerpt

The measurability gap ∆m= mA −m H formalizes Polanyi’s paradox (Polanyi, 1966) applied to verification: tasks become automatable not when they are simple, but when they can be measured (Catalini et al., 2025), creating a risk zone where execution is cheap but verification remains tacit and expensive.

Exact returned source excerpt

This framework generates a strict typology of work, partitioning the economy into four distinct regimes: theSafe Industrial Zone(cheap to automate, affordable to verify), the Runaway Risk Zone(cheap to automate, unaffordable to verify), theHuman Artisan Zone (hard to automate, verifiable), and thePure Tacit Zone(neither automatable nor verifiable).

Exact returned source excerpt

Together, these forces push firms toward a fragile “sandwich” topology: humans define intent, machines execute, and a shrinking, over-leveraged layer of humans underwrites outcomes.
