Native topic findings

  1. The cluster addresses conditional modernisation of legal payments and client-property safeguards, not AI performance or deregulation.
  2. Ownership and control of money determine payment routing; a processor account does not replace a qualifying trust account.
  3. Payment convenience does not shift processing and settlement risks onto client funds.
  4. Arizona couples legal paraprofessional participation with operational accountability and professional commitments.
  5. Trust-account selection accommodates administrative costs while preserving regulated custody and limited discretion.

Corpus framing of this source: Florida ethics guidance permits digital payments subject to confidentiality and trust-fund safeguards

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Florida Bar Professional Ethics Committee offers rule-based advisory guidance on payment-processing services such as Venmo and PayPal. It permits their use conditionally, distinguishes earned fees from entrusted funds, and specifies privacy, segregation, transfer and provider-investigation duties. The supplied excerpts concern payments, not generative AI, despite the packet’s broader title.

Corpus framing of this source: Arizona’s professional commitments and trust-account safeguards for lawyers and legal paraprofessionals

Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied excerpts from Arizona Supreme Court matter R-20-0034 set out professional commitments and detailed client-property rules that expressly include legal paraprofessionals alongside lawyers. They prescribe segregation, accounting, transaction safeguards and regulatory oversight, with limited exceptions and disciplinary protections. They provide regulatory requirements rather than empirical findings or predictions; several unchanged provisions and conditions are not reproduced.
