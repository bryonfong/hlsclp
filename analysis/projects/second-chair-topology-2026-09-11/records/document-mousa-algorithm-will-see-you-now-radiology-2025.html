Open topic: Clinical teaming: AI accuracy may worsen joint decisions

Corpus framing of this source: Why radiology AI benchmark gains may not translate into clinical performance

Corpus synthesis of this source: The algorithm will see you now argues that selective training data, hospital-specific conditions and clinicians’ responses to AI limit real-world performance. It acknowledges successful applications but calls for local retraining or revalidation and explains why autonomous deployment faces technical and regulatory barriers.

Corpus source-specific finding

Benchmark performance does not establish clinical effectiveness.

Exact returned source excerpt

Even a model for the precise question you need and in the hospital where it was trained is unlikely to perform as well in clinical practice as it did in the benchmark.

Exact returned source excerpt

In benchmark studies, researchers isolate a cohort of scans, define goals in quantitative metrics, such as the sensitivity (the percentage of people with the condition who are correctly identified by the test) and specificity (the percentage of people without the condition who are correctly identified as such), and compare the performance of a model to the score of another reviewer, typically a human doctor.

Corpus source-specific finding

Hospitals need to retrain or revalidate tools rather than assume results transfer between sites.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

Some of [these challenges](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01037-4) stemmed from avoidable experimental issues like overfitting, but others are indicative of deeper problems like differences in how hospitals record and generate data, such as using slightly different imaging equipment.

Corpus source-specific finding

Training-data selection favors easy cases and leaves models vulnerable to clinical ambiguity.

Exact returned source excerpt

Training datasets come with strict inclusion criteria, where the diagnosis must be unambiguous (typically [confirmed](https://blog.mozilla.ai/the-importance-of-ground-truth-data-in-ai-applications-an-overview/) by a consensus of two to three experts or a pathology result) and without images that are shot at an odd angle, look too dark, or are blurry.

Exact returned source excerpt

This skews performance towards the easiest cases, which doctors are already best at diagnosing, and away from real-world images.

Corpus source-specific finding

Demographic underrepresentation and missing metadata hinder equitable performance.

Exact returned source excerpt

Medical imaging datasets used for training also tend to have fewer cases from [children](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.06.25328913v1), women, and ethnic minorities, making their performance generally worse for these demographics.

Exact returned source excerpt

Many [lack](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9712398/) [information](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landig/article/PIIS2589-7500(21)00252-1/fulltext) about the gender or race of cases at all, making it difficult to adjust for these issues and address the problem of bias.

Human oversight can introduce errors when clinicians rely on incorrect or absent AI prompts.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

Another [review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3240751/), this time from 2011, found that when a system gave incorrect guidance, clinicians were 26 percent more likely to make a wrong decision than unaided peers.

Builds onWork redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: The algorithm will see you now: why radiology AI has augmented rather than replaced radiologists

Corpus synthesis of this source: Deena Mousa argues that benchmark gains in radiology AI have outpaced real-world diffusion and have not reduced radiologist employment. She attributes this to dataset and generalisation limits, human–AI interaction effects, regulation, liability and professional norms, radiologists’ broader task mix, and elastic demand for imaging. She predicts that comparable knowledge work may initially expand rather than contract when software raises productivity, while qualifying that major capability gains could change this dynamic.
