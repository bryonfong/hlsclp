Corpus framing of this source: Tech Competence

Corpus synthesis of this source: A practitioner-journalist’s tracker documents the state-by-state diffusion of lawyers’ technology-competence duties and provides background for later AI-specific professional obligations. It reports primary rules and opinions but is not itself a primary legal authority.

Corpus source-specific finding

California recognized technological competence through an ethics opinion rather than by formally adopting the corresponding change to its professional-conduct rules.

Exact returned source excerpt

California, while not having formally adopted the change to its rules of professional conduct, has issued an ethics opinion that expressly acknowledges a duty of lawyers to be competent in technology.

Corpus source-specific finding

California’s 2015 formal opinion requires litigators to possess e-discovery competence or associate with someone who does.

Exact returned source excerpt

2015-193](http://catalystsecure.com/components/com_wordpress/wp/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/CAL-2015-193-11-0004-06-30-15-FINAL.pdf) requires attorneys who represent clients in litigation either to be competent in e-discovery or associate with others who are competent.

Corpus source-specific finding

The California opinion grounds technological competence in the continuing duty to keep abreast of changes in legal practice, including technology’s benefits and risks.

Exact returned source excerpt

The opinion expressly cites the ABA’s Comment 8 and states: Maintaining learning and skill consistent with an attorney’s duty of competence includes “keeping abreast of changes in the law and its practice, including the benefits and risks associated with technology.” If you know of other states I have missed, please let me know.

Corpus source-specific finding

The supplied project analysis characterizes technology competence as an established ethical foundation that predates generative-AI guidance.

Exact returned source excerpt

## Relevance to the project This is the competence-mandate layer of the EDU lens's training-response story: before any state said anything about generative AI specifically, forty-plus US jurisdictions had already made technology competence an ethical duty.

Corpus source-specific finding

The tracker provides jurisdiction-level evidence of a gradual, textually varied diffusion of the duty and a baseline for studying later AI-era requirements.

Exact returned source excerpt

The tracker supplies the jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction factual substrate for corpus arguments about how the profession's regulatory apparatus absorbs technological change — a comment-level duty diffusing state by state over thirteen years, with textual divergence among adopters — and it is the base on which AI-era competence claims (state bar generative-AI guidance, judicial standing orders, CLE mandates) are being layered.
