Corpus framing of this source: Overseas outsourcing requires continuing lawyer and firm responsibility

Corpus synthesis of this source: New York City Bar Formal Opinion 2006-3 treats delegation to overseas non-lawyers as potentially economical and efficient, but insists on professional responsibility, supervision and quality control. Its reasoning rests on disciplinary rules and earlier ethics opinions, not empirical findings.

Corpus source-specific finding

Delegation offers efficiencies only while lawyers retain the client relationship and responsibility for the work.

Exact returned source excerpt

The potential benefits resulting from a lawyer’s delegating work to a non-lawyer cannot be denied.

Exact returned source excerpt

Such delegation is proper if the lawyer maintains a direct relationship with the client, supervises the delegated work, and has complete professional responsibility for the work product.

Corpus source-specific finding

Lawyers must define the delegated work and check its quality; supervision is not a blanket exemption from unauthorized-practice restrictions.

Exact returned source excerpt

But at the same time, to avoid aiding the unauthorized practice of law, the lawyer must at every step shoulder complete responsibility for the non-lawyer’s work.

In short, the lawyer must, by applying professional skill and judgment, first set the appropriate scope for the non-lawyer’s work and then vet the non-lawyer’s work and ensure its quality.

Corpus framing of this source: Overseas legal-support outsourcing requires retained lawyer responsibility and client safeguards

Corpus synthesis of this source: New York City Bar Formal Opinion 2006-3 permits overseas outsourcing of substantive legal support under the then-applicable New York Code of Professional Responsibility. The Committee recognises potential efficiencies but conditions delegation on supervision, confidentiality, conflicts safeguards, appropriate billing and, when necessary, informed advance client consent. Its contribution is ethical guidance grounded in rules and earlier opinions, rather than empirical findings or predictions.

Corpus source-specific finding

The opinion permits outsourcing substantive legal support, not independent legal practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

DIGEST: A New York lawyer may ethically outsource legal support services overseas to a non-lawyer, if the New York lawyer (a) rigorously supervises the non-lawyer, so as to avoid aiding the non-lawyer in the unauthorized practice of law and to ensure that the non-lawyer’s work contributes to the lawyer’s competent representation of the client; (b) preserves the client’s confidences and secrets when outsourcing; (c) avoids conflicts of interest when outsourcing; (d) bills for outsourcing appropriately; and (e) when necessary, obtains advance client consent to outsourcing.
