Predictive coding: Mature automation still needs transparency

Corpus synthesis: Combines reduced review effort with lawyer responsibility, measurement and contested disclosure costs rather than universal system approval.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Native topic findings

  1. Predictive coding makes e-discovery a mature test of legal automation: software reduces exhaustive review while lawyers retain responsibility for relevance judgments and process quality.
  2. Published studies support substantial reductions in review effort, but their results do not establish universal accuracy or realised client savings.
  3. Judicial acceptance concerns defensible, case-specific processes; it neither certifies every system nor automatically compels adoption.
  4. The operational dispute concerns training, measurement and stopping rules as much as the choice to automate.
  5. Transparency creates a governance trade-off between meaningful scrutiny, client protection and procedural cost.
  6. Automation shifts legal work toward supervision and technical competence, while employment and commercial effects remain less firmly measured.
  7. TAR supplies a precedent for generative-AI adoption, not proof that new tools will reproduce its performance or pass savings to clients.
