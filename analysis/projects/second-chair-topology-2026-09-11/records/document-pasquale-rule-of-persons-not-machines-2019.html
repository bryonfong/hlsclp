Corpus framing of this source: Legal automation’s limits: prediction, rules and reasoned standards

Corpus synthesis of this source: A Rule of Persons, Not Machines: The Limits of Legal Automation argues that most law requires explainable standards rather than either mechanical rules or prediction alone. Focusing on civil practice, the article acknowledges possible advantages of automation in extreme conditions, distinguishes those concessions from futurists’ replacement predictions, and warns that predictive tools can propagate judicial bias.

Corpus framing of this source: A Rule of Persons, Not Machines: The Limits of Legal Automation

Corpus synthesis of this source: The article distinguishes substitutive automation, which replaces lawyers, from complementary automation, which augments them. It accepts targeted automation for simple, low-stakes work and unmet legal needs, but argues that complex legal practice depends on language, explanation, discretion, appeal and accountable human governance. It recommends intelligence augmentation, human responsibility for algorithmic judgments and consumer protections against technology providers’ liability disclaimers.
