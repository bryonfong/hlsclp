Corpus framing of this source: Financialization as a shift from institutions to markets

Corpus synthesis of this source: Davis and Kim’s 2015 sociological review explains how expanding financial markets reshape corporations, households, inequality and political power. Their central contribution distinguishes market-based intermediation from the growing size of financial institutions. They synthesize historical and empirical research, qualify claims of global convergence, and explicitly treat their unifying institutional explanation as partly speculative.

Corpus framing of this source: Financialization of the Economy

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source presents financialization as a historically rooted, politically produced and institutionally varied transformation that shifts corporate strategy, employment, governance and activism toward financial-market logics. Drawing on cited historical, comparative and organizational research, it stresses both finance’s decentralizing potential and its destabilizing, exclusionary consequences. It reports—but rejects—a prediction of global convergence on a uniform American model and calls for broader sociological inquiry rather than offering a specific policy programme.
