SupportsWork redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Wavelength and the pre-GenAI legal-engineering model

Corpus synthesis of this source: Katharine Freeland presents legal engineering as an increasingly critical law-and-technology function that combines solution design, process improvement and client service. Drawing on examples and comments from Hogan Lovells and Osborne Clarke, the source argues that firms need agile technical capabilities and service offerings extending beyond legal advice, particularly during rapidly changing events such as the Covid-19 shutdown.

Corpus source-specific finding

Legal engineering was becoming a critical bridge between law and technology.

Exact returned source excerpt

‘Legal engineering’ is fast becoming a prized, and indeed critical, role at the interface between law and technology.

Exact returned source excerpt

To many, the idea of a ‘legal engineer’ is still unfamiliar, but the past few weeks of Covid-19 turmoil have shown how quickly the world can change.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source claims legal engineering can improve speed, efficiency and accuracy while reducing resource requirements.

Exact returned source excerpt

It makes legal processes quicker, more efficient and accurate at a fraction of the time, cost and manpower previously required.

Corpus source-specific finding

Hogan Lovells' response to the Covid-19 shutdown provides the source's main practical evidence.

Exact returned source excerpt

‘They are people who advise on, develop and create solutions, and are essential in keeping firms ahead of the curve.’ In recent weeks legal engineers have been using the platforms that Hogan Lovells built to review force majeure clauses in contracts that could be triggered by the global Covid-19 shutdown.

Exact returned source excerpt

Firms that have not developed such technology in recent years are at a clear disadvantage when multiple clients demand immediate support in such a fast-changing environment.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source predicts that technological agility will determine firms' ability to manage near-term disruption.

Exact returned source excerpt

Only those firms with agile technology capabilities are likely to mitigate the impact of the virus in the coming months.

Corpus source-specific finding

Law firms should broaden professional service beyond conventional legal advice.

Exact returned source excerpt

The burgeoning of what are essentially tech startups within (or affiliated to) law firms, and the scrutiny and self-evaluation that they prompt, show what law firms have learned from competing industries.

Exact returned source excerpt

‘Law firms have to provide a style of professional service to clients that goes beyond the law,’ says Dan Wright of Osborne Clarke Solutions, the tech arm of Osborne Clarke.

Corpus source-specific finding

Client expectations increasingly include technically agile tools and workflows.

Exact returned source excerpt

‘The Big Four realised the value of such inputs 20 years ago.’ Developments such as the smart contract – an automated tool that applies templates to new data entries before they are uploaded – used (among other functions) to enable blockchain in the supply chain, demonstrate the kind of technical agility that clients now expect.
