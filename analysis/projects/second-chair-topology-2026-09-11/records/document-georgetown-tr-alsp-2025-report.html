Open topic: Legal workflows: Immediate gains create competence risks

Corpus framing of this source: Alternative Legal Services Providers 2025: GenAI, ALSP demand and delivery-model strategy

Corpus synthesis of this source: Thomson Reuters reports that law firms and corporate legal departments generally expect GenAI to strengthen ALSP demand over the next three years by expanding services, streamlining processes and lowering costs. It also identifies a longer-term possibility that organisations will internalise more work, substantial variation in adoption, persistent but declining concerns, and stronger GenAI interest among firms already operating affiliate ALSPs. The TR Institute recommends matching delivery models to client goals and warns law firms against disregarding innovation.

Respondents predict a near-term net boost to ALSP use, despite possible longer-term substitution.

Builds onWork redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Alternative Legal Services Providers 2025: Strong Growth Amid Signs of a Bifurcating Market

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Thomson Reuters Institute maps ALSPs onto a broader legal-services spectrum, estimates rapid market growth, and documents uneven adoption by law firms and corporate legal departments. It finds that clients use ALSPs chiefly for cost, expertise, flexibility, technology-enabled delivery and high-volume work, while quality, confidentiality and data-location concerns persist. The report predicts that GenAI will expand ALSP use in the near term, although some buyers and firms expect eventual insourcing. It bases its findings on 2024 surveys and interviews, and recommends selecting delivery models around client goals, discussing innovation proactively and making affiliate-ALSP value more visible.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report replaces a simple law-firm-versus-ALSP distinction with a legal-services spectrum.

Exact returned source excerpt

Alternative Legal Services Providers 2025 4 A new legal services framework Since the Thomson Reuters Institute first launched this report, the ALSP market has grown significantly and has become increasingly complex as providers offer a broader and more diverse range of services.

Exact returned source excerpt

Given the evolution of the ALSP market, and the fact that some aspects of alternative legal services have become decidedly mainstream, we suggest a framework that positions different types of legal advice and services along a spectrum.

Corpus framing of this source: ALSP growth, divergent adoption and the changing mix of legal-service delivery

Corpus synthesis of this source: The 2025 report estimates rapid ALSP market growth and finds that existing users plan to deepen adoption while many non-users remain reluctant. It combines market estimates, surveys and interviews to examine sourcing choices, collaboration with law firms, persistent quality and confidentiality concerns, and GenAI’s uncertain effects. The Institute recommends matching delivery models to client needs rather than assuming either universal substitution or an unchanged traditional model.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report estimates strong growth, while distinguishing historical market data from current survey evidence.

Exact returned source excerpt

Among the key findings: • The ALSP industry is thriving, with an estimated market size of $28.5 billion in 2023 and an impressive 18% compound annual growth rate from 2021 to 2023.

Exact returned source excerpt

While this growth is slower than that seen in our last report published in January 2023, it nonetheless shows that the ALSP market is growing much more quickly than the market for traditional legal services.

Corpus source-specific finding

ALSPs occupy a continuum of services and both collaborate and compete with law firms.

Exact returned source excerpt

Given the evolution of the ALSP market, and the fact that some aspects of alternative legal services have become decidedly mainstream, we suggest a framework that positions different types of legal advice and services along a spectrum.

Exact returned source excerpt

While our definition of an ALSP continues to exclude pure technology companies, we have included reference to software products in this research in recognition of the fact that many ALSPs — and even some law firms — now sell software solutions directly to clients as part of their holistic offerings.

Corpus source-specific finding

Adoption is substantial but increasingly divides experienced users from reluctant non-users.

Exact returned source excerpt

Already, well more than half (57%) of corporate law departments rely on ALSPs for a range of services, from flexible resourcing to eDiscovery and litigation support.

Exact returned source excerpt

Some 35% of law firm respondents said that their firms use independent ALSPs to deliver products or services to clients.

Corpus source-specific finding

Corporate departments choose providers by task, cost, expertise and capacity rather than abandoning law firms wholesale.

Exact returned source excerpt

Over time, however, corporate law departments have become increasingly comfortable taking more discrete control over disaggregated matters and engaging ALSPs directly as part of matter workflows or even for entirely different lines of work.
