Exact returned source excerpt

In complying with her Rule 1.1 obligations, a lawyer who engages lawyers or nonlawyers to provide outsourced legal or nonlegal services is required to comply with Rules 5.1 and 5.3.

Corpus source-specific finding

Rule 5.1(b) supervision extends beyond lawyers within the same firm.

Exact returned source excerpt

1 states that “[p]aragraph (b) applies to lawyers who have supervisory authority over the work of other lawyers in a firm” (emphasis supplied), we do not believe that the drafters of the Model Rules intended to restrict the application of Rule 5.1(b) to the supervision of lawyers within “firms” as defined in Rule 1.0(c).

Exact returned source excerpt

A contrary interpretation would lead to the anomalous result that lawyers who outsource have a lower standard of care when supervising outsourced lawyers than they have with respect to lawyers within their own firm.

Corpus source-specific finding

Weak foreign training or disciplinary systems call for closer scrutiny, not categorical exclusion.

Exact returned source excerpt

The lack of rigorous training or effective lawyer discipline does not mean that individuals from that nation cannot be engaged to work on a particular project.

Exact returned source excerpt

What it does mean is that, in such circumstances, it will be more important than ever for the outsourcing lawyer to scrutinize the work done by the foreign lawyers – perhaps viewing them as nonlawyers – before relying upon their work in rendering legal services to the client.

Corpus source-specific finding

Cross-border outsourcing requires assessment of confidentiality and judicial-remedy risks.

Exact returned source excerpt

Consideration also should be given to the legal landscape of the nation to which the services are being outsourced, particularly the extent that personal property, including documents, may be susceptible to seizure in judicial or administrative proceedings notwithstanding claims of client confidentiality.

Exact returned source excerpt

Similarly, the judicial system of the country in question should be evaluated to assess the risk of loss of client information or disruption of the project in the event that a dispute arises between the service provider and the lawyer and the courts do not provide prompt and effective remedies to avert preju- dice to the client.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus framing of this source: ABA Opinion 08-451: Outsourcing preserves the lawyer’s professional responsibility

Corpus synthesis of this source: The ABA Standing Committee’s 2008 opinion permits outsourcing legal and nonlegal support services while retaining the responsible lawyer’s duties of competence, supervision, confidentiality, client communication and ethical billing. It offers Model Rule interpretations and practical safeguards, and describes potential cost and staffing benefits rather than presenting empirical findings.

Corpus source-specific finding

Outsourcing is permissible, but the lawyer remains responsible for competent representation.

Exact returned source excerpt

There is nothing unethical about a lawyer outsourcing legal and nonlegal services, provided the outsourcing lawyer renders legal services to the client with the “legal knowledge, skill, thoroughness and preparation reasonably necessary for the representation,” as required by Rule 1.1.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rule 1.1 does not require that tasks be accomplished in any special way.

Corpus source-specific finding

The Committee sees conditional economic and capacity benefits.

Exact returned source excerpt

The outsourcing trend is a salutary one for our globalized economy.

Exact returned source excerpt

Outsourcing affords lawyers the ability to reduce their costs and often the cost to the client to the extent that the individuals or entities providing the outsourced services can do so at lower rates than the lawyer’s own staff.

Corpus source-specific finding

Supervisory duties extend beyond the firm’s formal boundaries.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rule 5.1(b) states that “[a] lawyer having direct supervisory authority over another lawyer shall make reasonable efforts to ensure that the other lawyer conforms to the Rules of Professional Conduct.” Correlatively, Rule 5.3(b) requires lawyers who employ, retain, or associate with nonlawyers to “make reason- able efforts to ensure that the person’s conduct is compatible with the profes- sional obligations of the lawyer.” These provisions apply regardless of ^L3 Committee on Ethics and Professional Responsibility 08-451 whether the other lawyer or the nonlawyer is directly affiliated with the supervising lawyer’s firm.2 The challenge for an outsourcing lawyer is, therefore, to ensure that tasks are delegated to individuals who are competent to perform them, and then to oversee the execution of the project adequately and appropriately.

Exact returned source excerpt

A contrary interpretation would lead to the anomalous result that lawyers who outsource have a lower standard of care when supervising outsourced lawyers than they have with respect to lawyers within their own firm.

Corpus source-specific finding

Provider due diligence should address personnel, intermediaries and information security.

Exact returned source excerpt

At a minimum, a lawyer outsourcing services for ultimate provision to a client should consider conducting reference checks and investigating the background of the lawyer or nonlawyer providing the services as well as any nonlawyer intermediary involved, such as a placement agency or service provider.

Exact returned source excerpt

The lawyer also might consider interviewing the principal lawyers, if any, involved in the project, among other things assessing their educational background.

Corpus source-specific finding

Cross-border outsourcing calls for additional scrutiny, not a categorical prohibition.

Exact returned source excerpt

When engaging lawyers trained in a foreign country, the outsourcing lawyer first should assess whether the system of legal education under which the lawyers were trained is comparable to that in the United States.

Exact returned source excerpt

Also, the professional regulatory system should be evaluated to determine whether members of the nation’s legal profession have been incul- cated with core ethical principles similar to those in the United States, and whether the nation’s disciplinary enforcement system is effective in policing 2.

Corpus source-specific finding

Client disclosure and consent depend on the relationship and degree of supervision.

Exact returned source excerpt

In Formal Opinion 88-356,3 we opined that when a lawyer engaged the services of a temporary lawyer, a form of outsourcing, an obligation to advise the client of that fact and to seek the client’s consent would arise if the temporary lawyer was to perform independent work for the client without the close supervision of the hiring lawyer or another lawyer associated with her firm.

Exact returned source excerpt

Relying on Rule 1.2(a), requiring lawyers to consult with clients as to the means by which the clients’ objectives are to be pursued, Rule 1.4, relating to client communication, and Rule 7.5(d), prohibiting lawyers from implying that they practice in a partnership or other organiza- tion when that is not the fact, we concluded that clients are entitled to know who or what entity is representing them, and thus could veto the lawyer’s use of a temporary lawyer.

Corpus source-specific finding

Confidentiality requires informed consent and protective measures.

Exact returned source excerpt

Thus, where the relationship between the firm and the individuals perform- ing the services is attenuated, as in a typical outsourcing relationship, no information protected by Rule 1.6 may be revealed without the client’s informed consent.

Exact returned source excerpt

The implied authorization of Rule 1.6(a) and its Comment [5] thereto to share confidential information within a firm does not extend to outside entities or to individuals over whom the firm lacks effective supervi- sion and control.

Corpus source-specific finding

Outsourcing remains subject to fee and unauthorized-practice restrictions.

Exact returned source excerpt
