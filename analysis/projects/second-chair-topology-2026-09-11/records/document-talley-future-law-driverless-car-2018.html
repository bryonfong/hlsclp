Corpus framing of this source: Talley on data analytics as a complement to, not a categorical substitute for, lawyers

Corpus synthesis of this source: Eric L. Talley argues that data analytics will penetrate legal practice and research, including transactional analysis and potentially outcome prediction, but will more likely reshape lawyers’ work than eliminate demand for lawyers. He grounds this qualified prediction in existing and experimental applications of machine learning, the continuing need for legally trained human input, and law’s dynamic, context-dependent complexity. He recommends precise terminology and complementary technical skills for future legal professionals.

Corpus framing of this source: Talley: Data analytics will transform legal work without categorically displacing lawyers

Corpus synthesis of this source: Eric L. Talley argues that machine learning and related data-analytics techniques will spread from discovery and citation analysis into transactional practice, legal research and risk prediction. His corporate-opportunity-waiver study demonstrates large labour savings and strong classification performance, while also illustrating the continuing need for legally trained humans. He predicts changes in lawyers’ functions and law-firm staffing rather than the profession’s elimination, because supervised systems require human inputs, legal doctrine evolves through difficult-to-predict normative change, and law itself must govern automation.
