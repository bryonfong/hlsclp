SupportsAI insurance: Coverage cannot replace professional governance

Open topic: AI insurance: Coverage cannot replace professional governance

Corpus framing of this source: Lockton: AI liability, insurance coverage and risk management for law firms

Corpus synthesis of this source: Lockton’s June 2024 article maps law-firm AI risks onto insurance policies, anticipates greater underwriting scrutiny and recommends practical governance measures. It draws on SRA adoption figures, distinguishes expected coverage from possible future insurer requirements, and stresses uncertainty about evolving legal liabilities.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI adoption is advancing faster than guidance, creating potential liability and compliance risks.

Exact returned source excerpt

Law firms are no exception, with many firms taking advantage of the benefits of AI to optimise processes and drive cost efficiencies at scale.

Exact returned source excerpt

The uptake of AI is outstripping efforts to create guidelines and establish best practice, while new regulation is also set to create compliance risks.

Corpus source-specific finding

The application of existing law to AI remains uncertain.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, the General Data Protection Regulation (*(EU) 2016/679*) (EU GDPR) in the EU and the retained EU law version of the EU GDPR (UK GDPR) in the UK, or laws governing equality or consumer rights.

Exact returned source excerpt

In many cases, precisely how these laws will apply to the use of AI, and therefore the extent of liability that they will create, is not yet clear.

Corpus source-specific finding

Existing insurance policies may address different categories of AI-related claims.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the UK, solicitors’ PII policies must: - Comply with the SRA Minimum Terms and Conditions of Professional Indemnity Insurance (MTC) (contained in Annex 1 of the [SRA Indemnity Insurance Rules (opens a new window)](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/indemnity-insurance-rules/)); and - Provide coverage for claims based on “any civil liability” arising from private practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

PII policies should therefore respond where AI is used to perform legal duties and a claim against the insured later arises in relation to an alleged breach of those duties.

Corpus source-specific finding

Lockton characterises insurers as favouring managed adoption rather than either technological leadership or avoidance.

Exact returned source excerpt

In considering law firm insurance applications, many insurers will expect to see evidence of how firms are adapting to the changes and preparing for the future.

Exact returned source excerpt

This does not mean that firms are expected to lead the line with regards to implementing AI tools, but nor should they be overly averse to its potential benefits.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI use may require disclosure and prompt new underwriting questions or warranties.

Exact returned source excerpt

**Impact on insurance applications** As insurers’ awareness of AI risks for their insureds deepens, they are likely to seek further information and assurances from firms as part of their application and renewal processes.

Exact returned source excerpt

The extent of its use may therefore be disclosable to insurers under the pre-contractual duty of fair presentation under the Insurance Act 2015.

Corpus source-specific finding

Firms should establish accountable governance, ongoing monitoring and human verification skills.

Exact returned source excerpt

Examples of best practice measures to consider include: - Creating internal AI policies and risk frameworks that are detailed and adhered to.

Exact returned source excerpt

The firm should ensure that these are regularly updated as the AI technology and its use within the firm evolves.

Corpus source-specific finding

Proactive risk management and broker engagement may improve insurance terms.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, in making the most of the technology, firms should take proactive steps to identify and manage the risks that AI use also presents to avoid issues, breaches and potential claims.

Exact returned source excerpt

Doing so also assists the firm in addressing any concerns or information requests from insurers, and may therefore also assist in securing insurance on the best possible terms.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

In the case of law firms, this places responsibility for AI regulation with the SRA.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source reports emerging US state guidance, including confidentiality and human-verification requirements.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the US, no federal legislation on AI regulation has yet been passed, although certain states have begun to introduce guidelines or legal precedents relating to AI use.

Exact returned source excerpt

In November 2023, the State Bar of California approved guidance on [the use of GenAI in the practice of law (opens a new window)](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/ethics/Generative-AI-Practical-Guidance.pdf), stating (among other requirements) that lawyers must anonymise client information when inputting data into GenAI tools and review all outputs before submission to court.

Corpus source-specific finding

Firms should map applicable AI obligations into their risk mitigation strategies, including obligations under existing law.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt
