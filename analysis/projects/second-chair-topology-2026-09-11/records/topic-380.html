Native topic findings

  1. AI can improve knowledge-work performance, but task-level gains do not establish whole-job productivity or worker replacement.
  2. Contrasting coding results make task context and measurement central to the debate.
  3. Evidence that AI narrows performance gaps coexists with evidence that experienced or AI-skilled users capture more value.
  4. Assisted performance and durable skill formation can diverge.
  5. AI can redistribute tasks and some collaborative functions without eliminating human contribution.
  6. Adoption, preference and perceived savings are not substitutes for measured net benefits.
  7. Training, workflow design and verification condition deployment value, including in legal work.
  8. Productivity gains for novices do not imply stronger entry-level employment; observed labor-market evidence remains contested.
  9. Aggregate growth and distributional claims depend on assumptions beyond the experiments.
