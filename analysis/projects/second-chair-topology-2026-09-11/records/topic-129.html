Native topic record

Medical governance: Classification cannot replace lifecycle oversight

Corpus synthesis: Combines regulatory boundaries, planned modification, representative testing and public records without treating inventories as complete clinical evidence.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Evidence for deployed AI: Proxy measures do not establish real-world effectiveness · Adaptive medical devices: Planned modification requires lifecycle regulatory control

4 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 1 · Supports: 3

Native topic findings

  1. Medical-AI governance combines regulatory classification, independent professional review and lifecycle oversight rather than treating all AI alike.
  2. Regulatory boundaries influence development choices, but classification does not settle what oversight is appropriate.
  3. FDA proposes planned, risk-managed modification as a way to accommodate iterative AI development while maintaining oversight.
  4. Credible oversight requires representative data and clear distinctions between model development and performance testing.
  5. Public authorization records improve visibility but cannot serve as a complete census or a full account of clinical evidence.
  6. Transparency remains a developing governance measure rather than a demonstrated solution to safety or trust problems.
