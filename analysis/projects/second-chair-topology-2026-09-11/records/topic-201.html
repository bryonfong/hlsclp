Native topic record

Human-AI decisions: Self-knowledge conditions unrealized gains

Corpus synthesis: Shows case uncertainty and model error can reverse assistance benefits, favouring workflow experiments over routine deployment.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Evidence for deployed AI: Proxy measures do not establish real-world effectiveness · Clinical AI assistance: Standalone accuracy does not ensure better care

4 distinct stance-bearing works · Redirects: 1 · Supports: 3

Native topic findings

  1. Strong standalone AI performance does not guarantee better human–AI decisions.
  2. Who benefits depends on more than baseline skill or seniority; self-calibration offers a conditional explanation.
  3. Case-level uncertainty and AI error can turn assistance from helpful to harmful.
  4. The sources identify unrealized gains from combining information, but differ in how far they infer behavioral mechanisms.
  5. Practical recommendations favor testing alternative workflows rather than assuming routine assistance is optimal.
  6. Task-specific diagnostic results do not establish occupation-wide displacement or generalize directly to generative AI.
