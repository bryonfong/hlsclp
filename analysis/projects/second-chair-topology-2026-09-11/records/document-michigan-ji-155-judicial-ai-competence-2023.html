Corpus synthesis of this source: Michigan Judicial Ethics Opinion JI-155 (27 October 2023) calls on judges to understand AI and determine appropriate court practices. It reports safeguards adopted by some courts, recognises benefits from proper AI use and argues that evolving systems may require renewed legal scrutiny despite existing precedent.

Corpus synthesis of this source: Michigan Judicial Ethics Opinion JI-155 (27 October 2023) applies judicial competence and impartiality duties to AI. Its contribution rests on ethical interpretation, illustrative court responses and anticipated risks rather than quantitative findings.

Exact returned source excerpt

Competency with advancing technology is further required by Canon 3(B), which requires judicial officers to “maintain professional competence in judicial administration.” Legal knowledge, skills, thoroughness, and preparation are required for judicial officers to perform their duties.

Exact returned source excerpt

This includes knowing the benefits and risks associated with the technology that judicial officers and their staff use daily, as well as the technology used by lawyers who come before the bench.

Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt

Rule 1.1 of the Michigan Rules of Professional Conduct (MRPC) provides that “[a] lawyer shall provide competent representation to a client.” The comment to MRPC 1.1 expressly references technological competence.[[1]](#_ftn1) This need for competence applies to judicial officers as well.

Exact returned source excerpt

Specifically, Canon 3(A)(1) of the Michigan Code of Judicial Conduct provides that “[a] judge should be faithful to the law and maintain professional competence in it.” As the use of technology increases, so does the requirement to maintain competence in what is available, how it is used, and whether the use of the technology in question would affect a judicial decision.
