Open topic: Digital ethics: Existing duties extend to new media

Corpus framing of this source: Technology confidentiality duties and inadvertent disclosure

Corpus synthesis of this source: The ABA Commission on Ethics 20/20’s Revised Resolution 105A as Amended sets out professional-conduct rules and commentary on safeguarding client information, receiving accidentally disclosed material and respecting third persons’ rights. It distinguishes reasonable precautions and mandatory notification from discretionary responses and duties governed by other law; it offers normative guidance rather than empirical findings or predictions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Lawyers must take reasonable, context-sensitive precautions when transmitting client information.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus framing of this source: Technology competence, client communication and reasonable confidentiality safeguards

Corpus synthesis of this source: The ABA Commission on Ethics 20/20’s Revised Resolution 105A as Amended: Technology and Confidentiality links technological awareness to competent representation and sets out communication and confidentiality duties in Model Rules text and commentary. Its contribution is normative guidance rather than empirical findings: lawyers should understand relevant technology, support informed client decisions and take reasonable precautions against disclosure or unauthorized access.

Corpus framing of this source: Technology, competence and confidentiality under the ABA Model Rules

Corpus synthesis of this source: The ABA Commission on Ethics 20/20 proposes amendments to five Model Rules and their comments to integrate electronic communications, technology competence, client communication, confidentiality safeguards, screening and inadvertently transmitted electronic information into lawyers’ professional obligations. It uses reasonableness and professional-judgment standards rather than treating every unauthorized disclosure or inadvertent receipt as an automatic violation.

The resolution expressly updates the Model Rules to address technology and confidentiality.

Corpus source-specific finding

The treatment of inadvertently transmitted information extends to email, readable electronic information and metadata, but the metadata duty depends on notice.

Exact returned source excerpt

For purposes of this 222 Rule, ‘‘document or electronically stored information’’ includes, in addition to paper documents, 223 email and other forms of electronically stored information, including embedded data (commonly 224 referred to as “metadata”), that is email or other electronic modes of transmission subject to 225 being read or put into readable form.

Exact returned source excerpt
