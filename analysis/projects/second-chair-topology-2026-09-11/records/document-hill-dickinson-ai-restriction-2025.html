Corpus framing of this source: Hill Dickinson’s controlled adoption of workplace AI after a surge in usage

Corpus synthesis of this source: Legal Futures and BBC News report that Hill Dickinson restricted general access to public AI tools after monitoring showed heavy, partly non-compliant use. The firm presents the measure as controlled adoption rather than an AI ban, combining approval requirements, data safeguards, verification, training and human oversight. The reporting qualifies the usage figures and separates Hill Dickinson’s account from recommendations by the Information Commissioner’s Office and comments from the Law Society.

Corpus source-specific finding

Monitoring identified substantial use of public AI and writing tools, prompting concern about policy compliance.

Exact returned source excerpt

The email said: "We have been monitoring usage of Al tools, particularly publicly available generative Al solutions, and have noticed a significant increase in usage of, and uploading of files to, such tools." Hill Dickinson had detected more than 32,000 hits to ChatGPT over a seven-day period in January and February, and more than 3,000 visits to the Chinese AI service DeepSeek, as well as almost 50,000 hits to Grammarly, the writing assistance tool.

Exact returned source excerpt

The firm said much of the usage was not in line with its AI policy, and going forward the firm would only allow staff to access the tools via a request process.

Corpus source-specific finding

The raw hit counts do not establish the number of users or discrete sessions.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, it is not clear on how many occasions staff visited ChatGPT, DeepSeek, or Grammarly, or how many staff visited repeatedly, as several hits could have been generated by a user during every time they used the websites.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, it is understood that these were not individual instances but inputted prompts, where multiple prompts were likely to have happened in a single session.

Corpus source-specific finding

Hill Dickinson replaced general access with a case-by-case approval process rather than prohibiting AI outright.

Exact returned source excerpt

"This policy does not discourage the use of AI but simply ensures that our colleagues use such tools safely and responsibly – including having an approved case for using AI platforms, prohibiting the uploading of client information and validating the accuracy of responses provided by large language models.

Exact returned source excerpt

The firm is now only granting access to AI tools via a request process.

The firm advocates beneficial AI use subject to human oversight, confidentiality safeguards and verification.

Exact returned source excerpt

In a statement, the 200-partner practice said: "Like many law firms, we are aiming to positively embrace the use of AI tools to enhance our capabilities while always ensuring safe and proper use by our people and for our clients.
