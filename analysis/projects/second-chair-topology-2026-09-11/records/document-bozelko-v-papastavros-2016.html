Corpus framing of this source: Bozelko: expert proof of causation in legal malpractice

Corpus synthesis of this source: In Bozelko v. Papastavros, 323 Conn. 275, 147 A.3d 1023 (2016), the Connecticut Supreme Court holds that legal malpractice plaintiffs generally need expert testimony to establish causation, subject to exceptions for obvious cases. It affirms judgment against a plaintiff who supplied no expert evidence linking alleged defense failures to her criminal convictions, and distinguishes proof of causation from proof of deficient care.

Malpractice requires proof that the attorney's wrongdoing caused the claimed injury.

Generally, a plaintiff alleging legal malpractice must prove all of the following elements: "(1) the existence of an attorney-client relationship; (2) the attorney's wrongful act or omission; (3) _causation_ ; and (4) damages." The first component, causation in fact, requires us to determine whether the injury would have occurred but for the defendant's conduct.

Corpus framing of this source: Bozelko v. Papastavros: Expert testimony on causation in legal malpractice

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Connecticut Supreme Court held that a criminal-defence malpractice plaintiff needed expert testimony to connect counsel’s alleged failings to her convictions. It distinguished proof of breach from proof of causation, applied the case-within-a-case framework, rejected juror testimony as an adequate substitute, and affirmed summary judgment for the defendant.

Corpus source-specific finding

The court required expert testimony to establish causation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Even if we assume, without deciding, that the plaintiff raised allegations of gross negligence, we nevertheless conclude that an expert witness was required to prove that the defendant's alleged conduct and omissions, rather than the plaintiff's guilt of the crimes charged, were the cause of the plaintiff's convictions.

Exact returned source excerpt

[9](http://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4257872/bozelko-v-papastavros/#fn%20%20%20%20%209) Accordingly, the trial court properly granted the defendant's motion for summary judgment.
