Open topic: AI supervision: Retained responsibility is not strict liability

Corpus framing of this source: Oregon ethics opinion requires AI policies, supervision and tool-specific training

Corpus synthesis of this source: Oregon State Bar Formal Opinion No. 2025-205: Artificial Intelligence applies Oregon’s managerial and supervisory ethics duties to AI use by lawyers and nonlawyers. The excerpt prescribes firm policies and practical training, and identifies additional confidentiality precautions that may be necessary when using open AI models.

Corpus source-specific finding

Oregon’s managerial and supervisory duties extend to AI use.

Exact returned source excerpt

Responsibilities of Partners, Managers, and Supervisory Lawyers; Responsibilities of a Subordinate Lawyer; and Responsibilities Regarding Nonlawyer Assistance Oregon RPC 5.1 and 5.3 address the ethical duties of lawyers charged with mana- gerial and supervisory responsibilities and set forth those lawyers’ responsibilities with regard to the firm, subordinate lawyers, and nonlawyers.12 Managerial lawyers must create effective measures to ensure that all lawyers in the firm conform to the Oregon Rules of Professional Conduct, and supervisory lawyers must supervise subordinate lawyers and nonlawyer assistants to ensure that subordinate lawyers and nonlawyer assistants similarly conform to the rules.

Exact returned source excerpt

Managerial lawyers must establish clear policies regarding the law firm’s permissi- ble use of AI, including specifically GAI, and supervisory lawyers must make reasonable efforts to ensure that the firm’s lawyers and nonlawyers comply with their professional obligations when using such technologies.

Corpus source-specific finding

Supervisors must ensure training addresses the specific AI tools used.

Exact returned source excerpt

Supervisory obligations also include ensuring that subordinate lawyers and nonlawyers are trained, including in the ethical and practical use of AI relevant to their work, as well as on risks associated with AI use.

Exact returned source excerpt

Training must include the basics of the actual type of AI being used (rather than just AI in general), the capabilities and limitations of the tools, ethical issues in the use of that specific AI, and best practices for secure data handling, privacy, and confidentiality.

Corpus source-specific finding

Open AI models may require additional protection for sensitive client information.

Exact returned source excerpt

to use an open AI model, the lawyer may also need to take additional precautions against disclosure through the anonymization or redaction of sensitive client information.

Corpus framing of this source: Oregon ethics opinion requires verification and correction of AI-generated legal content

Corpus synthesis of this source: Oregon State Bar Formal Opinion No. 2025-205 links lawyers’ accuracy and candour obligations to human verification of generative AI output. It requires correction of false statements to courts while protecting client confidences and cites reported research to illustrate hallucination risks.

Open topic: Legal workflows: Immediate gains create competence risks

Corpus framing of this source: Oregon State Bar Formal Opinion No. 2025-205: Artificial Intelligence

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Oregon State Bar Legal Ethics Committee concludes that Oregon lawyers may use AI and generative AI, subject to an ongoing duty of technological competence under Oregon RPC 1.1. It grounds that qualification in AI’s broad integration into legal tools, generative AI’s capacity to create legal documents, and the rapidly changing benefits and risks of specific systems.

Corpus framing of this source: Oregon lawyers’ ethical duties when using artificial intelligence

Corpus synthesis of this source: Oregon State Bar Formal Opinion No. 2025-205 applies existing professional-conduct duties to lawyers’ use of AI. It addresses client communication, confidentiality, informed consent, vendor safeguards, supervision, verification, candor and billing, while distinguishing the risks of open and closed models.

Corpus source-specific finding

Lawyers should assess client communication and consent case by case.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, lawyers are allowed to take actions without consulting the client that are “impliedly authorized” by the representation.

Exact returned source excerpt

The use of word processing applications and research databases are generally considered to be impliedly authorized.

Corpus source-specific finding

Using client information in an open model generally requires informed consent or effective protective measures.

Exact returned source excerpt

If lawyers would like to use their clients’ information in an open model, lawyers must obtain their clients’ informed consent.

Exact returned source excerpt

“‘[I]nformed consent’ denotes the agreement by a person to a proposed course of conduct after the lawyer has communicated adequate information and explanation about the material risks of and reasonably available alternatives to the proposed course of conduct.” Oregon RPC 1.0(g).

Corpus source-specific finding

Closed models offer greater protection but do not eliminate confidentiality duties.

Exact returned source excerpt

In those models, data is processed and retained within a secure environment, which is critical for preserving the confidentiality of client information but limits the scope of AI’s learning capacity.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, the mere engagement of a closed model does not absolve the necessity for a lawyer to be vigilant.
