Corpus framing of this source: Why We Need Accountable Algorithms

Corpus synthesis of this source: Cathy O’Neil argues that algorithmic systems are neither inherently objective nor morally neutral: their builders select data, define success and assign the costs of error. Historical bias, opacity and weak avenues for appeal can therefore turn automated scoring into unaccountable discrimination. She recommends clean and relevant data, attention to privacy and error trade-offs, and industry-wide anti-discrimination and fairness standards because competitive incentives discourage individual firms from bearing the costs voluntarily. The supplied material also reports responses that dispute moral non-neutrality, emphasise government power or data collection, and question broad regulation.
