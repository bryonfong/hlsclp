Corpus framing of this source: Measuring the US justice gap through people’s experiences and fair resolutions

Corpus synthesis of this source: IAALS and HiiL’s 2021 report presents a nationwide survey of everyday legal problems and argues that access to justice requires fair resolutions across all income groups, not merely access to lawyers. It estimates a large unmet need, documents unequal burdens and outcomes, and recommends evidence-based, people-centred services, prevention and regulatory innovation. Its estimates rely on respondents’ perceptions and qualified population extrapolations.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report builds its findings on a broad, self-reported survey rather than court or lawyer-service records.

Corpus framing of this source: Justice Needs and Satisfaction in the United States of America — Legal Problems in Daily Life

Corpus synthesis of this source: IAALS and HiiL contribute a nationwide, people-centred account of Americans’ legal problems, their consequences, resolution paths, sources of help, interventions and justice-journey costs. They identify a large and unequal justice-resolution gap, distinguish frequent problems from high-impact ones, and argue for evidence-based, scalable, user-centred services that extend beyond courts and lawyers. They qualify causal interpretations, estimates and subgroup findings, and disclose survey and pandemic-period limitations.
