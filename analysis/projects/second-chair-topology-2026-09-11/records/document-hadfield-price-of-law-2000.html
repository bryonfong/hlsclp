The excerpt supports this argument with historical survey, staffing, funding and participation figures; it offers no explicit recommendation or prediction.

Corpus framing of this source: Hadfield: Structural Market Power Distorts the Allocation of Legal Services

Corpus synthesis of this source: Gillian K. Hadfield argues that the market for lawyers is structurally noncompetitive. Legal complexity, lawyers’ access to the state’s coercive dispute-resolution system, and the unified profession make prices respond more to clients’ wealth and stakes than to production costs. These dynamics channel legal effort toward corporate work and away from individuals’ justice needs. She treats reform proposals as preliminary, questions conventional price regulation, and identifies institutional unbundling and differentiated professional structures as possible directions.
