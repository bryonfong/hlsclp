Corpus source-specific finding

Japan combines three complementary governance instruments rather than relying on rigid AI regulation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Instead of rigid regulation, Japan relies on three pillars: (1) the non-binding Act on the Promotion of Research, Development and Utilisation of Artificial Intelligence-Related Technologies (the ‘AI Promotion Act’), which sets the strategic direction; (2) the 2024 AI Business Operator Guidelines (the ‘AI Guidelines for Business’), which translate principles into practice; and (3) guidance on the interpretation of existing statutes, such as the Copyright Act and the Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI) to address issues raised by generative AI.

# The AI Guidelines for Business: soft law in practice Published by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) and the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) in April 2024, the AI Guidelines for Business respond to global discussions, including those that took place during the 2023 G7 Hiroshima AI Summit.

Exact returned source excerpt

Structured according to three tiers, the Guidelines define the foundational values (human dignity, inclusion, sustainability), set out ten cross-sector principles (eg, fairness, privacy, safety) and offer useful tools, such as checklists and case studies.

Voluntary guidelines may influence legal interpretation, commercial scrutiny and industry standards.

Exact returned source excerpt

# Japanese copyright law and generative AI In May 2024, the Agency for Cultural Affairs, which is the agency responsible for copyright law and policy, published a document entitled the ‘General Understanding on AI and Copyright in Japan’ to clarify the interpretation of relevant copyright statutes in the context of generative AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

Article 30-4 permits non-expressive uses, such as data analysis for training purposes, without the need for authorisation by the rightsholder, so long as the output does not replicate the expressive works.

Corpus source-specific finding

Proposed personal-data revisions could ease consent requirements under restricted uses.

One notable aspect of the proposed revisions is the potential relaxation of the consent requirements for the provision of personal data to third parties, as well as the acquisition of publicly available sensitive personal information provided that specific conditions are met.

Exact returned source excerpt

These conditions include ensuring that the data is used exclusively for the creation of statistical information, with the term the ‘creation of statistical information’ being interpreted broadly to encompass AI development activities.

# Japanese copyright law and generative AI In May 2024, the Agency for Cultural Affairs, which is the agency responsible for copyright law and policy, published a document entitled the ‘General Understanding on AI and Copyright in Japan’ to clarify the interpretation of relevant copyright statutes in the context of generative AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

Predating the explosion of generative AI, Japan’s Copyright Act had introduced limitations to copyright to address the training of AI systems in 2020.

Corpus source-specific finding

For generated outputs, the source frames copyright liability around substantial similarity and dependency, with potential exposure extending beyond users to developers and service providers.

Exact returned source excerpt

Output liability depends on two factors: substantial similarity and dependency (*ikyo-sei*).

Exact returned source excerpt

If a generated output resembles a known work and that work was included in the training data, dependency is presumed.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source reports that Japan’s privacy regulator has treated the retention or reuse of personal data entered into AI systems as a potential APPI violation and has established compliance expectations without pursuing further public enforcement.

Exact returned source excerpt

In June 2023, the Personal Information Protection Commission (PPC) warned that inputting personal data into AI systems may violate the law if that data is retained or reused for training purposes.

Exact returned source excerpt

Key legal anchors include Article 18 (limiting use to disclosed purposes) and Article 27 (requiring consent for third-party provision of data).

Corpus source-specific finding

The source reports that the PPC is considering APPI revisions that could relax some consent restrictions for AI development under specified safeguards.

Exact returned source excerpt

At the same time, as part of the triennial review of Japan’s APPI, the PPC is considering a series of regulatory updates in response to the evolving data landscape.
