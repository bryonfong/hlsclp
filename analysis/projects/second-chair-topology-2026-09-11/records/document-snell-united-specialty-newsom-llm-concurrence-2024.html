Corpus source-specific finding

Snell’s bad-faith claim fails both because United lawfully denied coverage and because his investigation complaints do not meet the required standard.

Exact returned source excerpt

Snell advances what the Alabama Supreme Court calls an “abnormal” bad faith claim—which is focused on bad faith failure to investigate.

Exact returned source excerpt

A plaintiff advancing an “abnormal” bad faith claim must prove that the insurer (1) intentionally or recklessly failed to investigate a claim; (2) intentionally or recklessly failed to properly subject a claim to a cognitive evaluation or review; (3) manufactured a debatable reason to deny a claim; or (4) relied on an ambiguous portion of a policy as a lawful basis for denying a claim.

SupportsWork redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Snell v. United Specialty Insurance Co., 102 F.4th 1208 (11th Cir. 2024) — Judge Newsom's concurrence on LLMs and ordinary meaning

Corpus synthesis of this source: Judge Newsom tentatively proposes using LLM outputs as supplementary evidence of ordinary meaning, not as authoritative answers or substitutes for judicial judgment. He argues that broad training data, contextual language modelling, accessibility and research transparency could make LLMs useful—and sometimes preferable to surveys or corpus linguistics—while identifying hallucinations, demographic underrepresentation, manipulation, regional variation, prompt sensitivity and historical-language limitations. He recommends verification, disclosure, careful query design, broader datasets, comparison across models and continued human judgment.

Corpus source-specific finding

Use LLMs as an interpretive aid, not a decision-maker

Exact returned source excerpt

For all these reasons, and perhaps others I haven’t identified, it seems to me that it’s at least worth considering whether and how we might leverage LLMs in the ordinary-meaning enterprise— again, not as the be all and end all, but rather as one aid to be used alongside dictionaries, the semantic canons, etc.

Exact returned source excerpt

And I hope it’s clear by this point that I am not—not, not, not—suggesting that any judge should ever query an LLM concerning the ordinary meaning of some word (say, “landscaping”) and then mechanistically apply it to her facts and render judgment.
