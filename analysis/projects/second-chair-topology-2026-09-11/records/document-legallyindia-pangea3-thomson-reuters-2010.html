Corpus framing of this source: Thomson Reuters enters legal outsourcing through Pangea3 acquisition

Corpus synthesis of this source: Kian Ganz’s November 2010 Legally India report documents the acquisition, distinguishes the reported price from official confirmation, and sets out management’s strategic rationale. It also supplies context on Pangea3’s expansion, financing and ownership, Thomson Reuters’ legal-information businesses, and another recent outsourcing acquisition.

Corpus source-specific finding

The acquisition was confirmed, but its price remained unofficial.

The acquisition price would be in the range of $35 to 40m (Rs 157 to 200 crore) first reported [Business Standard](http://www.business-standard.

Corpus source-specific finding

Thomson Reuters management presented outsourcing as a strategic complement to legal information and workflow products.

Exact returned source excerpt

Its legal division’s president and chief executive officer said in a press release that legal outsourcing added a vital strategic complement to the company’s portfolio of “specialised information and workflow solutions”, and would be key to helping law firms and corporate legal departments be more “responsive and cost-effective”.

Exact returned source excerpt

"Pangea3 is true to our mission to help the legal system perform better, every day, worldwide; we will now bring to the legal marketplace a responsive, high-quality, transformative resource for a broad range of legal support work,” he added in the statement.

Corpus source-specific finding

Pangea3 had already announced substantial expansion plans before the acquisition.

Exact returned source excerpt

In June 2010 Pangea3 told *Legally India* that it was [planning to invest Rs 48 crore ($10.6m)](https://www.legallyindia.com/20100604922/Legal-Process-Outsourcing-LPO/pangea3-hires-18-from-campus-will-pump-rs-48-crore-into-delhi-to-outgrow-mumbai) in its Delhi office within two years to make the location outgrow its head office Mumbai.

Exact returned source excerpt

Pangea3 then had a headcount in Mumbai of 500, with 70 staff being based in Delhi.

Corpus source-specific finding

Private-equity investment preceded the sale, while the founders reportedly retained significant stakes.

Exact returned source excerpt

Pangea3 has seen two private equity investments since it was founded in 2004, with [Glenrock Group](http://www.hindustantimes.com/GlenRock-invests-in-Pangea3/Article1-81576.

) LLC investing $4m in 2006 and [Sequoia Capital](http://www.livemint.com/2007/07/17003646/Pangea3-ropes-in-Rs28-crore-fr.html) taking a $7m stake in 2007.

Corpus source-specific finding

The deal extended Thomson Reuters beyond legal-information services and accompanied other outsourcing acquisitions.

Exact returned source excerpt

Thomson Reuters, which has no businesses in the LPO space, acquired [Indlaw Communications](http://www.vccircle.com/500/news/thomson-reuters-buys-indian-legal-database-company) in 2009, which provides legal database services in India.

Exact returned source excerpt

The erstwhile Thomson Corporation also owns legal research database services such as Westlaw and legal publisher Sweet & Maxwell.
