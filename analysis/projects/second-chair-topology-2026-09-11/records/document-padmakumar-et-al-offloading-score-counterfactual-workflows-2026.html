Corpus framing of this source: Measuring cognitive offloading without equating high AI reliance with overreliance

Corpus synthesis of this source: Offloading Score: Measuring AI Reliance Through Counterfactual Workflows proposes a process-based reliance metric and tests it in a 40-person coding study. The authors report greater sensitivity to time pressure than usage-based measures, but find that substantial delegation can coexist with strong code understanding. They make overreliance judgments conditional on user goals and acknowledge substantial measurement and generalisability limits.

Corpus source-specific finding

The metric estimates cognitive work delegated, rather than simply counting AI use or accepted outputs.

Exact returned source excerpt

Note W ′ is defined with respect to an average user rather than the specific user U , allowing this framework to be reused across different users without requiring a personalized estimate for each individual.2 We quantify the cognitive effort offloaded to T by the scalar fraction m−n m , which measures the proportion of counterfactual workflow steps saved through the use of the tool.3 In general, m ≥ n, so the range of this value is between 0 and 1.
