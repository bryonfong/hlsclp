Corpus framing of this source: Trust and Reliance on AI in Education: AI Literacy and Need for Cognition as Moderators

Corpus synthesis of this source: Pitts, Rani and Mildort report a laboratory study of 432 computing students using an AI assistant on Python output-prediction tasks. They find that greater post-task trust predicted less selective reliance, principally through frequent acceptance of intentionally misleading recommendations. AI literacy and need for cognition moderated this relationship, but did not overcome its negative direction as trust rose. The authors recommend embedding verification and cognitive forcing into instruction and tool design, while qualifying the findings as task-specific and limited by self-report and coarse behavioural measures.
