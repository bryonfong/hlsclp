Corpus framing of this source: Harbor: AI uptake outpaces legal departments’ operating-model maturity

Corpus synthesis of this source: Harbor’s May 2026 survey press release argues that widespread AI uptake has not yet translated into scalable operations. It identifies workflow, talent and governance integration as the next priority and links stronger internal capabilities to more selective outside-counsel purchasing.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI engagement is nearly universal, but deployment depth varies.

Exact returned source excerpt

- **AI adoption is nearly universal, but depth varies**: 98% of legal departments report implementing or exploring AI, with 57% already live and 24% in pilot.

Corpus source-specific finding

Harbor identifies operating-model gaps, rather than technology availability alone, as the constraint on scale.

Exact returned source excerpt

The next phase of maturity will be defined by how well organizations align technology, workflows, talent, and governance into a cohesive operating model." Key findings from Harbor's 2026 Legal Department Maturity Index Survey include: - **Operating model maturity is lagging investment**: Despite near-universal AI adoption, most departments have not yet built the integrated workflows, governance structures, and talent models needed to scale what they have deployed.

Exact returned source excerpt

The departments pulling ahead are those integrating AI into core workflows and systems — not running it as a standalone tool.

- **Technology is the top strategic priority**: 81% of departments rank it as their primary operational focus for 2026, alongside firm and vendor management and financial discipline.
