Native topic findings

  1. Globalization expands corporate legal practice without producing a uniform Anglo-American profession.
  2. Economic opening creates legal demand, but the balance among states, markets and bars shapes who supplies it.
  3. Growing in-house purchasing power changes law-firm competition, but does not automatically confer internal authority or independence.
  4. Liberalization remains a distributional conflict, not an inevitable consequence of globalization.
  5. Corporate lawyers can shape governance, but influence over capital does not establish public benefit.
  6. Professional capacity depends on training and organizational practice, not simply graduate numbers or international scale.
  7. Outsourcing and cross-professional competition raise unresolved questions about restructuring and professional autonomy.
