Corpus framing of this source: Rapid in-house adoption of contract AI and supervised agents

Corpus synthesis of this source: Artificial Lawyer reports LegalOn’s 2026 survey as evidence that contract AI has moved into mainstream in-house use. It highlights reported efficiency gains and openness to attorney-supervised agents, while identifying incomplete playbooks as a constraint. The article attributes adoption partly to improved tools and separately reports LegalOn CEO Daniel Lewis’s view that teams will broaden deployment.

Corpus source-specific finding

The survey reports sharply accelerating contract-AI adoption among in-house legal teams.

# Inhouse Contract AI Use Accelerating — Survey *Artificial Lawyer — Artificial Lawyer (2026)* LegalOn’s new ‘*2026 State of AI for In-House Legal*‘ survey has found that contract AI adoption is accelerating very rapidly now, with more than half (52%) of inhouse legal teams already using or evaluating the technology for that use case, with active usage nearly quadrupling since 2024.

Exact returned source excerpt

This is a major change (see chart) and indicates that for many inhouse lawyers the use of AI tools is now ‘normal’.

Now, things are very different – in part no doubt because the tools are much more effective.

Corpus source-specific finding

Respondents report substantial potential and realised efficiency benefits from legal AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

Other key findings include: - 87% of respondents say AI would benefit pre-signature contract review and redlining, especially as legal teams spend an average of 3.1 hours reviewing a single contract, - 79% report reduced time spent on routine legal tasks, and 67% report faster business response and turnaround times, - 80% have also adopted, evaluated, or are learning about AI agents, - 78% are comfortable delegating first-pass contract review to an agent under attorney supervision.
