Corpus framing of this source: Legal AI as an opportunity for efficiency, human-centred practice and empirical reform

Corpus synthesis of this source: Jen Leonard and Bridget McCormack compare law with medicine to examine how technology could improve service delivery while elevating human skills. They attribute medicine’s efficiency critique to Dr. Rodman and identify it as a concern they partly contest; they also distinguish Claude’s reported ethical objection to legal experimentation from their own views. Their discussion argues that jurisdictional fragmentation, subjective decision-making and client-protection rules complicate legal technology and research, while recommending revised lawyer training, more objective measures and carefully designed randomized controlled trials. They repeatedly qualify their explanations and predictions as tentative.
