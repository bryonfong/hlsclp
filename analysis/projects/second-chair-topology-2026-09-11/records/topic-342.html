Native topic findings

  1. AI use is spreading faster than firm-wide integration, so adoption rates do not by themselves demonstrate operational transformation.
  2. Task-level benefits support augmentation claims, but they do not establish equivalent firm-wide productivity gains.
  3. Governance, training and implementation costs form a substantial gap between access to AI and dependable use.
  4. Financial scale may deepen competitive inequalities, but smaller firms’ agility offers a competing mechanism.
  5. Efficiency does not automatically become revenue, profit or client value.
  6. AI creates pressure to reprice legal work, but sources disagree about the urgency and extent of departure from hourly billing.
  7. Client pressure varies substantially, and automation could move work toward either ALSPs or technologically capable law firms.
  8. Augmentation dominates current accounts, while staffing and apprenticeship effects remain contested forecasts.
  9. Unequal access to AI may affect access to justice, but adoption and optimism are not evidence of a narrower justice gap.
