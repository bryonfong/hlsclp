Corpus framing of this source: Legal outsourcing to India: business models, professional change and responsibility for legal reform

Corpus synthesis of this source: Jayanth K. Krishnan’s 2007 article combines case studies, executive interviews and published reports to explain the development of U.S. legal outsourcing to India. It distinguishes commercial gains from benefits for ordinary Indians and proposes a modest fee on legal outsourcers to fund legal reform, grounded in ethical responsibility and investors’ economic interests.

Corpus framing of this source: Legal outsourcing creates benefits but also obligations to strengthen India’s justice system

Corpus synthesis of this source: The article examines legal outsourcing to India as a form of globalization and argues that foreign legal outsourcers benefit from Indian labour, institutions and public incentives while the wider population remains burdened by an underfunded, delay-ridden justice system. It combines market examples, court and budget data, fieldwork on reform initiatives, corporate-responsibility reasoning and business-risk cases to support a progressive legal-reform charge on outsourcing beneficiaries.
