Corpus framing of this source: How competing legal paradigms and professional communities shape investment treaty arbitration

Corpus synthesis of this source: Anthea Roberts’s 2013 article explains investment treaty arbitration as a hybrid system whose legal development reflects competing analogies, professional backgrounds and stakeholder interests. She traces the commercial arbitration paradigm’s early influence, the rise of public-oriented critiques and reforms, and possible institutional responses. Rather than endorse one comprehensive model, she recommends using multiple perspectives to expose blind spots and design hybrid solutions, while qualifying causal claims about arbitrators’ backgrounds and predictions about the system’s evolution.

Choosing a paradigm redistributes authority and changes practical legal answers.

Exact returned source excerpt

The public international law paradigm, for instance, focuses attention on the system’s treaty basis, thereby putting the treaty parties in a position of relative superiority to both investors (who are not treaty parties) and investment tribunals (which are presented as agents of the treaty parties).

The commercial arbitration par- adigm, by contrast, focuses attention on the disputing parties and emphasizes that the investor and host state are disputants subject to “equality of arms,” which tends to downgrade the rel- ative significance of states and elevate that of investors.

Corpus framing of this source: Clash of Paradigms: Actors and Analogies Shaping the Investment Treaty System

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source explains investment treaty arbitration as a hybrid, decentralized system whose unresolved rules invite competing analogies to commercial arbitration, public international law, domestic public law, trade law and human rights law. It argues that these paradigms shape legal outcomes, institutional power and perceptions of the system; traces a shift from investor-protective approaches toward more public-oriented ones; and recommends a plural, “between the poles” theory rather than reducing the system to any single paradigm.

Corpus source-specific finding

Competing analogies shape doctrine because the system is young, hybrid, vague and decentralized

Exact returned source excerpt

Choice of analogies represents an acute, though by no means the only, manifestation of the field’s underlying clash of paradigms.

Exact returned source excerpt

Different analogies often point to diverse solutions, but no metatheory exists for resolving when to rely on any particular analogy.

Corpus source-specific finding

Five paradigms reveal different parts of an irreducibly hybrid institutional structure

Exact returned source excerpt

CLASH OF PARADIGMS: WHAT IS REVEALED AND OBSCURED Different analogies often point to diverse solutions as a result of distinctions in the struc- tures, assumptions, and normative commitments of their underlying paradigms.62 This sec- tion identifies five paradigms—public international law, private international law, domestic public law (administrative or constitutional law), international trade law, and international human rights law (the last two being species of international public law)—and considers those paradigms in light of what we know about investment treaties, which is that: they are agree- ments entered into by states acting in their public capacity; they contain substantive obligations about the treatment of foreign investors, without generally specifying whether these obliga- tions create substantive rights or mere benefits for investors; and they create a procedural mech- anism for foreign investors to bring arbitral claims against host states.

Exact returned source excerpt

Investment tribunals differ from this commercial model because they are authorized by the treaty parties but constituted by the disputing parties.
