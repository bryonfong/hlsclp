TAR governance: Reduced review retains legal judgment

Corpus synthesis: Uses e-discovery to show defensible human–computer processes can save effort while oversight, relevance and stopping rules remain case-specific.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Native topic findings

  1. E-discovery provides an early test of whether automation can reduce legal work without sacrificing defensible results.
  2. The strongest comparative evidence supports particular human–computer processes, especially on precision and review effort, rather than universal algorithmic superiority.
  3. Judicial acceptance makes TAR available, but does not pre-approve every implementation or stopping decision.
  4. The principal governance disagreement concerns how much oversight preserves reliable disclosure without consuming TAR's savings.
  5. Automation reduces exhaustive review while retaining legal judgment, technical competence and responsibility for the process.
  6. GenAI's document-review results are promising but narrower than predictions that it will repeat TAR's adoption trajectory.
  7. The practical lesson advanced is to validate the complete review process, not rely on a technology label or transfer discovery results to all legal work.
