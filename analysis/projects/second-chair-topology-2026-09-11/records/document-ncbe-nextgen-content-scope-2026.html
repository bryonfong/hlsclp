Corpus framing of this source: NextGen UBE Content Scope for July 2026–February 2027

Corpus synthesis of this source: The National Conference of Bar Examiners specifies how the NextGen UBE will combine foundational-skills tasks, recalled doctrine and supplied legal resources across several doctrinal fields. The excerpts define examinable scope rather than present empirical findings or outside views; they make no broader prediction beyond what examinees may encounter during the stated administration period.

Corpus source-specific finding

The outline establishes a mixed-resource, cross-subject assessment model.

Exact returned source excerpt

Questions may test topics from more than one subject area.

Exact returned source excerpt

Examinees may expect that some questions will be presented with legal resources.

Corpus source-specific finding

The contracts scope follows the transaction from validity and interpretation through performance, breach, remedies and third-party rights.

Exact returned source excerpt

Mistake and misunderstanding This topic includes mutual mistake and unilateral mistake, including when a party bears the risk of the mistake.

Exact returned source excerpt

Fraud, misrepresentation, and nondisclosure This topic includes fraudulent misrepresentation, material misrepresentation, concealment, and nondisclosure, as well as the different remedies available for each.

Corpus source-specific finding

The evidence scope emphasizes admissibility analysis, proof mechanics and policy exclusions.

Exact returned source excerpt

Relevance This topic includes understanding that evidence remains relevant and may be admitted even if an opposing party offers to stipulate to it.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, a court may consider an offer to stipulate when judging the evidence’s probative value.

Corpus source-specific finding

The civil-procedure scope focuses on forum authority, litigation mechanics and case disposition.

Exact returned source excerpt

Specific jurisdiction includes application in a variety of contexts, such as intentional torts, contracts, “stream of commerce,” and e-commerce claims.

Exact returned source excerpt

General jurisdiction includes application to both individuals and corporations.
