Corpus framing of this source: Updating legal ethics for digital competence and confidentiality

Corpus synthesis of this source: The ABA Commission on Ethics 20/20 proposes amendments and practical guidance to adapt existing professional duties to digital practice. It links technology’s potential service and affordability benefits to reasonable confidentiality safeguards, clarifies electronic-information duties, and leaves contested metadata-access questions unresolved.

Corpus framing of this source: Technology competence and medium-neutral client communication

Corpus synthesis of this source: The ABA Commission on Ethics 20/20 proposes clarifying the Model Rules’ comments to make technology awareness explicit within existing competence duties and to extend responsiveness beyond telephone calls. Its reasoning rests on ethical interpretation and examples of ordinary digital practice, rather than empirical findings.

Corpus framing of this source: ABA Ethics 20/20: Updating Professional Duties for Technology and Confidentiality

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Commission argues that technology improves legal services but creates evolving confidentiality and communication risks that the Model Rules should address more explicitly. Resolution 105A combines targeted amendments to Rules 1.0, 1.1, 1.4, 1.6 and 4.4 with a recommended, continuously updated ABA resource. It frames most amendments as clarifications of existing duties rather than new obligations and supports the proposals with practical examples, earlier professional rules, and an extensive consultation process.

Corpus source-specific finding

Make technology-related confidentiality safeguards an express ethical duty

Exact returned source excerpt

Resolution 105a: Technology and Confidentiality  The Commission is proposing to amend Rule 1.6 of the Model Rules of Professional Conduct (Confidentiality of Information) to make clear that a lawyer has an ethical duty to take reasonable measures to protect a client’s confidential information from inadvertent disclosure, unauthorized disclosure, and unauthorized access, regardless of the medium used.

Exact returned source excerpt

This duty is already described in several existing Comments, but the Commission concluded that, in light of the pervasive use of technology to store and transmit confidential client information, this existing obligation should be stated explicitly in the black letter of Model Rule 1.6.
