Corpus framing of this source: AI both displaces and redirects high-skilled financial analysts

Corpus synthesis of this source: Grennan and Michaely use task-level data on sell-side security analysts to argue that AI acts simultaneously as a substitute for prediction work and a complement to social and complex analytical tasks. Greater AI exposure is associated with occupational exits and lower research novelty and compensation, but also with task reallocation, greater effort and improved forecast quality. The authors qualify the analysis as micro-level and sector-specific, disclose a TipRanks financial interest, and recommend investment in social skills to shape AI’s productivity effects.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors frame AI’s effects through a task-based substitution-versus-complementarity model.

Exact returned source excerpt

From a theoretical perspective, we rely on the canonical task-based framework to hy- pothesize about how AI affects analysts (Autor, Levy, and Murnane, 2003; Acemoglu and Autor, 2011; Brynjolfsson, Mitchell, and Rock, 2018).

Exact returned source excerpt

If analysts only task is prediction, the framework suggests that AI directly substitutes for analysts resulting in job loss.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study builds task-specific microdata and uses an instrumental-variables approach to connect AI exposure with analysts’ work and labour outcomes.

Exact returned source excerpt

We construct a dataset at the stock-analyst-quarter level to explore the implications of AI for analysts’ jobs, including their employment status, stocks that they cover, time spent on tasks requiring different skills, the quality of their work output, and indirectly their compensation.

Exact returned source excerpt

soft skills.1 The first proxy applies natural language processing (NLP) techniques to earnings call transcripts to quantify the complexity and content of analysts’ questions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Greater AI exposure produces evidence of direct displacement and defensive shifts in stock coverage.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exploiting variation in AI’s power across stocks, we show analysts with portfolios that are more exposed to AI are more likely to reallocate efforts to soft skills, shift coverage towards low AI stocks, and even leave the profession.

Exact returned source excerpt

Analysts also respond by reallocating effort toward social, soft-information and more complex tasks that AI finds harder to reproduce.

Exact returned source excerpt

Finally, we find increased exposure to AI is associated with improved product quality: more accurate, less biased forecasts and improved consensus forecasts.

Exact returned source excerpt

In addition, we find evidence consistent with analysts exerting greater effort, perhaps from reallocating efforts toward complementary tasks that rely on social skills.

The authors predict that high-skilled work will combine some displacement with a broader shift toward social skills and complex thought.

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Artificial Intelligence and High-Skilled Work: Evidence from Analysts

Corpus synthesis of this source: Grennan and Michaely use task-specific data on sell-side security analysts and variation in AI intensity across covered stocks to examine substitution, task complementarity, work quality and compensation. They argue that AI both displaces analysts and redirects those who remain toward interpersonal, qualitative and complex work, improving forecasts while weakening the market value of analyst recommendations.

Corpus source-specific finding

Research design and analytical framework

Exact returned source excerpt

We investigate this concern using novel, task-specific data for security an- alysts.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exploiting variation in AI’s power across stocks, we show analysts with portfolios that are more exposed to AI are more likely to reallocate efforts to soft skills, shift coverage towards low AI stocks, and even leave the profession.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI exposure is associated with occupational displacement, especially among highly accurate analysts

Exact returned source excerpt

The analysts are quitting and leaving the profession at higher rates once the stocks that they cover become high AI intensity stocks.

Exact returned source excerpt

Analyst departures disproportionately occur among highly accurate analysts, leaving for non-research jobs.

Corpus source-specific finding

Analysts who remain shift coverage away from stocks where AI is stronger

Exact returned source excerpt

Columns 1 and 2 show that when AI intensity increases for a stock, the analyst is less likely to initiate coverage of that stock and Columns 3 and 4 show that analysts are more likely to end coverage of the stock when AI intensity increases.

Exact returned source excerpt

We apply a comprehensive fixed effect structure in both panels, including fixed effects for analyst, the analyst’s main industry of coverage, and year.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI redirects work toward complex questions, soft information and client interaction

Exact returned source excerpt

Specifically, we find that analysts ask more questions and the complexity of their questions increases when AI intensity is higher.

Exact returned source excerpt

Consistent with the complementary tasks hypothesis, we see analysts reduce their questions about easy-to-measure topics like sales and profits, but they increase their questions about harder-to-measure topics like brand and engagement.

Corpus source-specific finding

Greater AI exposure coincides with better forecasts and evidence of higher effort

Exact returned source excerpt

Using variation within analyst-stock pairs and our IV strategy, we find statistically significant evidence to suggest that when AI intensity increases, analysts’ accuracy increases and their bias decreases.

Exact returned source excerpt

This estimate comes from a very tight regression specification that includes employer, main industry of coverage, and year fixed effects.

Corpus source-specific finding

Improved output does not prevent declining research novelty and inferred compensation

Exact returned source excerpt

However, increased ex- posure to AI reduces the novelty in analysts’ research which reduces compensation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Finally, given that the market reaction to an analyst’s recommendation is an indication of its value, we examine the market’s response to changes in analysts’ recommendations to indirectly infer how their compensation may be changing in response to AI.

Corpus source-specific finding

Overall conclusion, safeguard and prediction

Exact returned source excerpt

The key takeaways of our paper is that the relation between AI and the future of high- skilled work is likely to involve some direct substitution but also greater incentives for ana- lysts to use their soft skills – ones that cannot be automated – to engage with management and clients and to develop more complex investment ideas.

Exact returned source excerpt

As more resources are delegated to AI systems, a potentially important safeguard is ensuring that any human predictions made alongside the AI recommendations remain of high quality.
