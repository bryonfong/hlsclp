Native topic findings

  1. LSC frames technology as infrastructure for expanding civil legal aid and self-help, not as a standalone solution to the justice gap.
  2. The agenda shifts attention from purchasing tools to building organizations’ capacity to implement and share them.
  3. Grant commitments demonstrate sustained investment, while participants question whether funding arrangements support timely, scalable experimentation.
  4. Project examples show localized efficiency and engagement gains, not an established aggregate reduction in unmet legal need.
  5. LSC promotes AI as one component of a broader delivery strategy, with practical usefulness and client protections as conditions for adoption.
  6. The practical accountability challenge is to move from grant compliance and activity measures to funded evaluation of client outcomes.

Access to justice & civil society · Adoption at scale (2025)

Corpus framing of this source: LSC’s seven-part agenda for technology-enabled civil legal assistance

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Next Frontier: Harnessing Technology to Close the Justice Gap (LSC Technology Summit Report 2024-2025) presents LSC’s stakeholder-informed agenda for expanding legal aid capacity and self-help. It combines unmet-need statistics, project examples and practitioner observations with recommendations on organizational capacity, shared resources, funding, data, evaluation, responsible AI and accessible tools. LSC treats technology as a necessary supporting resource, not a sufficient solution to under-resourced civil legal services.

Corpus source-specific finding

Technology should expand both professional assistance and self-help, but cannot close the justice gap alone.

Exact returned source excerpt

AT ISSUE LSC’s 2022 Justice Gap report found that LSC grantees had to turn away one of every two (49 percent) of the requests for legal help they received in the prior year.

Exact returned source excerpt

The survey also affirmed that many low-income Americans don’t seek legal help in the first place because of concerns about the cost or because they don’t feel the civil legal system works for them.

Corpus source-specific finding

The recommendations build on an established funding network and a consultative summit process.

Exact returned source excerpt

As noted in the Introduction, the summit process included: a full- day workshop ahead of LSC’s Innovations in Technology conference in Charlotte, North Carolina, in February 2024; virtual workgroup conversations over a number of months; extensive stakeholder interviews and focus groups with field experts and LSC grantees; and more.

Exact returned source excerpt

With extensive input and support from civil legal service practitioners, technology experts and other stakeholders, the 2024-2025 LSC Technology Summit set out to identify how LSC can best support its grantee network — comprising 130 independent nonprofit civil legal services organizations across the country — to adopt and leverage the latest technologies, including artificial intelligence (AI).
