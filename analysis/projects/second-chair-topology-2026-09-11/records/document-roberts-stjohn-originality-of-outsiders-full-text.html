Corpus synthesis of this source: The Originality of Outsiders: Innovation in the Investment Treaty System draws on interdisciplinary literature to explain why outsiders may develop disruptive innovations more readily than insiders. Corpus framing of this source: How officials’ insider–outsider status shapes investment treaty reform

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Originality of Outsiders: Innovation in the Investment Treaty System links officials’ professional backgrounds to the reforms they propose. Drawing on four case studies and interdisciplinary innovation literature, the authors identify cognitive freedom, cross-field experience and weaker vested interests as reasons outsiders may favour disruptive change, while acknowledging barriers to recognition and unresolved questions about individual influence.

Corpus source-specific finding

Four case studies associate arbitration insiders with sustaining reform and outsiders with proposals to displace ISDS.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the above four case studies, we see insider officials with deep roots in the arbitra- tion community propose sustaining innovations on behalf of the USA and Bahrain, while outsider officials with no roots in the arbitration community propose disruptive innovations that would displace ISDS on behalf of the EU and Brazil.

Exact returned source excerpt

This pattern ech- oes observations about innovation in other fields: insiders are more likely to propose innovations with the intention of sustaining and updating existing practices, whereas outsiders are more likely to develop innovations that may disrupt or displace existing practices.

Corpus source-specific finding

Sustaining and disruptive innovation describe a spectrum, not a binary.

Exact returned source excerpt

In business, a distinction is often drawn between sustaining innovations that improve upon a product but do not threaten its market (compare a typewriter with an electronic type- writer) and disruptive innovations that threaten to replace the product all together (compare a typewriter with a personal computer).17 Such sustaining and disruptive innovations are not binary.

Exact returned source excerpt

Instead, they represent ideal types at either end of a spec- trum, so innovations can be more or less sustaining or disruptive.

Corpus source-specific finding

Cross-field intermediaries can translate and recombine ideas.

Exact returned source excerpt

Sociologists Michel Crozier and Erhard Friedberg use the term ‘marginal secant’ (which can be translated as influential outsider or smuggler) to describe an individual who is marginal to any particular world but skilled at mediating across or between worlds.28 Individuals who cross between fields are likely to be able to trans- late insights from one field into another or to be more flexible in their thinking and better able to combine insights in novel ways.

Exact returned source excerpt

Knowledge of multiple fields can make it easier for individuals to see from multiple perspectives or to imagine doing things in a different way.

Corpus source-specific finding

Vested interests and established networks can steer insiders toward preserving existing arrangements.

Exact returned source excerpt

In transnational legal settings, intermediaries play important roles as go-betweens translating global scripts for local realities or translating local grievances into global frameworks.29 Florian Grisel argues that the grand old men of commercial arbitration were themselves secant marginals, individuals who were marginal to their national legal systems (often because they were immigrants) but skilled at mediat- ing across worlds (in particular, across civil law and common law) and, in doing so, crafted arbitration as a hybrid of various legal traditions.30 A third trait of outsiders is that they are unlikely to have vested financial and repu- tational interests that would lead them to favour sustaining innovations over disrup- tive ones.

Exact returned source excerpt

Insiders often have professional interests in maintaining and improving the status quo, which favours them privileging sustaining innovations while attempting to quash disruptive innovations before those can gain traction.

Corpus framing of this source: Officials’ backgrounds shape innovation in investment treaty reform

Corpus synthesis of this source: In The Originality of Outsiders: Innovation in the Investment Treaty System, Anthea Roberts and Taylor St John draw on five years of observation and interviews to explain why officials outside investment arbitration tend to propose disruptive ISDS reforms, while insiders tend to improve the existing system. They identify institutional allocation of policy responsibility as an important influence, without treating officials’ backgrounds as a complete explanation or equating originality with better reforms or successful adoption.

Corpus source-specific finding

Outsider officials tend to propose disruptive reforms; insiders tend to propose sustaining reforms.

Exact returned source excerpt

Based on five years of para-ethnographic observation and interviews with officials involved in ISDS re- form, and informed by the interdisciplinary innovation literature, we explore how individuals who have not spent their careers within the field of investment arbitration (and are perceived as ‘outsiders’ by those within that field) have developed more disruptive reform proposals while arbitral insiders have typically proposed sustaining reforms.

Exact returned source excerpt

We illuminate these dy- namics in the ISDS reform debates with case studies of four actors: the USA, the European Union, Bahrain and Brazil.

Corpus framing of this source: Officials’ insider–outsider status and innovation in ISDS reform

Corpus synthesis of this source: The article uses sustained participant observation, interviews, UNCITRAL interventions and state submissions to argue that officials’ professional backgrounds help shape whether states pursue sustaining or disruptive reforms of investor–state dispute settlement. Case studies of the EU, USA, Brazil and Bahrain illustrate the proposed pattern, while the authors qualify it by emphasizing state policy, institutional reallocations, negotiating power, continuity and networks.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors argue that proximity to ISDS practice influences reform preferences: insiders tend to preserve and update the system, whereas outsiders tend to propose more disruptive alternatives.

Exact returned source excerpt
