Native topic findings

  1. An hourglass labour market can sustain employment growth while weakening job quality and routes into better-paid work.
  2. The report links occupational hollowing-out to blocked progression, but offers qualified explanations rather than a demonstrated single cause of weak productivity.
  3. Employer evidence points to skills mismatch, not simply a shortage of jobs or older workers obstructing younger entrants.
  4. Employment-preserving flexibility carried uneven costs, especially for workers needing secure, part-time work.
  5. The policy disagreement concerns what counts as success and how far public intervention should extend.

The Hourglass Economy: An analysis of London's labour market

Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Automation debate (2015-2022)

Corpus framing of this source: London’s recovery concealed weaker job quality, stalled progression and widening low pay

Corpus synthesis of this source: The London Assembly Economy Committee’s February 2016 report examines London’s labour market mainly over 2008–2015. It combines commissioned GLA Economics analysis, official statistics, employer surveys and witness evidence to argue that employment growth alone cannot establish labour-market success. Its hourglass account concerns the erosion of mid-skilled occupations across London’s economy. It recommends mayoral action on skills, employment conditions, flexible hiring, living wages and devolution.
