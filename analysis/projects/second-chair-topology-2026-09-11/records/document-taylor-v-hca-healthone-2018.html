Corpus framing of this source: Taylor v. HCA-HealthONE: software failures, excusable neglect and relief from dismissal

Corpus synthesis of this source: Taylor v. HCA-HealthONE LLC, 2018 COA 29, 417 P.3d 943 addresses relief from a non-merits dismissal after counsel attributed missed service deadlines to docketing-software failures. The majority requires reconsideration under the full Craig test, without deciding whether the malfunction excuses counsel. Judge Berger separately argues for immediate reinstatement and doctrinal reform focused on client fault and prejudice.

The majority requires the full three-factor Craig inquiry rather than treating attorney carelessness as dispositive.

Corpus framing of this source: Taylor v. HCA-HealthONE LLC: service notice, excusable neglect and computer failure

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Colorado Court of Appeals held that Rule 4(m) requires notice before a court dismisses an action for untimely service, but not notice issued only after the service deadline. Although the dismissal itself was valid, the majority vacated the denial of Rule 60(b) relief because the district court failed to apply the controlling three-factor Craig test and remanded for a fact-intensive assessment. Judge Berger agreed reversal was required but argued that the existing record compelled reinstatement, treated the unexplained software failure as excusable neglect, and urged reconsideration of confusing doctrine.

Corpus source-specific finding

The majority interpreted Rule 4(m) to require notice before dismissal, not necessarily after the service deadline expires.

Exact returned source excerpt

¶ 25 We therefore conclude that the rule requires notice before dismissal, but does not require notice after expiration of the service deadline.

Exact returned source excerpt

But a notice of noncompliance and possible dismissal at some time in the future would then create, in every case, a new deadline for effectuating service—that is, a “specified time” within which “service [must] be made.” Thus, Taylor's reading effectively renders the dismissal option superfluous, a result we avoid in construing the rule.

Corpus source-specific finding

The majority rejected the defendants’ argument that Malm created a heightened or bright-line excusable-neglect rule for service beyond sixty-three days.

Exact returned source excerpt

¶ 44 We are not persuaded that Malm announced any bright-line rule, much less a rule that, where the statute of limitations has run, a complaint is subject to dismissal if not served within sixty-three days, except upon a showing of specific obstacles to service.

Exact returned source excerpt

In our view, Malm stands for the more uncontroversial proposition that an extraordinary delay in effecting service—a delay measured in years rather than days—can be justified only by extraordinary circumstances.
