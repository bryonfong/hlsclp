Outsourcing fees: Delegation cannot justify double recovery

Corpus synthesis: Preserves professional duties across providers while distinguishing service charges, pass-through costs, consent and continuing oversight.

Native topic findings

  1. Outsourcing changes who performs legal work, not the responsible lawyer’s professional obligations.
  2. Effective delegation requires provider diligence and continuing oversight, not reliance on credentials alone.
  3. Confidentiality and conflicts require safeguards across the provider chain; client consent does not cure every risk.
  4. Disclosure and consent standards vary with the provider’s role, the lawyer’s control and client expectations.
  5. Billing guidance distinguishes reasonable service fees from pass-through costs and rejects double recovery.
  6. Supervision is not blanket permission to delegate every legal function, including to computers.
  7. Potential savings and capacity gains remain contested once supervision, quality and employment effects enter the analysis.
  8. A broader ethical critique asks who benefits beyond the immediate client and provider.
