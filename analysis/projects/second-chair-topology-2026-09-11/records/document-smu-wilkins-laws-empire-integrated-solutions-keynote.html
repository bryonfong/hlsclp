Corpus framing of this source: David B. Wilkins on integrated legal solutions amid global disruption

Corpus synthesis of this source: The conference account presents Wilkins’s argument that globalization, overlapping crises and digital transformation are reshaping clients’ problems and legal markets. He calls on lawyers to move beyond technical legal analysis toward multidisciplinary problem-solving while preserving the profession’s human-capital foundations. It separately reports Calvin Phua’s opening assessment of Singapore’s legal-sector resilience and future.

Corpus source-specific finding

Wilkins argues that global forces are transforming legal services while local context remains consequential.

Exact returned source excerpt

Professor Wilkins started by highlighting the “interesting times” which we live in today, which have transformed the legal services market, and, more importantly, the world which our clients inhabit.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, domestic legal markets are increasingly becoming shaped by global markets and foreign investment, though local institutions and cultures remain critical.

Corpus source-specific finding

Wilkins identifies three overlapping crises as accelerants of change and argues that they have simultaneously elevated and challenged law.

Exact returned source excerpt

These changes have also, in recent years, become “turbocharged” by three related crises: (**1**) the recent global health crisis, (**2**) the volatile global economic system (exacerbated by the recent Russian-Ukraine conflict) and (**3**) the increasing global focus on sustainability, stakeholders and social justice.

Exact returned source excerpt

Professor Wilkins then observed that these have made the law more important than ever.

Corpus source-specific finding

Wilkins recommends that lawyers redefine themselves as collaborative problem-solvers rather than solely legal technicians.

Exact returned source excerpt

Instead of being mere legal technicians, lawyers must now see themselves as problem solvers.

Exact returned source excerpt

This is because the law, while necessary, is not the only thing which is needed to solve the complex problems of today; other stakeholders would inevitably be involved.

Corpus source-specific finding

Wilkins predicts that legal services will remain dependent on human capital rather than being replaced by technology in the foreseeable future.

Exact returned source excerpt

Nonetheless, Professor Wilkins continued, many fundamental aspects pertaining to legal work will not, and should not change.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, the legal business will, at least for the foreseeable future, continue to remain one which depends on human capital, rather than one which is replaced by technology.

Corpus source-specific finding

Wilkins urges lawyers to harness technology despite its disruptive effects because clients are already digitally transformed.

Exact returned source excerpt

To end off, Professor Wilkins stressed that to address the “disruptions” in our world today, we must harness technology (even though technology is itself also becoming increasingly disruptive).

Exact returned source excerpt

After all, although technology is new for lawyers, our clients have been living with digital transformation for years.

Corpus source-specific finding

The account separately reports Phua’s view that Singapore’s resilience rests on the rule of law, reliable institutions and strong cross-institutional relations, while warning against complacency.

Exact returned source excerpt

In his speech, Mr Phua set the context of the conference by providing some insights on how Singapore can continue to thrive in today’s “broken”, “balanced” and “borderless” world, highlighting three “R”s which has served the nation well, namely, (**1**) our strong *rule of law,* (**2**) our *reliable* government, economy, society, transportation system, etc., as well as (**3**) the strong *relations* between our government, our judiciary and our industries.

Exact returned source excerpt

He cautioned, however, that we cannot rest on our laurels, and that we must continue to be careful of and actively address the “headwinds” ahead of us, before concluding by expressing his optimism about the future of Singapore’s legal industry and legal profession.
