Corpus framing of this source: Law firms weigh buying, building and flexible AI architectures

Corpus synthesis of this source: The article reports contrasting law-firm AI investment strategies through named executives and one anonymous innovation officer. It frames build versus buy as a business and technology decision, highlights provisional and hybrid approaches, and anticipates consequences for vendors dependent on licensing revenue.

Corpus source-specific finding

Scaling generative AI has made platform strategy a business decision as well as a technology choice.

Exact returned source excerpt

The race to scale as the legal industry adopts generative AI technology highlights the build or buy debate ongoing at DLA Piper and other firms that’s unfolded since ChatGPT transformed the marketplace in 2022.

Exact returned source excerpt

“We spend a lot of time thinking about to what extent are we going to build our own independent AI platform” versus a more flexible “open architecture” strategy, said Jane Rogers, a member of Ropes & Gray’s management committee.

Ropes & Gray currently prefers buying because technology changes too quickly to justify proprietary development.

Exact returned source excerpt

Ropes & Gray, for its part, has opted to buy.

Exact returned source excerpt

“The technology is changing so quickly now that it doesn’t appear to us at this point that doing something proprietary makes sense,” said Ropes & Gray Chief Information Officer Marsha Stein.

Corpus source-specific finding

Another large firm combines internal workflows with interchangeable models and treats subscription platforms as transitional.

Exact returned source excerpt

A chief innovation officer at another large law firm said that while the firm hadn’t built its own platform from scratch, lawyers have access to an internally built AI platform that integrates proprietary workflows and allows users to swap out large language models.

Exact returned source excerpt

For this firm, the subscription legal AI platforms like Harvey and Legora are “a bridge,” said the executive, who was granted anonymity to speak freely about law firm spending.

Hybrid strategies challenge an all-or-nothing build-versus-buy framing, with implications for licensing-dependent vendors.

Exact returned source excerpt

Harvey co-founder Gabriel Pereyra said he sees a false choice that wrongly assumes “either firms are going to build everything themselves, or a company like us or a model provider is going to build everything themselves.” For now, firms said they are evaluating their investment decisions through a similar lens, prioritizing flexibility to swap out their AI tools for the latest technology.

That compares with boutique firms spending a bigger percentage of their budgets on new tech, relying largely on outside expertise.
