Open topic: Cross-border outsourcing: Oversight follows the provider chain

Corpus framing of this source: Offshore legal outsourcing: practical safeguards without transferring professional responsibility

Corpus synthesis of this source: The New York City Bar’s Committee on Professional Responsibility uses its August 2009 report to translate ethics rules and advisory opinions into practical guidance for offshore substantive legal work. It describes cost and capacity benefits but makes ethical permissibility conditional on competence, supervision, confidentiality, conflict checks, appropriate disclosure and consent, and avoiding unauthorized practice. Its contribution is normative and scenario-based, not an empirical assessment or forecast.

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Ethical framework for outsourcing legal services overseas

Corpus synthesis of this source: The report accepts that offshore legal outsourcing can be ethically permissible but argues that outsourcing does not reduce the New York lawyer’s professional responsibility. Its substantive contribution is a practical, scenario-based framework for managing competence and supervision, confidentiality, conflicts, client disclosure and consent, billing, and unauthorized practice risks. It relies principally on professional-conduct rules, bar opinions, legal authorities and operational risk analysis rather than empirical outcome data.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report treats offshore outsourcing as conditionally permissible and fills a practical gap left by existing ethics opinions.

Exact returned source excerpt

In 2006, the Committee on Professional and Judicial Ethics of the New York City Bar issued an advisory opinion concluding that a New York lawyer may outsource legal support services offshore ethically provided that the lawyer (i) rigorously supervises the non-lawyer to avoid aiding in the unauthorized practice of law and to ensure competent representation of the client; (ii) takes steps to ensure the preservation of client confidential information; (iii) attends to conflict checking to avoid conflicts of interest; (iv) bills appropriately for the services provided; and (v) obtains advance client consent when appropriate.

Exact returned source excerpt

3 Recently, the American Bar Association issued an opinion similarly approving outsourcing of legal services, noting that “[t]here is nothing unethical about a lawyer outsourcing legal and nonlegal services, provided the outsourcing lawyer renders legal services to the client with the ‘legal knowledge, skill, thoroughness and preparation reasonably necessary for the representation’ as required by Rule 1.1 [of the ABA Model Rules of Conduct].” 4 Ethics
