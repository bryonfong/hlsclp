Open topic: Legal workflows: Immediate gains create competence risks

Corpus framing of this source: Patent-based measurement of occupational exposure to AI

Corpus synthesis of this source: Michael Webb develops and historically tests a general-purpose method that matches patent language to occupational tasks. He finds that AI exposure differs sharply from earlier automation: it concentrates in high-skill, highly educated and older-worker occupations. Conditional projections suggest lower 90:10 wage inequality but potentially greater inequality at the top. Webb treats these projections as speculative because they depend on historical substitution patterns, patent coverage and other strong assumptions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Webb introduces a replicable, general-purpose exposure measure based on patent–task overlap.

Exact returned source excerpt

The text of patents contains information about what technologies do, and the text of job descriptions contains informa- tion about the tasks people do in their jobs.

Exact returned source excerpt

These two text corpuses can be combined to quantify how much patenting in a particular technology has been directed at the tasks of any particular occupation.

SupportsWork redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: The Impact of Artificial Intelligence on the Labor Market

Corpus synthesis of this source: The paper develops an automated, patent-based measure of occupational exposure to AI, software and robots, validates it against historical measures and case studies, and uses it to describe which tasks, occupations and demographic groups AI is most likely to affect. It predicts that AI exposure differs markedly from earlier automation while stressing that exposure is not an observed worker outcome and that complementarity, new tasks, labour supply and mobility create substantial uncertainty.

Corpus source-specific finding

The paper contributes an automated, updateable measure of technological exposure based on patenting activity.
