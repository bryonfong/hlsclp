Open topic: Justice chatbots: Actionable help exceeds correct information

Corpus framing of this source: Audit of a Nevada self-help chatbot: useful answers, recurring procedural gaps and context errors

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Audit Report for Self-Help Chatbot evaluates user sessions with subject-specific rubrics and practical-impact labels. It documents useful assistance alongside missing next steps, inappropriate procedural recommendations, failures to retain context and uneven use of website materials. The report distinguishes observed answer defects from tentative explanations and qualifies what session data can establish about actual users.

Corpus source-specific finding

The audit evaluates practical usefulness as well as correctness, while acknowledging limits in its usage data.

Exact returned source excerpt

We developed subject-matter specific rubrics to orient ourselves to the material and to evaluate broad questions (e.g., "What are my options").

Exact returned source excerpt

We also developed fallback rubrics for one-off or specific questions, to evaluate whether an answer was clear, correct, complete, and actionable.

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Audit Report for Nevada Self-Help Chatbot

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Duke Center on Law & Technology audits Nevada’s court-operated, family-law RAG chatbot using all logged 2025 sessions available to the researchers, topic-specific rubrics, impact labels and product testing. The report finds that the chatbot often supplies useful basic information but performs unevenly on actionability, forms, ambiguity, conversational context, urgent safety situations, language access, mobile usability, citations and knowledge-base maintenance. It recommends tighter website integration, more specific links, improved search and technical fixes, while cautioning that the findings assess one evolving product rather than LLMs generally. A Nevada AOC foreword separately endorses transparent, continuing evaluation and human oversight.

Corpus source-specific finding

The audit evaluates a specific court RAG product intended to help self-represented litigants find family-law information.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Self-Help Chatbot was created to help self-represented litigants locate publicly available court information related to family law matters.

Exact returned source excerpt

Architecturally, the chatbot is a retrieval augmented generation (RAG) system.
