Native topic findings

  1. The central competitive question is who can turn AI into differentiated legal delivery, not who can purchase the strongest model.
  2. Build versus buy is usually a portfolio decision, with trade-offs in control, cost, expertise and platform dependence.
  3. Kirkland’s reported investment illustrates why strategic classifications must remain time-bound.
  4. Scale may reinforce incumbent advantages, but agility and clearer incentives could favour smaller firms and in-house teams.
  5. AI blurs market boundaries as firms, ALSPs, platforms and corporate departments build overlapping capabilities.
  6. Reported deployments support practical capability building, but provide limited evidence of durable competitive superiority.
  7. The economic dispute concerns whether AI expands existing practices or replaces associate-heavy delivery models.
  8. Corporate buyers may drive change through both insourcing and demands for demonstrable improvements from providers.
  9. Practical strategy requires governed workflows, sustained organisational investment and measurement beyond adoption.
