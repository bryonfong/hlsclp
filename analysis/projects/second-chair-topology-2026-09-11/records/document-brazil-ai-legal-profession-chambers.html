Corpus framing of this source: Brazil’s proposed risk-based AI regulation and existing legal obligations

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Chambers Brazil guide outlines proposed AI risk assessments and oversight, recommends that businesses prepare for tighter controls, and identifies existing laws that may already affect AI development and use.

Corpus source-specific finding

The proposed framework requires suppliers to assess AI risk before market placement or use.

Exact returned source excerpt

Every AI system shall undergo a preliminary assessment conducted by the supplier to classify its degree of risk, and risk assessments must be undertaken prior to the AI system being placed on the market or used in service.

Exact returned source excerpt

- High-risk AI systems include AI systems that (i) serve as security devices in critical infrastructures (eg, traffic control, water and electricity supply networks), (ii) are used in credit assessments, (iii) are used in certain autonomous vehicles, (iv) have applications in the healthcare sector, (v) are used in biometric identification systems, and (vi) are used to aid criminal investigations and public security, among other uses.

Corpus source-specific finding

The guide advises businesses to prepare for EU-style risk-based controls.

Exact returned source excerpt

Companies developing or deploying AI in Brazil must prepare for a risk-based regulatory framework similar to the EU AI Act.

Exact returned source excerpt

High-risk and excessive-risk AI systems will face stricter scrutiny or outright prohibition, and businesses must evaluate their technologies accordingly.

Corpus source-specific finding

The guide distinguishes expected regulatory oversight from the current designation.

Exact returned source excerpt

The executive branch is expected to designate a competent authority, which will be the agency or entity of the federal public administration responsible for implementing and overseeing Brazil’s Proposed AI Regulation.

Exact returned source excerpt

For now, the Brazilian Data Protection Authority has been designated as the AI regulatory body.

Corpus source-specific finding

Existing non-AI-specific laws may also govern AI development and use.

Exact returned source excerpt

***Other laws affecting AI*** There are various laws that do not directly seek to regulate AI but may affect its development or use in Brazil, including: - Law No 13,709/2018 (the “General Data Protection Law”; *Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados Pessoais* – LGPD), which provides for the processing of personal data; - Law No 8,078/1990 (the “Consumer Protection Code”; CDC), which provides for consumer protection; - Law No 9,279/1996 (the “Industrial Property Law”), which provides for patents (inventions), trade secrets and the enforcement of IP law; and - Law No 9,610/1998 (the “Copyright Law”), which provides for authors’ rights and those related to them.

Corpus framing of this source: Brazil’s AI transition: existing duties, proposed risk regulation and innovation policy

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Chambers Brazil guide, updated in May 2025, surveys AI adoption, legal obligations, enforcement and public investment. It argues for innovation alongside fundamental-rights protection and practical organisational oversight. Its contribution combines legal interpretation, reported regulatory actions, illustrative deployments and survey data; predictions about legislation and national leadership remain prospective. The Law and Practice and Trends and Developments sections sometimes describe the status of proposed governance institutions differently.

Exact returned source excerpt

AI tools are already being used to efficiently search databases containing case law, legislation and legal commentary.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

Judicial and public-sector deployments illustrate efficiency ambitions and rights-based limits.

Exact returned source excerpt

Brazil’s judicial system, for example, employs four AI-powered tools named “Victor” (analyses and classifies the huge number of court procedures in the whole country; this system does not decide anything but indicates the nationwide significance of each case), “Rafa 2030” (this system brings Brazilian society closer to achieving the United Nations’ 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) , “Vitória” (An AI system that groups cases by analysing common patterns and similarities) and “Maria” (this systems prepares a pre-text of judgment reports but does not present any decisions).

Exact returned source excerpt

All of these tools were developed in-house by the Supreme Federal Court (STF).

Corpus source-specific finding

AI liability follows negligence and consumer-defect rules rather than a wholly separate regime.

Exact returned source excerpt

Under this framework, a party may be held liable if it fails to exercise reasonable care in the design, development, deployment or supervision of an AI system.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the context of AI, negligence may arise from a failure to implement adequate human oversight or to intervene when the system generates erroneous or harmful outputs.

Corpus source-specific finding

In mid-2024, the ANPD initiated proceedings against social media platforms regarding the processing of personal data for training their generative AI models.

Exact returned source excerpt

These actions were based on concerns that the platforms’ reliance on “legitimate interest” as a legal basis did not sufficiently safeguard user rights – particularly in relation to transparency, clear user notification and accessible opt-out mechanisms.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus framing of this source: Brazilian AI governance: data protection, professional duties and patentability

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Chambers guide explains how Brazil’s LGPD, OAB ethics guidance and patent law apply to AI. It emphasises harm mitigation where trained-model data cannot readily be deleted, purpose limitation and risk controls, human verification and confidentiality in professional services, potential liability for inadequate oversight, and technical-effect requirements for AI-related patents.

Corpus source-specific finding

LGPD rights apply to harmful AI outputs, but deletion rights face technical limits once training data becomes embedded in model parameters.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the context of generative AI, this right may be invoked when a model generates false or defamatory outputs relating to an individual.

Exact returned source excerpt

The LGPD also grants the right to data deletion, but this presents challenges in AI systems, especially those based on deep learning.

Corpus source-specific finding

Organisations must define AI data uses in advance, minimise personal-data processing and assess risks.

Exact returned source excerpt

The LGPD further requires that personal data be used only for specific, legitimate and clearly defined purposes.

Exact returned source excerpt

In AI contexts, organisations must specify in advance how data will be used – whether for training, fine-tuning or generating content.

Corpus source-specific finding

Brazilian legal ethics guidance requires lawyers to supervise generative-AI work and preserve confidentiality.

Exact returned source excerpt

Legal Tech #### **** 9.1 AI in the Legal Profession and Ethical Considerations In early 2025, the Federal Council of the Brazilian Bar Association (*Ordem dos Advogados do Brasil*; OAB) approved guidelines to steer the ethical use of generative AI in legal practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

These recommendations require lawyers to supervise AI-generated outputs, ensure client confidentiality and comply with the principles set out in the OAB Code of Ethics and Discipline.

Corpus source-specific finding

Professionals must verify AI output and implement quality controls; inadequate oversight can expose both the professional and the firm to liability.

Exact returned source excerpt

Professionals who make use of AI tools within their services are required to exercise a high standard of due diligence, particularly in verifying the accuracy and reliability of AI-generated outputs.

Exact returned source excerpt

Where erroneous or biased recommendations are produced by such systems, liability may extend to both the individual professional and their firm, especially where there has been a failure to implement appropriate oversight and quality control mechanisms.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI use in professional services remains subject to traditional confidentiality and client-data protections.

Exact returned source excerpt

Furthermore, AI systems used in the provision of professional services must comply with the same strict standards of confidentiality and client data protection that apply to traditional professional conduct.

Exact returned source excerpt

This includes ensuring that personal or sensitive information processed by AI tools is safeguarded in accordance with both the LGPD and applicable codes of ethics governing the profession.

Corpus source-specific finding

Brazil excludes software per se from patentability but may protect AI-based computer-implemented methods that solve technical problems and produce verifiable technical effects.

Exact returned source excerpt

Under current legislation, computer programmes considered “software per se” are not eligible for patent protection.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, computer-implemented methods – including those based on AI – that produce a verifiable technical effect applicable to an industrial activity may be eligible for protection.
