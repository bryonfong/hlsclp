Corpus framing of this source: Harvey LAB: task-level legal benchmarking with all-pass grading

Corpus synthesis of this source: Harvey describes LAB’s construction, detailed task rubrics and strict completion standard. It argues that incomplete issue detection can undermine high-stakes legal work, proposes using rubric criteria for model comparison and agent training, and acknowledges gaps in benchmark coverage.

Corpus source-specific finding

LAB translates real client matters into associate-level tasks, but its initial coverage remains incomplete.

Exact returned source excerpt

**“All-pass grading reflects how high-stakes legal work is reviewed in practice — there is no partial credit for catching most of the issues.” ### Practice area distribution The change-of-control task is one of 1,250 tasks in LAB, spanning 24 legal practice areas.

Exact returned source excerpt

Large firms have hundreds of distinct practices, so this initial release focuses on a representative set of transactional, advisory, regulatory, and litigation work that associates regularly encounter.

Corpus source-specific finding

The change-of-control rubric tests both issue detection and integrated legal analysis.

Exact returned source excerpt

Evaluating a task For the change-of-control task, the rubric contains 57 criteria covering nine legal issues planted across the matter.

Exact returned source excerpt

Each issue is broken into four to nine criteria, covering the underlying facts, severity rating, financial exposure, and recommended action.

Corpus source-specific finding

Harvey requires every criterion to pass before counting a task as complete.

Exact returned source excerpt

Importantly, a task is marked complete only if every criterion passes, a concept we refer to as **all-pass grading**.

Exact returned source excerpt

A deal-team report that identifies eight of ten risks is not 80% useful; it is materially incomplete.

Corpus source-specific finding

Harvey proposes reusing rubric criteria for automated judging, comparisons and training feedback.

Exact returned source excerpt

The same criteria can be applied by an LLM judge, used to compare models and harness changes, and fed back into agent training loops as per-criterion reward signals.

Corpus framing of this source: Harvey’s LAB: benchmarking long-horizon legal agents through reviewable work product

Corpus synthesis of this source: Harvey’s May 2026 announcement presents LAB as an open-source benchmark intended to guide legal-agent deployment and accelerate development. Its substantive contribution lies in matter-based tasks, expert rubrics and all-pass completion grading—not demonstrated model performance: the supplied material postpones initial results and a leaderboard.

Corpus source-specific finding

Harvey argues that legal agents need benchmarks beyond short-horizon legal reasoning.

Exact returned source excerpt

To date, there has not been a benchmark that illustrates agent progress for long-horizon legal work.

Exact returned source excerpt

Existing evaluations, including [LegalBench](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/legalbench/), [CUAD](https://www.atticusprojectai.org/cuad/), [LEXam](https://lexam-benchmark.github.io/), and our own earlier work on BigLaw Bench, have graded short-horizon reasoning: read a contract, answer a question, compare cases, or analyze an argument.

Corpus source-specific finding

LAB tests open-ended assignments within a closed client-matter environment.

Exact returned source excerpt

It is built to mirror how legal work is actually delivered at a law firm, and we’ve mapped each step of the agent execution and evaluation process to its legal counterpart: - Instructions: **The agent’s instructions are written as a request for work from a partner to an associate.

Exact returned source excerpt

Instructions are short, averaging just fifty words.

Corpus source-specific finding

The initial dataset offers substantial but explicitly incomplete practice coverage.

Exact returned source excerpt

**“All-pass grading reflects how high-stakes legal work is reviewed in practice — there is no partial credit for catching most of the issues.” ### Practice area distribution The change-of-control task is one of 1,250 tasks in LAB, spanning 24 legal practice areas.

Exact returned source excerpt

The first version of LAB includes more than 1,200 agent tasks across 24 legal practice areas, and is evaluated by over 75,000 expert-written rubric criteria.

Corpus source-specific finding

All-pass grading treats omitted requirements as material incompleteness.

Exact returned source excerpt

Importantly, a task is marked complete only if every criterion passes, a concept we refer to as **all-pass grading**.

Exact returned source excerpt

A deal-team report that identifies eight of ten risks is not 80% useful; it is materially incomplete.

Corpus source-specific finding

Harvey frames LAB as a guide to selective delegation and human review.

Exact returned source excerpt

By articulating where agents can do all, some, or none of a task, LAB helps law firms measure the ROI of AI investments and where such investments can augment their teams’ work.

Exact returned source excerpt

Understanding where agents are capable across practice areas allows firms to identify opportunities to deploy them in order to accelerate client value while moving lawyers to a high-leverage delegate and review pattern.

Corpus source-specific finding

The launch defers performance comparisons while promising normalized submissions.

Exact returned source excerpt

We're intentionally launching LAB without a leaderboard because we expect the dataset to evolve over time and we want to work with the community to ensure results are clear and intuitive in how they convey agent performance.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the coming weeks we will work with research partners to get baseline results on LAB and publish a leaderboard to provide perspective on the current state of legal agents.

Corpus source-specific finding

Open sourcing is intended to improve both the benchmark and agents themselves.

Exact returned source excerpt

We are open-sourcing LAB to give model providers, agent builders, researchers, and law firms a shared way to measure progress on long-horizon legal agents.

Exact returned source excerpt

We are already seeing LAB being used by both our internal teams for product evaluation and the larger research community to explore open-weight post-training, auto-research, memory, domain-specific legal skills, and harness optimizations for long-horizon agent work.
