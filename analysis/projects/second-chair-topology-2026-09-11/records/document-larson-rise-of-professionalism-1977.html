Corpus framing of this source: Professional power as the construction of market shelter and social status

Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied publisher description and interpretive notes present Magali Sarfatti Larson’s The Rise of Professionalism as a historical account of how occupations turn expertise into protected markets and collective status. They identify credentialing, state recognition and professional ideology as central mechanisms. The notes extend this account to AI; that extension is not Larson’s own prediction.

Corpus source-specific finding

Larson explains professional privilege through historical market construction rather than disinterested service.

Exact returned source excerpt

Magali Sarfatti Larson dismantles functionalist and ideal‐typical accounts that naturalize professional prestige by invoking disinterested service and esoteric knowledge.

Exact returned source excerpt

Drawing on Freidson’s practice-centered sociology and Gramsci’s theory of intellectuals, Larson reframes professionalization as a historical project: the collective construction of market shelter and social closure that translates scarce expertise into monopoly rents, jurisdictional control, and middle-class status.

Corpus source-specific finding

The professional project combines market control with collective upward mobility.

Exact returned source excerpt

Professionalization is a collective, intentional-in-effect historical project with two faces that must be analysed together: (1) a **market project** — creating and controlling a market for expert services where none naturally exists, because expertise is an intangible commodity that must first be standardised so that producers can be certified and substitutes excluded; and (2) a **collective mobility project** — converting the market shelter into social status and middle-class respectability for the whole occupational group.

Exact returned source excerpt

Standardised training and credentialing simultaneously produce the standardised producers the market project needs and the badges of status the mobility project needs — "educational institutions became engines of stratification." Entry regulation (degrees, examinations, licensure) is where scarcity is manufactured and maintained: the settlement by which "expertise becomes property." This is the mechanism this project's AI argument engages: a technology that mass-produces the performance of expertise attacks the scarcity that the credential system exists to manufacture.

Corpus source-specific finding

Professional autonomy depends on political recognition, not independence from the state.

Exact returned source excerpt

Through a comparative analysis centered on England and the United States—the “purer” laboratories of laissez-faire—she traces how associations, credentialing systems, and claims to autonomy were negotiated with state and elite patrons, how educational institutions became engines of stratification, and why self-regulation insulates professions while binding them to political authority.

Exact returned source excerpt

By distinguishing market control from collective mobility—and showing how they converge in the organization of knowledge and the regulation of entry—Larson provides a general model that travels across law and medicine to aspiring occupations while excluding nonmarket corps (military, clergy).

Corpus source-specific finding

Professional ideology survives the shift from independent practice to salaried employment.

Exact returned source excerpt

Larson shows how the liberal-capitalist figure of the free practitioner gives way to the salaried specialist in corporate and bureaucratic settings, even as the professional ideal persists—and hardens—into an ideology that legitimates inequality and occupational closure.

Exact returned source excerpt

Case materials, historical synthesis, and theoretical argument cohere into a powerful explanation of why professionals resist union identities, how client status reciprocally stratifies practitioners, and what happens to professional authority under “revolutionary” social change.
