# Majority of General Counsel Indicate Openness to Using AI in Nearly Every Major Legal Use Case, According to The General Counsel Report *FTI Consulting — FTI Consulting (issued jointly with Relativity) (2025)* # Majority of General Counsel Indicate Openness to Using AI in Nearly Every Major Legal Use Case, According to The General Counsel Report Washington, D.C., March 26, 2025 — More than two-thirds of general counsel have expressed interest in using generative artificial intelligence within the legal department, according to part two of [The General Counsel Report 2025*](https://ftitechnology.com/l/gc-report-2025) from FTI Consulting, Inc.

Exact returned source excerpt

Generative AI use has also increased over the past year, with 44% of general counsel saying their legal teams are now using generative AI, compared to 28% in 2024 and 20% in 2023.

Corpus source-specific finding

Comfort with AI varies by application and region.

Exact returned source excerpt

In addition to plans to purchase or improve the use of tools spanning contract lifecycle management, enterprise legal management, matter management and automation, legal department leaders expressed high comfort levels using generative AI for legal use cases including legal research, e-discovery, document review, contract analysis and more.

Exact returned source excerpt

- General counsel are least comfortable with the use of generative AI in investigations, with 47% rating their openness at a one or two on a scale of one (not at all comfortable) to five (extremely comfortable).

Corpus source-specific finding

Technology enthusiasm coexists with readiness gaps and heavier evaluation workloads.

Exact returned source excerpt

- The report revealed a slight improvement in technology readiness — 85% of general counsel said they felt minimally or not at all prepared to face the risks of generative AI, down from 93% in 2024.

Exact returned source excerpt

- Even with widespread recognition of the value of technology, 75% of legal departments lack a technology roadmap, making it more difficult to plan and effectively allocate budget.

Corpus source-specific finding

Outside counsel remains the leading reported response to rising risk and demand.

Exact returned source excerpt

- Increased reliance on law firms was the most popular strategy general counsel cited for addressing increasing risk and escalating demand, with 79% confirming they turn to outside counsel more frequently.

Corpus source-specific finding

FTI’s Sophie Ross recommends expert-led experimentation rather than unqualified adoption.

Exact returned source excerpt

“A growing number of general counsel are exploring how AI can make their teams more efficient and effective,” said [Sophie Ross](/experts/sophie-ross), Global Chief Executive Officer of FTI Technology.

Exact returned source excerpt

“However, this increasing comfort level must be balanced against an assortment of persistent concerns, including the technology’s viability, availability, reliability and cost.

Corpus source-specific finding

Relativity’s David Horrigan interprets adoption growth as evidence of lasting transformation.

Exact returned source excerpt

“The latest data from the 2025 edition of *The General Counsel Report* confirm what our research had previously indicated, namely that generative AI is not a flash-in-the-pan fad, but rather a fundamentally transformative technology that is reshaping the practice of law,” said [David Horrigan](https://www.linkedin.com/in/david-horrigan-3a482a6/), Discovery Counsel and Legal Education Director at Relativity.

Exact returned source excerpt

“Last year, less than a third of general counsel said their teams were using generative AI, but that figure jumped to 44% in the 2025 report, underscoring that generative AI is an important technology for the legal profession and its chief legal officers.” *The General Counsel Report 2025* is available for download [here](https://ftitechnology.com/l/gc-report-2025).
