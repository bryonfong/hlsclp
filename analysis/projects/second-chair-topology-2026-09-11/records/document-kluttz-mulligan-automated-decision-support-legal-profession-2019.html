Corpus framing of this source: Predictive coding reshapes legal judgment, supervision and client–provider relationships

Corpus synthesis of this source: Kluttz and Mulligan use qualitative interviews about e-discovery to show how predictive coding changes professional work and distributes expertise and control among lawyers, clients and technical providers. They acknowledge evidence of efficiency gains but distinguish controlled performance studies from actual practice. Their central recommendations call for shared testing standards, combined legal and technical oversight, and contestable systems that keep lawyers actively engaged in judgment.
