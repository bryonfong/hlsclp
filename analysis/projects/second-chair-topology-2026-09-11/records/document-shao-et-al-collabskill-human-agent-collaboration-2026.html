I.2 Manual Rubric Audit For realistic occupational tasks, manual grading is hard to scale: GDPval reports that grading each comparison took human experts an hour on average (Patwardhan et al., 2025), and Remote Labor Index reports human evaluators spending around 30 minutes per task for pairwise preference judgments (Mazeika et al., 2025).

Exact returned source excerpt

While a more scalable approach is to use LLM-as-a-judge provided with fine-grained rubrics, many task sources do not come with rubrics or only cover correctness, and manual rubric construction remains expensive, motivating our reference-free approach where we employ an agent to generate rubrics automatically.

Corpus source-specific finding

A 27-task audit reports substantial but incomplete agreement with official GDPVal criteria.

Exact returned source excerpt

We validate rubric quality by sampling 3 tasks per sector from GDPVal (27 tasks total) and manually comparing our auto-generated rubrics against the official hand-authored GDPVal rubrics9 , which were released after the original GDPVal paper (Patwardhan et al., 2025).

Exact returned source excerpt

The generated rubrics achieve a recall of 82.1% against the official criteria (998 of 1,216 official rubric items are covered) and a precision of 92.2% (297 of 322 of our items are grounded in official criteria), with authors judging all 322 of the generated rubric items as important for evaluating task quality.

Corpus framing of this source: CollabSkill: evaluating human–agent collaboration on realistic occupational work

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source introduces a performance-based framework that evaluates real workers and AI agents jointly on occupational tasks. It argues that autonomous benchmarks cannot stand in for collaboration evaluation, reports that agent interface, worker skill and interaction quality materially affect outcomes, measures how hands-on use changes worker attitudes, and identifies sampling, scoring and model-formulation limitations.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors frame human–agent collaboration as an important but under-evaluated mode of AI use, distinct from autonomous performance.

Exact returned source excerpt

(2025) find that augmentation comprises 57% of interactions versus 43% for full automation in Claude usage.

Exact returned source excerpt

While recent benchmarks have focused on evaluating fully autonomous agents on occupational tasks (Patwardhan et al., 2025; Mazeika et al., 2025; Vidgen et al., 2025; 2026), effective human augmentation cannot be assumed to follow directly from strong autonomous performance as collaborative outcomes are shaped by many factors, including human AI literacy, the collaborative behaviors of models, and interaction design (Shao et al., 2024; Zou et al., 2025; Mozannar et al., 2025; Haupt & Brynjolfsson, 2025).

Corpus source-specific finding

CollabSkill combines realistic task infrastructure with a Bayesian rating model that estimates human and agent contributions.

To this end, we introduce CollabSkill, a framework for evaluating human-agent collaboration on realistic, economically valuable tasks with real human workers.

Exact returned source excerpt

3.1 Overview A datapoint in CollabSkill is one completed evaluation episode in which a human works with an AI agent on a task instance under a fixed scoring function.

The framework evaluates deliverables across multiple dimensions and uses automated rubric generation and LLM judging to make evaluation scalable.
