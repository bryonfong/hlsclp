Open topic: Legal workflows: Immediate gains create competence risks

Corpus framing of this source: Expertise — David Autor and Neil Thompson

Corpus synthesis of this source: Autor and Thompson develop an expertise-based framework for explaining how automation changes labour demand, wages and employment. They argue that task quantity matters, as conventional task models propose, but that the scarcity of the expertise embedded in affected tasks also determines labour’s value. Their evidence covers the computer revolution’s last four decades and is explicitly illustrative rather than definitive; they propose the framework as applicable to AI and other technological eras while leaving macroeconomic implications undeveloped.

Corpus framing of this source: Expertise: How automation reshapes occupational wages and employment

Corpus synthesis of this source: David Autor and Neil Thompson develop an expertise-and-indivisibility framework that separates changes in occupational expertise requirements from changes in task quantities. Using task classifications and US occupational data from 1980–2018, they argue that automation can raise wages while lowering employment when it removes relatively inexpert tasks and leaves expert tasks behind, with the reverse effects when it removes relatively expert tasks.
