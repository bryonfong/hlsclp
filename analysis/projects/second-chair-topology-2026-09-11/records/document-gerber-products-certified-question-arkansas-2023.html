Corpus framing of this source: Gerber: corrective-fee malpractice claims and an unanswered certified question

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Arkansas Supreme Court rescinded certification without deciding whether a malpractice plaintiff can recover corrective fees without proving a better outcome in the underlying litigation. The supplied opinion recounts the Eighth Circuit’s alternative causation analysis and preserves disagreement over whether Arkansas’s highest court should resolve the issue.

Corpus source-specific finding

The Arkansas Supreme Court declined to resolve the substantive malpractice question.

Exact returned source excerpt

WYNNE, Associate Justice Pursuant to Rule 6-8 of the Rules of the Arkansas Supreme Court and Court of Appeals, this court accepted the following question of law certified by the United States District Court for the Eastern District of Arkansas: Can plaintiff establish proximate cause in a legal malpractice action and recover corrective fees, which resulted from legal negligence, even without proving that the result of the underlying action would have been more favorable to the plaintiff but for the legal negligence?

As explained below, we determine that certification was improvidently granted and rescind our decision to answer the certified question.

Corpus framing of this source: Corrective fees as malpractice damages without a traditional case within a case

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source recounts the Eighth Circuit’s treatment of Gerber’s malpractice claim after an alleged negligent waiver of attorney-client privilege. It distinguishes Gerber’s effort to recover remediation costs from a conventional claim that counsel’s negligence changed the outcome of underlying litigation, and reports the Eighth Circuit’s view that ordinary causation-and-damages principles may provide a route to proving proximate cause in these unusual circumstances.
