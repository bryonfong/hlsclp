Chatbot liability: Correct links do not cure misinformation

Corpus synthesis: Applies negligent-misrepresentation law to contradictory automated advice, with liability turning on reliance, loss and defences rather than error alone.

Native topic findings

  1. Existing negligent-misrepresentation law can hold a business responsible for misleading chatbot advice.
  2. A link to the correct policy did not defeat reasonable reliance on contradictory chatbot advice.
  3. The outcome depended on proof of reliance, loss and defences, not merely the existence of an error.
  4. The case establishes responsibility for automated advice, not findings about generative-AI technology or error rates.

Corpus framing of this source: Moffatt v. Air Canada: liability for misleading chatbot fare advice

Corpus synthesis of this source: The British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal found Air Canada liable for negligent misrepresentation after a customer relied on incorrect chatbot advice about retroactive bereavement fares. The decision addresses responsibility for website information, reasonable customer reliance, evidentiary failures and compensatory damages; it does not establish the chatbot’s underlying technology.

The evidence showed conflicting fare instructions and a timely refund request.
