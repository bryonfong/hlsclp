Native topic findings

  1. The central dispute concerns the durability of Big Law’s economic model, not whether legal demand or large firms will disappear.
  2. Competing theories locate firms’ durable value in risk-sharing, reputation and coordination rather than size alone.
  3. Sophisticated corporate buyers weaken firms’ pricing power while retaining reasons to purchase outside expertise.
  4. Competition for portable business transforms partnership from a secure destination into continuing performance competition.
  5. Staffing leverage can support partner returns while undermining apprenticeship and exposing firms to demand shocks.
  6. Training and promotion structures raise inclusion and retention questions that financial performance alone cannot answer.
  7. Recent prosperity supports resilience claims but also reveals segmentation rather than uniform market security.
  8. Scale can provide defensive protection, client coordination or market power; the evidence does not support a universal move toward smaller firms.
  9. Technology and alternative providers create opportunities for operational redesign, not a demonstrated inevitability of incumbent replacement.
  10. Evidence about elite firms cannot stand in for the entire profession or for access to justice.
