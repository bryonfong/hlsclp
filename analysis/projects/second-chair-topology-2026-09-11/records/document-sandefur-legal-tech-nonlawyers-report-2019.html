Corpus framing of this source: Legal Tech for Non-Lawyers: Report of the Survey of US Legal Technologies

Corpus synthesis of this source: The report maps US digital legal technologies available to non-lawyers as of Fall 2018 and compares their functions with documented access-to-justice needs. It finds broad subject-matter coverage but limited, fragmented assistance: tools mainly provide information, lawyer referrals and document production rather than helping users recognize legal problems and pursue them through resolution. The report attributes these limitations partly to unauthorized-practice rules and human resistance to institutional change, and recommends evidence-guided regulatory reform.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report provides a time-bounded, access-to-justice-focused survey rather than a comprehensive registry.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Survey reports on the state of the field as of Fall 2018.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Survey identified 322 technologies designed for use by non-lawyers in US jurisdictions (see Appendix B for a listing).

Corpus source-specific finding

No general-purpose tool supports the full path from recognizing a justice problem to pursuing a remedy.

Exact returned source excerpt

SERVICES TOOLS PROVIDE No existing tool is a “one stop shop” for justice problems generally.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, there is no tool that consists of a natural language interface that diagnoses the legal aspects of a user’s life situation, offers possible routes to solution, and then facilitates taking action toward a solution by compiling evidence of a complaint and creating or filing a legal document with a court or other agency.

The report identifies a substantial mismatch between available services and how people experience justice problems.

Exact returned source excerpt

Source: Survey of US Legal Technologies WHAT TOOLS PROVIDE VERSUS WHAT PEOPLE WANT OR NEED There is a substantial mismatch between the services tools offer and what is known about the assistance wanted or needed by the American public.

Exact returned source excerpt

While some tools do exist that serve the broad legal problem areas Americans most frequently encounter, the services the tools actually offer are not those that research suggests Americans want or need when they face justice problems.

Corpus source-specific finding

Diagnostic tools could address the mismatch, but very few exist.

Exact returned source excerpt

In a context where many people experience justice problems that they do not recognize as legal or remediable, tools that offer diagnosis – explaining to someone that their problem involves specific legal aspects, such as violations of housing codes or wage and hour laws – and provide options for response could be particularly valuable.

Exact returned source excerpt

There are very few such tools.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors attribute limited functionality partly to unauthorized-practice rules.

Exact returned source excerpt

A key reason for the limitations in what available tools provide is the current regulatory regime for legal services in the United States, which limits most assistance with legal problems to services provided by licensed attorneys (Rhode and Ricca 2013).

Exact returned source excerpt

In particular, unlike in other jurisdictions (for example, the United Kingdom), non-lawyers in the United States are usually prohibited from offering legal advice.

Corpus source-specific finding

Institutional adoption presents a separate human and social barrier.

Exact returned source excerpt

Even when rules change formally, clerks, judges and other courthouse staff can persist in older patterns, refusing to recognize documents that are officially approved by the court system.

Exact returned source excerpt

The barriers here are not technological, but human.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report recommends evidence-guided reform of unauthorized-practice rules.

Exact returned source excerpt

A growing evidence base suggests that non-lawyers can provide effective services for some kinds of justice problems without risk to consumer protection (see, e.g., Rhode and Ricca 2013; Sandefur and Clarke 2018).

Exact returned source excerpt

This evidence base can help guide sensible revisions to the rules about unauthorized practice that would permit the expansion of what both human non-lawyers and digital tools can provide.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report predicts that digital legal technology can empower people and communities if social and regulatory constraints are addressed.

Exact returned source excerpt
