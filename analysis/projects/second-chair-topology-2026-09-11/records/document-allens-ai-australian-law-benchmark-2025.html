Corpus framing of this source: Allens’ 2025 Australian-law benchmark: substantial gains, persistent reliability limits

Corpus synthesis of this source: Allens reports marked improvement in general-purpose LLMs’ Australian-law answers, but finds uneven reliability, weak citations and recurring jurisdictional errors. Its expert-graded benchmark supports lawyer-supervised use rather than direct reliance, while acknowledging important limits to its testing and inconsistencies in the supplied citation-score reporting.

Corpus source-specific finding

The benchmark measures repeatable Australian-law answer quality, not whether AI outperforms lawyers.

Exact returned source excerpt

For this year's report, we tested 7 market-leading LLMs against our benchmark of 30 questions, and assessed their responses at the standard of a 5 years' post-admission Australian qualified lawyer.

Exact returned source excerpt

Responses were evaluated on substance, citations and clarity by a panel of 23 senior Allens legal experts in April 2025.

Corpus source-specific finding

Average performance improved sharply, but strong answers did not establish dependable performance.

Exact returned source excerpt

- The average *substance *score of all the LLMs we tested was higher in 2025 (4.3** out of **5**) than in 2024 (**1.92** out of **5**), confirming the overall improvement across the board through the year, including with the appearance of the new generation of reasoning models (such as DeepSeek and OpenAI o1).

Exact returned source excerpt

- The *clarity* score also showed clear improvement since 2024.

Corpus source-specific finding

Model choice depends on the legal task; reasoning models do not dominate universally.

Exact returned source excerpt

This year's results demonstrate that the OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek R1 reasoning models performed well across the board, especially in 'difficult' areas where most LLMs struggled, such as tax, employment and dispute resolution.

Exact returned source excerpt

Notably, GPT-4o significantly outperformed the later OpenAI o1 on banking and real estate law queries.

Corpus source-specific finding

Citation failures remain substantial, and the supplied passages give inconsistent citation rankings.

Exact returned source excerpt

- When compared with our 2024 results, the LLMs in general produced higher average *citation *scores; however, all LLMs on occasion produced citations that were incorrect (a *citation* score of 1 out of 3) or fictional (a *citation *score of 0 out of 3).

Exact returned source excerpt

#### Flights of fancy Despite the request to provide citations, in 23% of answers the underlying case law or legislation was either completely absent or just made up.

Exact returned source excerpt

Some of the citations that appeared in answers were real, but reflected the law of another jurisdiction without recognising the difference in law.

Exact returned source excerpt

Nearly all responses by all LLMs referred in some way to the 'doctrine of equivalents', which is part of patent law in jurisdictions such as the US or the UK, but not Australia.

Exact returned source excerpt

- Overall, GPT-4o performed less effectively when its web search function was disabled.

Exact returned source excerpt

In some instances, web results appeared to have diluted the quality of the responses.

Exact returned source excerpt

Notably, the 2025 edition does not include various legal-industry specific tools such as Harvey, Thomson Reuters's CoCounsel* *and LexisNexis's Lexis+ AI*.* While Allens continues to benchmark and leverage several of these (and other) products, none are included in this report to avoid drawing broad performance comparisons between general purpose products and products that have been optimised for specific use-cases or user-interactions, or are 'multi-model' and use different AI models for different tasks.

Exact returned source excerpt

Allens recommends expert-supervised augmentation while making broader claims about limits to replacement.

Corpus framing of this source: Allens’ Australian-law benchmark: expert grading, scope limits and legal-AI development

Corpus synthesis of this source: The 2025 Allens AI Australian law benchmark explains its repeatable, practitioner-graded approach to assessing legal LLMs, acknowledges what its tests omit, and surveys emerging benchmarks and products. The supplied material presents methodological reasoning and qualified expectations rather than numerical performance results.

Corpus source-specific finding

Allens tracks Australian legal-advice capabilities through an annual, repeatable benchmark.

Exact returned source excerpt

When we released the first edition of the Allens AI Australian law benchmark in 2024 alongside the Links*AI* English law benchmark, our reports were some of the few projects in the world that sought to provide measurable and repeatable tests to gauge the performance of generative AI in a legal context.

Exact returned source excerpt

### Our methodology Each year, in conjunction with Linklaters' LinksAI *English law benchmark, we ask leading publicly available LLMs to answer a set of 30 legal questions from 10 separate practice areas that an Australian qualified lawyer may be routinely called upon to respond to or explain in the course of their legal practice.

Corpus source-specific finding

Expert Australian-law grading aims to capture nuance and identify foreign-law intrusion.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our methodology distinguishes itself from other emerging benchmarks in a number of ways: - **human-grading**: the emergence of LLM-as-judge frameworks for assessing LLM performance has allowed benchmarks to be compiled and updated quickly by removing human grading from the process.

Exact returned source excerpt

While the relative benefits of LLM-marked and human-marked frameworks have been discussed elsewhere, a human-marking process allows our benchmark to consider and distinguish nuanced behaviours that arise with each question.

Corpus source-specific finding

Scaled marking assesses response quality and variability, at a higher resource cost.

Exact returned source excerpt

- **scaled question-by-question marking:** grading by Allens' knowledge management and specialist teams allows our benchmark to ascribe a wider analog range of scores to a response (rather than binary correct / incorrect assessments) allowing for an assessment of the average quality of an LLM's responses (instead of its aggregate performance against a multi-question or multi-choice dataset) and the degree to which each LLM's responses can be expected to vary in quality for any given question.

Exact returned source excerpt

While marking by expert legal practitioners is necessarily more resource-intensive than other automated or programmatic marking solutions, it has complemented Allens' broader adoption of LLMs across its existing legal practice and continues to facilitate an ongoing consideration of how such tools can be effectively and reliably used in day-to-day practice.

Corpus source-specific finding

Single-prompt consistency limits what the benchmark can establish about practical use.

Exact returned source excerpt

### Omissions and extensions Refined LLM querying ** To ensure consistency when comparing LLMs and to control for variations in usage by testers, the Allens AI Australian law benchmark uses repeatable 'single prompt' questions across all queries.

Exact returned source excerpt

Such prompting, however, does not explore the results that can be achieved by carefully refining queries for each LLM or by interrogating and testing LLM responses in subsequent prompts.

Corpus source-specific finding

Allens situates its methodology within a growing landscape of external testing.

Exact returned source excerpt

In 2025, a number of new legal benchmarks have been launched that are of interest, including: - the *CaseLaw, ContractLaw and LegalBench* benchmarks published by ai* ***that consider LLM performance against Canadian case law, contract law and legal reasoning tasks; - targeted studies of LLM performance including by Stanford's Human-Centered AI Intelligence team ([AI on Trial: Legal Models Hallucinate in 1 out of 6 (or More) Benchmarking Queries](https://dho.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/Legal_RAG_Hallucinations.pdf)); and - further information released by legal LLM providers about their internal benchmarking processes, such as that released by Thomson Reuters for its CoCounsel tool ([Legal AI Benchmarking: CoCounsel - Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/innovation/legal-ai-benchmarking-cocounsel/)).

Corpus framing of this source: Allens’ methodology for benchmarking LLMs on Australian law

Corpus synthesis of this source: The 2025 Allens AI Australian law benchmark describes a repeatable, lawyer-scored assessment of legal answers. The supplied material explains the test design and intended comparisons, but reports no performance results or recommendations.

Corpus source-specific finding

The benchmark tests Australian-law explanation and contractual interpretation across practice areas.

Exact returned source excerpt

Each question has been selected to require an LLM to summarise an aspect of Australian law, to interpret and explain a contractual provision, or to undertake some blend of each of these tasks.

Exact returned source excerpt

#### Allens AI Australian law benchmark methodology 30 difficult legal questions from across 10 separate practice areas - LLMs primed with a common context prompt with 'single prompt' queries - Questions repeated three times to control and test for random hallucination and variance - Scoring performed by Allens' knowledge management and subject area specialist lawyers - Responses scored against* substance*, *citations *and *clarity* marking rubrics.

Corpus source-specific finding

Reusing the 2024 questions supports comparisons over time and between models.

Exact returned source excerpt

The 2025 edition of this benchmark has asked the same questions as were posed in 2024, in order to deliver both an absolute assessment of LLM performance over time, and to allow for the relative assessment of different LLMs at a given moment in time.

Corpus source-specific finding

The protocol standardises prompting and repeats single-shot queries to test variability.

Exact returned source excerpt

Provide a concise answer to the question below applying Australian law.

Exact returned source excerpt

Cite any relevant statutes, regulation, guidance or case law'.* To control for random hallucination and variation in user queries and responses, each question was asked of each LLM three times as a 'single shot' query without further interrogation.

Corpus source-specific finding

Specialist lawyers score substance, citations and clarity, with central moderation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Responses were graded against three scoring rubrics for substance (assessing the accuracy and correctness of each response), citations (assessing the quality, correctness and accuracy of evidence cited by each LLM) and clarity (assessing how well the response explained the relevant issues) by teams of Allens' knowledge management and subject matter specialists, with a central team moderating scores awarded by different practice groups.
