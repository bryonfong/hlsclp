Corpus framing of this source: Knowledge Management in Law Firms

Corpus synthesis of this source: A literature-based analysis of why law firms need knowledge management, what obstructs it and how firms should implement it. The paper treats knowledge—especially lawyers’ tacit, experience-based know-how—as an intangible asset whose systematic capture and transfer can improve service, efficiency, continuity and competitiveness. It principally synthesises cited studies and commentators rather than reporting original empirical results, and it qualifies its business case by emphasising cultural resistance, technology’s limits, scarce lawyer time, weak return metrics and the billable-hour model.

Corpus source-specific finding

The paper defines knowledge management as the organisational transfer of knowledge, particularly the difficult conversion of tacit experience into explicit, reusable knowledge.
