Native topic findings

  1. The central proposition is that credible AI evaluation must test professional task execution, not just the quality of isolated answers.
  2. Open-ended professional work creates a tension between reproducible scoring and context-sensitive judgment.
  3. Reported task-level results show useful autonomy alongside substantial limits, without measuring job replacement.
  4. Realistic tasks do not by themselves guarantee valid or reproducible measurements.
  5. Automated judging and self-correction remain evaluation tools whose reliability requires separate assessment.
  6. The supplied evidence supports a measurement agenda more strongly than conclusions about legal reliability, productivity or economic displacement.
