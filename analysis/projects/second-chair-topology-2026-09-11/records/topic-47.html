Solicitor entry: Diverse routes yield unequal attainment

Corpus synthesis: Separates access from examination outcomes, identifying apprenticeships as promising while leaving causes of pass-rate disparities unresolved.

Native topic findings

  1. Diverse entry to solicitor qualification does not imply equal attainment.
  2. Measured associations explain only part of attainment differences and do not settle their causes.
  3. Apprenticeships provide the SRA's clearest positive evidence for an inclusive qualification route.
  4. Disability findings offer qualified reassurance rather than a universal answer to concerns about fairness.
  5. Pass-rate variation and weaker outcomes for some routes make preparation a practical concern.
  6. The consumer-survey extract cannot establish a broader link between demographic diversity and legal-service choice.

The SQE four years on – facts and figures

Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Current wave (2026)

Corpus framing of this source: Four years of SQE results: diverse entry, uneven outcomes and strong apprentice performance

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Solicitors Regulation Authority synthesises SQE results and Kaplan analyses to describe participation, attainment and qualification routes. It highlights diverse entry and strong apprentice and adjusted neurodivergent candidate performance, while acknowledging ethnic attainment gaps and substantial unexplained score variation.

Corpus source-specific finding

The SRA attributes changing pass rates to cohort performance rather than changing assessment standards.

Exact returned source excerpt

It covers eight SQE1 and 12 SQE2 assessments taken between November 2021 and July 2025, and more than 30,000 candidates assessed in 50 countries.

Exact returned source excerpt

While the standard remains the same for each sitting, the pass rate (the percentage of the cohort that passes) varies, because the performance of the candidate cohorts varies.

Corpus source-specific finding

SQE entrants span ethnic and socio-economic backgrounds, and women form a majority.

Exact returned source excerpt

## The ethnic make-up of SQE entry cohorts is diverse The SQE entry cohort, which includes candidates living in the UK and in other countries, is more ethnically diverse than the UK population, when compared with the 2021 census data.

Exact returned source excerpt

It is also more diverse than the solicitor profession.

Corpus source-specific finding

Prior academic attainment provides the strongest measured associations with scores, but most variation remains unexplained.

Exact returned source excerpt

The two factors on which we have data that have the most impact on performance for SQE1 relate to university ranking and past academic achievement.

Exact returned source excerpt

This multivariate analysis shows that the rank of the university attended by a candidate and their degree classification together explain about 22% of the variance in candidates' SQE1 scores - 11.9% for university rank and 10.6% for degree classification.

Corpus source-specific finding

Ethnic attainment gaps persist, while adjusted socio-economic associations are small.

Exact returned source excerpt

There is a long-standing issue in legal assessments, as well as other professional and academic assessments: the overall pass rate for White candidates is higher than the overall pass rate for candidates of other ethnicities.

Exact returned source excerpt

Research we commissioned from the [University of Exeter](/sra/research-publications/potential-causes-differential-outcomes-legal-professional-assessments/) before the first SQE assessments were taken showed there are multiple, complex factors combining to cause the difference.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

Disability outcomes and a targeted neurodivergence analysis offer reassurance, within the populations studied.

Corpus source-specific finding

Resitters, previously qualified lawyers and candidates whose first language is not English face lower reported pass rates.

Exact returned source excerpt

- Resitting candidates** – those who fail their first attempt and choose to re-sit generally have lower pass rates than candidates sitting for the first.

Exact returned source excerpt

Typically, around 20% of those who resit SQE1 pass.

Corpus source-specific finding

The SRA cautions LPC graduates against relying on their previous studies alone and endorses the training market's response.

Exact returned source excerpt

- **LPC graduates** – those who have completed the LPC, who can qualify as a solicitor by taking SQE2 – generally have lower pass rates.

Exact returned source excerpt

This may reflect the fact that the LPC focuses on testing a candidate's readiness to start a training contract, whereas the SQE tests whether a candidate is ready to practise as a solicitor, and so is more demanding.
