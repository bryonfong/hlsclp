  4. Consumer-price inflation requires a separate assessment from aggregate wage growth.

OECD Employment Outlook 2023: Artificial Intelligence and the Labour Market

Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Generative shock (2023-2024)

Corpus framing of this source: OECD simulations suggest limited aggregate wage effects from minimum-wage increases

Corpus synthesis of this source: OECD Employment Outlook 2023: Artificial Intelligence and the Labour Market estimates direct and spillover wage effects in France, Germany, the United Kingdom and the United States. It attributes cross-country differences to wage distributions and minimum-wage coverage, acknowledges possible underestimation during high inflation, and distinguishes its wage simulations from reported research on consumer-price effects.

Corpus source-specific finding

A simulated 1% minimum-wage increase raises aggregate wages by approximately 0.03% in the United States to 0.2% in France.

Exact returned source excerpt

This exercise suggests that a minimum wage increase of 1% can be expected to have an effect on aggregate wage growth between 0.03% (in the United States, using the share of minimum wage earners of state-level minimum wages) and 0.2% (in France).

Exact returned source excerpt

The impact of a 1% increase in the minimum wage is simulated using estimates of spillover effects from the literature and taking the share of the minimum wage earners in a baseline year (2018 for France and Germany, an average of 2019, 2021 and 2022 for the United Kingdom and 2022 for the United States) – see Box 1.8 for more details.

Corpus source-specific finding

The calculation combines wage increases near the minimum wage with literature-based spillovers higher up the wage distribution.

Exact returned source excerpt

The direct effect is estimated by a 1% increase in hourly wages at, below or slightly above the minimum wage (threshold set at 105% of the minimum wage).

Exact returned source excerpt

For the employees at or below the threshold, the average hourly wage and the average number of hours worked are calculated using EU-SES (2018) for France and Germany, the UK-LFS (2019 and 2021-22) for the United Kingdom and the CPS (2022) for the United States.

Corpus source-specific finding

Minimum-wage coverage, spillover strength and wage compression explain national differences.

These cross-country variations can be explained by the difference in the share of minimum wage earners, the magnitude of the spillover effects and the shape of the wage distribution.
