Native topic record

Training fair use: Lawfulness depends on particular copying

Corpus synthesis: Separates acquisition, retention, training and distribution while disputing whether noninfringing competition constitutes copyright market harm.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Legal institutions and professional practice: Substantive outcomes depend on context, capacity and judgment · AI-training fair use: Lawfulness turns on specific copying and market substitution

4 distinct stance-bearing works · Supports: 4

Native topic findings

  1. AI-training fair use turns on the particular use and competitive purpose, not on a blanket exemption for AI.
  2. The central disagreement concerns whether noninfringing AI competition can count as copyright market harm.
  3. Potential training-license markets raise a related dispute over circular reasoning.
  4. Acquisition, retention, training and distribution require distinct analysis; lawful training does not automatically excuse a pirated library.
  5. Evidence of substitution, not general predictions about AI, determines the reach of the rulings.
  6. The practical stakes extend beyond output filtering to data provenance, copying records and the boundary between law and editorial expression.
