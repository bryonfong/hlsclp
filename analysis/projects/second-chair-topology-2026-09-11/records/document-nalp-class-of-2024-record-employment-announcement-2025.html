Corpus framing of this source: NALP reports record Class of 2024 employment but warns of hiring headwinds

Corpus synthesis of this source: NALP's September 2025 research announcement documents exceptionally strong graduate employment, expanding private-practice hiring and rising overall pay. It attributes a lower private-practice median salary largely to compositional and reporting changes, interprets reduced job searching as a satisfaction signal, and forecasts weaker BigLaw and federal hiring with smaller graduating classes providing a possible buffer.

Corpus source-specific finding

The larger Class of 2024 achieved record employment despite concerns about market absorption.

Exact returned source excerpt

With an overall employment rate of 93.4%, this figure was 0.8 percentage points higher than the previous record set last year by the Class of 2023.

Exact returned source excerpt

Among one of the several high-water marks set by this year's class is the percentage of graduates who were employed as of March 17, 2025, approximately ten months post-graduation.

Corpus source-specific finding

Attorney employment reached its highest share under the current classifications.

Exact returned source excerpt

(See [Table 1](#table1).) In addition to the improvement in the employment rate, another historic high obtained by the Class of 2024 was the percentage of graduates working in jobs for which bar admission is required or anticipated, more commonly known as attorney positions.

Exact returned source excerpt

This figure grew by 2.2 percentage points to 84.3% - marking a new all-time high dating back to when NALP introduced the current job classifications in 2001.

Corpus source-specific finding

Overall median pay rose, while the private-practice median fell without widespread starting-salary cuts.

Exact returned source excerpt

For the sixth consecutive year, the overall median salary increased —climbing by 5.6% to $95,000.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, for the first time since the Class of 2011, the median private practice salary declined, falling by 3.0% to $160,000.

Corpus source-specific finding

Private practice absorbed record numbers, with employment concentrated at the smallest and largest firms.

Exact returned source excerpt

In total, 20,810 graduates obtained jobs in private practice - the highest number on record.

Exact returned source excerpt

Not only did the percentage of law firm jobs continue to rise this year, but with a larger class, this resulted in more than 2,260 additional graduates employed in private practice compared to the Class of 2023.

Corpus source-specific finding

NALP interprets record-low job searching among employed graduates as evidence of satisfaction.

Exact returned source excerpt

Across all employment sectors, just 6.8% of employed Class of 2024 graduates were seeking a different job, also an all-time low.

Exact returned source excerpt

This is another indicator of the strength of the job market and demonstrates that graduates are relatively satisfied with their post-law school employment.

Corpus source-specific finding

NALP advises future graduates to prepare for weaker BigLaw and federal hiring, while allowing for resilience.

Exact returned source excerpt

This figure spiked during the Great Recession, peaking at nearly 25% for the Class of 2011, and subsequently declined in each year since, except for a small pandemic-related bump in 2020 (See [Chart 2](#ch2).) Although the market was extremely strong for the Class of 2024, future law graduates should brace for a likely contraction in BigLaw and federal hiring.

Exact returned source excerpt

NALP's [*Perspectives on 2024 Law Student Recruiting*](/perspectivesonrecruiting) report indicates that many firms pulled back on their 2024 and 2025 summer associate hiring, suggesting that there will be fewer graduates employed by large firms over the next few years.
