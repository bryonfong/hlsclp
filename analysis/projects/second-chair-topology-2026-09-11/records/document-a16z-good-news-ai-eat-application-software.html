Corpus framing of this source: Good News: AI Will Eat Application Software

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source argues that AI will enlarge and restructure application software rather than destroy it. Cheaper code, lower migration friction and agentic business models will intensify competition and expose weak incumbents, but workflow knowledge, network effects, brand, proprietary data, scale and counterpositioning will continue to support durable businesses. Its evidence consists mainly of business-model reasoning and company examples rather than quantitative analysis.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source rejects the premise that cheaper code makes software companies commodity businesses.

Exact returned source excerpt

The market is treating “software” as though it were a commodity input—as if the value of a software company resided in its code, and cheaper code meant more competition and therefore cheaper companies.

Exact returned source excerpt

But code is never where the value has lived: if code is where the value was, these companies would have never gotten so big in the first place.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI will produce a larger software market by making previously uneconomic customers, workflows and products viable.

Exact returned source excerpt

We are nowhere near saturating the world’s demand for high-quality software.

Exact returned source excerpt

And as code becomes cheaper, we should just expect to see the market demand *more*.

Corpus source-specific finding

Margins remain uncertain, but the source expects industry scale and profit pools to endure.

Exact returned source excerpt

Maybe gross margins settle into a different steady-state: maybe pricing power is diminished because switching costs give procurement teams more leverage in vendor negotiations, but AI also supports margin expansion due to a much more efficient use of labor.

Exact returned source excerpt

But no matter where margins end up, we expect that scale will expand dramatically: because as our colleague Anish Acharya likes to say, [the world is still short software](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-02-05/acharya-world-is-still-short-software-video).

Corpus source-specific finding

Lower migration friction will weaken switching-cost moats and pressure vendors that depend on lock-in.

Exact returned source excerpt

It’s definitely true that AI is changing the friction and the cost-benefit analysis associated with switching vendors: agents can assist with a lot of migration work that used to be a headache.

Exact returned source excerpt

So it means legacy companies with “[hostages, not customers](https://a16z.com/the-greenfield-strategy-ai-native-startup-bingo/),” to borrow a phrase from our colleague Alex Rampell, will feel a lot more pressure than they’re used to.

Corpus source-specific finding

Network effects, scale, brand and proprietary data remain meaningful, although AI changes their relative strength.

Exact returned source excerpt

In fact, insofar as AI makes the network more powerful—you can just do much more with a network than you could before—we should expect to see AI make these network effects *more powerful* than they were before.

Exact returned source excerpt

Harvey and Hebbia are building finance and legal collaboration spaces that connect service providers and clients, and soon their agents, on a single system: the more people and agents who use these platforms, the more valuable the platforms become.

Corpus source-specific finding

Embedded workflow knowledge and process engineering may become the strongest application-software moat.

Exact returned source excerpt

” Application software can be thought of as a *stored process*—it encodes opinions about how the function of an organization should operate, and those opinions calcify over years and decades of use into something that is inseparable from the organization itself.

Exact returned source excerpt

Successful app software companies are the ones that co-evolve with their clients around these workflows.

Corpus source-specific finding

Agentic products create counterpositioning opportunities through business models incumbents cannot readily adopt.

Exact returned source excerpt

It’s when the new company has a business model which, for whatever reason, is unattractive for the incumbent company to compete against.

Exact returned source excerpt

The “agent” model of doing work and replacing tasks is certainly going to create some counterposition opportunities for new startups to challenge incumbents.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source predicts a bifurcation: durable, differentiated vendors will thrive while thin, outdated offerings face genuine pressure.

Exact returned source excerpt

AI isn’t going to destroy the software industry; it’s going to split it into two parts.

Exact returned source excerpt

Frontend tools that serve primarily as thin wrappers around commodity functionality and do relatively little beyond presenting data in a slightly more convenient format are vulnerable.
