Open topic: Legal procurement: Hourly control contains fee exceptions

Corpus framing of this source: FDIC outside-counsel rules: controlled referrals, negotiated rates and auditable billing

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Outside Counsel Deskbook (revised April 2026) sets out the FDIC’s contractual and administrative requirements for retaining, supervising and paying domestic and foreign law firms. Its contribution is prescriptive rather than empirical: it establishes negotiated-rate controls, conditional alternatives to hourly fees, approval requirements and audit remedies, not findings about their effectiveness or predictions about the legal market.

Corpus framing of this source: FDIC requirements for outside counsel: AI, security, billing and matter governance

Corpus synthesis of this source: The revised Deskbook sets binding operational and professional requirements for law firms representing the FDIC. It regulates generative-AI use, assigns firms broad responsibility for information security, prescribes invoice detail and staffing discipline, centralises communications, controls records through and after representation, and preserves continuing confidentiality and conflict duties. It warns that breaches can lead to non-payment, contractual remedies, termination or professional sanctions, while qualifying its security guidance as neither exhaustive nor a safe harbor.
