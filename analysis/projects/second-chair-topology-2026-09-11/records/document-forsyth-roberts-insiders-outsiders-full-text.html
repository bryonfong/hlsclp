Corpus framing of this source: Combining outsider innovation with insider diffusion to sustain system change

Corpus synthesis of this source: Insiders and Outsiders: The Role of Human Agents and Networks in System Change develops a theoretical framework from cross-disciplinary literature and illustrates it with PNG SARV research. The authors argue that hybrid individuals and insider–outsider coalitions can combine complementary strengths, while stressing practical requirements, risks of capture and exclusion, and the framework’s non-normative purpose.

Innovation and diffusion require different strengths.

Exact returned source excerpt

We develop a general theoretical framework for identifying the strengths and weaknesses of insiders and outsiders in terms of innovation (generating new ideas) and diffusion (promoting the adoption of new ideas).

Exact returned source excerpt

Separating out these two elements of the change process helps in explaining why outsiders typically have an advantage when it comes to originating innovations, particularly radical ones, whereas insiders frequently have an advantage when it comes to diffusion.

Corpus framing of this source: Insiders, outsiders and networked strategies for systemic change

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source develops an interdisciplinary framework for explaining how insiders, outsiders, hybrid actors and cross-boundary networks facilitate or impede innovation and diffusion in complex regulatory and governance systems. Drawing on prior literature and ethnographic fieldwork, it argues that effective change often combines outsiders’ disruptive capacity with insiders’ diffusion capacity, while identifying implementation conditions, capture concerns and risks faced by actors who bridge institutional boundaries.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors broaden analysis beyond regulatory capture to examine how roles and networks shape systemic change.

Exact returned source excerpt

In contrast, we adopt an interdisciplinary approach to understand how various actors contribute to change within systems, including regulatory environments.

Exact returned source excerpt

By integrating insights from diverse disciplines and domains, we create a framework to examine how different roles and networks interact to either facilitate or impede change.

Corpus source-specific finding

The framework associates outsiders with innovation because they bring external perspectives, diverse networks and fewer commitments to the status quo.

Exact returned source excerpt

The wide span of outsiders' networks can enhance their ability to innovate because they are able to leverage ideas from one context to apply to another.

Exact returned source excerpt

Mark Granovetter's ([1973](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/#rego12645-bib-0033)) seminal study on the strength of weak ties (where some of the best connections and opportunities come from weaker connections in social networks) underlines the importance of marginal connections in diffusing information and facilitating the transfer of knowledge across systems.

Corpus source-specific finding

Insider experience and interests can inhibit disruptive innovation, even though insiders remain important to diffusion.

Exact returned source excerpt

Insiders often have professional and personal interests in maintaining and improving the status quo, and not seeking to catalyze major changes in power, which favors them privileging sustaining innovations while attempting to quash disruptive innovations before those can gain traction.

Exact returned source excerpt

Insiders' experience can also work against innovation when it comes to being open to trying a new approach that may have previously been tried but failed.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors’ central synthesis combines outsider-led disruption with insider-led diffusion.

In Section  [3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/#rego12645-sec-0009), we identify two strategies for leveraging the strengths of both insiders and outsiders, through identifying actors with insider and outsider characteristics and cross-border networks between insiders and outsiders.

## 5 Conclusion We suggest that driving effective and sustainable change is often a combination of both disruptive innovation, typically generated by outsiders, and effective diffusion, typically carried out by insiders.

For instance, the scholarship of regulatory capture focuses on strategies to mitigate outsider influence on regulatory agencies, including through institutional design (some scholars advocate for regulatory structures that limit the opportunity for capture, such as term limits for regulators, increased transparency or independent funding); enhanced accountability and oversight (e.g., strengthening oversight mechanisms, including external audits, public accountability measures, and increased transparency, to help mitigate capture); and promoting countervailing interests (e.g., encouraging the involvement of diverse stakeholders, such as consumer advocacy groups and other public interest organizations, to provide a counterbalance to industry influence) (Ayres and Braithwaite [1991](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/#rego12645-bib-0003); Barkow  [2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/#rego12645-bib-0004); Baxter  [2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/#rego12645-bib-0006); Rex  [2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/#rego12645-bib-0062)).
