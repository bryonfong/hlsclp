Corpus framing of this source: SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12: Model risk management across development, validation and governance

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Federal Reserve and OCC's 2011 supervisory guidance broadens model oversight beyond validation to the full model lifecycle. Drawing on supervisory and industry experience rather than a new empirical study, it recommends proportionate controls, independent and influential challenge, continuing performance assessment, and bank accountability for both internal and vendor models. Its scope concerns quantitative banking models; its warnings describe conditional risks rather than quantified forecasts.

Corpus source-specific finding

Manage model risk across the lifecycle, with rigor proportionate to exposure.

Exact returned source excerpt

Previous guidance and other publications issued by the OCC and the Federal Reserve on the use of models pay particular attention to model validation.[Fot2note Based on supervisory and industry experience over the past several years, this document expands on existing guidance—most importantly by broadening the scope to include all aspects of model risk management.

Exact returned source excerpt

The expanding use of models in all aspects of banking reflects the extent to which models can improve business decisions, but models also come with costs.

Corpus source-specific finding

Model risk includes both faulty models and misuse of otherwise sound models.

Exact returned source excerpt

OVERVIEW OF MODEL RISK MANAGEMENT For the purposes of this document, the term model refers to a quantitative method, system, or approach that applies statistical, economic, financial, or mathematical theories, techniques, and assumptions to process input data into quantitative estimates.

Exact returned source excerpt

The definition of model also covers quantitative approaches whose inputs are partially or wholly qualitative or based on expert judgment, provided that the output is quantitative in nature.[Fo3tnote Models are simplified representations of real-world relationships among observed characteristics, values, and events.

Corpus source-specific finding

Validation reduces risk but cannot eliminate it.

Exact returned source excerpt

Even with skilled modeling and robust validation, model risk cannot be eliminated, so other tools should be used to manage model risk effectively.

Exact returned source excerpt

Among these are establishing limits on model use, monitoring model performance, adjusting or revising models over time, and supplementing model results with other analysis and information.

Corpus source-specific finding

Effective challenge requires objectivity, expertise and authority to secure changes.

Exact returned source excerpt

A guiding principle for managing model risk is "effective challenge" of models, that is, critical analysis by objective, informed parties who can identify model limitations and assumptions and produce appropriate changes.

Exact returned source excerpt

Effective challenge depends on a combination of incentives, competence, and influence.

Corpus source-specific finding

Validation is an ongoing, multi-part assessment, not a one-time approval.

Exact returned source excerpt

All model components, including input, processing, and reporting, should be subject to validation; this applies equally to models developed in-house and to those purchased from or developed by vendors or consultants.

Benchmarking and back-testing provide distinct, qualified evidence.

Exact returned source excerpt

Benchmarking is the comparison of a given model's inputs and outputs to estimates from alternative internal or external data or models.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, differences do not necessarily indicate that the model is in error.

Corpus source-specific finding

Validation findings must affect deployment and remediation decisions.

Exact returned source excerpt

If significant deficiencies are noted as a result of the validation process, use of the model should not be allowed or should be permitted only under very tight constraints until those issues are resolved.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus framing of this source: SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12: Comprehensive Model Risk Management

Corpus synthesis of this source: The OCC and Federal Reserve guidance broadens supervisory attention from model validation alone to a bank-wide framework covering model development, implementation, use, validation, governance and controls. It treats models as imperfect, context-dependent tools whose risks require effective challenge, continuing empirical testing, documented accountability and proportionate oversight, including for vendor models and external resources.

Corpus source-specific finding

Models create benefits and risks that banks must manage comprehensively.

Exact returned source excerpt

The expanding use of models in all aspects of banking reflects the extent to which models can improve business decisions, but models also come with costs.

Exact returned source excerpt

There are also the potential indirect costs of relying on models, such as the possible adverse consequences (including financial loss) of decisions based on models that are incorrect or misused.

Corpus source-specific finding

Banks should assess model risk both model by model and across the institution.

Exact returned source excerpt

Model risk increases with greater model complexity, higher uncertainty about inputs and assumptions, broader use, and larger potential impact.

Exact returned source excerpt

Banks should consider risk from individual models and in the aggregate.

Corpus source-specific finding

Effective challenge is the organising principle for independent scrutiny.

Exact returned source excerpt

A guiding principle for managing model risk is "effective challenge" of models, that is, critical analysis by objective, informed parties who can identify model limitations and assumptions and produce appropriate changes.

Exact returned source excerpt

Comprehensive validation combines conceptual review, ongoing monitoring and outcomes analysis.

Exact returned source excerpt

MODEL VALIDATION Model validation is the set of processes and activities intended to verify that models are performing as expected, in line with their design objectives and business uses.

Exact returned source excerpt

If outcomes fall consistently outside this acceptable range, then the models should be redeveloped.[PageBreak] Page 10 Key Elements of Comprehensive Validation An effective validation framework should include three core elements: • Evaluation of conceptual soundness, including developmental evidence • Ongoing monitoring, including process verification and benchmarking • Outcomes analysis, including back-testing 1.

Corpus source-specific finding

Development and conceptual validation should test theory, data, assumptions, judgment and robustness.

Exact returned source excerpt

A sound development process will produce documented evidence in support of all model choices, including the overall theoretical construction, key assumptions, data, and specific mathematical calculations, as mentioned in Section IV.

Exact returned source excerpt

As part of model validation, those model aspects should be subjected to critical analysis by both evaluating the quality and extent of developmental evidence and conducting additional analysis and testing as necessary.

Corpus source-specific finding

Monitoring and outcomes analysis should continue throughout production use.

Exact returned source excerpt

Ongoing monitoring is essential to evaluate whether changes in products, exposures, activities, clients, or market conditions necessitate adjustment, redevelopment, or replacement of the model and to verify that any extension of the model beyond its original scope is valid.

Exact returned source excerpt

This monitoring should continue periodically over time, with a frequency appropriate to the nature of the model, the availability of new data or modeling approaches, and the magnitude of the risk involved.

Validation results should drive restrictions, remediation or rejection rather than merely produce findings.

Exact returned source excerpt

If significant deficiencies are noted as a result of the validation process, use of the model should not be allowed or should be permitted only under very tight constraints until those issues are resolved.
