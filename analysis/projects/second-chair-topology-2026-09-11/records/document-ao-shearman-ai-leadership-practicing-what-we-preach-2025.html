Corpus framing of this source: AI leadership: Practicing what we preach — regulatory fragmentation and practical client support

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source combines Peter Van Dyck’s and Alex Touma’s observations on divergent AI regulation with examples of the firm’s compliance tools and advisory work. It presents regulatory simplification as welcome relief for clients and describes demand for coordinated legal, technical and cross-jurisdictional expertise. Its evidence consists of reported regulatory developments and client examples; its efficiency claims reflect the firm’s own account.

Corpus source-specific finding

Van Dyck sees limited international adoption of the EU’s AI regulatory approach and reports clients struggling with overlapping rules.

Exact returned source excerpt

In recent years, the EU has seen a wave of complex and interrelated regulations such as the AI Act, Data Act, Data Governance Act, and Digital Services Act.

Exact returned source excerpt

“We’re seeing a lot of our clients struggle with how these frameworks all fit together, and how they can comply with all of them at the same time,” notes Peter.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source presents proposed EU simplification as client relief, not as an already enacted pause.

Exact returned source excerpt

Most importantly, the Commission published a Digital Omnibus package on November 19, 2025, which—together with several other simplifications—intends to pause the implementation of the AI Act’s provisions on high-risk AI systems for a period of up to 16 months.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Digital Omnibus package also intends to make several other changes to simplify the regulatory regime, including in relation to data protection and cybersecurity law.

Corpus source-specific finding

US federal policy favours AI expansion while state requirements create a continuing compliance patchwork.

Exact returned source excerpt

While the Biden Administration had issued executive orders setting forth guardrails for the development and deployment of AI technology, the Trump Administration revoked these.

Exact returned source excerpt

It then issued its own executive orders focusing on the domestic expansion of AI technology.
