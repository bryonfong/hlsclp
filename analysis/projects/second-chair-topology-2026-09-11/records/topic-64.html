Native topic record

Delegated error: Client relief need not excuse counsel

Corpus synthesis: Contrasts contextual tests for corrected neglect, balancing client protection, judicial discretion and consistent procedural enforcement.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Legal institutions and professional practice: Substantive outcomes depend on context, capacity and judgment · Lawyer liability: Error or ethical breach does not itself establish malpractice

2 distinct stance-bearing works · Supports: 2

Native topic findings

  1. Delegation and software reliance do not eliminate lawyer responsibility, but resulting errors need not automatically forfeit a client's case.
  2. Both majorities require contextual consideration, but apply different tests and reach different procedural outcomes.
  3. The central disagreement concerns whether harmless, promptly corrected neglect still needs an affirmative justification.
  4. Judicial discretion creates a separate tension between individualized justice and consistent enforcement.
  5. Client protection supplies a rationale for relief distinct from excusing counsel's conduct.
  6. The practical implications center on checking critical deadlines, documenting failures and seeking relief promptly.

Corpus synthesis of this source: Pincay v. Andrews, 389 F.3d 853 (9th Cir. 2004) (en banc), upholds a discretionary extension after a paralegal misread an appeal deadline. The majority rejects categorical exclusions from excusable neglect while retaining attorney responsibility. Berzon's concurrence and Kozinski's dissent develop competing accounts of equitable balancing, professional competence and appellate oversight.

The majority permits the late appeal through contextual balancing, not approval of the underlying mistake.

Exact returned source excerpt

Andrews's counsel learned about the error when Pincay relied upon the judgment as being final in related bankruptcy proceedings, and Andrews promptly tendered a notice of appeal together with a request for an extension within the 30-day grace period.

Corpus synthesis of this source: Taylor v. HCA-HealthONE LLC, 2018 COA 29, 417 P.3d 943 addresses relief from a non-merits dismissal after counsel attributed missed service deadlines to docketing-software failures. The majority requires reconsideration under the full Craig test, without deciding whether the malfunction excuses counsel. Judge Berger separately argues for immediate reinstatement and doctrinal reform focused on client fault and prejudice.
