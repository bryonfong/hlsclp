SupportsAssisted inequality: Productivity gains need not improve careers

Open topic: Assisted inequality: Productivity gains need not improve careers

Corpus framing of this source: ILO: Generative AI is more likely to transform work than eliminate occupations

Corpus synthesis of this source: ILO Working Paper 96 estimates global occupational exposure to GPT-4, finding greater potential for augmentation than full automation, with marked occupational, income-group and gender differences. Working Paper 140 develops a more granular assessment with NASK. The supplied material measures potential exposure rather than observed job losses and distinguishes demonstrated capabilities from disputed predictions about AI’s future.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors ground their analysis in demonstrated capabilities, not a settled forecast of AI development.

Exact returned source excerpt

The launch of ChatGPT marked an important advance in the public’s exposure to AI tools.

Exact returned source excerpt

Given these significant advancements, it is not surprising that concerns over potential job loss have resurged.

Corpus source-specific finding

The task-based method estimates potential substitution and complementarity across global employment.

Exact returned source excerpt

This study builds on the method developed by the ILO (Gmyrek, Berg and Bescond, 2023 - GBB hereafter), which used task descriptions from the 4-digit International Standard Classification of Occupations (ISCO-08) and the GPT-4 model to estimate task-level and occupational scores of exposure to GenAI technology and then derives global employment estimates based on official ILO labour force data.

Exact returned source excerpt

Analyses of employment effects of automation that use the task-based approach, including this study, attempt to capture two central consider- ations: (i) the ability of a specific technology to automate a given task and (ii) the character of ex- isting occupations in the labour market, based on the detailed tasks performed by these occupa- tions.

Corpus source-specific finding

The 2023 estimates concentrate high exposure in clerical work and favour augmentation over occupational elimination.

Exact returned source excerpt

Despite representing an upper-bound estimate of exposure, we find that only the broad occupation of clerical work is highly exposed to the tech- nology with 24 per cent of clerical tasks considered highly exposed and an additional 58 percent with medium-level exposure.

Exact returned source excerpt

For the other occupational groups, the greatest share of highly ex- posed tasks oscillates between 1 and 4 per cent, and medium exposed tasks do not exceed 25 per cent.

Corpus source-specific finding

Potential effects differ sharply by country income and gender, while infrastructure limits adoption.

Exact returned source excerpt

The potential employment effects, whether augmenting or automating, vary widely across coun- try income groups, due to different occupational structures.

Exact returned source excerpt

In low-income countries, only 0.4 per cent of total employment is potentially exposed to automation effects, whereas in high-income countries the share rises to 5.5 percent.

Corpus source-specific finding

GPT shifts near-term automation exposure toward knowledge work.

Exact returned source excerpt

While early studies of potential AI adoption identified low-skill, repetitive and routine jobs as those with the highest potential of automa- tion (e.g., McKinsey 2016; Frey and Osborne 2017), in which a computer-based system could be coupled with a machine to replace a human in manual production jobs (Autor 2015; Acemoglu and Restrepo 2020), more recent literature has highlighted the ability of Machine Learning sys- tems to improve their performance in non-routine tasks (Brynjolfsson et al., 2018; Ernst et al., 2019; Webb 2019; Lane and Saint-Martin 2021).

Exact returned source excerpt

We argue that the emergence of GPT reinforces this shifting picture, due to its refined ability to perform cognitive tasks, such as analysing text, drafting documents and messages, or searching through private repositories and the web for additional information.

Corpus source-specific finding

The refined index responds to technological advances with more granular task data.

Exact returned source excerpt

Since the release of the 2023 estimates, there have been further technological advances in GenAI, including the introduction of Large Language Models (LLMs) with expanded capabilities in image recognition and audio and video production.

Exact returned source excerpt

Moreover, many new GenAI applications have been developed as a result of the possibility of linking GenAI models with Application Programming Interface (API) tools.

SupportsLegal workflows: Immediate gains create competence risks

Open topic: Legal workflows: Immediate gains create competence risks

Corpus framing of this source: GenAI occupational exposure: job transformation, unequal risks and policy-dependent outcomes

Corpus synthesis of this source: Across the 2023 and 2025 working papers, Gmyrek and co-authors develop and refine a global, task-based measure of generative-AI exposure. They expect job transformation to dominate full occupational automation, while warning that partial automation can still reduce labour demand and worsen job quality. Their estimates describe technological potential, not observed displacement, and their recommendations emphasise worker participation, training, labour protections and country-specific analysis.

Corpus source-specific finding

The studies measure GenAI-specific task exposure rather than predict realised job losses.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our study specifically targets GenAI tools, isolating the effects of this recent family of AI from broader AI applications, such as the use of Machine Learning (ML) algorithms or image recog- nition in industrial production.

Exact returned source excerpt

By modelling the intersection of these two dimensions, one arrives at a range of possible projections of how such technology could interact with occupational tasks, leading either to their full automation through technology-driven substitution, or to a transformation, through partial automation and human-machine complementarity.

Corpus source-specific finding

The methodological contribution combines task scoring with internationally comparable employment data.

Exact returned source excerpt

Applying a similar approach to the International Standard Classification of Occupations (ISCO-08), we conduct some 25,000 high frequency API calls, fine-tuned at the level of occupational definitions, job titles, tasks and country income classifications.

Exact returned source excerpt

It uses the GPT-4 model to estimate occupational and task-level scores of ex- posure to GPT technology and subsequently links these scores to official ILO statistics to derive global employment estimates.

Corpus source-specific finding

The 2025 refinement adds worker and expert assessments and replaces broad outcome categories with four exposure gradients.

Exact returned source excerpt

The first step in our analysis is based on Poland’s national 6-digit classification of occupations that includes nearly 30,000 tasks.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

The 2025 estimates lower overall exposure while retaining pronounced gender and income differences.

Exact returned source excerpt

Overall, the revised global employment estimates of GenAI exposure are lower than in our 2023 paper, though still, one in four jobs globally (24%) has some degree of exposure, and one in three jobs in high-income countries.

Exact returned source excerpt

3.3% of global employment falls into the highest exposure category, albeit with significant differences between female (4.7%) and male employment (2.4%).

Corpus source-specific finding

Practical experience lowers some clerical scores, while multimodal and agentic capabilities raise exposure in digitised professions.

Exact returned source excerpt

Clerical oc- cupations continue to have the highest exposure levels, albeit with some important downward revisions to individual tasks scores.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors warn that job quality and international inequality may matter as much as aggregate employment totals.

Exact returned source excerpt

Moreover, the likely ramifications on job quality might be of greater consequence than the quantitative impacts, both with respect to the new jobs created because of the technology, but also the potential effects on work intensity and autonomy when the technology is integrat- ed into the workplace.

Exact returned source excerpt

While the technology can allow the more routine tasks that one does to be automated, potentially leaving time for more engaging work, it can also be implemented in a way that limits workers agency or acceler- ates work intensity.

Corpus source-specific finding

The recommended response combines human-centred adoption, binding workplace rules and participatory governance.

Exact returned source excerpt

Ultimately, we argue that in the realm of work, generative AI is neither inherently good nor bad, and that its socioeconomic impacts will largely depend on how its diffusion is managed.

Exact returned source excerpt

The questions of power balance, voice of the workers affected by labour market adjustments, respect for existing norms and rights, and adequate use of national social protection and skills training systems will be crucial elements for managing AI’s deployment in the workplace.

Corpus framing of this source: Generative AI job quality depends on worker voice and enforceable protections

Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied ILO excerpts argue that AI augmentation can improve work or intensify managerial control, depending on implementation and worker participation. They anticipate demand for microtask labour, identify protection gaps in platform work, and recommend workplace consultation and labour regulation developed jointly by workers, employers and governments.

Corpus source-specific finding

Augmentation does not guarantee better jobs.

Exact returned source excerpt

While the technology can allow the more routine tasks that one does to be automated, potentially leaving time for more engaging work, it can also be implemented in a way that limits workers agency or acceler- ates work intensity.

Exact returned source excerpt

In warehouses an automated, “voice-picking” system directs warehouse staff to pick certain products in the warehouse, while using data collection to monitor workers and set the pace of work (Matopoulos 2011).

Corpus source-specific finding

Worker participation shapes implementation and acceptance of technology.

Exact returned source excerpt

As a result, whether the effect of technology on working conditions is positive or negative depends in large part on the voice that workers have in the design, implementation and use of technology.

Exact returned source excerpt

This can take place either through formalized set- tings, such as works councils or guidance provided in collective bargaining agreements, or less formally, in workplaces where there is a high degree of employee engagement, such as in organ- izational structures that support teamwork, problem-solving and decentralized decision-making (ILO 2023c).

Corpus source-specific finding

Workplace consultation needs legal safeguards against unequal power.

Exact returned source excerpt

In addition to consultation at the workplace, there is also need for laws that regulate AI’s appli- cation at the workplace.

Exact returned source excerpt

To date, much of the discussion on regulation of AI has ignored its pos- sible effects on working conditions (Moore 2023).

Corpus source-specific finding

Tripartite governance should make regulation participatory and adaptable.

Exact returned source excerpt

The design and application of such regulations is best crafted through tripartite systems, in which workers’, employers’ and governments representatives engage with equal voice.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

This dramatic decrease in the cost and ease of entry to the LLM market points to an increase in de- mand for domain-specific labelled datasets curated by microtask workers.
