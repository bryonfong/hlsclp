Corpus framing of this source: FTC challenge to DoNotPay’s “robot lawyer” claims and resulting compliance order

Corpus synthesis of this source: The FTC complaint characterises DoNotPay’s human-lawyer, website-diagnostics and subscription-feature claims as false, misleading or unsubstantiated. It supports that position with evidence about the system’s technology, training and testing, attorney involvement, consumer complaints and actual feature capabilities. The final order restricts future professional-performance claims and imposes recordkeeping, reporting and monitoring duties. The supplied material offers no predictions; it sets binding requirements rather than recommendations.

Corpus source-specific finding

The FTC says DoNotPay marketed its service as a human-like “robot lawyer” across major digital channels.

Exact returned source excerpt

Through the Apple App Store, the Google Play Store, the website donotpay.com, and social media accounts such as on X.com and TikTok, Respondent has disseminated or caused to be disseminated advertisements for the DoNotPay Service that include the statements and depictions in Exhibits A through L to this Complaint.

Exact returned source excerpt

Respondent’s advertisements called the DoNotPay Service the world’s first robot lawyer, capable of suing anyone at the press of a button.

The FTC classifies the human-lawyer performance representations as false, misleading or unsubstantiated when made.

Corpus source-specific finding

The FTC bases its substantiation case on gaps in legal training, quality testing and expert verification.

Exact returned source excerpt

DoNotPay did not test whether the Service’s law-related features operated like a human lawyer.

Exact returned source excerpt

DoNotPay has developed the Service based on technologies that included a natural language processing model for recognizing statistical relationships between words, chatbot software for conversing with users, and an Application Programming Interface (“API”) with OpenAI’s ChatGPT.

Corpus source-specific finding

Consumer complaints provided additional evidence of incomplete legal intake and unusable outputs.

Exact returned source excerpt

DoNotPay subscribers who used the law-related features have complained that the Service did not ask them to submit information relevant to their breach of contract claims, failed to consider important legal issues, and generated legal documents that were not fit for use.

Corpus source-specific finding

The FTC says DoNotPay’s website-diagnostics and included-feature claims did not match the service’s actual capabilities.

Exact returned source excerpt

DoNotPay has promoted a website diagnostic feature of the Service that purportedly would check a consumer’s small business website for hundreds of federal and state law violations based solely on the consumer’s email address.

Exact returned source excerpt

This feature purportedly would detect legal violations that, if unaddressed, would potentially cost a consumer $125,000 in legal fees.
