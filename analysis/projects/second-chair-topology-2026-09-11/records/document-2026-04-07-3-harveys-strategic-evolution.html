Open topic: Agentic infrastructure: Harvey tests vertical vendor durability

Corpus framing of this source: Harvey’s platform strategy and the unresolved challenge of everyday adoption

Corpus synthesis of this source: Anthea Roberts and David Wilkins interpret Harvey as an emerging infrastructure and ecosystem business that could reposition law firms within professional services, rather than merely a legal chatbot. Their 2026 essay and May update combine reported product, commercial and adoption developments with strategic hypotheses. Neither author has used Harvey, and they leave its product quality, sustained usage, competitive defensibility and broader economic effects unresolved.

## Building for the Third Path In our [previous piece](https://clp.law.harvard.edu/ai-and-the-legal-profession/essays/law-is-the-gateway-drug.html), we described three paths that technology creates for any profession: doing the same thing cheaper and faster, expanding demand through lower costs, or doing fundamentally different things.

Exact returned source excerpt

But Harvey's outer rings — the platform, Shared Spaces, and the ecosystem play — are infrastructure for the third path: the one where law's role in professional services gets redefined, not just accelerated.

Corpus source-specific finding

Purchasing does not establish usage-level product-market fit; engagement may be sharply bimodal.

Exact returned source excerpt

a16z's own analysis acknowledged that Harvey is "there and available" at major firms but "not heavily used yet." The simple explanation is that lawyers are technology averse and have been doing things the same way for a long time.

Exact returned source excerpt

Harvey hasn't yet created the kind of pull where the tool reshapes how you work rather than slotting into how you already work — the kind of pull that is an unmistakeable sign of product-market fit at the use level rather than the purchasing level.

Corpus source-specific finding

The May 2026 update strengthens the ecosystem interpretation without proving widespread engagement.

Exact returned source excerpt

**Harvey moved further into the ecosystem-governor role.** On 5 May 2026, Harvey launched a library of 500-plus purpose-built legal agents, an Agent Builder that lets firms create custom agents in plain English, and a public benchmark called Legal Agent Bench (LAB) — 1,200-plus tasks across 24 practice areas, evaluated by 75,000-plus expert-written rubric criteria.

Exact returned source excerpt

Owning the benchmark is a classic ecosystem-governor move — whoever defines what "good" looks like in the category shapes what everyone else builds toward.

Corpus source-specific finding

Competitors and direct foundation-model partnerships make Harvey’s defensibility uncertain.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

Both arguments have force, and the second of them — the question of whether vertical legal AI can hold its position as the underlying foundation models become more capable and more direct in their pursuit of the legal market — is one we genuinely don't know the answer to.

Corpus source-specific finding

Ecosystem expansion must ultimately rest on active use, and may otherwise disguise unresolved product-market fit.

Exact returned source excerpt

It has to provide enough value within the existing framework of what lawyers do — the cheaper-and-faster path, the governance and trust that gets it adopted — while simultaneously trying to move the best lawyers and the most forward-thinking firms into the next phase of what professional services is and where law fits into that.

Exact returned source excerpt

You can't govern an ecosystem people aren't actively using — and the adoption gap remains real.
