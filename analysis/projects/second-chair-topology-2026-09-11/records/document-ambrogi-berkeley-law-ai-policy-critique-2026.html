Corpus framing of this source: Ambrogi: broad AI restrictions risk leaving graduates unprepared to supervise AI

Corpus synthesis of this source: Bob Ambrogi’s 2026 LawSites article critiques Berkeley Law’s restrictions on student AI use while reporting the school’s rationale and exceptions. He accepts the need to protect foundational analytical skills but argues that supervised classroom practice should also teach students to verify AI output. His warning about future client harm is a prospective argument, not a measured outcome.

Corpus source-specific finding

Berkeley restricts AI throughout graded work but permits specified exceptions and other uses.

Exact returned source excerpt

| LawSites ** UC Berkeley School of Law has adopted one of the most restrictive student AI policies of any top law school, barring the use of generative AI for nearly every step of producing graded work — and prohibiting it outright in any exam.

Exact returned source excerpt

The rule, which [the school released last week](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/AI-Final-Policy-26.pdf), forbids the use of AI for “conceptualizing, outlining, drafting, revising, translating, or editing any work submitted for credit.” It also bars students from uploading course materials — assignments, readings, slides, class recordings — into generative AI systems, and limits permitted research use to the narrow task of identifying sources such as cases, statutes or secondary materials.
