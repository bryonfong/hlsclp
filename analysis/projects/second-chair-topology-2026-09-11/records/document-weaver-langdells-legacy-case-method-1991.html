Corpus framing of this source: Langdell’s case method: intellectual training, institutional acceptance and an enduring pedagogical legacy

Corpus synthesis of this source: Langdell's Legacy: Living with the Case Method (1991) traces the shift from apprenticeship and lectures to case-based university education. The article argues that teaching methods have not sufficiently evolved with changing understandings of law, while documenting the case method’s intellectual aims, contested reception and practical successes. Its supplied excerpts offer historical evidence and criticism, but no detailed reform programme.

Corpus framing of this source: Weaver on the case method’s legacy, limits and reform

Corpus synthesis of this source: In Langdell's Legacy: Living with the Case Method (1991), Russell L. Weaver combines historical accounts and pedagogical argument to explain the case method’s dominance and assess its educational value. He credits its development of independent analysis but argues that excessive repetition and doctrinal emphasis obscure lawyers’ practical roles. He recommends more varied teaching and integration of lawyering into substantive courses, rather than wholesale replacement by lectures.

Corpus framing of this source: Langdell’s legacy: retain case analysis, but teach the work of lawyering

Corpus synthesis of this source: In this 1991 article, the author examines why the case method persists, recognises its analytical benefits, and argues that legal education too often obscures how lawyers shape facts, arguments and outcomes. Drawing on historical commentary, teaching experience and a classroom questionnaire, the article recommends integrating lawyering into substantive courses, changing assessment incentives and using complementary teaching methods.
