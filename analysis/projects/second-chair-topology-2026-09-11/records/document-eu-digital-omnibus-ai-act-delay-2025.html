Corpus framing of this source: Digital Omnibus on AI: delayed high-risk rules, compliance simplification and contested safeguards

Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied Commission proposal and EPRS briefing explain the rationale for simplifying AI Act implementation, trace changes through the reported 2026 legislative agreement, and catalogue stakeholder disagreements. The Commission advocates innovation-friendly compliance; EPRS reports legislative developments and others’ views rather than presenting them as its own recommendations or an official parliamentary position.

Corpus source-specific finding

The Commission frames simplification as a response to implementation delays, within the AI Act’s innovation and rights-protection objectives.

Exact returned source excerpt

These amendments seek to address implementation issues and reduce the regulatory burden arising from the AI Act.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, its timely application has faced delays, particularly regarding the designation of national competent authorities and the publication of harmonised standards and compliance tools for high- risk AI requirements.

Corpus source-specific finding

The reported agreement replaces standards-dependent postponement with fixed high-risk application dates.

Exact returned source excerpt

Digital Omnibus on AI The main changes proposed are as follows: • Application timeline of high-risk AI rules: the Commission proposes to amend the date of application of high-risk AI rules (initially 2 August 2026), by linking the date of application to the availability of harmonised standards or other tools supporting compliance with the AI Act.

Exact returned source excerpt

Once the Commission confirms the availability of such tools, high-risk rules would apply six months afterwards for Annex III systems (by 2 December 2027 at the latest) and 12 months afterwards for Annex I systems (by 2 August 2028 at the latest).

Corpus source-specific finding

Negotiators restored selected safeguards while adding prohibited practices and delaying content marking.

Exact returned source excerpt

• Obligation for registration: providers would no longer have to register AI systems in the EU database if they conclude that these systems are not high-risk due to their narrow or procedural tasks.

Exact returned source excerpt

·facilitating compliance with the data protection laws by allowing providers and deployers of all AI systems and models to process special categories of personal data for ensuring bias detection and correction, with the appropriate safeguards; ·a broader use of AI regulatory sandboxes and real-world testing, that will benefit European key industries such as the automotive industry, and facilitating an EU-level AI regulatory sandbox which the AI Office will set up as from 2028; ·targeted changes clarifying the interplay between the AI Act and other EU legislation and adjusting the AI Act’s procedures to improve its overall implementation and operation.

Corpus source-specific finding

The proposal reduces procedural burdens and changes responsibility for AI literacy.

Exact returned source excerpt

• Single application for conformity assessment: conformity assessment bodies that apply for a designation would be able to submit a single application and undergo a single assessment procedure.

Exact returned source excerpt

• Post-market monitoring: the requirement for a harmonised template for the post-market monitoring plan is removed.

Corpus source-specific finding

Enforcement proposals strengthen the AI Office but preserve debate over national and sectoral responsibilities.

Exact returned source excerpt

• Centralised enforcement: the EU's AI Office would supervise the compliance of AI systems integrated into very large online platforms or search engines, 2 as well as the compliance of AI systems based on general-purpose AI models where the system and model are provided by the same entity.

Exact returned source excerpt

In addition, the Council clarified the responsibilities of the AI Office regarding the supervision of AI systems built on general-purpose AI models, specifying that this applies where the model and the system are developed by the same provider or by providers that are part of the same undertaking.

Corpus framing of this source: Digital Omnibus on AI: delayed high-risk rules, simplified compliance and stronger EU oversight

Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied Commission proposal argues that implementation bottlenecks justify postponing and simplifying parts of the AI Act while preserving substantive protections. The accompanying EPRS briefing reports projected savings, disputes over safeguards and legislative scrutiny, and the 2026 agreement. Proposed provisions and reported agreement outcomes remain distinct below.

Corpus source-specific finding

The Commission frames simplification as a response to implementation failures, not a reduction in protection.

Exact returned source excerpt

These include delays in designating national competent authorities and conformity assessment bodies, as well as a lack of harmonised standards for the AI Act’s high-risk requirements, guidance, and compliance tools.

Exact returned source excerpt

Such delays risk significantly increasing the compliance costs for businesses and public authorities and slowing down innovation.
