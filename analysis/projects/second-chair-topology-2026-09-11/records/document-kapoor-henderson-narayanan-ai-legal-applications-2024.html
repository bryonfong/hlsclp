Corpus synthesis of this source: In this 2024 position paper, Sayash Kapoor, Peter Henderson and Arvind Narayanan argue that current evidence does not establish that AI will redefine legal practice. They distinguish information processing, expert reasoning and judgment, and future prediction; examine why common evaluations overstate capability; and recommend contextual assessments, verification and narrowly bounded assistive uses.

Corpus source-specific finding

Information processing offers evaluable, mostly incremental improvements for legal experts.

Exact returned source excerpt

This is because: • There is generally a clear correct answer: given information about the features used in the model and the model’s output, it is easy to determine if the model’s output is correct, such as in the task of categorizing legal requests by area of law [48].2 • There is high observability of the features relevant for decision making: The features relevant for using AI for information-processing tasks are available as inputs to the AI system.

Exact returned source excerpt

For these reasons, while large language models offer improvements over existing tools — possibly in terms of accuracy but especially in terms of cost, by decreasing the amount of task-specific software development required — they do not drastically change legal information processing for experts.
