Corpus framing of this source: CPSU submission on government AI oversight, bias and public trust

Corpus synthesis of this source: The CPSU argues that government AI can reproduce errors and amplify bias at speed and scale, while automation bias may lead people to trust flawed outputs. Drawing on a May 2024 member survey and citing Digital Rights Watch, it recommends explainable decision-making, properly resourced human oversight, citizen-centric safeguards, worker consultation, independent social-impact review and continuing risk assessment. It frames these measures as necessary to maintain confidence in government decisions and public trust.

### Threat of algorithmic bias and error The ways in which AI systems can cause harm are not always predictable and can occur at an alarming speed and scale.

Exact returned source excerpt

More complex than automated decision-making processes, algorithms learn from human inputs and can perpetuate and exacerbate bias in decision-making and outcomes to a much greater extent.

Further, 'automation bias' can cause individuals to defer to or place greater trust in algorithmic decision-making than independent decision-making.

> "At all stages human oversight is needed to review the AI output and ensure it is not perpetuating biases and is factually accurate." The government should adopt robust citizen-centric frameworks that ensure ethical and effective measures for any implementation of AI in order to mitigate this risk.

Given the risks are dynamic, changing with new inputs and data or when integrated into a new environment, a vigorous risk-based approach with regular monitoring and assessments is required to account for such changes.
