Corpus framing of this source: Evaluating General-Purpose AI with Psychometrics

Corpus synthesis of this source: The paper argues that predefined task benchmarks cannot adequately evaluate versatile general-purpose AI. It proposes construct-oriented evaluation grounded in psychometric theory to predict performance on unforeseen tasks, explain capability patterns and impose stronger reliability and validity standards. It supports the approach with findings from human psychometrics and cross-model factor analysis, while warning that human psychological tests cannot simply be transferred to AI and identifying unresolved questions about how psychometric concepts apply to models.

Corpus source-specific finding

Rigorous AI evaluation serves safety, fairness, scientific understanding and resource-allocation goals.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the rapidly evolving field of artificial intelligence (AI), rigorous evaluation of AI systems allows for effective mitigation of risks associated with the AI systems and deepened understanding of their under- lying technologies [1].

Exact returned source excerpt

It is crucial for preventing devastating outcomes in high-stake applications like autonomous driving and medical diagnosis [2], eliminating risks such as the propagation of racist, sex- ist, ableist, extremist, and other harmful ideologies [3], and avoiding the reinforcement of biases against marginalized communities [4, 5].
