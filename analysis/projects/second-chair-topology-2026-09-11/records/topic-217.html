Professional independence: Regulation should follow client vulnerability

Corpus synthesis: Allocates authority by harm and organizational conditions, rejecting one regulator or practice setting as universally superior.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Native topic findings

  1. Professional independence is an institutional achievement, not an automatic consequence of self-regulation or market success.
  2. Allocate regulatory authority by client vulnerability and type of harm, rather than select one regulator for all legal practice.
  3. Disciplinary patterns reveal uneven coverage, but do not by themselves establish bias or superior corporate-lawyer ethics.
  4. Corporate gatekeeping requires authority and reciprocal obligations across the client–counsel relationship.
  5. Evidence supports attention to organizational conditions, but does not establish a universally superior practice setting or regulatory intervention.
  6. Context-sensitive regulation faces objections about rule content, fragmentation and cumulative enforcement.
  7. Reform proposals range from coordinated oversight to redrawing professional boundaries, with important unresolved trade-offs.
  8. The professional-function arguments inform regulatory design but do not establish claims about AI capability or justify monopoly by themselves.
