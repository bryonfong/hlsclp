Native topic findings

  1. The cluster frames expertise as accumulated, domain-specific skill acquisition rather than simply time served.
  2. Musician comparisons associate accomplishment with accumulated practice, without establishing a universal hours threshold.
  3. Measured performance supports a domain-specific account more directly than a general cognitive-motor advantage.
  4. Talent, reporting error and contrary findings qualify the practice-based explanation.
  5. The practical implication is to organise sustained, relevant practice rather than count experience alone; application beyond music remains an extrapolation.

Corpus framing of this source: Deliberate practice and musical expertise: associations, mechanisms and limits

Corpus synthesis of this source: Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Römer link musical expertise to sustained, accumulated deliberate practice. Their violinist comparisons and pianist experiments support domain-specific skill acquisition rather than a general speed advantage, while leaving talent–practice relationships unresolved and acknowledging measurement limitations.
