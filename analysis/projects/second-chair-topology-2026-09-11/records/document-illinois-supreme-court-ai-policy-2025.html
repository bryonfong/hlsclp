Open topic: Court governance: Assistive use retains human accountability

Corpus framing of this source: Illinois Supreme Court: authorized AI use with human accountability and safeguards

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Illinois Supreme Court Policy on Artificial Intelligence, effective 1 January 2025, authorizes legally and ethically compliant AI use without requiring disclosure in pleadings. It presents efficiency and access-to-justice benefits as potential gains, not measured findings, and sets requirements for verification, competence, confidentiality and continuing human responsibility.

Corpus source-specific finding

Authorize compliant AI use without requiring disclosure in pleadings.

Exact returned source excerpt

The use of AI by litigants, attorneys, judges, judicial clerks, research attorneys, and court staff providing similar support may be expected, should not be discouraged, and is authorized provided it complies with legal and ethical standards.

Exact returned source excerpt

Disclosure of AI use should not be required in a pleading.

Corpus source-specific finding

Require human verification and tool understanding while retaining professional accountability.

Exact returned source excerpt

All users must thoroughly review AI-generated content before submitting it in any court proceeding to ensure accuracy and compliance with legal and ethical obligations.

Exact returned source excerpt

Prior to employing any technology, including generative AI applications, users must understand both general AI capabilities and the specific tools being utilized.

Corpus source-specific finding

Balance potential benefits against threats to judicial integrity and litigants' rights.

Exact returned source excerpt

The integration of AI with the courts is increasingly pervasive, offering potential efficiencies and improved access to justice.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt
