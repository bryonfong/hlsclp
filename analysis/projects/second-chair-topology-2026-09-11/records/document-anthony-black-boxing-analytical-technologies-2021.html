Corpus framing of this source: Black boxing analytical technologies in knowledge work

Corpus synthesis of this source: Callen Anthony reports a two-year inductive ethnography of four investment-bank groups. The study distinguishes two ways of validating technology-produced financial analyses and argues that partitioning work by seniority can foster black boxing, whereas co-construction supports awareness of algorithms. It links black boxing to altered knowledge outputs, weaker junior expertise development and career consequences. The supplied abstract offers no explicit recommendations or predictions.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study identifies two organisational paths for validating technology-produced financial analysis.

Exact returned source excerpt

I conducted a two-year inductive ethnographic study of the use of analytical technologies across four groups in an investment bank and found two distinct paths that these groups used to validate financial analyses through what I call "validating practices": actions that confirm whether a produced analysis is trustworthy.

Corpus source-specific finding

Validation does not necessarily entail understanding the technology’s calculations.

Exact returned source excerpt

Surprisingly, engaging in these practices does not necessarily equate to understanding the calculations performed by the technologies.

Exact returned source excerpt

Knowledge workers are generally considered experts who develop deep understanding of their tools, but recent observations suggest that in some situations, they may black box their analytical technologies, meaning they trust their tools without understanding how they work.

Corpus source-specific finding

Partitioning and co-construction allocate analytical work differently across seniority levels.

Exact returned source excerpt

In one path, validating practices are partitioned across junior and senior roles: junior bankers engage in assembling tasks and use the analytical tools to perform analysis, while only senior bankers interpret the analysis.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the other path, junior and senior members engage in co-construction: junior bankers do both assembling and interpreting tasks, and senior bankers engage in interpreting and provide feedback on junior bankers' reasoning and choices.

Corpus source-specific finding

The two paths produce contrasting levels of algorithmic understanding.

Exact returned source excerpt

Both junior and senior bankers in the partitioning groups routinely black boxed the algorithms embedded in their technologies, taking them for granted without understanding them.

Exact returned source excerpt

By contrast, bankers in the co-construction groups were conscious of the algorithms and understood their potential impact.

Corpus source-specific finding

Black boxing affects work products and junior bankers’ professional development.

Exact returned source excerpt

I found that black boxing influenced the knowledge outputs of these bankers and constrained the development of junior members' expertise, with consequences for their career trajectories.
