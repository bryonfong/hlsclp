Malpractice insurance: Reported AI claims remain hard to isolate

Corpus synthesis: Places emerging claims within broader loss trends and disputed coverage, where sanctions and insurer concern do not measure incidence.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Native topic findings

  1. AI malpractice has entered reported claims experience, but its incidence and contribution to rising losses remain uncertain.
  2. AI exposure sits within a broader malpractice market already facing severe losses and rising defense costs.
  3. Court sanctions demonstrate misconduct, not insured malpractice losses, and insurance generally does not reimburse punishment.
  4. The central coverage disagreement concerns whether unchecked AI use remains ordinary insured negligence.
  5. Exclusions and affirmative AI products coexist, making policy wording more informative than broad claims about market direction.
  6. Insurance underwriting may turn AI governance into a commercial requirement, but its pricing effects remain largely predictive.
  7. Immature loss histories constrain actuarial pricing and reserving even as insurers respond to emerging exposure.
  8. The prevailing practical proposition favors controlled adoption, not AI avoidance or transfer of professional responsibility to technology.
