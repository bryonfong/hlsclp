Corpus synthesis of this source: Artificial Wisdom? A Potential Limit on AI in Law (and Elsewhere) develops a tentative philosophical argument that even artificial superintelligence may lack the first-person perspective needed for moral judgment. It distinguishes practical, conscious deliberation from debates about whether free will objectively exists, suggesting that this distinction could preserve a human role in law.

Corpus framing of this source: Artificial Wisdom? A Potential Limit on AI in Law (and Elsewhere)

Corpus synthesis of this source: The essay offers a conceptual, rather than empirical, account of a possible boundary on AI’s role in law. It predicts that artificial superintelligence could surpass humans in factual, technical and persuasive legal work, but argues that an inability to select ultimate ends or make moral judgments may preserve a human role. It separately supports consumer-benefiting competition from legal AI while warning that economy-wide automation could require radical changes in distribution.

Corpus framing of this source: Artificial Wisdom? Moral judgment as a potential limit on legal AI

Corpus synthesis of this source: Joshua P. Davis’s 2019 essay develops a conditional philosophical argument for an enduring human role in law, even if artificial superintelligence surpasses human factual reasoning. It draws on jurisprudence and debates about consciousness, free will and personal identity, without claiming to prove that machines cannot acquire subjectivity. It also distinguishes this possible limit from protection against economic displacement.

Corpus source-specific finding

The proposed limit depends on three contested premises, not a demonstrated technological barrier.

Exact returned source excerpt

This Essay suggests three propositions may hold true that would justify an ongoing—perhaps permanent—role for human beings: (1) that moral judgment is necessary for legal and judicial practice; (2) that the first-person perspective (or subjectivity) is necessary for moral judgment; and (3) that AI is incapable of attaining the first-person perspective.

Exact returned source excerpt

This Essay does not make a full version of any of those arguments.
