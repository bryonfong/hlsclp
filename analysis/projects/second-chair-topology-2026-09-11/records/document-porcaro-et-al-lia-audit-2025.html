Corpus framing of this source: LIA audit identifies unactionable guidance and missing legal-help links

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Audit Report for Legal Information Assistant (LIA) documents terse child-custody guidance, missing referrals to existing resources and an eviction answer that appears mismatched to the user's question. Session counts support its criticism of practical usefulness; a small synthetic prompting experiment suggests a possible improvement rather than establishing a general remedy.

Corpus source-specific finding

Child-custody answers often fail to give users actionable next steps.

Exact returned source excerpt

Of the 1,031 sessions related to child custody, over half (560) started with LIA’s suggested question: How do I get custody of my child?

Exact returned source excerpt

In 310 of those sessions, this was LIA’s complete response: To get custody of your child, you need to fill out a form and give it to the court.

Corpus source-specific finding

LIA rarely connects custody users to available resources and never links directly to LANC's custody packet.

Exact returned source excerpt

But of the over 1,000 sessions related to child custody, just 110 included a link to a NC Courts page.

Exact returned source excerpt

By contrast, 143 sessions included some kind of refusal from LIA.

Corpus framing of this source: Early independent audit of Legal Aid of North Carolina’s Legal Information Assistant

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Duke Center on Law & Technology presents a time-bound, product-level audit of LIA’s early deployment. It combines manual review of session data and synthetic testing to document usage, answer quality, guardrail failures, language access, accessibility, privacy, safety and feedback issues; recommends technical and governance improvements; and cautions against treating the findings as either a final verdict on LIA or a general assessment of large language models. The foreword separately praises LANC’s early auditing, conservative design and iterative governance as a model for responsible public-interest AI.

Corpus source-specific finding

The audit provides an early baseline rather than a definitive or current assessment.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our findings cover LIA interactions between launch and December 18, 2024.

Exact returned source excerpt

Methodology We manually reviewed and tagged every session in our provided dataset.

Corpus source-specific finding

Usage was brief, heavily concentrated in English and split substantially between supported and unsupported subjects.

Exact returned source excerpt

Most LIA sessions are brief: 61.7% of sessions end after one or two user messages.

Exact returned source excerpt

Nearly 90% of sessions end after 5 or fewer user messages.

Corpus source-specific finding

LIA itself shaped a large share of conversations, but its suggested questions often exposed internal inconsistencies.

Exact returned source excerpt

In total, approximately 30% of messages sent to LIA are suggested by LIA.

Exact returned source excerpt

In at least 563 instances across 357 sessions, LIA refused to answer a question it suggested.

Corpus source-specific finding

A prominent eviction answer was frequently too basic, while links were rarely used and sometimes inaccessible or broken.

Exact returned source excerpt

Approximately 1,371 sessions covered eviction- related topics.

Exact returned source excerpt

Yet more than half the time (320/575), LIA’s answer is too basic to be helpful.

Corpus source-specific finding

Off-book guardrails operated inconsistently and could blur responsibility for unvetted answers.

Exact returned source excerpt

In more than half of off-book sessions, LIA provided some information on the topic requested.

Exact returned source excerpt

This information was generated directly by LIA’s underlying foundation model and was not based on any resources that LANC provided.

Corpus source-specific finding

LIA did not adequately answer strong demand for information about LANC itself.

Exact returned source excerpt

LIA is not meeting demand for information about LANC.

Exact returned source excerpt

In general, LIA struggled to answer questions about LANC beyond providing a phone number and a link to apply online.

Corpus source-specific finding

Spanish access and performance were materially weaker than English access and performance.

Exact returned source excerpt

Of the 6,395 user sessions, just 67 (1%) were in Spanish.

Exact returned source excerpt

This suggests that Spanish speakers are struggling to find and use LIA.

Privacy and confidentiality protections required substantial strengthening.

Exact returned source excerpt

Moreover, it is not clear that LIA’s confidentiality disclaimer is sufficient to protect people who use LIA.

Exact returned source excerpt

For another, LIA users may have conflicts with LANC clients, and may disclose personal or case information that is viewable by LANC staff.

Limited evidence suggested appropriate crisis responses, with possible need for stronger protocols as use grows.

Exact returned source excerpt

In a small sample, LIA appropriately responds to imminent harm cases.

Exact returned source excerpt

We found very few sessions in the dataset where the person using LIA exhibited a risk a imminent harm to themselves or to others.

LIA addressed only a fraction of legal-information demand, while LANC’s broader resource navigation also impeded access.
