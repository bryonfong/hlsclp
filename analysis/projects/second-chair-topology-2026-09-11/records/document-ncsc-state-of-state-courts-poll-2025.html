Corpus framing of this source: State of the State Courts 2025: Public confidence, access barriers and emerging risks

Corpus synthesis of this source: NCSC and GBAO Strategies contribute a national survey of registered voters on confidence in state courts, perceived equality, information sources, AI, judicial security and access to justice. The source distinguishes respondents’ views from NCSC’s interpretation and reform recommendations, while limiting the findings to a national snapshot rather than state-level estimates.

Corpus source-specific finding

Confidence in state courts remains comparatively high and stable.

Exact returned source excerpt

This year's survey finds that 62% of respondents express a great deal or some trust in our state court system.

Exact returned source excerpt

Last year's survey placed that number at 63%, and the 2023 figure was 61%, suggesting that public trust in state courts is stable.

Corpus source-specific finding

Confidence coexists with a growing perception that justice is unequal.

Exact returned source excerpt

Over the decade we've been conducting this annual survey, we have asked how well state courts provide "equal justice to all." The percentage of Americans agreeing that state courts provide equal justice has slipped from 62% in 2014 to 44% this year.

Corpus source-specific finding

Younger voters assess state courts more positively than older voters.

Exact returned source excerpt

The 18-29 age cohort is considerably sunnier in its views of state courts than any other age group.

Exact returned source excerpt

Generation Z voters are three points more likely to agree that state courts are fair and impartial; six points more likely to agree that state courts are transparent and accountable; and nine points more likely to agree that state courts are both innovative and hardworking.
