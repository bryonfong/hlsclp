Corpus framing of this source: Clifford Chance’s risk-led path to firmwide generative AI and Copilot

Corpus synthesis of this source: Caroline Hill reports how Clifford Chance’s IT, risk and innovation leaders explain the firm’s early, large-scale adoption of generative AI. They attribute the rollout to years of technical preparation, early use-case governance, data controls, client engagement, mandatory training and strategic alignment with Microsoft—not to a sudden decision. The account provides implementation experience and selection reasoning rather than measured evidence of productivity or financial returns.

Corpus source-specific finding

Long-term technical preparation preceded the apparently rapid rollout.

Exact returned source excerpt

As with a lot of overnight decisions, it was years in the making, say the IT and risk heads that led the way.* In February this year, Clifford Chance became one of the first organisations – not just law firms – to decide to deploy Microsoft Copilot and Viva Suite globally; a huge investment that felt to some far too quick, as the industry struggled to get to grips with not just the governance of Gen AI but also the cost implications.

Exact returned source excerpt

But here Clifford Chance was being feted by Microsoft for "taking its digital transformation journey to the next level, as one of the first organisations globally to deploy both Copilot for Microsoft 365 and Viva Suite at scale across its entire workforce." Both Microsoft and Clifford Chance itself pointed out that this February 2024 announcement followed the deployment in 2023 of Clifford Chance Assist, developed on Microsoft's Azure OpenAI platform, which was rolled out to the entire firm after trials with over 1800 users.
