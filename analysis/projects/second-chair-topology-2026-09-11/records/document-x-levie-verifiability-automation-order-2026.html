Corpus framing of this source: Verifiability, not difficulty, determines automation order

Corpus synthesis of this source: Aaron Levie argues that objective and scalable verification can make technically difficult work easier to automate than judgment-intensive knowledge work. He predicts that less-verifiable domains will require applied-AI systems, redesigned processes and new ways to test work over time, rather than relying on model capability alone.

Corpus source-specific finding

Levie’s principal claim is that verifiability, rather than task difficulty or value, will shape which work AI automates first.

Exact returned source excerpt

# Verifiability, not difficulty, sets the order of automation *Aaron Levie — X (@levie) (2026)* We're going to be in for a strange dynamic which is that some of the "hardest" work in the world is actually prone to automation first, particularly due to its verifiability.

Exact returned source excerpt

Math, cyber, and code -while being insanely hard and high value fields- have the benefit of being able to be tested that it's correct objectively.

Corpus source-specific finding

Objective verification supports both model training and dependable deployment.

Exact returned source excerpt

This has two immediate benefits: the training of the models offers clearer reward signals, and then the running of the models allows you to know that it's working properly because you can test the results in a scalable way.

Corpus source-specific finding

Judgment-intensive domains resist immediate verification because outcomes depend on context, preferences, risk tolerance and delayed feedback.

Exact returned source excerpt

Conversely, in other domains of work, there's much less instant verifiability.

Exact returned source excerpt

Which legal clauses your client will agree to, what marketing campaign to run with based on changing sentiment, which message your sales prospect will want to hear, what financial targets and budget to set for a business, and so on.

Corpus source-specific finding

Increasing model capability alone will not deliver the full gains from automation in less-verifiable knowledge work.

Exact returned source excerpt

The implications of this distinction are that -even as model capability continues to increase exponentially- there will be a lot done at the applied AI layer than just the the model itself, and much of the processes themselves will even need to change over time to get the full gains from automation.

Exact returned source excerpt

We may even need all new capabilities to be able to "test" knowledge work over time as we have had with software.
