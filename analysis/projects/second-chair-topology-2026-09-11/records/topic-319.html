Native topic findings

  1. AI-assisted learning may raise average performance without equalizing outcomes: interaction skills and structured guidance shape who benefits.
  2. Randomized access to ChatGPT produced higher short-term exam scores, but the setting limits generalization.
  3. Differential attrition qualifies the score advantage and does not establish why LLM access encouraged persistence.
  4. The evidence links novice gains to AI interaction competence, but does not show that novices generally benefit more.
  5. Preferences cannot substitute for competence assessment when targeting AI training.
  6. Conceptual roadmaps offer a promising but weakly supported complement to AI access.

Generative AI and the Productivity Divide: Human-AI Complementarities in Education and Knowledge Work

Academia & research · Current wave (2026)

Corpus framing of this source: LLM self-study improves average scores, while guidance and competence shape equitable benefits

Corpus synthesis of this source: Generative AI and the Productivity Divide: Human-AI Complementarities in Education and Knowledge Work reports a randomized student self-study experiment framed as a model for workplace upskilling. It finds higher scores and lower attrition with LLM access, cautions that preferences do not reliably indicate competence, and presents weak statistical evidence that conceptual roadmaps help novices. The supplied evidence concerns short-term learning about LLMs, rather than direct workplace productivity.
