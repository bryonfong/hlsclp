Corpus framing of this source: Architectural innovation can turn incumbent expertise into a barrier to adaptation

Corpus synthesis of this source: Architectural Innovation: The Reconfiguration of Existing Product Technologies and the Failure of Established Firms uses photolithographic equipment history to explain how changes in component relationships can undermine established firms despite strong engineering skills and substantial investment. The authors distinguish failures to recognise new architectures from failures to implement them, while acknowledging other causes of commercial decline and limits to their organisational assumptions.

Corpus framing of this source: Architectural Innovation: The Reconfiguration of Existing Product Technologies and the Failure of Established Firms

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source argues that the radical–incremental distinction misses innovations that alter relationships among existing components. Through photolithographic-alignment cases, it explains how established firms’ accumulated knowledge, organizational structures and communication routines can obstruct recognition and implementation of such architectural changes, contributing to lost market leadership. It qualifies this account as bounded-rationality-based and approximate, acknowledges other causes of decline and calls for further research on history, culture and organizational information structures.

Corpus source-specific finding
