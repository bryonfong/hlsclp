Court adoption: Assistance must preserve judicial authority

Native topic findings

  1. Judicial AI adoption raises a governance question: how to automate and assist court work without transferring judicial authority.
  2. Reported deployments show a widening range of court applications, but their maturity varies.
  3. Evidence of activity should not be read as evidence of faster, fairer or more accurate justice.
  4. The main tension concerns the criteria for success, rather than an explicit disagreement over whether courts should use AI.
  5. Human authority requires operational safeguards, including verification, disclosure and support for judicial actors.
  6. Governance arrangements differ in legal force and emphasis, limiting any claim of a single global model.
