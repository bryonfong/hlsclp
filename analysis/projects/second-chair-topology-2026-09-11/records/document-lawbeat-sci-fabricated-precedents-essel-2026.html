The observation came while a Bench of Justices P S Narasimha and Alok Aradhe set aside orders passed by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) and the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) after finding that the adjudicating authority had relied on non-existent and AI-hallucinated judicial precedents.

Corpus framing of this source: Supreme Court requires human control and verification when AI is used in adjudication

Corpus synthesis of this source: LawBeat reports that the Supreme Court set aside insolvency orders founded on fabricated AI-generated precedents, defined such reliance as a rule-of-law failure, and prescribed safeguards without prohibiting legitimate AI use.

Corpus source-specific finding

The Court held that a decision based on fake or hallucinated material is legally unsustainable because it subverts the rule of law.

Exact returned source excerpt

A decision of a Court or an adjudicating authority based on material which is fake and hallucinated is no decision at all, and it amounts to subversion of the rule of law.

Exact returned source excerpt

Such a decision is unsustainable and has to be set aside at the earliest,"* Court held.

Corpus source-specific finding

The Court endorsed AI only as an aid under complete human control.

Exact returned source excerpt

While acknowledging AI's potential to improve efficiency, it warned that unregulated reliance on the technology could gradually infiltrate legal practice and judicial decision-making, ultimately replacing independent human reasoning.

Exact returned source excerpt

Court stressed that AI should remain only an aid to adjudication, with human decision-makers retaining complete control over the process.

Corpus source-specific finding

The Court demanded independent verification of AI-generated precedents and attached professional consequences to failures.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Bench reiterated that there would be zero tolerance for the production, citation or reliance upon AI-generated precedents without independent verification.

Exact returned source excerpt

It held that citing such material amounts to professional misconduct by advocates, while judges relying on fabricated precedents commit a serious lapse.

Corpus source-specific finding

The Court limited its concern to presenting fabricated material as authentic, rather than banning legitimate AI use.

Exact returned source excerpt

Clarifying that its observations do not prohibit the legitimate use of AI, Court said its concern was limited to the presentation of fake or hallucinated material as authentic judicial precedents.

Corpus source-specific finding

The Court directed the Bar Council of India to develop an institutional response.

Exact returned source excerpt

To address the issue institutionally, Court directed the Bar Council of India to constitute a committee to examine the growing problem of AI-generated fake authorities.

Exact returned source excerpt

The committee has been asked to frame guiding principles and recommend disciplinary action against advocates who submit such fabricated material before courts.
