Corpus framing of this source: Cosgrove v. Grimes: objective care, not subjective good faith, governs attorney negligence

Corpus synthesis of this source: In Cosgrove v. Grimes, 774 S.W.2d 662 (Tex. 1989), the court rejects good faith as an excuse for attorney negligence and applies the reasonably prudent attorney standard using information available at the relevant time. The supplied excerpts describe the underlying filing errors and evidence supporting negligence findings, but do not disclose the ultimate recovery or disposition.

Corpus source-specific finding

The court rejects a subjective good-faith defense to attorney negligence.

Exact returned source excerpt

Some courts have held that if an attorney makes an error in judgment, but acted in good faith and in what the attorney believed was the client’s best interest, the attorney is not liable for malpractice.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the instant case the jury found that Grimes had acted in good faith in relying on the information Cosgrove allegedly furnished to Grimes, and the trial court rendered judgment for Grimes.

Corpus source-specific finding

The standard asks what a reasonably prudent attorney would do with the information then available.

Exact returned source excerpt

A lawyer in Texas is held to the standard of care which would be exercised by a reasonably prudent attorney.

Exact returned source excerpt

The jury must evaluate his conduct based on the information the attorney has at the time of the alleged act of negligence.
