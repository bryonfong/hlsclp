Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied Florida Bar ethics material applies existing professional duties to generative AI rather than predicting its ultimate impact. It recognises potential efficiency gains but calls for confidentiality safeguards, oversight policies, human verification, reasonable charges and compliance with ethics and advertising rules.

Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt

Confidentiality When using generative AI, a lawyer must protect the confidentiality of the client’s information as required by Rule 4-1.6 of the Rules Regulating The Florida Bar.

Exact returned source excerpt

The ethical duty of confidentiality is broad in its scope and applies to all information learned during a client’s representation, regardless of its source.

Corpus source-specific finding

Lawyers must establish AI oversight policies by analogy with nonlawyer supervision.

Exact returned source excerpt

Oversight of Generative AI While Rule 4-5.3(a) defines a nonlawyer assistant as a “a person,” many of the standards applicable to nonlawyer assistants provide useful guidance for a lawyer’s use of generative AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

First, just as a lawyer must make reasonable efforts to ensure that a law firm has policies to reasonably assure that the conduct of a nonlawyer assistant is compatible with the lawyer’s own professional obligations, a lawyer must do the same for generative AI.

Corpus source-specific finding

Human verification and ultimate professional responsibility remain with the lawyer.

Exact returned source excerpt

Second, a lawyer must review the work product of a generative AI in situations similar to those requiring review of the work of nonlawyer assistants such as paralegals.

Exact returned source excerpt

Lawyers are ultimately responsible for the work product that they create regardless of whether that work product was originally drafted or researched by a nonlawyer or generative AI.

Corpus source-specific finding

Responsible adoption also requires security precautions, technological competence and reasonable charges.

Exact returned source excerpt

Rule 4-1.6(e) also requires a lawyer to “make reasonable efforts to prevent the inadvertent or unauthorized disclosure of, or unauthorized access to, information relating to the client’s representation.” Further, a lawyer’s duty of competence requires “an understanding of the benefits and risks associated with the use of technology[.]” Rule 4-1.1, Comment.

Exact returned source excerpt

Due to these concerns, lawyers using generative AI must take reasonable precautions to protect the confidentiality of client information, develop policies for the reasonable oversight of generative AI use, ensure fees and costs are reasonable, and comply with applicable ethics and advertising regulations.
