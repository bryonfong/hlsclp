Corpus framing of this source: Hyles v. City of New York: courts cannot compel a responding party to use TAR

Corpus synthesis of this source: Magistrate Judge Andrew J. Peck denied Hyles’ request to require New York City to use technology-assisted review (TAR) rather than keyword searching. The opinion endorses TAR’s general efficiency and encourages discovery cooperation, but holds that responding parties ordinarily control their ESI-production methods, subject to reasonable and proportional results. It also applies proportionality through staged custodial searches and a limited date range, while anticipating that wider adoption could eventually make refusal to use TAR unreasonable.

Corpus source-specific finding

The court squarely held that a requesting party cannot force a responding party to use TAR merely because it prefers TAR to keyword searching.

Exact returned source excerpt

Since the search methodology issue arose in this case before the City spent much, if any, money on searching for responsive ESI, this case squarely raises the issue of whether the requesting party can have the Court force the responding party to use TAR.
