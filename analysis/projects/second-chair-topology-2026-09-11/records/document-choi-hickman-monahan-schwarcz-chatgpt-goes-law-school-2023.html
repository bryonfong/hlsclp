Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: ChatGPT Goes to Law School: Exam Performance, Educational Risks and Legal-Practice Uses

Corpus synthesis of this source: The study blind-graded uniformly prompted ChatGPT answers alongside real law-school exams. It finds polished writing and occasional competitive answers, but substantially weaker and highly uneven legal analysis, especially in issue spotting, relevance and applying rules to facts. The authors predict useful lawyer-assistance applications, warn about unregulated exam use, and recommend assessment rules and AI-aware legal education while preserving core research and reasoning skills.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study used standardized generation and blind comparative grading across varied law-school exams.

Exact returned source excerpt

ChatGPT Goes to Law School 389 One of us generated all of these answers using ChatGPT and formatted them to match actual exams written by students.10 This co-author only generated answers, grading no exams as part of the study.

Exact returned source excerpt

To do so, the co- author used a uniform set of prompts for all of the exam questions.11 Thus, for any given question, ChatGPT’s output was generated without any human intervention other than copying and pasting the content of the exam question along with a standard prompt.

Corpus source-specific finding

ChatGPT performed unevenly but well enough, under stated assumptions, to earn a J.D.

Exact returned source excerpt

Although the weaknesses in ChatGPT’s performance substantially outweighed the strengths in our study, this was relative to some of the best law students in the country, virtually all of whom will pass the bar exam and most of whom will become successful practicing lawyers.

Exact returned source excerpt

On the whole, ChatGPT thus performed surprisingly well on a broad array of law school exam types and was able to hold its own, particularly when its answers focused on the correct issues.

Corpus source-specific finding

Its principal strengths were polished style, suitable tone and high-level organization.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, in response to a Constitutional Law essay question requesting an answer in the form of a memo to counsel evaluating potential claims, ChatGPT’s answer maintained appropriate tone and formatting throughout.

Exact returned source excerpt

ChatGPT’s essay answers were typically clear and well crafted—perhaps even suspiciously so compared with real students writing a time-limited exam.

Corpus source-specific finding

Its central analytical weaknesses were poor issue spotting, misplaced focus and inadequate application of law to facts.

Exact returned source excerpt

Weaknesses in Writing Essays However, ChatGPT’s essay answers also contained consistent problems and errors that cumulatively made it a much worse student than the average.

Exact returned source excerpt

ChatGPT often struggled to spot issues when given an open-ended prompt, a core skill on law school exams.

Corpus source-specific finding

General-purpose training, prompt design and model behavior constrained legal performance.

Exact returned source excerpt

ChatGPT also occasionally misunderstood technical terms contained within exams.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, it misunderstood the term “lump sum payment” in the Employee Benefits exam, perhaps because ChatGPT is a general-purpose language model and the phrase is not widely used outside of certain financial settings.

Corpus source-specific finding

Context limits required prompting trade-offs, while concise, strategically placed instructions improved results.

Exact returned source excerpt

Language models are constrained by their “context window,” the number of words they can refer to when generating the next word.

Exact returned source excerpt

Context windows are analogous to working memory in humans, and language models will struggle to remember content past the limit of their context window.
