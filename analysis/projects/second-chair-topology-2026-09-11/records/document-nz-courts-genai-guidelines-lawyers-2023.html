Corpus framing of this source: New Zealand courts: lawyers retain responsibility for GenAI use

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Courts of New Zealand’s 7 December 2023 guidance applies existing professional duties to lawyers’ use of GenAI in court and tribunal proceedings. It recommends qualified human verification, protection of sensitive information and attention to bias, while allowing use without routine disclosure. Its contribution is practical judicial guidance grounded in explanations of technological risks, rather than measured performance findings.

Corpus framing of this source: Court guidance on generative AI’s capabilities and legal reliability risks

Corpus synthesis of this source: Guidelines for Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Courts and Tribunals — Lawyers explains how generative AI works and links its design and training data to legal accuracy, privacy and evidentiary risks. The supplied excerpts offer qualitative guidance rather than measured findings, identifying language-processing uses while warning against treating convincing outputs as reliable facts.

Corpus source-specific finding

The guidelines describe chatbots as language predictors best suited to language-processing tasks.

Exact returned source excerpt

LLMs analyse a large amount of training text to predict the probability of the next best word in a sentence given the context.

Exact returned source excerpt

LLMs have been further trained on ideal human written responses to prompts, and on survey results, about which responses sounds most natural or best mimic human dialogue.

Corpus source-specific finding

Limited New Zealand legal coverage constrains the accuracy of chatbot answers.

Exact returned source excerpt

Generally, the text used to train GenAI comes from various internet sources, such as webpages, online books, and social media posts.

Exact returned source excerpt

As GenAI chatbots output the next best word based on the text contained in their LLMs dataset, the source, quality, diversity and any bias of that text will determine the responses received.

Corpus source-specific finding
