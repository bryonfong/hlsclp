Native topic record

Employment screening: Audits reveal bias without remedying it

Corpus synthesis: Treats Local Law 144 as procedural transparency with scope, data and renewal limits rather than proof of fairness.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Evidence for deployed AI: Proxy measures do not establish real-world effectiveness · Automated hiring audits: Transparency exposes bias without ensuring fair screening

5 distinct stance-bearing works · Supports: 5

Native topic findings

  1. NYC Local Law 144 establishes procedural safeguards for automated hiring and promotion, not evidence that auditing eliminates bias.
  2. Coverage turns on how employers use automated outputs, not simply whether a human remains involved.
  3. The regime leaves important employment uses outside its defined scope.
  4. Audit requirements make demographic disparities visible but do not prescribe a response to adverse results.
  5. Audit compliance requires attention to data sufficiency, disclosure and renewal, not merely obtaining a report.
  6. Transparency gives affected people information and request routes, but no guaranteed alternative selection process.
  7. Enforcement separates procedural violations from discrimination claims and allows penalties to accumulate.
