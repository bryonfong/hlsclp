Builds onBig Four: Integration matters more than replacement

Open topic: Big Four: Integration matters more than replacement

Corpus framing of this source: How Global 100 firms respond to Big Four legal services

Corpus synthesis of this source: Robert J. Couture’s March/April 2021 article draws on leadership interviews at 20 Global 100 firms to distinguish three responses: no operational change, complementary partnerships, and integrated multiprofessional delivery. He links these choices to geographic reach and work sophistication, reports uneven competitive awareness, and recommends contingency planning rather than a single universal strategy.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study examines strategic intent through interviews, against a backdrop of expanding Big Four legal capability.

Exact returned source excerpt

While the Big Four (or Big Five) have been threatening entry into the legal market since the late ’90s, they have been quietly adding capability, which now places their law practices in more than 85 countries with a legal workforce of more than 10,000 attorneys.

Exact returned source excerpt

A few examples: In 2015 DLA Piper launched Noble Street, a new corporate advisory arm of the firm “focused on financing, corporate and M&A activities within the media, entertainment, technology and sports sectors.” In 2017 Hogan Lovells began offering consulting services for its clients in the financial services sector after bringing aboard a former director from PricewaterhouseCoopers’ own regulatory consulting practice.

Corpus source-specific finding

Competitive awareness and reported exposure vary substantially, with gaps in firms’ own information.

Exact returned source excerpt

## Statistical responses In looking at the entirety of the responses of all firms, a relatively small number of firms had a good grasp of the Big Four’s activities, with little more than a third of those interviewed having a high degree of familiarity with the activity of the accounting firms.

Exact returned source excerpt

Just less than half of the firms were casually aware of the Big Four presence (primarily outside the United States), and less than a quarter had virtually no knowledge of their participation in legal markets.

Corpus source-specific finding

Geographic reach and revenue per lawyer help explain strategic choices, but the proxy has limitations.

Exact returned source excerpt

Building a matrix of RPL versus number of countries with offices provides the scatter plot in Figure A.

Exact returned source excerpt

All charts are derived from ALM Intelligence data.

Corpus source-specific finding

Most interviewed firms made no changes, but inaction reflected both deliberate assessment and limited analysis.

Exact returned source excerpt

## Strategy 1: Not changing operation or focus The majority of the firms interviewed have chosen to make no changes in their operation due to Big Four presence or competition.

Exact returned source excerpt

The majority of the firms who have chosen to make no changes—more than three-quarters—have physical locations in 10 countries or fewer.

Corpus source-specific finding

High-end global firms often partner with the Big Four to supplement their capabilities.

Exact returned source excerpt

These firms typically have higher-end or more-sophisticated practices that would be considered having “bet the business” rather than “run the business” capabilities.

Exact returned source excerpt

These firms often will bring in the Big Four (or other capable consulting firms) to assist a client in larger projects that require process expertise, technology capabilities, or scale that the law firm cannot provide.

Corpus source-specific finding

Integrated delivery makes the law firm responsible for implementation, not merely legal advice.

Exact returned source excerpt

## Strategy 3: Transforming the firm to provide multiprofessional integrated solutions The final cohort are firms with a broad geographic presence that typically have lower RPL, with a profile of “run the business” work for clients.

Exact returned source excerpt

They plan to expand their professional practice offerings to better compete, which often means hiring billable professionals who are not lawyers to deliver integrated solutions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Integration also responds to client opportunity, while respondents disagree over regulatory protection.

Exact returned source excerpt

## One additional strategic observation One additional strategic approach had nothing to do with the Big Four entering the legal marketplace.

Exact returned source excerpt

These firms, with a high RPL and operating in a limited global footprint, have decided to build fully integrated, multiprofessional delivery teams because they sensed a client need combined with a business opportunity.

Corpus source-specific finding

Multiprofessional delivery could alter lawyers’ careers, but the positive evidence is anecdotal.

Exact returned source excerpt

Throughout the research process it also became clear that these changes in the legal marketplace will also provide different career opportunities and experiences for lawyers.

Exact returned source excerpt

In discussions with a former senior partner of a large firm, now at one of the Big Four, he described his experience as “refreshing” (perhaps because his objectives are now revenues, not hours and rates).

Corpus source-specific finding

Couture recommends testing assumptions and preparing executable alternatives for every strategy.

Exact returned source excerpt

Those pursuing a partnering strategy with other entities must take into consideration the risk of potentially subverting their client relationships by introducing new (and highly competent) strategic advisors to their clients, or perhaps losing some of their best professionals to enticing offers from their new business partners.

Exact returned source excerpt

Firms that choose to remake their businesses into an integrated solutions firm may be underestimating the effort of the transformation process, both technical and cultural, required to “cross the chasm” (per Geoffrey Moore, author of [*Crossing the Chasm*](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/crossing-the-chasm-3rd-edition-geoffrey-a-moore?variant=32130444066850)).

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: The Global 100 Responds to the Big Four

Corpus synthesis of this source: Robert J. Couture uses interviews with 20 Global 100 firms and ALM Intelligence data to examine elite law firms’ awareness of Big Four legal-services activity, their exposure to competition and their strategic responses. He segments firms by international footprint and revenue per lawyer (RPL), identifies three principal responses plus a separate client-opportunity-led model, and argues that firms should assess the threat rigorously, account for execution and relationship risks, and maintain contingency plans. He treats interviewees’ observations as reported views rather than his own findings and limits the study’s generalisability to the sampled firms.

Corpus source-specific finding

The research finds limited and uneven awareness of Big Four legal-market activity.

Exact returned source excerpt
