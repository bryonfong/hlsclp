Corpus framing of this source: Generative AI could automate substantial work while complementing most jobs and lifting productivity

Corpus synthesis of this source: Briggs, Kodnani and colleagues estimate generative AI’s potential labour-market and macroeconomic effects using occupation-level task data, scenario analysis and evidence from earlier technologies. They predict material automation exposure and possible near-term disruption, but argue that partial task automation, worker reemployment and productivity gains could ultimately produce large economic benefits. They repeatedly condition these estimates on uncertain AI capabilities, adoption, displacement and labour-market responses.

Corpus framing of this source: Goldman Sachs on generative AI, labour exposure and economic growth

Corpus synthesis of this source: Briggs, Kodnani, Hatzius and Pierdomenico model generative AI’s potential macroeconomic effects through task-level occupational exposure, labour substitution and complementarity, worker reemployment and productivity gains.
