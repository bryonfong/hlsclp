Corpus framing of this source: Algorithms at Work: Algorithmic Control as Contested Terrain

Corpus synthesis of this source: Kellogg, Valentine and Christin synthesize interdisciplinary research on workplace algorithms through labor process theory. They recast algorithms from efficiency tools into contested systems of organizational control, develop the “6 Rs” framework, compare algorithmic control with technical and bureaucratic control, examine worker outcomes and qualifications, identify emerging algorithmic occupations and forms of worker resistance, and set out organizational, legal and research responses. Their review draws on more than 1,100 empirical studies, supplemented by practitioner and case material where technologies had outpaced peer-reviewed research.

These studies emphasize the benefits to employers of algorithmic technologies in terms of economic value, based on improved efficiency in decisionmaking, coordination processes, and organizational learning.

Exact returned source excerpt

Through this review, we find that employers are using algorithms to control workers through six main mechanisms, which we call the “6 Rs”—they are using algorithms to direct workers by restricting and recommending, evaluate workers by recording and rating, and discipline workers by replacing and rewarding.

Corpus framing of this source: Algorithmic control: worker risks, organisational choices and resistance

Corpus synthesis of this source: Algorithms at Work: The New Contested Terrain of Control uses labor process theory to examine how employers exercise algorithmic control and how workers experience and contest it. The review highlights risks to livelihoods while stressing variation across organisations, individuals and countries. It proposes research on worker participation, training and collective governance rather than treating technological outcomes as inevitable.
