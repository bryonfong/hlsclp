Court governance: Assistive use retains human accountability

Native topic findings

  1. Courts are converging on an assistive, human-accountable model of AI use.
  2. Verification is the principal operational safeguard, but it must be independent and proportionate to the task.
  3. Task-specific boundaries are replacing blanket bans, especially around evidence and consequential decisions.
  4. Confidentiality and security controls turn on tool architecture, data handling and institutional control.
  5. Jurisdictions disagree most visibly over approval and disclosure, not over human responsibility.
  6. Institutional governance must extend beyond individual verification to lifecycle oversight and contestability.
  7. Efficiency and access-to-justice benefits remain plausible but mostly unproven and may create new inequalities.
  8. The evidence base supports urgent governance more strongly than claims of operational effectiveness.
