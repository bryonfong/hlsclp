Corpus framing of this source: From Practice to Field: A Multilevel Model of Practice-Driven Institutional Change

Corpus synthesis of this source: The study develops a practice-driven account of institutional change from an embedded case study of an Anglo-German global law firm. It argues that everyday attempts to complete urgent, consequential work can alter professional practices, consolidate into organizational routines and diffuse quietly across a field, even without a strategic change project or prior regulatory authorization.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors challenge macro-level and actor-centred accounts of institutional change.

Exact returned source excerpt

6 Critiques of Existing Approaches to Institutional Change Despite these advances, our current understanding of institutional change remains selective because existing approaches primarily adopt a macro level of analysis.

Exact returned source excerpt

Hence, although Thornton and Ocasio (2008: 119) state that institutional logics may get transformed ‘through various mechanisms’, the exigencies of everyday work have generally not been considered as one of them, leading Delbridge and Edwards (2008: 304) to conclude that what we have is but ‘a simplified view of change’.

Corpus source-specific finding

The evidence comes from a revelatory but bounded Anglo-German law-firm case.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our research site, dubbed Justitia, is a global law firm that emerged from an Anglo- 9 German merger in the early 2000s to advise corporations, banks, and financial institutions on large international transactions.

Exact returned source excerpt

Embedded in two separate jurisdictions with distinct ‘institutional legacies’ (Morgan & Quack, 2005), the two offices allowed us to study both sides of an institutional divide while eliminating organizational variation (see Pratt, Rockmann, & Kaufmann, 2006).

Corpus source-specific finding

Cross-border work exposed a collision between English client-service and German fiduciary professional logics.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Collision of Local Practices Before the merger, and for some time thereafter, the ‘germanizing’ discussions were marked by a clash of professional practices and their underpinning logics.

Exact returned source excerpt

Having to think ‘beyond your own jurisdiction’ introduced a complexity to lawyers’ everyday practice which revealed a mutual lack of understanding.

Corpus source-specific finding

Novel institutional complexity produces change when urgency and consequential failure turn it into a work-level crisis.

Exact returned source excerpt

Importantly, it is not the existence of institutional complexity per se that precipitates change, but the novelty of this complexity, for, as recent accounts suggest, organizations can accommodate competing logics in various ways (Binder, 2007; Pache & Santos, 2010; Reay & Hinings, 2009; Tracey, Phillips, & Jarvis, 2011).

Exact returned source excerpt

novel institutional complexity is combined with the urgency of tight deadlines and with costly consequences of failing to negotiate agreement, the situation escalates into an autogenic, work-level ‘crisis’ (Hermann, 1963).

The model links three recursive mechanisms: situated improvising, normative reorientation and unobtrusive embedding.

Exact returned source excerpt

We label these core mechanisms situated improvising, reorienting the normative network, and unobtrusive embedding.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

The lawyers’ improvisations generated a client-oriented hybrid practice rather than simple convergence on one national model.

Exact returned source excerpt

Emergence of a Hybrid Practice Motivated by the pressure of ‘getting the deal done’ and enabled by a growing awareness and understanding of institutional alternatives, both English and German lawyers grew more receptive to alternative ways of working to cope with the complexities involved in ‘germanizing’ contracts.

Exact returned source excerpt

Conceding that ‘there is no point in drafting an English-law loan agreement until you understand the jurisdiction you are lending to’, English lawyers pragmatically began to prepare transactions with an initial ‘local-law due diligence’ in an effort to pre-empt delays and frustrations.

Corpus source-specific finding

Organizational coordination consolidated dispersed improvisations into a common practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

Overlapping memberships of these teams, however, acted as natural conduits for the diffusion of the emerging practice and connected the dispersed pockets of awareness.

Exact returned source excerpt

Moreover, because they were inevitably working under significant time pressures, lawyers began to re-use previous improvisations, and to adopt particularly successful improvisations as they learned them from their colleagues.

Corpus source-specific finding

Institutional distancing amplified change through cosmopolitanism, feedback and elite status.

Exact returned source excerpt

Cosmopolitan members are more open to alternative ways of working, continuous positive feedback encourages and amplifies practice deviations, and status provides structural assurance that new activities and solutions are legitimate within and beyond the organization, thereby reducing resistance to the emergence, justification and diffusion of new ways of working.

Exact returned source excerpt

That is, positive feedback encourages and escalates deviations from established norms, whereas negative feedback counteracts deviations.

Corpus source-specific finding

Practice participation, templates, syndication and client pressure diffused change quietly across the field.

Exact returned source excerpt

24 Initially, within this context, the new practice diffused naturally through the repeated interactions within this small elite network of firms engaged in international banking transactions.

Exact returned source excerpt

In this elite network, firms watched and learned from each other and aligned their practices in order to expedite deals.

Corpus source-specific finding

BRAK’s limited oversight and defensive accommodation enabled post-hoc endorsement of the new professional logic.

Exact returned source excerpt

Aware that their internal practices were shielded from inspection, international law firms did not consider BRAK relevant, especially compared to their peers whom they considered more relevant for how they conducted their business.

Exact returned source excerpt

In consequence, the enquiry did not penetrate to the working practices deep inside the international firms, but instead turned its focus to a legal analysis of visible practices such as advertising, recruitment, forms of incorporation, the management of conflicts of interest, and their compliance with professional law.

Corpus source-specific finding

Practice-driven change is purposeful in the immediate task but neither strategically directed nor random.

Exact returned source excerpt

The locus of change is the practice and the ‘entrepreneur’ is the practitioner at work.

Exact returned source excerpt

Practitioners only act as institutional entrepreneurs, however, in that their situated improvising may break with the dominant logic in the field, but not in the sense that they deliberately intend to unseat existing institutional arrangements (Battilana et al., 2009).

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors qualify generalization and propose comparative research on context, coordination, practice composition and regulation.

Exact returned source excerpt

In advancing a practice approach to institutional change, however, we must be mindful that our findings have been derived from a study of professionals working within one organization, an elite international law firm created by merger.

Exact returned source excerpt

Future research should, therefore, explore whether the observed mechanisms operate in other professions, jurisdictions, or sectors of society – and whether they do so in similar ways to those outlined here.
