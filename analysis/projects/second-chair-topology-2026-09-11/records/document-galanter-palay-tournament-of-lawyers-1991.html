Corpus framing of this source: The promotion tournament as an explanation of large-law-firm growth

Corpus synthesis of this source: Marc Galanter and Thomas Palay’s 1991 book explains large-firm organisation and expansion through human-capital exchange and promotion incentives. The supplied record reconstructs the argument from publisher material and later scholarship rather than the full book, and separately reports subsequent criticism and revision.

- *The tournament.* "[T]he firm holds a tournament in which all of the associates in a particular 'entering class' compete" (100), and "awards the prize of partnership to the top n percent of the contestants" (100), judged on "their production of two goods: high quality legal work and their own human capital" (100).

Partnership is deferred compensation — a "superbonus" the firm implicitly promises to evaluate associates for throughout the probationary period (100; the chance of partnership is part of current compensation, 94).

Fixed-share promotion generates exponential growth, but the book questions its sustainability.

- *The growth engine.* Because the "governance mechanism for the sharing of [a founding partner's] human capital" requires promoting a fixed share of each class, each cohort of new partners must in turn be leveraged with new associates — so the firm grows exponentially (99).

Corpus source-specific finding

Later scholarship challenges the original assumptions and extends competition beyond partnership.

Exact returned source excerpt

## How the later literature holds it Wilkins & Gulati 1998 (held) systematise the model into seven assumptions — universal competition, equal chances, no sabotage problem, partner/firm interest identity, two career periods, promotion as reward for associate production, and transparent rules — and argue that none accurately describes contemporary elite firms.

Exact returned source excerpt

Galanter & Henderson 2008 (held), with Galanter as co-author, corroborate "some of the core theoretical insights" (the smooth upward trajectory of partnership ranks against cyclical associate hiring) while replacing the single tournament with an "elastic tournament" that extends past partnership into an equity core and a widening mantle of off-track lawyers and non-equity partners.
