Native topic findings

  1. Corporate legal departments have shifted from subordinate support functions toward strategic centres of legal judgment, purchasing and corporate governance.
  2. Evidence shows substantial gains in US general counsels’ authority and professional standing, but department size alone does not measure influence or success.
  3. In-house purchasing power weakened exclusive law-firm relationships without producing a simple transfer of work or a spot market for legal services.
  4. Failure to contain costs through internalisation alone helped create a diversified corporate legal supply chain.
  5. The GC’s dual identity as business partner and independent guardian remains the central normative disagreement.
  6. The in-house model is spreading internationally through adaptation rather than convergence on a uniform US endpoint.
  7. The transformation has widened career mobility and practical responsibility while leaving future organisational outcomes open.
