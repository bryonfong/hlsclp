Native topic record

Aggregate productivity: Task speedups shrink during scaling

Corpus synthesis: Shows projections depend on adoption, verification and difficult residual tasks, while reallocation can preserve employment.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Native topic findings

  1. The central dispute concerns how task-level AI gains translate into aggregate productivity, employment and welfare—not whether AI can accelerate selected tasks.
  2. Large differences in productivity projections depend on adoption, scaling and generalisability assumptions.
  3. Verification and tasks that remain difficult can sharply reduce the value of raw speedups.
  4. Broad adoption does not establish intensive use or conversion of saved time into additional output.
  5. Task substitution can coexist with stable employment because reallocation and firm growth offset direct losses.
  6. Larger gains for novices in some tasks do not establish lower wage inequality or stronger entry-level hiring.
  7. Early macroeconomic and labour-market indicators remain mixed and do not resolve causal attribution.
  8. GDP, worker welfare and household value can move differently.
  9. Practical implications centre on measuring complete workflows, distributional effects and the mechanisms behind aggregate outcomes.
