Open topic: Chatbot liability: Correct links do not cure misinformation

Corpus framing of this source: Moffatt v. Air Canada: liability for misleading chatbot fare advice

Corpus synthesis of this source: The British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal found Air Canada liable for negligent misrepresentation after a customer relied on incorrect chatbot advice about retroactive bereavement fares. The decision addresses responsibility for website information, reasonable customer reliance, evidentiary failures and compensatory damages; it does not establish the chatbot’s underlying technology.

Corpus source-specific finding

The evidence showed conflicting fare instructions and a timely refund request.

Exact returned source excerpt

They included the screenshot from the chatbot that set out the 90-day window to request a reduced rate and confirmed they had filled out the refund form and provided a death certificate.

Exact returned source excerpt

The webpage says, in part, the bereavement policy does not apply to requests for bereavement consideration after travel has been completed.

Corpus source-specific finding

The tribunal applied negligent misrepresentation rather than treating chatbot errors as a separate liability category.

Exact returned source excerpt

Moffatt does not use the words specifically, by saying they relied on Air Canada’s chatbot, I find they are alleging negligent misrepresentation.

Corpus source-specific finding

Air Canada remained responsible for information delivered through its chatbot.

Exact returned source excerpt

Air Canada argues it cannot be held liable for information provided by one of its agents, servants, or representatives – including a chatbot.

Exact returned source excerpt

In effect, Air Canada suggests the chatbot is a separate legal entity that is responsible for its own actions.

Corpus source-specific finding

The tribunal found customer reliance reasonable despite the link to correct information.

Exact returned source excerpt

It also does not explain why customers should have to double-check information found in one part of its website on another part of its website.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

Missing evidence defeated the contractual defence and supported the fare valuation.

Exact returned source excerpt

To the extent Air Canada argues it is not liable due to certain terms or conditions of its tariff, I note it did not provide a copy of the relevant portion of the tariff.

Exact returned source excerpt

It did not, so it has not proven a contractual defence.

Corpus source-specific finding

The tribunal awarded the demonstrated loss, not the full amount requested.

Exact returned source excerpt

Moffatt has made out their claim of negligent misrepresentation and is entitled to damages.

Exact returned source excerpt

Moffatt is entitled to be put in the position they would have been in if the misrepresentation had not been made.
