SupportsBenchmark success: Average scores conceal complete-task failure

Open topic: Benchmark success: Average scores conceal complete-task failure

Corpus framing of this source: Allens’ Australian law benchmark: unreliable standalone advice, qualified scope for lawyer assistance

Corpus synthesis of this source: Allens reports February 2024 testing of five general-purpose AI implementations on Australian legal questions. It finds uneven accuracy, weak critical reasoning, citation defects and inappropriate reliance on foreign law. The authors recommend expert verification and organisational safeguards, while predicting assistance rather than replacement of lawyers and proposing repeat testing as tools evolve.

Corpus source-specific finding

Allens introduces a repeatable Australian benchmark to replace anecdote with systematic testing.

Exact returned source excerpt

A repeatable benchmark is needed to systematically test, compare and track over time, developments in generative AI's ability to answer legal questions.

Exact returned source excerpt

In consultation with Linklaters LLP, Allens has developed the Allens AI Australian Law Benchmark (*Allens AI Benchmark*) to test the ability of LLMs to answer legal questions under Australian law.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study tests lay-user substitution, not specialised legal AI or the whole lawyer role.

Exact returned source excerpt

We tested general-purpose implementations of market-leading (at the time of the test: February 2024) LLMs, approximating how a lay user might try to answer legal questions using AI, instead of a human lawyer.

Exact returned source excerpt

We tested the question set against five different models, being GPT-4, Gemini 1, Claude 2, Perplexity and LLaMa 2.

Corpus source-specific finding

GPT-4 led the tested field, but none provided dependable unsupervised legal advice.

Exact returned source excerpt

The strongest overall performer was GPT-4, followed by Perplexity.

Exact returned source excerpt

LLaMa 2, Claude 2 and Gemini-1 tested relatively similarly.

Corpus source-specific finding

Citation failures and foreign-law contamination undermine Australian-law answers.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, some tools demonstrated: an inability to choose authoritative legal sources (cases, legislation or authoritative texts) over unauthoritative ones (such as a law firm publication); - a tendency to manufacture (hallucinate) case names; - a tendency to name a correct source but attribute a fictional extract or choose an incorrect pinpoint citation; or - a tendency only to cite a whole piece of legislation without specifying a section reference.

Exact returned source excerpt

In particular, despite being asked to answer from an Australian law perspective, many of the responses cited authorities from UK and EU law, or incorporated UK and EU law analysis that is not correct for Australian law.

Corpus source-specific finding

Useful drafting assistance still requires expert verification and governance.

Exact returned source excerpt

LLMs performing at the level of GPT-4 could have a practical role in assisting legal practitioners to summarise relatively well-understood areas of law.

Exact returned source excerpt

GPT-4 appears capable of, for example, preparing a sensible first draft of the law in some cases.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors treat the results as time-bound and plan to test newer tools.

Exact returned source excerpt

- In the short time since we ran our tests, we have already seen major new versions of several of these LLMs released (including LLaMa 3, released on 4 March 2024, Gemini 1.5, released for public preview on 9 April 2024, Claude 3, released on 18 April 2024 and GPT-4o released on 13 May 2024).

Exact returned source excerpt

We intend to rerun this benchmarking exercise in the future over new tools, and as new LLMs and other AI tools are released onto the market.

Corpus source-specific finding

Allens predicts augmentation rather than lawyer replacement, even if models reach benchmark parity.

Exact returned source excerpt

- Even if (and when) LLMs achieve or surpass parity with the benchmark, the role and importance of the human lawyer will comfortably endure.

Exact returned source excerpt

The ability to answer questions of law in a succinct and correct manner is but a fraction of what is required in the daily travail of an Australian lawyer, whose role today is more akin to a strategic adviser.

Corpus synthesis of this source: Allens presents a repeatable, firm-published benchmark of general-purpose LLMs answering Australian-law questions as a lay user might use them. It finds limited drafting and summarisation value but substantial reliability, reasoning, hallucination and jurisdiction-transfer risks. Corpus framing of this source: Allens benchmark: Australian legal advice requires expert supervision

Corpus synthesis of this source: Allens reports February 2024 testing of general-purpose chatbots on Australian legal questions. GPT-4 performed best, but the authors found no tested tool reliable enough to provide advice involving critical reasoning without expert supervision. They distinguish potential assistance with legal summaries from autonomous legal advice.
