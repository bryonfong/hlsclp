Native topic findings

  1. The cluster shifts the AI governance debate from writing rules to building institutions capable of implementing them.
  2. Regulatory markets would combine publicly determined outcomes with competing private methods, while retaining substantial government oversight.
  3. The sources differ over whether oversight should prioritize demonstrated outcomes or development processes.
  4. Accountability requires access to models, identifiable responsible parties and protection for independent research.
  5. Market structure is both a regulatory target and a constraint on effective oversight.
  6. Cross-border coordination need not mean uniform regulation, but national authority alone may leave gaps.
  7. Practical recommendations emphasize credible controls and enforcement, while liability and adoption effects remain uncertain.
