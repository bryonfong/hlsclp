Clinical teaming: AI accuracy may worsen joint decisions

Corpus synthesis: Emphasizes self-calibration, selective assignment and local testing rather than assuming seniority or human involvement ensures benefit.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Evidence for deployed AI: Proxy measures do not establish real-world effectiveness · Clinical AI assistance: Standalone accuracy does not ensure better care

5 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 4 · Redirects: 1

Native topic findings

  1. AI’s standalone accuracy does not establish that human–AI teams perform better.
  2. Who benefits depends on more than seniority or baseline skill; self-knowledge and case-level confidence may matter.
  3. Human involvement can transmit AI errors, but the proposed mechanisms differ and remain qualified.
  4. Selective task assignment may outperform routine assistance, but autonomy is a conditional design choice rather than an established remedy.
  5. Deployment needs local and individual testing because benchmark results may not transfer to clinical workflows.
  6. Task-level substitution and time savings do not directly imply occupation-wide job losses.
  7. Radiology offers evidence about collaboration design, not a universal forecast for generative AI or other professions.
