Open topic: AI adoption: Enthusiasm cannot establish investment value

Corpus framing of this source: Legal departments report effective AI use but weak ROI tracking and persistent trust concerns

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source reports survey findings on legal departments’ AI use, implementation barriers, spending and expectations. It contrasts users’ perceived efficiency gains and frequent use with uncertainty about investment value, and reports a strong preference for lawyer-controlled decision-making rather than AI autonomy.

Corpus source-specific finding

Users report high perceived effectiveness and frequent engagement with AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

Nearly universal satisfaction marks early AI implementations, with 97% of users reporting their tools are somewhat (63%) or highly (34%) effective.

Exact returned source excerpt

Among active users, 68% engage with AI tools daily or weekly, suggesting these technologies are becoming integral to legal workflows.

Corpus source-specific finding

Trust and privacy concerns exceed cost and integration barriers.

Exact returned source excerpt

Sixty percent of respondents cited “lack of trust or quality in AI outputs” as their top implementation challenge, followed closely by data privacy concerns (57%).

Exact returned source excerpt

These concerns far outweighed traditional barriers like cost (33%) or system integration issues (36%).

Corpus source-specific finding

Perceived efficiency gains do not yet establish measured investment value.

Exact returned source excerpt

### ROI Still A WIP While 83% of users cite increased efficiency as the primary benefit of AI adoption, formal measurement of return on investment remains underdeveloped.

Exact returned source excerpt

Only 7% of departments use specific KPIs to track AI value, and 40% of respondents remain “not sure yet” whether their AI tools are worth the investment.

Corpus source-specific finding

Spending visibility remains limited, prompting a suggestion to improve budget tracking.

Exact returned source excerpt

Monthly spending on AI tools remains modest for most departments, with 26% spending under $100 per month and only 9% spending more than $2,000 monthly.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, 44% of respondents didn’t know their department’s AI spending, suggesting budget tracking may also need refinement.

Corpus source-specific finding

Respondents expect AI to change legal work but prefer lawyers to retain control.

Exact returned source excerpt

Sixty-four percent believe AI will automate some tasks without replacing lawyers, while 26% expect more fundamental changes to how in-house lawyers operate.

Exact returned source excerpt

Only 10% anticipate minimal impact.

# Legal Departments Show Growing AI Adoption But Implementation Challenges Remain, New Survey Finds *Bob Ambrogi — LawSites / LawNext (2025)* By Bob Ambrogi, LawSites, 5 June 2025 A new benchmarking study reveals that artificial intelligence adoption in corporate legal departments is gaining momentum, with 38% of surveyed teams already using AI tools and another 50% actively exploring implementation.

Exact returned source excerpt

The inaugural *AI in Legal Departments: 2025 Benchmarking Report*, published this week by Counselwell and Spellbook, surveyed 256 in-house legal professionals across North America to understand current AI adoption patterns and future expectations.

Corpus source-specific finding

Trust, data privacy and measurement remain implementation barriers.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, significant barriers around trust, data privacy, and measurement persist as the legal profession navigates this technological shift.

Corpus source-specific finding

Contract tasks lead current AI use, and ChatGPT leads platform adoption.

Exact returned source excerpt

Sixty-four percent of AI users employ tools for contract drafting, review, and analysis, followed by legal research (49%) and document translation (38%).

Exact returned source excerpt

### ChatGPT Leads Tool Adoption When it comes to specific platforms used by legal departments, ChatGPT dominates, with 74% usage among AI-adopting respondents.

Corpus source-specific finding

Respondents anticipate expanding AI use into legal operations and automation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Looking ahead, respondents expect significant expansion into operational areas over the next 1-2 years, with 32% planning to adopt legal operations and workflow automation tools and 28% considering document automation.

Exact returned source excerpt

“The results show a clear trend: legal departments are starting with foundational AI applications like contract drafting and legal research, but are preparing to expand their AI usage into broader operational areas,” the report states.
