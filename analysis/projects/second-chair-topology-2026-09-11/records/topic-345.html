Native topic record

Skill gaps: Assisted equality can hide learning divides

Corpus synthesis: Separates immediate performance from underlying competence, interaction skill and durable unassisted learning.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Legal-service market restructuring: Bounded AI gains become structural change through institutional redesign · Legal-service restructuring: Bounded automation redistributes work, value and risk

10 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 5 · Supports: 5

Native topic findings

  1. AI can narrow assisted-performance gaps without equalizing underlying skills; the distributional effect depends on the task and outcome measured.
  2. Learning and discretionary use can instead reward stronger users, challenging a universal equalization claim.
  3. AI interaction competence may create a new performance divide that conventional academic measures miss.
  4. Assisted success does not reliably translate into unassisted learning, and the evidence includes both harm and modest carry-over.
  5. Cognitive engagement and output integration offer plausible explanations, but most interaction-pattern evidence remains observational.
  6. Targeted instruction and structured guidance can improve outcomes; access or additional exposure alone may not.
  7. Adoption, enthusiasm and perceived success are inadequate substitutes for measuring performance and retained competence.
