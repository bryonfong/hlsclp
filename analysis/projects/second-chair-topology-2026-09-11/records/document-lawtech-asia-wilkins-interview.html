Open topic: Associate training: Automation threatens expertise reproduction

Corpus framing of this source: Wilkins on AI task substitution and redesigning junior-lawyer training

Corpus synthesis of this source: Professor David B. Wilkins predicts substantial AI substitution of legal tasks while advocating augmentation of young lawyers. He urges the profession to preserve writing-based learning, question untested apprenticeship traditions and human-accuracy assumptions, and support experimentation. His examples illustrate his reasoning rather than establish measured performance comparisons.

Corpus source-specific finding

ChatGPT could replace many legal tasks, though not all legal work.

Exact returned source excerpt

While no one should publish or submit to court something that was handed in by a first-year lawyer, the difference in time and cost to get that document out between a first-yearer and ChatGPT is revolutionary.

Exact returned source excerpt

Then imagine the various tasks it might start to do more quickly, and as good as, if not better than, a mid-year associate.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI-assisted training should augment young lawyers without losing the thinking skills developed through writing.

Exact returned source excerpt

How does this affect the training of legal professionals?*** **Professor Wilkins: **This question behooves us to think of how we can utilise this technology to augment our young lawyers.

Exact returned source excerpt

To share an example, Dirk Hartung (Bucerius Law School) has been using ChatGPT to test his own knowledge about new things that he’s learning.

Corpus source-specific finding

Training should target necessary skills rather than perpetuate untested traditions of repetitive work.

Exact returned source excerpt

Having said that, we make all kinds of assumptions about what we need to make young lawyers do to make them “good lawyers”, such as anecdotes on how one cannot be a good lawyer unless you spend all night at the printer or in the library.

Exact returned source excerpt

But has anyone ever tested or verified that these tasks are in fact necessary to produce good lawyers?

Corpus source-specific finding

AI accuracy expectations should not rely on an assumed perfect human comparator.

Exact returned source excerpt

[In an interview in the HLS Center on the Legal Profession’s digital magazine on *The Practice* with Jason Boehmig (Ironclad)](https://clp.law.harvard.edu/knowledge-hub/magazine/issues/generative-ai-in-the-legal-profession/assisting-knowledge-workers/), Jason shared how he had to improve the accuracy of contract analytical tools to 99%, because no lawyer will ever use a tool for understanding what is in a contract that is less than 99% accurate.

Exact returned source excerpt

But who has ever tested the accuracy of the bleary-eyed associate reading through boxes of documents in a windowless basement of warehouses in Phoenix, Arizona in August?

Corpus source-specific finding

Singapore should provide institutional support and capital for legal experimentation.

Exact returned source excerpt

If Singapore wants to be a hub for law and legal innovation, there must be systems in place to channel the investment and desire to the right places.

Exact returned source excerpt

I believe our young students and legal professionals still have the desire to innovate.
