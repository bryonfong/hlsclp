Corpus framing of this source: AI as cognitive extension: co-creation requires active human direction

Corpus synthesis of this source: In her April 2026 essay, Anthea Roberts rejects a clean division between human and AI authorship. Drawing on extended-mind theory, her research practice, a reported Go study and an analogy with legal teams, she argues that AI can expand thinking while acknowledging cognitive risks. Her central qualification is that humans must actually perform higher-level thinking rather than delegate that too.

Corpus framing of this source: AI as Part of the Extended Mind

Corpus synthesis of this source: Anthea Roberts argues that asking whether a person or AI produced intellectual work imposes an artificial division on a fundamentally collaborative cognitive process. Drawing on her own working practices and the extended-mind thesis associated with Andy Clark and David Chalmers, she presents agentic AI as another external resource incorporated into human thinking, while distinguishing her position from the concern she reports that writing itself constitutes thinking.

Corpus source-specific finding

Roberts characterises work with agentic AI as co-creation rather than as output cleanly attributable to either human or machine.

Exact returned source excerpt

Because as somebody who works closely with agentic AI, almost everything about it is co-created.

Exact returned source excerpt

The idea that there's a clean line — my thinking on one side, the machine's on the other — doesn't match how any of it actually works.

Corpus source-specific finding

She distinguishes thinking from writing and qualifies the distinction by acknowledging that writing can be a mode of thought for some people.

Exact returned source excerpt

I'm used to hearing concerns about people relying on AI on the basis that "writing is thinking." For many people, their thoughts only become clear as they wrestle with them through the writing process.

Exact returned source excerpt

But to me, writing isn't necessarily thinking, though it can be.
