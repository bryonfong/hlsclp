Corpus framing of this source: A benchmark model of fraud and efficiency in credence-goods markets

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source develops a simple framework that reproduces much of the credence-goods literature by varying four assumptions—customer homogeneity, commitment after diagnosis, treatment verifiability and expert liability. It explains how pricing and institutional conditions can prevent overcharging, undertreatment and overtreatment, then argues that these ideal conditions often fail in repair and medical markets. The authors present the model as a benchmark rather than a complete account of observed expert misconduct.
