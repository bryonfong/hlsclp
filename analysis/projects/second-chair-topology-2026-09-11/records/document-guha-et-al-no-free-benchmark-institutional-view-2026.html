Corpus synthesis of this source: The paper frames legal AI benchmarking as an institutional problem rather than a purely technical exercise. It argues that public performance information is scarce and existing benchmarks poorly represent practical legal work; maps trade-offs in benchmark design and among potential benchmark providers; identifies consequences for procurement, innovation, education and governance; and proposes resource-sensitive, collaborative and targeted approaches without claiming that any institutional arrangement can eliminate the trade-offs. The paper reports no original underlying dataset.
