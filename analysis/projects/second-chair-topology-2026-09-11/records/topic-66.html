Justice chatbots: Actionable help exceeds correct information

Corpus synthesis: Evaluates whether self-help systems connect users to procedures and emergency resources, distinguishing practical assistance from plausible text.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Native topic findings

  1. The audits frame actionable assistance—not merely correct legal information—as a central test of self-help chatbots' access-to-justice value.
  2. Failure to connect users to existing forms and resources limits practical assistance.
  3. Context and procedural-selection errors can make otherwise plausible information inappropriate for the user.
  4. Emergency assistance exposes triage gaps that aggregate impact labels do not fully capture.
  5. Remediation must distinguish deficient source materials from failures to use available guidance; prompting results remain preliminary.
  6. Session audits establish answer defects and usage patterns, not population-level access-to-justice outcomes or causal effects.

Audit Report for Legal Information Assistant (LIA)

Academia & research · Adoption at scale (2025)

Corpus framing of this source: LIA audit identifies unactionable guidance and missing legal-help links

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Audit Report for Legal Information Assistant (LIA) documents terse child-custody guidance, missing referrals to existing resources and an eviction answer that appears mismatched to the user's question. Session counts support its criticism of practical usefulness; a small synthetic prompting experiment suggests a possible improvement rather than establishing a general remedy.

Corpus source-specific finding

Child-custody answers often fail to give users actionable next steps.

Exact returned source excerpt

In 310 of those sessions, this was LIA’s complete response: To get custody of your child, you need to fill out a form and give it to the court.

Audit Report for Self-Help Chatbot

Academia & research · Current wave (2026)

Corpus framing of this source: Audit of a Nevada self-help chatbot: useful answers, recurring procedural gaps and context errors

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Audit Report for Self-Help Chatbot evaluates user sessions with subject-specific rubrics and practical-impact labels. It documents useful assistance alongside missing next steps, inappropriate procedural recommendations, failures to retain context and uneven use of website materials. The report distinguishes observed answer defects from tentative explanations and qualifies what session data can establish about actual users.

Corpus source-specific finding

The audit evaluates practical usefulness as well as correctness, while acknowledging limits in its usage data.

Exact returned source excerpt

We developed subject-matter specific rubrics to orient ourselves to the material and to evaluate broad questions (e.g., "What are my options").

Exact returned source excerpt

We also developed fallback rubrics for one-off or specific questions, to evaluate whether an answer was clear, correct, complete, and actionable.

Corpus source-specific finding

The chatbot often supplies helpful information, but usefulness varies by task and does not always mean users can act.
