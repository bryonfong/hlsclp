Harvey currently targets human-in-the-loop augmentation rather than autonomous legal decision-making, with existing professional review structures adapted to manage AI errors. He predicts near-term end-to-end automation for bounded work such as NDAs but not complex matters, sees practice-specific hybrid pricing and optimization within hourly billing as more plausible than wholesale abolition of the billable hour, and presents AI’s larger value as improving firm profitability and legal outcomes at scale. He expects lawyers’ value to shift from producing text toward judgment, system design and higher-level abstraction, supported by auditability, explainability and governance. Interviewers separately raise concerns about reasoning errors, supervision, pricing, training and regulatory change; Pereyra expressly endorses auditability and welcomes experimentation in legal-service models.
