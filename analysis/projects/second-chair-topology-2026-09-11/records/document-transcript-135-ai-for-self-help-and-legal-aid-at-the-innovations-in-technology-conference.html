Corpus framing of this source: AI for Self-Help and Legal Aid: Responsible Adoption, Access and Regulatory Reform

Corpus synthesis of this source: The conference panel argues that public use of generative AI for legal problems will expand regardless of legal-aid participation, making deliberate engagement urgent. Across distinct contributions, Cat Moon emphasises human-centred experimentation and technology competence; Quinten Steenhuis calls for safer, easier-to-build tools and UPL reform; Scheree Gilchrist prioritises client-centred design, education and partnerships; and Conor Malloy favours AI as a controlled companion rather than a standalone service. The speakers also identify engineering costs, privacy, moderation, hallucination, bias, regulation and unequal access as constraints, while treating several claimed solutions and future developments as predictions rather than established findings.
