AI insurance: Exclusions operate beyond negligent hallucinations

Corpus synthesis: Shows policy wording, defence costs, aggregation and reporting can allocate AI risk independently of professional competence.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

2 distinct stance-bearing works · Supports: 2

Native topic findings

  1. Insurance terms can allocate AI-related legal risk independently of whether lawyers use AI competently.
  2. Hamilton Select’s AI exclusion turns on actual or alleged use, not solely on hallucinations or negligent reliance.
  3. The SRA regime protects claimants while preserving limited insurer recourse against insureds.
  4. Defence-cost treatment and related-claim aggregation materially affect the protection behind headline limits.
  5. AI coverage review must include ordinary exclusions, reporting duties and pricing—not just the AI endorsement.

Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Automation debate (2015-2022)

Corpus framing of this source: SRA minimum indemnity terms: mandatory coverage, claimant protections and limited insurer recourse

Corpus synthesis of this source: The SRA Minimum Terms and Conditions of Professional Indemnity Insurance sets regulatory requirements rather than presenting empirical findings or predictions. The supplied extracts establish the scope and minimum value of cover, restrict insurers’ ability to withdraw protection, specify permitted exclusions and reimbursement rights, and govern claims handling and policy continuity. They contain no express assessment of AI-related risks.
