Open topic: Billable hour: Faster matters need not reduce profits

Corpus framing of this source: AI savings intensify banks’ pressure on law-firm fees

Corpus synthesis of this source: “Wall Street Banks Push Big Law to Cut Fees Because of AI” argues that potential AI efficiencies undermine established hourly pricing. It describes unsettled alternatives and firms’ rate increases, while acknowledging rising staffing and technology costs. The source frames the dispute as a negotiation over who pays for AI adoption and who captures its savings.

Corpus source-specific finding

High associate rates sharpen the argument for passing potential AI efficiencies to clients.

Exact returned source excerpt

Bloomberg Law reported in 2023 that one first-year Sullivan & Cromwell associate on the FTX bankruptcy case was billed at $1,310 an hour, while lawyers who graduated in 2021 were often billed at $960 an hour.

Exact returned source excerpt

Those were bankruptcy court filings, not a theoretical pricing sheet.

Corpus source-specific finding

Alternative pricing remains unsettled, but the author predicts firms cannot indefinitely evade the hourly-billing debate.

Exact returned source excerpt

The replacement model is still messy.

Exact returned source excerpt

Some firms are testing fixed fees for defined work.

Corpus source-specific finding

Firms have responded by raising remaining hourly rates, but client willingness limits that strategy.

Exact returned source excerpt

The early response from many firms has been to raise the rate on the hours that remain, rather than give clients a clean discount on hours removed.

Exact returned source excerpt

That strategy works only while clients keep paying.

Corpus source-specific finding

Growing demand and costs give firms a partial counterargument to fee reductions.

Exact returned source excerpt

Citi's Law Firm Group and Hildebrandt Consulting reported that law firm revenue grew 11.3% in the first nine months of 2025, helped by 1.9% demand growth and strong inventory entering the year.

Exact returned source excerpt

Expenses rose 9.1% over the same period, with headcount up 2.9% and firms leaning into a more senior and expensive lawyer mix.

Corpus source-specific finding

Clients expect firms both to fund AI adoption and to return the resulting savings.

Exact returned source excerpt

Clients want firms to buy the tools, train lawyers on them and move faster.

Exact returned source excerpt

Then they want the savings handed back at the negotiating table.
