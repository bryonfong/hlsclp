Corpus framing of this source: Private tech power, public accountability and information governance

Corpus synthesis of this source: The episode’s principal contribution is Hannah Bloch-Wehba’s account of how governments increasingly depend on private technology companies for infrastructure, data and algorithmic tools, while conventional constitutional, democratic and evidentiary safeguards struggle to reach those hybrid arrangements. She uses government cloud contracts, criminal-law trade-secret claims and Google’s geofence-warrant practices to illustrate hidden corporate control. She also develops an information-law framework for complementary AI transparency mechanisms, critiques efforts to deter state AI regulation and cautiously predicts major changes in higher education and the wider information ecosystem. A separate introductory advisory segment by Sam Moore recommends intelligible, risk-based AI policies for legal organisations.
