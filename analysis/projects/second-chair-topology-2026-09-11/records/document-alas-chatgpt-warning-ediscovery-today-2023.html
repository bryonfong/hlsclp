Corpus framing of this source: ALAS warns that ChatGPT creates legal and insurance risks without replacing lawyers’ judgment

Corpus synthesis of this source: Doug Austin reports Isha Marathe’s April 2023 coverage of ALAS’s warning to law-firm policyholders. The substantive position belongs to ALAS and its loss-prevention executive Mary Beth Robinson: ChatGPT raises confidentiality, accuracy and disclosure concerns, but firms should understand new technology rather than reject innovation outright. The material reports prospective risks, not documented violations or measured error rates.

These include: ABA model [rule 1.1](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_1_competence/), which requires attorneys to provide competent representation to clients; [rule 1.4](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_4_communications/), which requires lawyers to be transparent and consult their clients  “about the means by which the client’s objectives are to be accomplished,”; [rule 8.4](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_8_4_misconduct/) which states it is professional misconduct to be dishonest about a legal process with a client; and [rule 1.6](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_6_confidentiality_of_information/) which requires attorneys to be upfront about the circulation of a client’s information.
