Corpus framing of this source: Gerber: corrective legal fees can support malpractice claims without an underlying loss

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Eighth Circuit majority predicts that Arkansas law permits recovery of fees proximately caused by counsel’s negligence without proof that the underlying lawsuit would have ended differently. It reverses summary judgment but leaves negligence, causation and timeliness to be established on remand. Judge Erickson disputes the state-law prediction and recommends certification to the Arkansas Supreme Court.

Corpus framing of this source: Corrective attorney fees as legal-malpractice damages without an underlying judgment

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Eighth Circuit held that Arkansas law may permit a client to recover attorney fees spent correcting counsel’s negligence even when the alleged injury does not depend on the outcome of underlying litigation. The majority reversed dismissal and remanded for Gerber to prove negligence, proximate cause and timeliness. Judge Erickson objected that the majority expanded Arkansas malpractice law through the Restatement rather than deferring to the state court.
