Corpus framing of this source: Thomson Reuters v. ROSS: protected legal editorial content, AI training and fair use

Corpus synthesis of this source: Judge Bibas’s February 11, 2025 opinion revises his 2023 ruling, grants partial summary judgment on infringement of 2,243 Westlaw headnotes subject to unresolved copyright-validity facts, and rejects ROSS’s fair-use defense. The court distinguishes protected editorial expression from judicial opinions, emphasizes competitive purpose and potential training-data markets, and expressly confines the case to non-generative AI.

Corpus framing of this source: Thomson Reuters v. ROSS: renewed summary judgment on copyright infringement and fair use

Corpus synthesis of this source: Judge Bibas revises the court’s 2023 ruling and grants Thomson Reuters partial summary judgment on direct infringement and fair use. The opinion finds protected originality and obvious copying and substantial similarity for 2,243 Westlaw headnotes, rejects Ross’s defenses, and treats Ross’s use of headnotes to build a competing non-generative legal-research system as commercial and non-transformative. It limits the ruling to the record before it and leaves disputed copyright validity and other materials for trial.
