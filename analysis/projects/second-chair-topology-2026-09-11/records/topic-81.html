Native topic record

Legal malpractice: Error alone cannot establish liability

Corpus synthesis: Requires deficient care, causation and substantiated loss, distinguishing corrective-fee theories from ethical discipline and adverse outcomes.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Legal institutions and professional practice: Substantive outcomes depend on context, capacity and judgment · Lawyer liability: Error or ethical breach does not itself establish malpractice

7 distinct stance-bearing works · Supports: 7

Native topic findings

  1. Malpractice turns on objectively deficient care and causally connected loss, not merely an error, adverse result or honest intention.
  2. The claimed injury determines the causation inquiry; corrective fees raise a contested alternative to proving a lost underlying case.
  3. Gerber leaves the corrective-fee question without a definitive Arkansas Supreme Court answer.
  4. Plaintiffs must substantiate causation and damages; obvious neglect or general allegations of extra fees do not suffice.
  5. Ethical discipline and alternative claim labels do not substitute for the requirements of civil malpractice.
  6. Recognizing a potentially recoverable injury does not remove evidentiary or timing barriers.
