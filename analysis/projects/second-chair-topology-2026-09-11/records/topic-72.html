Native topic record

Medical insurance: Information gaps resist complete contracting

Corpus synthesis: Balances adverse selection, moral hazard, trust and oversight, distinguishing historical coverage gaps from proof of their cause.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Cross-domain institutional evidence: Innovation and market correction remain conditional on incentives, gatekeeping and measurement · Licensing and insurance under uncertainty: Protective institutions remain incomplete and can exclude

2 distinct stance-bearing works · Supports: 2

Native topic findings

  1. Uncertainty and unequal information can prevent medical markets from delivering mutually beneficial insurance and care.
  2. The debate concerns the relative importance of adverse selection and moral hazard, not whether only one exists.
  3. Historical coverage gaps support concern about incomplete insurance, but do not establish adverse selection as their cause.
  4. Professional trust and institutional oversight respond to information gaps, but remain imperfect substitutes for enforceable guarantees.
  5. Insurance design trades off protection, participation, administrative expense and patient choice.
  6. The policy implication favours broader risk protection, while leaving institutional design and feasibility open.

Corpus framing of this source: Insurance, uncertainty and professional trust in medical care

Corpus synthesis of this source: In Uncertainty and the Welfare Economics of Medical Care, Arrow develops a welfare-economic case for broader insurance while examining incentive problems, administrative costs, unequal risks and incomplete coverage. He treats professional trust and institutional controls as responses to imperfect markets and information, and distinguishes ideal-insurance results from tentative institutional explanations and policy implications.
