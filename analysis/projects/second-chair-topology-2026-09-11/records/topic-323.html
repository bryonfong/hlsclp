Native topic findings

  1. The in-house counsel movement shifts control over corporate legal work toward buyers, without necessarily displacing law firms.
  2. The main economic disagreement concerns whether informed buyers erode Big Law’s foundations or sustain new forms of interdependence.
  3. Insourcing can substitute for outside work while expanding total legal demand; headcount alone cannot establish savings or authority.
  4. Buyer control operates through both unbundling and concentrated partnerships, rather than one universal sourcing model.
  5. Global diffusion produces locally differentiated authority, not inevitable convergence on the U.S. model.
  6. Greater corporate influence does not settle whether internal lawyers exercise independent professional judgment.
  7. Corporate monitoring can reduce lawyer–client agency problems without replacing public accountability.
  8. Shared careers and training sustain interdependence, while gains in status remain uneven.
  9. AI renews the buyer-power thesis but may also expose internal lawyers to the efficiency pressures they apply to firms.
