Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: The Next Trillion-Dollar Company No Longer Selling You Software. It Will Sell You the Work

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source argues that AI-native firms will create more durable value by delivering accountable work and outcomes rather than selling productivity software. It identifies incumbent copilot vendors’ customer incentives as an obstacle to automation, presents outsourced professional services as the clearest entry market for autopilot firms, and treats governance as a source of competitive advantage. It also reports supporting forecasts and survey findings from Gartner and IDC and attributes the prediction that autopilot-native firms will prevail to Bek.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source argues that firms selling completed work and standing behind outcomes have a more durable model than tool vendors, whose potential declines as frontier models improve. It predicts that the next trillion-dollar company will follow this work-delivery model and states Human Haus’s intention to participate.

Exact returned source excerpt

The tool-builders are not disappearing — but their ceiling is lower than it once appeared, and the model improvement arms race makes that ceiling drop a little further with each new frontier release.

Exact returned source excerpt

The firms that sell the work — that stand behind outcomes, that deploy intelligence by design rather than by accident — are building something more durable.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source claims that incumbent copilot companies face a structural conflict: customers buy productivity tools to preserve and improve their existing economics, not to eliminate the human work on which those economics depend.
