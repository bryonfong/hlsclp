Corpus framing of this source: Tracey Yurko on collaboration, Big Four scale and regulatory limits

Corpus synthesis of this source: In Developing an Ecosystem of Service Providers: A Conversation with Tracey Yurko, Yurko describes a cost-saving collaboration between her department, a law firm and PwC. She advocates combining providers’ strengths while preserving law firms’ strategic role, and conditionally predicts a Big Four advantage if practice restrictions change. Wilkins raises distinct questions about competitive encroachment and outside investment.

Corpus source-specific finding

A collaborative pilot cut costs by more than 50% while retaining law-firm strategic expertise.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, for one of the pilots we ran, we priced the project two ways: (1) for a law firm to do the whole project and (2) to have a law firm partner with us and PwC.

Exact returned source excerpt

We were able to cut the cost by more than 50% by doing a collaboration.

Yurko sees complementary provider roles rather than dispensing with law firms.

Exact returned source excerpt

Law firms also have deep experience and reputations for giving great strategic and technical legal advice, which the Big Four and other ALSPs don’t yet have.

Corpus source-specific finding

Yurko conditionally gives the Big Four an advantage in integrated services and scale.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

When I think about the goals of our legal and regulatory department and the kind of partner that we want to be our internal business clients, one thing that’s been really critical for us is for our providers, including PwC, to understand the business.

She contrasts law firms’ legal expertise, reputation and talent advantage with the Big Four’s business knowledge, integrated services and scale, while stressing that US practice restrictions and auditor-independence requirements constrain the Big Four.
