Corpus framing of this source: LegalBench: task-specific evidence on LLM legal reasoning

Corpus synthesis of this source: LegalBench introduces a collaborative 162-task benchmark and evaluates 20 LLMs. The authors find substantial variation across tasks, models and prompts, recommend task-specific assessments, and qualify their findings with limitations concerning training-data transparency, jurisdictional coverage and the 2023 model sample.

Corpus synthesis of this source: LegalBench describes a manually constructed, elementary contract-classification benchmark, explains its doctrinal significance, and proposes applications in contract review. The authors distinguish its simple examples from harder mixed-purpose contracts; the supplied material reports no model performance results.

Corpus source-specific finding

The task tests governing-law classification on 100 simple contracts.

Exact returned source excerpt

Construction process The dataset was manually created to test an LLM’s ability to determine whether a contract is governed by the UCC or by the common law.

Exact returned source excerpt

The dataset is composed of 100 descriptions of simple contracts such as “Alice and Bob enter into a contract for Alice to sell her bike to Bob for $50” (UCC) and “Aria pays Owen $100 to mount a television on the wall of her living room” (common law).

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors explain why the governing-law distinction matters through differences in contract rules.

Exact returned source excerpt

The UCC only requires that a goods contract include the good being sold and the quantity.

Exact returned source excerpt

If any other term is missing from the contract (such as price or delivery), it will be filled in by UCC default rules.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors frame the task as a basic legal-reasoning and subject-matter recognition benchmark.

Exact returned source excerpt

First, it provides a measure of an LLM’s legal reasoning ability relative to a human lawyer (who would almost certainly score a 100% on the task).

Exact returned source excerpt

Second, it demonstrates an LLM’s ability to determine the subject matter of a legal text, which has implications for the use of LLMs for legal tasks far beyond contract classification.

Corpus source-specific finding

Contract sorting and mixed-purpose classification are proposed applications, not demonstrated outcomes.

Exact returned source excerpt

Third, this task could prove useful in the context of contract lifecycle management (CLM) in which a CLM software product could automatically sort contracts by subject matter for review purposes.

Exact returned source excerpt

Fourth, while the sample contracts in the dataset were simple and easily identifiable as either UCC or common law contracts, real-world mixed purpose contracts can be difficult to classify and sometimes generate costly litigation.
Builds onLegal benchmarks: Realistic tasks expose uneven competence

Open topic: Legal benchmarks: Realistic tasks expose uneven competence

Corpus framing of this source: LegalBench: task-specific measurement of legal reasoning, not certification for deployment

Corpus synthesis of this source: LegalBench contributes an interdisciplinary, open-source benchmark of 162 legal tasks and a six-part reasoning typology. Its 2023 evaluations show substantial variation across tasks and prompts. The authors advocate lawyer-led benchmark design while stressing that benchmark scores neither establish readiness to replace lawyers nor substitute for context-specific safety and compliance assessments.

Corpus source-specific finding

Existing benchmarks inadequately capture few-shot legal reasoning and practical legal use cases.

Exact returned source excerpt

The majority of existing benchmarks, for example, focus on tasks which models learn by finetuning or training on task-specific data [21].

Exact returned source excerpt

These benchmarks do not measure the aspects of LLMs which generate excitement for law—namely, their ability to perform many different tasks using only few-shot prompts.

Corpus source-specific finding

LegalBench makes legal expertise central to benchmark construction.

Exact returned source excerpt

3 Over the past year, the authors of this paper—drawing from their diverse legal and computer science backgrounds—came together to assemble 162 tasks (from 36 different data sources), each of which measures a specific type of legal reasoning.

Exact returned source excerpt

L EGAL B ENCH was constructed from a mix of existing legal datasets (restructured for the few-shot LLM paradigm), and hand-crafted datasets created and contributed by legal professionals (included as authors on this work).

Corpus source-specific finding

A six-part typology provides a shared vocabulary for assessing distinct capabilities.

Exact returned source excerpt

3 The L EGAL B ENCH typology L EGAL B ENCH identifies six types of legal reasoning that LLMs can be evaluated for: (1) issue-spotting, (2) rule-recall, (3) rule-application, (4) rule-conclusion, (5) interpretation, and (6) rhetorical-understanding.

Exact returned source excerpt

To enable cross-disciplinary conversations about LLMs in the law, we additionally show how popular legal frameworks for describing legal reasoning—which distinguish between its many forms—correspond to L EGAL B ENCH tasks, thus giving lawyers and LLM developers a common vocabulary.

Corpus source-specific finding

Standardized infrastructure supports reproducible, task-appropriate evaluation.

Exact returned source excerpt

L EGAL B ENCH systematizes and standardizes these tasks for LLM evaluation, specifying potential prompts, in-context demonstrations, and metrics.

Exact returned source excerpt

First, each L EGAL B ENCH task is accompanied by extensive documentation describing how the task is performed, its legal significance, and the construction procedure.

Corpus source-specific finding

The empirical results support task-by-task judgments rather than blanket claims of legal competence.

Exact returned source excerpt

Finally, we use L EGAL B ENCH to evaluate 20 LLMs from 11 different families, across a range of size points.

Exact returned source excerpt

Results from these models were retrieved between May and August of 2023.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

Coverage limitations constrain generalization to legal practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

As a result, the initial round of L EGAL B ENCH tasks does not involve longer documents.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

Benchmark performance does not justify deployment or lawyer replacement on its own.
