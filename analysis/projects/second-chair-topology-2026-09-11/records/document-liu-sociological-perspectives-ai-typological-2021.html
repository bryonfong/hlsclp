Corpus framing of this source: Sociological perspectives on artificial intelligence: A typological reading

Corpus synthesis of this source: Zheng Liu synthesises sociological research that treats AI as scientific knowledge, technical application and cultural construction. The article’s principal contribution is to show that AI is socially constituted, socially embedded and socially constituting: institutions, values, labour and cultural agendas shape its development, while its applications redistribute power and transform social relations. Liu reports competing findings on employment and autonomous systems, distinguishes empirical evidence from speculative predictions, and recommends closer collaboration between social scientists and AI practitioners.

Corpus source-specific finding

Liu frames sociological analysis as an examination of both AI’s social formation and its social consequences.

Exact returned source excerpt

It analyses the social dimensions of AI's technoscientific features––their social nature and ramifications––and tries to make sense of AI products and research from a sociological perspective.

Exact returned source excerpt

Given that Forsythe's research was published almost 30 years ago, its importance lies less in its specific findings and more in the fact that it makes us ask, “Why do these problems, exposed nearly 3 decades ago, still persist today?” For some sociologists, AI is not only a socially constituted enterprise, but also socially constituting in the sense that when implemented in a social environment an AI system can assume social roles, enact social practices and form social relations.
