Builds onAI insurance: Coverage cannot replace professional governance

Open topic: AI insurance: Coverage cannot replace professional governance

Corpus framing of this source: Generative AI creates conditional insurance coverage risks for lawyers

Corpus synthesis of this source: In his August 2025 ALPS article, Mark Bassingthwaighte supports generative AI’s potential to improve legal services but urges lawyers to examine insurance implications. He presents conditional risk-management arguments rather than adjudicated coverage outcomes, focusing on unverified output, unsupervised client-facing tools, and confidentiality breaches.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI-related negligence is not typically excluded outright, but coverage may depend on whether lawyers provided professional services.

Exact returned source excerpt

** ****Malpractice Involving AI Output** At the time of this writing, lawyers’ professional liability (LPL) policies typically do not exclude coverage for claims alleging negligence arising as a result of the use of generative AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

That said, coverage may depend on whether the conduct at issue meets the policy’s definition of “professional services.” Don’t assume that it always will.

Corpus source-specific finding

Blind reliance on AI output could undermine coverage through two distinct routes.

Exact returned source excerpt

If this lawyer is unable to demonstrate that she exercised reasonable care and due diligence with her use of the AI tool, then an insurer could argue that no professional service was ever provided because the lawyer simply chose to blindly rely on third-party technology.

Exact returned source excerpt

If the subject lawyer did make a deliberate decision to blindly accept the output as accurate, this act might also trigger a policy’s intentional act exclusion.

Corpus source-specific finding

Unsupervised client-facing AI could expose firms to uninsured claims.

Exact returned source excerpt

- ** ****AI Interfacing with Clients or the General Public** If a law firm markets AI-generated content or tools (e.g., an online chatbot or a DIY legal form generator) directly to clients or the public and a malpractice claim arose out of that service, would this be a covered claim?

Exact returned source excerpt

Here again, depending upon the specifics of the situation and the jurisdiction in which the alleged negligence occurred, the answer could well be no for two reasons.

Corpus source-specific finding

Confidentiality breaches may fall outside malpractice coverage; cyber insurance offers only conditional protection.

Exact returned source excerpt

- ** ****Confidentiality and Data Security** Feeding sensitive or confidential client information into a generative AI tool, especially one that is cloud-based and accessible to the public or not specifically designed for secure use by legal professionals, could result in a data breach or unauthorized access to this client information potentially giving rise to a claim.

Exact returned source excerpt

The reason is that most LPL policies have exclusions related to intentional acts or breaches of confidentiality that are not the result of a negligent act.
