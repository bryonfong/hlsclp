Corpus framing of this source: New York Part 161: existing duties and independent verification of AI-assisted court papers

Corpus synthesis of this source: 22 NYCRR Part 161 grounds its approach to AI-assisted court papers in existing submission duties. It recommends against mandatory disclosure of AI use, permits discretionary court-specific rules, and provides a model rule requiring awareness of AI limitations and independent checks for fabricated material.

Corpus source-specific finding

Existing submission duties justify not requiring disclosure of AI use.

Exact returned source excerpt

Since those duties and responsibilities already apply to all submissions, regardless of whether AI tools were used, attorneys and parties should not be required, upon submitting papers, to disclose to the court that they have used AI in the preparation of such papers.

Exact returned source excerpt

Under existing authority, by signing a paper and submitting it to this court, an attorney or party certifies that the paper does not contain any false material factual statement or any frivolous legal argument Cree, e.g, 22 NYCRR 130-1.1, 130-1.1a), and an attorney who submits any paper to this court is additionally bound by the Rules of Professional Conduct.

Corpus source-specific finding

Courts retain discretion to adopt AI-specific rules.

Exact returned source excerpt

## Section 161.4 Model Rule A court may, in its discretion, implement a part rule governing the use by attorneys and parties of artificial intelligence tools in preparing papers submitted to the court.

Exact returned source excerpt

If a court determines that such a rule is appropriate, the court is encouraged to adopt the model rule set forth in Appendix A of this Part.

Corpus source-specific finding

The model rule expects users to understand AI limitations and independently verify papers.

Exact returned source excerpt

## Section 161.Appendix A Model Rule Regarding Use of Artificial Intelligence Technology in Preparing Court Papers Every attorney or party who uses an artificial intelligence (AI) tool in preparing any paper submitted to this court is expected to understand that tool's capabilities and limitations.

Exact returned source excerpt

Attorneys and parties need to be aware that AI tools, among other risks and limitations, can generate fabricated information or fictitious citations to authority (commonly known as hallucinations).

Corpus source-specific finding

Existing submission and professional duties support independent verification

Exact returned source excerpt

Under existing authority, by signing a paper and submitting it to this court, an attorney or party certifies that the paper does not contain any false material factual statement or any frivolous legal argument Cree, e.g, 22 NYCRR 130-1.1, 130-1.1a), and an attorney who submits any paper to this court is additionally bound by the Rules of Professional Conduct.

Exact returned source excerpt

Accordingly, any attorney or party who uses an artificial intelligence tool, as defined in 22 NYCRR 161.2(a), in preparing any paper, as defined in 22 NYCRR 161.2(b), filed in or submitted to this court or served on another party in a case before this court is required to carefully review the paper and independently ensure that it contains no fabricated or fictitious cases, statutes, or other material.
