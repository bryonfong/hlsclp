AI copyright: Product purpose reshapes market harm

Corpus synthesis: Examines public law, editorial expression, transformativeness and substitution without treating AI training as categorically lawful or unlawful.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Legal institutions and professional practice: Substantive outcomes depend on context, capacity and judgment · AI-training fair use: Lawfulness turns on specific copying and market substitution

6 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 2 · Supports: 4

Native topic findings

  1. The debate turns on the particular copying, product function and protected market—not whether AI training is categorically lawful.
  2. Legal-information copyright separates public law from editorial expression, but the extent of that separation remains contested.
  3. Transformativeness depends on whether courts emphasize the internal training operation or the resulting product’s purpose.
  4. Market definition divides protection against copyright injury from protection against ordinary competition.
  5. Kadrey’s market-dilution reasoning challenges the view that non-infringing outputs necessarily avoid cognizable market harm.
  6. Acquisition, retention, digitisation and training require distinct analysis rather than a single justification for the entire data pipeline.
  7. Procedural posture and evidentiary limits constrain claims about what the ROSS litigation establishes.
  8. The practical stakes concern access to legal information, incentives for curation and the cost of lawful AI development.
