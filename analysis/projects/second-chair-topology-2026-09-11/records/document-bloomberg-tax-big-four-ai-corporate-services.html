Corpus framing of this source: Big Four Firms Embrace AI to Revamp Corporate Service Offerings

Corpus synthesis of this source: Amanda Iacone reports that the Big Four are repositioning managed services as an AI-enabled growth model: multi-year engagements to operate corporate functions can generate recurring revenue, improve margins, deepen client relationships and create advisory opportunities. The article supports this account with firm growth targets, hiring and service examples, while qualifying it through limited segment-level revenue disclosure and warnings about technology-company competition.

Corpus source-specific finding

The article’s principal claim is that AI disruption is pushing the Big Four from primarily advising clients toward multi-year contracts under which they redesign and operate corporate back-office functions.

# Big Four Firms Embrace AI to Revamp Corporate Service Offerings *Amanda Iacone — Bloomberg Tax (2026)* The Big Four accounting and consulting firms are looking to stay competitive as artificial intelligence shakes up their industry by pivoting to focus on multi-year contracts meant to reinvent and run corporate back-office suites.

Exact returned source excerpt

## New Business Model The pivot to running core business functions in lieu of selling advice reflects a broader shakeup in the consulting industry as AI shifts cost structures for firms and attracts new competitors to the market.

Managed services extend beyond conventional outsourcing by transferring responsibility for critical functions while combining process redesign, automation and experienced human support.

Managed services offerings go beyond outsourcing, with firms taking ownership of critical tasks like anti-money laundering compliance for financial institutions or protecting data and IT infrastructure from cyber threats.

The firms install new processes for their clients, with fresh technology to automate and streamline the work.

Client demand for AI-enabled workflow redesign is driving growth, while shared technology platforms provide the economic rationale for buying managed services rather than building equivalent capabilities internally.

Demand from clients to re-imagine their workflows with AI is fueling the boom in managed services the four firms provide.

For corporate managers under pressure to weave cutting edge [agentic AI](https://news.bloombergtax.com/financial-accounting/big-four-firms-roll-out-ai-that-can-handle-routine-tasks-solo) throughout the company, the pitch is simple: rely on a portfolio of AI agents the Big Four firms have already built and tested, with a library of new capabilities in the works, rather than recreating similar improvements on their own.

Agentic AI is said to reverse the historically labour-intensive, low-margin economics of managed services, while recurring contracts can stabilise revenue and support cross-selling.

Historically those offerings required a large workforce and delivered thin profit margins, compared to high-priced advisory projects.
