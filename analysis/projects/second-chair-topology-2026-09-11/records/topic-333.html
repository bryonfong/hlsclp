Legal benchmarks: Realistic tasks expose uneven competence

Corpus synthesis: Finds strong bounded performance alongside serious reasoning and sourcing failures, with conclusions contingent on prompts, coverage and grading.

Declared lineage: Evidence for deployed AI: Proxy measures do not establish real-world effectiveness · Legal-AI effectiveness: Complete legal tasks and resolved problems outrank benchmark scores

27 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 18 · Supports: 7 · Unclear: 2

Native topic findings

  1. Legal-AI competence must be measured on realistic, task-specific work rather than inferred from exams or generic benchmarks.
  2. Expert rubrics make open-ended evaluation more diagnostic, but no grading method has emerged as definitive.
  3. Measured performance remains uneven: strong results on bounded tasks coexist with serious failures on difficult reasoning, sourcing and open-ended work.
  4. Hallucinations and unsound reasoning remain central reliability problems, and retrieval or correct final answers do not eliminate them.
  5. Benchmark conclusions are highly contingent on coverage, prompts, data and institutional choices.
  6. A deeper disagreement concerns whether current weaknesses reflect remediable engineering gaps or a fundamental mismatch with legal reasoning.
  7. Practical use should remain capability-specific, in-domain and human-supervised; benchmarking should inform rather than substitute for deployment decisions.
