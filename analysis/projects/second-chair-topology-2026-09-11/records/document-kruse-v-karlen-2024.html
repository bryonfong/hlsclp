Corpus framing of this source: Kruse v. Karlen: dismissal for major briefing defects and fictitious AI-linked citations

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Missouri appellate court held that a self-represented appellant’s extensive procedural violations prevented meaningful review and required dismissal. It separately treated the submission of fictitious authorities—apparently produced with generative-AI assistance—as an abuse of the judicial system, stressed that AI does not excuse false legal citations, and warned represented and self-represented parties that the court will not permit such conduct.

Corpus source-specific finding

The court dismissed the appeal because multiple major briefing violations prevented meaningful appellate review, not because of minor technical imperfections.

Exact returned source excerpt

Despite our preference to review the merits where only minor shortcomings do not impede meaningful review, “[d]eficient briefing runs the risk of forcing this Court to assume the role of advocate by requiring us to sift through the legal record, reconstruct the statement of facts, and craft a legal argument on the appellant's behalf.” Hutcheson, 656 S.W.3d at 41 (internal quotation omitted).

Exact returned source excerpt

Here, numerous major violations of the Rules of Appellate Procedures preclude meaningful review.
