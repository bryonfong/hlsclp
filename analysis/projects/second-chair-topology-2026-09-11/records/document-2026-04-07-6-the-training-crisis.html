Open topic: AI simulation: Practice substitutes remain unvalidated

Corpus framing of this source: The Training Crisis: combining simulation with consequential practice

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Training Crisis argues that real client work offers learning conditions simulations cannot fully reproduce, without defending existing apprenticeship arrangements as necessary or effective. It urges the profession to develop a mixed training model as traditional junior work potentially shrinks. Its reasoning rests on practical examples and a pilot-training analogy rather than reported empirical findings.

Corpus source-specific finding

Real consequences change how junior lawyers learn.

Exact returned source excerpt

The pressure of real consequences sharpens attention in ways that practice exercises may not.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

Valuing practice does not justify preserving current apprenticeship arrangements.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

As David often points out, no one has ever demonstrated that bleary-eyed associates reading documents at 3AM in a windowless warehouse in Phoenix Arizona in August is an efficient or effective way of teaching how to spot attorney-client privilege material, let alone doing this multiple times over the course of years.

Corpus source-specific finding

The profession should investigate a combined training model rather than choose between simulation and apprenticeship.

Exact returned source excerpt

The question isn't simulation versus apprenticeship.

Exact returned source excerpt

Instead, the question is what combination --- accelerated simulations, deliberate mentorship, real-world exposure, AI-assisted feedback --- can produce sufficient domain expertise in a sufficient number of junior lawyers if the volume of traditional junior work shrinks.
