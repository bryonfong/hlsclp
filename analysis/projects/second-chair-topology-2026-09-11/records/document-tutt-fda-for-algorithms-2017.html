Corpus framing of this source: An FDA for Algorithms: opacity, safety and regulatory standards

Corpus synthesis of this source: An FDA for Algorithms argues that machine learning’s probabilistic, emergent operation makes failures difficult to anticipate and explain. It uses Watson, Tesla and pharmaceutical analogies to frame safety problems, and proposes federal explainability and liability standards while acknowledging technical uncertainty and policy trade-offs.

Corpus framing of this source: An FDA for Algorithms

Corpus synthesis of this source: The article makes an early case for a specialised federal regulator for sophisticated algorithms, modelled partly on the FDA. It argues that machine learning’s growing capability, opacity, unpredictability, potential dangerousness and diffuse responsibility make existing state, tort and sector-specific regulation inadequate. It proposes risk-tiered standards, transparency, liability rules and possible pre-market approval, while acknowledging innovation trade-offs, capture risks and unresolved institutional-design questions.
