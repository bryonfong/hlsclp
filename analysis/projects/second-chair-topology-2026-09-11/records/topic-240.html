Agent benchmarks: Protocol scores are not competence

Corpus synthesis: Requires validated scoring, controlled environments, cost and uncertainty measures, with automated judges separately evaluated.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Evidence for deployed AI: Proxy measures do not establish real-world effectiveness · Benchmark validity: Workplace realism strains reproducible scoring

8 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 5 · Supports: 2 · Unclear: 1

Native topic findings

  1. Agent benchmark scores measure performance under a protocol, not professional competence or deployment readiness by themselves.
  2. Comparability requires validated scoring, controlled environments and clear attribution of performance gains.
  3. Realistic interaction, unseen-task testing and benchmark maintenance solve different validity problems.
  4. Useful comparisons must measure accuracy alongside operating cost and statistical uncertainty.
  5. Automated judges promise scalable diagnosis, but their validity remains a separate question.
  6. Benchmark limitations can understate assisted usefulness as well as overstate autonomous capability.
  7. Better benchmarks support governance, but the governance reviews reject benchmark-only assurance.
