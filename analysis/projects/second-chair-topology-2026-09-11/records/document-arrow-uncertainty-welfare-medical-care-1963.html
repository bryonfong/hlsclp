Corpus synthesis of this source: In Uncertainty and the Welfare Economics of Medical Care, Arrow develops a welfare-economic case for broader insurance while examining incentive problems, administrative costs, unequal risks and incomplete coverage. Corpus framing of this source: Uncertainty, trust and the economic role of professional institutions

Corpus synthesis of this source: In Uncertainty and the Welfare Economics of Medical Care, Arrow explains medical-market institutions as responses to uncertain outcomes, unequal information and incomplete insurance. He argues that trust, professional ethics and entry controls can offer substitute guarantees while also restricting competition. The contribution is a qualified theoretical framework supported by stylized observations and reported evidence, not a definitive policy programme; Arrow explicitly extends parts of the reasoning to other professions.

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source explains why medical care departs from the competitive-market model. It links illness uncertainty, incomplete insurance, information asymmetry, trust, externalities, moral hazard, increasing returns and professional norms to distinctive institutions and argues that some conditions support collective intervention or broad insurance. It qualifies several empirical and predictive claims and separates its own reasoning from positions it reports and critiques.
