Corpus framing of this source: A six-step governance programme for EU AI Act readiness

Corpus synthesis of this source: Julia Apostle and Sarah Schaedler of Orrick offer practical compliance advice ahead of 2 August 2026. Their November 2025 article interprets the Act and European Commission guidance rather than presenting empirical research. It recommends mapping AI, identifying organisational roles and applicable rules, classifying risk, updating contracts and embedding these assessments in ongoing governance.

# The EU AI Act: 6 Steps to Take Before 2 August 2026 *Julia Apostle, Sarah Schaedler — Orrick Herrington & Sutcliffe LLP (2025)* 8 minute | November.10.2025 The EU AI Act’s provisions have started to apply, including those relating to prohibited AI systems and general-purpose AI models (GPAIM).

Build a documented, repeatable inventory based on functionality and legal definitions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Use risk classification alongside role allocation as the foundation for governance.

Exact returned source excerpt

Understanding the risk classification of AI systems and AI models, as well as the company’s role in relation to these, is the foundation for AI governance (see Step 6).

Exact returned source excerpt

A company should determine whether any of the AI systems it uses or develops may be classified as prohibited or high risk under the Act.

Corpus source-specific finding

Update transactions and contracts to address compliance and changing responsibility.

Exact returned source excerpt

Make sure any in-progress agreements and acquisitions covering AI products will reflect the requirements of the AI Act when the Act comes into force.

Exact returned source excerpt

This will likely require changes to contract terms as well as due diligence and revised procurement processes.

Corpus source-specific finding

Make governance a leadership-backed, cross-functional and continuing compliance process.

Exact returned source excerpt

“AI governance” refers to the company’s internal policies and processes to ensure its use and development of AI aligns with its mission, risk profile, legal obligations and ethical priorities.

Exact returned source excerpt

An effective AI governance framework also requires support from senior leadership.
