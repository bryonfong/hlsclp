Corpus framing of this source: TREC 2008 Legal Track: realistic e-discovery evaluation, promising retrieval results and measurement limits

Corpus synthesis of this source: The overview evaluates automated and interactive retrieval of litigation documents, rather than legal advice generation. Its principal contribution combines more practice-oriented evaluation designs with evidence that some automated approaches beat a negotiated Boolean baseline, while stressing attorney interaction, assessment uncertainty and limits on generalising from the test collection.

Corpus source-specific finding

Evaluate retrieval against discovery obligations and human review costs.

Exact returned source excerpt

The key goal of the TREC Legal Track is to develop and apply objective criteria for comparing methods for searching large heterogeneous collections using topics that approximate how real lawyers would go about propounding discovery in civil litigation, and to create a large, representative (unstructured and heteroge- neous) test collection.

Exact returned source excerpt

Important aspects of this task include a focus on returning sets of documents for subsequent human review (rather than ranked lists), the need to accommodate topics that return relatively ^Llarge result sets (which necessitates sampling for assessment), the importance of recall in those result sets (since in real settings many requests for production state that “all” such evidence is to be produced), and the importance of precision in those result sets (to reduce unnecessary review costs).

A Estimation of Metrics — Interactive Task As described more fully above (Section 4.2), evaluation in Interactive Task follows a four-step protocol, whereby (i) stratified samples are drawn for each of the target topics, (ii) a first-pass manual review is conducted of each of the samples, (iii) those first-pass assessments are, if a participating team chooses, appealed and adjudicated, and (iv) estimates of each team’s performance, as measured by recall, precision, and F1 , are obtained.

Corpus framing of this source: Overview of the TREC 2008 Legal Track

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source reports the 2008 Ad Hoc, Relevance Feedback and redesigned Interactive e-discovery retrieval tasks. Its main contribution is an evaluation framework that combines realistic production-oriented outputs, sampling-based recall/precision/F1 estimates, a new materiality-oriented relevance category, attorney-led topic clarification and adjudication. It finds promising automated and interactive results, but stresses assessor disagreement, OCR quality, topic breadth, threshold constraints and small samples as important limits.

Corpus source-specific finding

The track redesigned evaluation around operational e-discovery outputs and balanced effectiveness measures.

Exact returned source excerpt

Shifting the principal evaluation measure in the Ad Hoc task from recall to F1 and shifting the reference point from one specified by us (B) to one specified by each participating team (K) resulted in greater task fidelity, modeling the real operational requirement of e-discovery systems to return a set of documents, not just an unbounded ranked list.

Exact returned source excerpt

• The concept of “highly relevant” documents as a third category for purposes of assessment was intro- duced (in addition to last year’s “relevant” and “not relevant”).
