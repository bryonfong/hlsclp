Corpus source-specific finding

The response classifies the transaction as a non-consumer finance lease.

Exact returned source excerpt

A "finance lease" requires three things, all satisfied here: - **(i) The lessor did not "select, manufacture, or supply the goods."** Client picked the model and negotiated the refrigeration specs directly with Manufacturer; Lessor merely bought the units.

Exact returned source excerpt

✔ - **(ii) "The lessor acquires the goods… in connection with the lease."** Lessor purchased the Trailers from Manufacturer for $85,000 each specifically to lease them to Client.

Corpus source-specific finding

Acceptance makes rent independent of equipment defects under both statute and contract.

Exact returned source excerpt

Client accepted the Trailers — it took delivery and operated them for roughly four months before the compressor failures (acceptance under §§ 8.2A‑515/‑516).

Exact returned source excerpt

Client cannot stop paying — the statutory and contractual "hell or high water" rule **Statutory rule — § 8.2A‑407.** "In the case of a finance lease that is not a consumer lease the lessee's promises under the lease contract become **irrevocable and independent upon the lessee's acceptance of the goods**" (subsection (1)).

Corpus source-specific finding

The response places warranty liability and manufacturer-insolvency risk on the supplier side, not the financier.

Exact returned source excerpt

Virginia law **expressly excludes finance leases from the implied warranties** of merchantability (§ 8.2A‑212) and fitness (§ 8.2A‑213), the Lessor made **no express warranty** (§ 8.2A‑210; the Lease contains none), and the Lease channels equipment claims to Manufacturer.

Exact returned source excerpt

2A‑209(1), "the benefit of a supplier's promises… and of all warranties, whether express or implied, under the supply contract, extends to the lessee to the extent of the lessee's leasehold interest under a finance lease… but is subject to the terms of the warranty and of the supply contract and all defenses or claims arising therefrom." Manufacturer's two‑year warranty on the mechanical and refrigeration components therefore runs through to Client, and Client retains any independent rights it has against Manufacturer (§ 8.2A‑209(4)).

Corpus source-specific finding

The response acknowledges validity defenses but finds none available on the stated facts.

Exact returned source excerpt

No applicable escape hatch on these facts The hell‑or‑high‑water rule does **not** bar defenses going to the **formation or validity of the lease itself** — e.g., fraud in the inducement or unconscionability (*C & J Vantage*, recognizing such carve‑outs; see also Va.

Exact returned source excerpt

But nothing in the facts suggests any wrongdoing by Lessor: Lessor delivered/paid for conforming trailers and **affirmatively gave Client the complete supply contract disclosing Manufacturer's warranty terms before signing.

Corpus source-specific finding

The response recommends continued payment or a negotiated workout alongside bankruptcy and third-party claims.

Exact returned source excerpt

**Consequence of stopping payment.** Ceasing payments puts Client **in default**, exposing it to acceleration, repossession, and a deficiency claim plus the Lessor's other remedies under the Lease and §§ 8.2A‑523 *et seq.* Withholding rent is not a permissible self‑help remedy here.

Exact returned source excerpt

### Practical bottom line Client should **keep paying Lessor** (or negotiate a workout) and direct its defect remedies to **Manufacturer's bankruptcy estate** and any other responsible third parties.
