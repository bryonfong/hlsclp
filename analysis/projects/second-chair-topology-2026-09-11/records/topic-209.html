Native topic record

AI procurement: Deployed outcomes outrank adoption counts

Corpus synthesis: Advocates lifecycle evaluation, tailored contracts, data rights and exit capacity instead of vendor demonstrations or purchase price alone.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Evidence for deployed AI: Proxy measures do not establish real-world effectiveness · AI governance implementation: Formal oversight must become enforceable lifecycle control

3 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 3

Native topic findings

  1. This cluster treats AI governance as a procurement and institutional-learning problem: buyers need evidence of deployed value, not adoption counts or vendor demonstrations.
  2. Evaluation should measure real-world outcomes against existing practice and continue throughout deployment.
  3. Procurement speed creates a trade-off with tailored safeguards, rather than a clear choice between good and bad acquisition methods.
  4. Contracts determine whether buyers can test systems, protect data and hold vendors accountable.
  5. Value for money depends on lifecycle costs, continued performance and the ability to exit—not simply the initial purchase price.
  6. The central institutional disagreement concerns whether existing legal processes can deliver meaningful oversight or merely formal compliance.
  7. Institutional learning must capture failures and feed later purchasing decisions; repositories alone cannot supply that knowledge.

Corpus synthesis of this source: In Artificial Intelligence Acquisitions: Agencies Should Collect and Apply Lessons Learned to Improve Future Procurements, GAO examines procurement trade-offs, six recurring acquisition challenges, and gaps in institutional learning. Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Adoption at scale (2025)

Corpus framing of this source: M-25-22: Performance-based, accountable federal AI procurement

Corpus synthesis of this source: M-25-22 sets requirements and recommendations across the federal AI acquisition lifecycle. It treats efficiency gains as an opportunity contingent on fit-for-purpose systems, competition, testing and risk management—not as an empirically demonstrated result. Its contribution is an operational procurement framework grounded in existing federal authorities and M-25-21, rather than a study of AI performance.

Corpus framing of this source: Making real-world AI evaluation a routine obligation through public law

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Law of Evaluation: Beyond Benchmarks for AI Governance argues that AI oversight should combine existing administrative and procurement obligations with causal testing, ongoing quality assurance and independent evaluation. It documents gaps between legal authority and agency practice, proposes risk-tiered evaluation and institutional funding, and recommends enforceable testing rights for public and private buyers.
