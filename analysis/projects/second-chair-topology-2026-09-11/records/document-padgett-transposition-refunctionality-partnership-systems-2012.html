Open topic: Organisational invention: Cross-domain ties reshape institutions

Corpus framing of this source: Florentine partnership systems as inventions of interconnected social networks

Corpus synthesis of this source: John F. Padgett explains the emergence of Florentine partnership systems through feedback among business, political and kinship networks. His historical account distinguishes organizational invention from incremental innovation, links partnership formation to post-Ciompi political mobilization, and traces its institutionalization through elite marriage and social class. He offers a mechanism-based explanation rather than a predictive theory or an account of inevitable modernization.

Corpus framing of this source: Transposition and Refunctionality in Florentine Organizations and Partnerships

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source develops a network-coevolution account of organizational change and applies it to Renaissance Florentine banking partnerships. It argues that people carry ties and learned interaction rules across organizational and social domains, reshaping both organizations and themselves. Its empirical analysis finds that political mobilization affected cambio banking but not international merchant-bankers’ partnering, while natal-family influence declined without disappearing and in-law relationships gained economic importance.
