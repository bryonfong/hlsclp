Corpus framing of this source: Federal judges’ AI use: broad experimentation, limited routine adoption and divided expectations

Corpus synthesis of this source: Artificial Intelligence in Federal Courts: A Random-Sample Survey of Judges reports self-reported adoption, applications, training, chambers policies and attitudes. It finds that legal research leads judicial AI use, while limited training, uncertain oversight and reliability concerns complicate adoption. The authors distinguish survey findings from their interpretations and respondents’ predictions, and acknowledge sampling and measurement limitations.

Corpus framing of this source: Federal judges’ AI use, governance and attitudes: a random-sample survey

Corpus synthesis of this source: The study contributes survey evidence on how responding federal judges and chambers personnel use AI, which tools and tasks they favour, how chambers govern use, and how judges assess benefits and risks. It finds broad but generally infrequent adoption, concentrated in legal research and document review; uneven training and policies; a moderate association between personal and professional use; and an almost even division between optimism and concern. It supplements aggregate results with judges’ reported reasoning, predictions and recommendations, while stressing sampling, measurement and generalisability limits.
