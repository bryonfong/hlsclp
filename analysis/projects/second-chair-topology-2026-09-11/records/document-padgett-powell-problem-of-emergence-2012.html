Corpus framing of this source: Organizational invention through interacting, self-reproducing networks

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Problem of Emergence develops a relational theory of organizational novelty: practices move between domains, and some innovations reshape wider systems through feedback carried by people and skills. The authors combine historical case studies with autocatalytic models, distinguish organizational reproduction from invention, and identify system susceptibility to tipping as an unresolved research problem.

Corpus framing of this source: The Problem of Emergence: Network Folding and Organizational Novelty

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source develops a relational theory of organizational emergence by combining social-network analysis, autocatalytic models inspired by biochemistry, agent-based simulations and historical case studies. It explains novelty as the transposition and propagation of networked people, practices and protocols across overlapping domains, while emphasizing that resilient systems normally resist change and that shocks, turnover and other catalysts have effects conditional on network structure.
