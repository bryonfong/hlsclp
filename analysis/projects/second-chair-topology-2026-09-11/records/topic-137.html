Native topic findings

  1. Human–model agreement supports AI-based measurement, but does not establish ground truth or real-world impact.
  2. Validation results vary substantially by classification task, limiting broad claims of human-level performance.
  3. Fine-grained taxonomies expose a tension between exact agreement and economically defensible labeling.
  4. Missing context and imperfect representations of work can limit validity even when annotators agree.
  5. Sampling, annotation guidance and metric choice constrain how far validation results transfer.
  6. The supplied validation evidence does not establish satisfaction, task success or a reliance–outcome relationship.
