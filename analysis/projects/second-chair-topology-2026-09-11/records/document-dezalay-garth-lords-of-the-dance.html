Corpus framing of this source: Elite legal brokers connect institutional change to the reproduction of power

Corpus synthesis of this source: The article develops a Bourdieusian, comparative-historical account of elite lawyers as double agents operating within professional hierarchies and wider fields of power. It argues that their combinations of family connections, legal learning and political resources let them broker—and contain—institutional change. European histories provide the main evidence; imperial relationships and US-led globalization extend the argument, with explicit limits on its generality.

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source uses a field-theoretical and historical account to explain how elite lawyers and legal scholars help reconstruct state power while cultivating the legal field’s legitimacy and relative autonomy. It applies this account to medieval institutional change, imperial expansion and the conservative organization of French legal education, while reporting Christophe Jamin’s call to modernize legal teaching.
