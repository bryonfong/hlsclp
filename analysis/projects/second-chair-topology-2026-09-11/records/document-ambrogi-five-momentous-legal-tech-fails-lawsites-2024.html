Corpus framing of this source: ROSS: early promise, litigation-driven shutdown and a continuing legal dispute

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Five Most Momentous Legal Tech Fails traces ROSS’s rise and attributes its operational collapse to the financing and acquisition constraints caused by Thomson Reuters’ lawsuit. The author distinguishes disputed allegations from business consequences and reports that litigation continued after shutdown.

Corpus source-specific finding

ROSS gained substantial early recognition and investor interest.

Exact returned source excerpt

It started in 2014 at the University of Toronto as a student-built entrant in a cognitive-computing competition staged by IBM to develop applications for its Watson computer.

Exact returned source excerpt

It quickly gained momentum and international attention, drawing major investors, including Denton’s NextLaw Labs, and its founders were invited to participate in the prestigious Y-Combinator startup incubator.

Corpus source-specific finding

The author attributes ROSS’s shutdown to the lawsuit’s commercial effects, not to an established finding of wrongdoing.

Exact returned source excerpt

But the outlook for ROSS changed almost overnight when it was [sued by Thomson Reuters in 2020](https://www.lawnext.com/2020/05/thomson-reuters-sues-ross-intelligence-claiming-theft-of-proprietary-data.html) on allegations that it surreptitiously stole content from Westlaw to build its own competing legal research product.

Exact returned source excerpt

While ROSS vehemently denied the allegations, the lawsuit crippled its ability to raise new financing or explore potential acquisition opportunities.

Corpus source-specific finding

ROSS’s closure did not end the litigation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Yet even though the company is no longer operating, it continues to fight the lawsuit, with its defense and counterclaims funded by insurance coverage.

Exact returned source excerpt

As of this writing, the lawsuit is ongoing.

Corpus framing of this source: Cloud practice-management failures and Gavelytics’ unexpected closure

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source uses Firm Manager and Gavelytics to illustrate prominent legal-technology failures. It traces Firm Manager’s entry into an increasingly crowded cloud practice-management market, its technical and commercial difficulties, its attempted rebuild and eventual closure. It also presents Gavelytics’ shutdown as surprising because the company had appeared to be a growing litigation-analytics leader. The supplied text ends with an incomplete fundraising figure and offers no recommendations.

Corpus source-specific finding

Cloud-based law practice management became an increasingly crowded market before and after LexisNexis entered it.

Exact returned source excerpt

The year 2008 saw the launches of the first two cloud-based law practice management platforms, Clio and Rocket Matter, followed in 2009 by the launch of MyCase.

Exact returned source excerpt

In the years that followed, a number of similar products came to market, such as PracticePanther, Zola Suite (now CARET Legal), and CosmoLex.

Corpus source-specific finding

Firm Manager suffered early performance and adoption problems, and its later reconstruction did not prevent withdrawal.

Exact returned source excerpt

Unfortunately, the product got off to a rocky start, with major performance issues, and it had difficulty gaining traction in what was fast becoming a crowded market.

Exact returned source excerpt

It later went back to the drawing board and rebuilt the product almost from the ground up, releasing the retooled version in 2016 as Firm Manager 2.0.

Corpus source-specific finding

Gavelytics’ 2022 shutdown appeared surprising despite warning signs known to its founder.

Exact returned source excerpt

Gavelytics.** When the litigation analytics company Gavelytics shut down in 2022, it was a shock to almost everybody but the founder.

Before closing, Gavelytics had been regarded as a litigation-analytics leader and had expanded its product.

Exact returned source excerpt
