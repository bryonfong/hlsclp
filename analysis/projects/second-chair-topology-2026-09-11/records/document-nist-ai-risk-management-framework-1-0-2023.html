Corpus framing of this source: NIST AI RMF 1.0: contextual, continuous governance of AI risks

Corpus synthesis of this source: NIST offers voluntary, cross-sector guidance for managing AI’s potential harms and benefits throughout its lifecycle. Its contribution is an operational framework, not an empirical demonstration of effectiveness: it connects governance, contextual assessment, measurement and risk response while acknowledging uncertain metrics, organizational constraints and unresolved questions about human oversight.

Corpus source-specific finding

The framework promotes responsible AI through flexible risk management rather than uniform requirements.

Exact returned source excerpt

1.1 Understanding and Addressing Risks, Impacts, and Harms In the context of the AI RMF, risk refers to the composite measure of an event’s probability of occurring and the magnitude or degree of the consequences of the corresponding event.

Exact returned source excerpt

When considering the negative impact of a potential event, risk is a function of 1) the negative impact, or magni- tude of harm, that would arise if the circumstance or event occurs and 2) the likelihood of occurrence (Adapted from: OMB Circular A-130:2016).

AI risks – and benefits – can emerge from the interplay of technical aspects combined with societal factors related to how a system is used, its interactions with other AI systems, who operates it, and the social context in which it is deployed.

Corpus framing of this source: NIST AI RMF 1.0: A voluntary, lifecycle-wide framework for managing AI risk

Corpus synthesis of this source: NIST contributes a practical, rights-preserving and non-prescriptive framework for organizations that design, develop, deploy or use AI. It treats AI risk as socio-technical and context-dependent, organizes risk management into GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE and MANAGE, and supplements the Core with use-case and temporal Profiles. The document primarily supplies normative guidance and structured practices rather than empirical proof of effectiveness; it acknowledges unsettled metrics, shifting risk tolerances, tradeoffs among trustworthiness characteristics and open research questions about human-AI interaction.
