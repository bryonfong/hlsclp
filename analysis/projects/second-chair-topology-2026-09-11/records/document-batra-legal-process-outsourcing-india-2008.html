Corpus framing of this source: Global convergence, knowledge management and professional values

Corpus synthesis of this source: Legal Process Outsourcing In India describes a legal sector reshaped by global consolidation and overlapping professional services. It recommends treating legal practice as a knowledge-based business and studying market changes while preserving professional values. The excerpt offers qualitative observations rather than documented empirical findings.

Global expansion and cross-professional competition blur the boundaries of legal practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

Increasingly, law firms are merging into large global enterprises that often also provide ancillary services such as investment advice, consulting services, and venture capital.

Exact returned source excerpt

Simultaneously, other professional service firms including accounting firms, investment banks and consulting firms have begun to offer services that overlap with those offered by lawyers, and in some parts of the world, legal services themselves.

Corpus framing of this source: India’s legal outsourcing opportunity: cost advantages, expanding capabilities and governance barriers

Corpus synthesis of this source: Hemant Batra’s India Law Journal article presents legal process outsourcing (LPO) as a major opportunity for Indian legal-service providers. He combines definitions, illustrative cost comparisons and reported industry forecasts with warnings about confidentiality, privilege and political resistance. His optimistic outlook depends on stronger security, supervision and the industry overcoming these barriers; the article’s legal and market claims describe its historical context, not current conditions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Globalisation and professional adaptation create opportunities for Indian legal providers.

Exact returned source excerpt

Globalization has thus expanded the internal and external demand for legal services.

Exact returned source excerpt

These new Law firms in India primarily engage and specialize in designing equity and loan instruments, writing infrastructural contracts, power contracts, drafting of project finance contracts, finalizing transnational investments, joint ventures and technology transfer contracts.

Corpus source-specific finding

Batra defines LPO as specialised knowledge work that clients could otherwise perform internally.

Exact returned source excerpt

Legal Process Outsourcing (LPO) is a highly specialized aspect of Knowledge Process Outsourcing (KPO).

Exact returned source excerpt

According to me, it’s an act or process by which a customer/client out-sources or contracts out fiscal or accounting or consulting or legal work which in the ordinary course of business practice is capable of being handled/executed in-house by the concerned customer/client or at their own end, as the case may be.

Corpus source-specific finding

LPO has moved beyond transcription into documentation and litigation support.

Exact returned source excerpt

Documentation would include commercial agreements, transactional documents, trans-national agreements, regulatory documents, business advisory services, charters etc.

Exact returned source excerpt

Litigation Support Services cover case law research, docketing, preparation of pleadings etc.

Corpus source-specific finding

Labour-cost differences underpin the commercial case, illustrated rather than established through measured outcomes.

Exact returned source excerpt

For instance, a US based FMCG Corporation might engage a LPO to examine packages of 1000 different items manufactured by them.

Exact returned source excerpt

By way of another example, a US based tobacco company fighting a legal battle in Australia may require British judgments in support of their contention before an Australian Court.

Corpus source-specific finding

Reported forecasts and patent-service activity support an optimistic growth narrative, not demonstrated future outcomes.

Exact returned source excerpt

According to a study by the United States-based Forester Research, the current annual value of legal outsourcing which is worth $80 million can rise up to $4 billion and can fetch 79,000 jobs in India by 2015.

Exact returned source excerpt

According to the BPO Council of the Associated Chambers of Commerce and Industry (ASSOCHAM), "India is already known for its large and increasing pool of quality engineering graduates, many of who are being attracted to the patent services segment of legal BPO." Estimates state that by early 2006, there were over 400 professionals engaged in providing patent services such as literature searches, prior-art searches, technology and patentability assessment, patent claim mapping, etc, from India.

Corpus source-specific finding

Confidentiality, privilege and political resistance constrain cross-border expansion.

Exact returned source excerpt

A major hindering factor is the reluctance of foreign clients to outsource work to India, based primarily on worries about the security and confidentiality of their information, given that data-protection and confidentiality laws have not been codified yet in India.

Exact returned source excerpt

Another area of concern for foreign clients is the loss of attorney-client privilege available in their home jurisdictions, since the Indian Advocates Act, 1961 does not extend this privilege to foreign legal work outsourced to India.

Corpus source-specific finding

Stronger security and active supervision are necessary conditions for viable outsourcing.

Exact returned source excerpt

Presently, India does not have codified data protection laws, but various commercial forums and associations, such as NASSCOM, are working in close association with the government to enact data protection laws that are on par with those in force internationally.

Exact returned source excerpt

It is certain that Indian companies have realized that they need to scale up their security mechanics in order to cater to and allay these concerns.
