Buyer authority: In-house growth need not displace firms

Corpus synthesis: Distinguishes purchasing power, strategic integration, spending, professional independence and internationally varied organizational change.

Native topic findings

  1. The in-house counsel movement shifted authority toward corporate buyers, not simply legal work away from firms.
  2. Greater purchasing power does not establish lower spending or displacement of outside firms.
  3. Strategic integration supports the authority claim, but superior advice and independence remain distinct, unproven propositions.
  4. International diffusion shows organizational change, but not uniform convergence on the U.S. model.
  5. In-house careers broadened, but professional appeal should not be confused with employment security.
  6. Women’s representation rose alongside in-house prestige, but the sources disagree on how far this demonstrates equality.
  7. Turnover and institutional memory qualify the prediction that empowered buyers will continually replace incumbent firms.
