AI supervision: Retained responsibility is not strict liability

Corpus synthesis: Requires provider scrutiny, instructions and proportionate systems while distinguishing delegated error from automatic discipline.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Legal institutions and professional practice: Substantive outcomes depend on context, capacity and judgment · Delegated legal work: responsibility follows the provider chain without becoming strict liability

18 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 4 · Supports: 14

Native topic findings

  1. Delegation and AI use change how legal work is performed, not lawyers’ continuing duties of competence and supervision.
  2. Reasonable supervision requires proportionate systems, provider scrutiny and clear instructions—not a uniform review procedure.
  3. Retained responsibility does not mean automatic disciplinary liability for every delegated error.
  4. AI guidance converges on proactive governance but differs in prescription and in how it characterises the tool.
  5. Cassata gives concrete judicial force to verification, technological competence and prompt correction.
  6. Consent and responsibility allocation depend on the service and cannot displace other legal obligations.
  7. Permission to delegate work is distinct from permission for nonlawyer ownership; Arizona changes the ownership framework while retaining lawyer-led safeguards.
