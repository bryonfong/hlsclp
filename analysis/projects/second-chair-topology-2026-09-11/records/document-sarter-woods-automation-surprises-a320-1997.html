Open topic: Automation safety: Coordination matters beyond workload reduction

Corpus framing of this source: Automation safety depends on human–machine coordination

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source interprets cockpit automation difficulties as recurring failures of human–machine cooperation, not isolated glitches or a simple choice between blaming people and blaming automation. It draws on aviation investigations and related medical studies to recommend human-centered design. Much of the supplied material consists of bibliographies and repeated publication lists rather than additional substantive findings.

Builds onWork redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Automation safety requires coordinated design, observable behaviour and sustained human expertise

Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied substantive excerpts come from Woods and Sarter’s 1998 NASA companion report, Learning from Automation Surprises and "Going Sour" Accidents, rather than the separately listed 1997 Human Factors article. The authors synthesise aviation research and operational evidence to explain automation failures as human–machine coordination breakdowns. They acknowledge automation’s economic and safety benefits while recommending better feedback, cooperative control, continuous learning and coordinated organisational change.
