Human response: Stronger assistance can reduce effort

Native topic findings

  1. AI assistance changes human effort and decisions, so stronger assistance does not guarantee higher productivity.
  2. Contrasting productivity predictions reflect different model assumptions, not an empirical disagreement.
  3. Verification accuracy and signal use condition the benefits of combining human expertise with AI.
  4. The contract and credence-goods contributions establish formal equilibrium restrictions, not direct claims about AI adoption.
  5. The practical implication is to test behavioural responses and joint performance rather than infer gains from AI capability alone.
