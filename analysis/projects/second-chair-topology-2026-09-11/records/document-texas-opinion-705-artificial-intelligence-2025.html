Corpus framing of this source: Texas Opinion 705: Lawyers retain responsibility for AI-assisted work

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Texas Professional Ethics Committee applies existing professional duties to generative AI, emphasising verification, competence, client interests and awareness of court-specific requirements. It presents its guidance as a non-exhaustive snapshot, supported by reported sanctions for fabricated citations rather than measured model error rates.

Corpus framing of this source: Texas Ethics Opinion 705 on AI-assisted billing and disclosure

Corpus synthesis of this source: The opinion addresses how generative AI affects hourly legal fees and notes a federal appellate decision declining a special AI-disclosure rule for briefs. It distinguishes compensable time actually spent using and verifying AI output from impermissible billing for time saved through greater efficiency.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI may improve legal-service efficiency and affect fees

Exact returned source excerpt

**Fees** It’s not hard to imagine how the effective use of generative AI tools might impact the fees that lawyers charge—after all, one of the most promising aspects of these tools is the possibility for lawyers to provide legal services more efficiently.

Corpus source-specific finding

Lawyers generally may bill for time actually spent using and verifying AI output

Exact returned source excerpt

In most typical hourly arrangements (depending on the agreement), a lawyer will likely be able to charge the client for the actual time the lawyer spends using a generative AI program for purposes of the representation, including to refine the program’s outputs and check the work.

Lawyers may not bill hourly for AI-generated time savings

Exact returned source excerpt

A lawyer may not, however, charge hourly fees for the time that was “saved” by using the generative AI program.

Exact returned source excerpt

As the District of Columbia Bar Association explained: [I]t goes without saying that a lawyer who has undertaken to bill on an hourly basis is never justified in charging a client for hours not actually expended.
