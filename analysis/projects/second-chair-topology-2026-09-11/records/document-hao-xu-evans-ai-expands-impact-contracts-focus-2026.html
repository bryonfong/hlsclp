Corpus framing of this source: Artificial intelligence tools expand scientists' impact but contract science's focus

Corpus synthesis of this source: The study analyses AI adoption and its association with scientific productivity, visibility, careers, team composition and the breadth of collective inquiry. It finds rapid adoption and individual professional advantages alongside narrower topic coverage and weaker engagement across scientists, which it interprets as a tension between individual advancement and collective scientific reach.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors frame AI as increasingly embedded in scientific knowledge production, while noting both demonstrated technical applications and unresolved questions about its system-wide effects.

Exact returned source excerpt

Perhaps more than any other domain, AI tools have become deeply entwined with the process of knowledge production, yielding findings that attract disproportionate attention in various scientific fields [[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07727v4#bib.bibx1)].

For example, AlphaFold learns known protein structures to accurately predict the unexplored ones, circumventing the capital and human cost of conventional structural inference and recently granted a 2024 Nobel Prize [[9](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07727v4#bib.bibx9), [17](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07727v4#bib.bibx17)].

Corpus framing of this source: AI adoption expands scientific careers while concentrating collective research

Corpus synthesis of this source: Hao, Xu, Li and Evans analyse natural-science publications from 1980–2025 and report a tension between AI adopters’ professional gains and narrower collective exploration. They combine publication, career, citation-network and paper-embedding analyses, test alternative explanations and robustness, and qualify their preliminary generative-AI findings.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study examines AI use in natural science, not AI-method development, across three accelerating adoption waves.

Exact returned source excerpt

We conduct a large-scale quantitative analysis of the impact of AI on scientists and science, covering 41,298,433 research papers spanning from 1980 to 2025 in the OpenAlex dataset [[29](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07727v4#bib.bibx29)], with patterns corroborated using the Web of Science [[30](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07727v4#bib.bibx30), [31](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07727v4#bib.bibx31)].

Exact returned source excerpt

Notably, we do not focus on computer science or mathematics, fields that develop AI methodologies directly, but rather on papers that augment research in natural science fields by adopting AI, primarily covering decades involving development and deployment of conventional machine learning algorithms and also extending to a necessarily more preliminary analysis of the latest generative AI techniques.
