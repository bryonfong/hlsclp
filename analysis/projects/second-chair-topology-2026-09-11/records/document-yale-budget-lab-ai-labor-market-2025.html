Corpus framing of this source: Early AI labour-market evidence shows aggregate stability, with uncertain effects on younger workers

Corpus synthesis of this source: Martha Gimbel, Molly Kinder, Joshua Kendall and Maddie Lee’s 2025 Budget Lab analysis examines U.S. employment over the 33 months following ChatGPT’s release. They find no discernible economy-wide disruption, distinguish potential AI exposure from actual usage, and caution that aggregate stability neither rules out subgroup effects nor predicts AI’s eventual impact.

Corpus synthesis of this source: Evaluating the Impact of AI on the Labor Market: Current State of Affairs reports stable employment and unemployment patterns across AI-exposure measures. It stresses that usage estimates depend heavily on missing-task assumptions, warns against treating Claude users as representative of the economy, and calls for broader, privacy-protected data sharing.

Corpus source-specific finding

Employment shares across relative AI-exposure groups remain stable since ChatGPT’s launch.

Exact returned source excerpt

For our purposes, we categorize occupations into three groups using their exposure score quintile; an occupation has the lowest degree of exposure if it falls in the first two quintiles, a medium degree if in the 3rd and 4th quintiles, and the highest degree if in the top quintile.

Exact returned source excerpt

In other words, these metrics look at *relative* not *absolute* exposure.

Unemployment patterns show no clear increase in generative-AI exposure.
