Corpus framing of this source: Legal Services Board: adapting legal regulation for responsible AI use

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Legal Services Board argues that existing transparency duties provide a foundation for AI-related disclosure, while explainability, ethical use and lawyers’ risk-management capabilities should shape evolving regulation. It qualifies this approach by noting that AI adoption and risk vary across legal services, favouring regulator-specific assessment and mitigation.

Corpus framing of this source: Legal Services Board: technology-neutral, evidence-led oversight of AI regulation

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Legal Services Board argues that existing outcome-focused oversight and statutory guidance currently provide a proportionate basis for responsible AI use in legal services. It assigns frontline risk assessment to individual regulators, stresses transparency, explainability, ethics and continuing competence, and reserves stronger oversight or enforcement for evidence of regulatory failure. It also notes a potential regulatory-perimeter issue where an AI application developed by a regulated firm may not itself fall within legal-services regulation.

Corpus source-specific finding

The LSB favours outcome-based, technology-neutral oversight aligned with the Government’s non-prescriptive AI framework.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our approach aligns with the Government’s approach, as set out in DSIT’s phase 1 guidance on AI, which notes that the AI regulatory principles are voluntary and non- prescriptive, and that how they are considered is at regulators’ discretion10.

Exact returned source excerpt

Additionally, several of the considerations in the guidance touch on the AI regulatory principles set out in the Government’s framework on AI reflecting the importance of issues such as accountability, explainability, mechanisms for contestability and redress and fairness when developing regulatory frameworks.

Corpus source-specific finding

Existing sector activity supports a decentralised approach to AI guidance.

Exact returned source excerpt

Some of the legal service regulators and representative bodies have already been considering the benefits and risks of AI adoption within their segments of the legal services sector as part of their overall approaches to technology and innovation.

Exact returned source excerpt

This includes the publication of AI-related guidance and advice to assist their regulated communities, including: ▪ the SRA’s Risk Outlook Report on the use of artificial intelligence in the legal market (November 2023); ▪ the Law Society’s guidance on generative AI – Generative AI: the essentials (November 2023); ▪ the BSB’s Guidance on ChatGPT and safely and effectively navigating AI in legal practice (October 2023); and ▪ the Bar Council’s Guidance for Barristers on generative AI - Considerations when using ChatGPT and Generative AI Software based on large language models (January 2024).

Corpus source-specific finding

The LSB is integrating AI into broader competence and strategic policy work.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our wider policies, rules and guidance assist regulators to consider and address some of the broader regulatory risks that may be affected by the adoption and use of technology and AI in the sector.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, our policy statement on Ongoing Competence requires regulators to ensure that the legal professionals in their regulated communities remain competent throughout their careers – this could include using regulator ongoing competence requirements to ensure legal professionals understand how developments in technology, such as AI, and other innovations in the sector, impact their roles as legal professionals.
