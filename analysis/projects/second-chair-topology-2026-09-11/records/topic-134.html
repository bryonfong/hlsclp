Responsible AI: Risk governance resists fixed checklists

Corpus synthesis: Uses NIST’s contextual lifecycle framework, where measurement informs trade-offs and stopping decisions without defining acceptable risk.

Native topic findings

  1. NIST frames responsible AI as continuous, context-specific risk governance rather than compliance with a uniform checklist.
  2. Trustworthiness depends on deployment conditions and tradeoffs, not technical accuracy alone.
  3. Measurement should inform decisions without creating false certainty about operational risk.
  4. Risk management includes deciding against AI and stopping deployment, but the framework does not set acceptable risk thresholds.
  5. Human oversight is an application-specific control, not an automatic guarantee of safety.
  6. Implementation requires organizational commitment, while the framework’s effectiveness remains an evaluation question.

Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)

Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Generative shock (2023-2024)

Corpus framing of this source: NIST AI RMF 1.0: contextual, continuous governance of AI risks

Corpus synthesis of this source: NIST offers voluntary, cross-sector guidance for managing AI’s potential harms and benefits throughout its lifecycle. Its contribution is an operational framework, not an empirical demonstration of effectiveness: it connects governance, contextual assessment, measurement and risk response while acknowledging uncertain metrics, organizational constraints and unresolved questions about human oversight.

Corpus source-specific finding

The framework promotes responsible AI through flexible risk management rather than uniform requirements.

Exact returned source excerpt

1.1 Understanding and Addressing Risks, Impacts, and Harms In the context of the AI RMF, risk refers to the composite measure of an event’s probability of occurring and the magnitude or degree of the consequences of the corresponding event.

Exact returned source excerpt

When considering the negative impact of a potential event, risk is a function of 1) the negative impact, or magni- tude of harm, that would arise if the circumstance or event occurs and 2) the likelihood of occurrence (Adapted from: OMB Circular A-130:2016).

Corpus source-specific finding

AI risk arises from technical systems interacting with people and deployment contexts.

Exact returned source excerpt

AI risks – and benefits – can emerge from the interplay of technical aspects combined with societal factors related to how a system is used, its interactions with other AI systems, who operates it, and the social context in which it is deployed.

Exact returned source excerpt

AI systems, for example, may be trained on data that can change over time, sometimes significantly and unexpectedly, affecting system function- ality and trustworthiness in ways that are hard to understand.

Corpus source-specific finding

Trustworthiness requires balancing multiple characteristics in context.

Exact returned source excerpt

Characteristics of trustworthy AI systems include: valid and reliable, safe, secure and resilient, accountable and trans- parent, explainable and interpretable, privacy-enhanced, and fair with harmful bias managed.

Exact returned source excerpt

Creating trustworthy AI requires balancing each of these characteristics based on the AI system’s context of use.

Corpus source-specific finding

GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE and MANAGE form an iterative lifecycle process, not a checklist.

Exact returned source excerpt

As illus- trated in Figure 5, the Core is composed of four functions: GOVERN , MAP, MEASURE , and MANAGE.

Exact returned source excerpt

Governance is designed to be a cross-cutting function to inform and be infused throughout the other three functions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Context mapping and diverse participation should inform whether an AI system proceeds.

Exact returned source excerpt

After completing the MAP function, Framework users should have sufficient contextual knowledge about AI system impacts to inform an initial go/no-go decision about whether to design, develop, or deploy an AI system.

The information gathered while carrying out the MAP function enables negative risk pre- vention and informs decisions for processes such as model management, as well as an initial decision about appropriateness or the need for an AI solution.

Corpus source-specific finding
