SupportsLegal workflows: Immediate gains create competence risks

Open topic: Legal workflows: Immediate gains create competence risks

Corpus framing of this source: AI, Skill, and Productivity: The Case of Taxi Drivers

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source reframes AI’s labour-market impact around productivity and within-occupation skill differences rather than occupational exposure alone. It uses taxi drivers and an AI demand-forecasting navigation tool as a controlled setting for studying augmentation, then cautiously considers implications for other prediction-intensive jobs. The supplied excerpts describe the research question, setting, mechanism and qualifications, but do not provide the study’s estimated productivity effects by skill.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI may affect a different part of the skill distribution than earlier technologies

Exact returned source excerpt

The distributional consequences of AI could be fundamentally different from those of past technologies, such as IT and robotics, which are considered to be skill augmenting and inequality enhancing.1 Whereas past technologies have replaced the rou- tine and manual tasks of low-skilled workers, AI may replace non-routine cognitive tasks of typical high-skilled workers (Webb 2020).

Corpus source-specific finding

The study shifts attention from occupational displacement to productivity and skill heterogeneity within occupations

Exact returned source excerpt

Prior works on the labor-market consequences of AI focus primarily on job displacement across occupations–identifying the types of occupations that are more exposed and replace- able by AI.2 To better understand the impacts of AI on the labor market, however, the fundamental factor to consider is how productivity, which plays a crucial role in determin- ing employment and wages, is affected by AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

Furthermore, past studies implicitly assume that all workers within the occupation are uniformly affected by AI, missing substantial within-occupation heterogeneity of skills for the tasks that can be replaced by AI.3 To fill the gap, we study the impact of AI on productivity across different worker skills in the context of taxi drivers.

Corpus source-specific finding

Taxi driving provides a measurable and comparatively controlled empirical setting

Exact returned source excerpt

First, a worker’s individual productivity is easily measured by the length of time it takes to catch customers; each driver works independently and has considerable discretion as to how they find customers.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our data show that over one half of drivers’ working time is devoted to searching for (and the remaining to carrying) customers, excluding breaks.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI Navi augments customer-search decisions through demand prediction

Exact returned source excerpt

The particular AI we study is called “AI Navi,” which helps drivers find customers when a taxi is cruising.

Exact returned source excerpt

The AI suggests routes based on predicted demand to maximize the prob- ability of catching customers, given the current location.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors cautiously extend the mechanism to other prediction-intensive occupations

Exact returned source excerpt

To the extent that the core skill of jobs involves a prediction task from patterns of data, such as paralegals to identify unusual clauses and pathologists to detect malign tumors, our results may also be applicable to such occupations.

Exact returned source excerpt

Whether our finding can be generalized to other settings is left for future research.

Corpus source-specific finding

Full occupational displacement may be slower than task-level augmentation

Exact returned source excerpt

20 Although taxi drivers as an occupation might be completely displaced once self-driving cars with demand- forecasting AI is achieved, such a drastic transformation may take time, because the information required for driving tasks, such as the road environment, is much less regularized than information required for demand-forecasting tasks, such as passengers’ location.

Exact returned source excerpt

As Autor (2015) points out, automating a task is much costlier under a non-regularized environment than a regularized environment, and the cost is likely to exceed the wage saving.

Corpus source-specific finding

Demand expansion could turn greater convenience into broader welfare gains

Exact returned source excerpt

improved convenience stimulates further demand for taxis, the market could expand and social welfare might improve.

Corpus framing of this source: AI navigation reduces taxi search time, chiefly for lower-skilled drivers

Corpus synthesis of this source: AI, Skill, and Productivity: The Case of Taxi Drivers reports short-run productivity gains from AI-assisted customer search. Its central finding is that lower-skilled drivers benefit most, narrowing but not eliminating skill-based productivity differences. Supplementary analyses examine selective AI use, route compliance, model specification, learning over time and fares.

Corpus source-specific finding

Benefits appear immediately, with no detected additional improvement during the four-week trial.

Exact returned source excerpt

Table A3 shows that AI’s positive impact is immediate and observed already in the first two weeks, which is reassuring, because drivers are more likely to randomly experiment with turning it on and off at the beginning of the trial period, mitigating the concern that the timing of switching on AI could be endogenous to local demand.
