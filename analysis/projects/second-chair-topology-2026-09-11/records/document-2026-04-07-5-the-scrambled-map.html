Builds onAgentic infrastructure: Harvey tests vertical vendor durability

Open topic: Agentic infrastructure: Harvey tests vertical vendor durability

Corpus framing of this source: AI dissolves the boundaries between legal-service competitors, investors and buyers

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Scrambled Competitive Map argues that neither incumbent-versus-challenger nor copilot-versus-autopilot frameworks adequately explain legal-market change. Drawing on reported investments, product launches and acquisitions, the authors describe overlapping supplier roles, capital that shapes delivery and demand, and clients accumulating their own legal knowledge. They distinguish current incumbent strength from uncertain long-term durability and offer a diagnostic framework rather than a settled strategy.

Corpus source-specific finding

Record law-firm profits and structural disruption can coexist.

Exact returned source excerpt

Kirkland & Ellis --- the highest-grossing firm in the world --- is reported to be spending around \$500 million to build artificial intelligence it will own outright: more than \$100 million this year, hundreds of millions over three to four years, 180 technologists trained on how 250 of its own lawyers work, funded out of the firm's own revenue rather than licensed from anyone else.

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding
