Corpus framing of this source: Preserving independent legal judgment while preparing students to use AI

Corpus synthesis of this source: University of Chicago Law School’s AI strategy statement describes a curriculum that combines independent thinking, adaptable AI skills and selective experimentation. It grounds its approach in stakeholder consultation, a literature review and institutional experience, and distinguishes initiatives already introduced from planned course expansion and writing-assessment redesign.

Open topic: AI instruction: Timing divides educational reform

Corpus framing of this source: Chicago Law’s strategy: preserve independent reasoning while teaching responsible AI use

Corpus synthesis of this source: The University of Chicago Law School’s July 2026 strategy sets out differentiated AI policies for foundational courses, writing, electives and clinics. It draws on institutional experience, stakeholder consultation and a literature review, rather than reporting measured outcomes from its proposed pilots. Its central aim is to protect demanding human learning while preparing graduates to use, critique and supervise AI as technology changes.

The school grounds its strategy in consultation and existing curricular investment.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

We do not want to deter uses of AI that can *increase *students’ effort and engagement, such as asking AI to clarify background concepts while reading before class or asking AI to generate practice problems while studying.

Corpus synthesis of this source: The 2026 strategy statement proposes rapid but continually revisable adaptation to AI rather than prohibition. It combines AI-resilient assessment, preservation of independent legal reasoning and writing, instruction in supervising AI, greater emphasis on human-centred legal skills, pedagogical experimentation, clinical safeguards and explicit course-level policies. The statement supports its approach primarily with institutional initiatives and curriculum plans, while qualifying predictions about AI’s professional impact and acknowledging that fast technological change makes the strategy provisional.
