Corpus framing of this source: Nathan Lambert on reasoning-model generalisation and an RL renaissance

Corpus synthesis of this source: Lambert argues that chain-of-thought reasoning and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards will generalise beyond code and mathematics, potentially making RL a central method of language-model training. He grounds this thesis in chain of thought’s fit with transformer computation, improved RL infrastructure, DeepSeek-R1’s broad benchmark performance and early adoption signals, while qualifying it with domain, price and performance trade-offs and dependence on better generators and verifiers.

# Why reasoning models will generalize / An unexpected RL Renaissance (on RLVR) *Nathan Lambert — Interconnects (Substack); author at the Allen Institute for AI (Ai2), lead on Tülu 3 (2025)* ## Full Text - "Why reasoning models will generalize" (28 January 2025) *People underestimate the long-term potential of "reasoning."* This post is early to accommodate some last minute travel on my end!* The new models trained to express extended chain of thought are going to generalize outside of their breakthrough domains of code and math.

Exact returned source excerpt

Here is the only assumption of this piece — chain of thought is a natural fit for language models to “reason” and therefore one should be optimistic about training methods that are designed to enhance it generalizing to many domains.**[2](#footnote-2) By the end of 2025 we should have ample evidence of this given the pace of the technological development.

Corpus source-specific finding

Chain of thought helps transformers reason by decomposing problems, allocating computation and retaining intermediate state.

Exact returned source excerpt

We ask the model to work step by step because it helps it manage complexity, especially in domains where the answer requires precision across multiple specific tokens.

Exact returned source excerpt

Chain of thought reasoning allows information to be naturally processed in smaller chunks, allowing the large, brute force probability distribution to work one token at a time.

Corpus source-specific finding

Current results provide early evidence of benefits beyond conventional reasoning benchmarks.

Exact returned source excerpt

[ChatBotArena](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/chatbotarena-the-future-of-llm-evaluation) has all of the new models, from o1, Gemini-Thinking, and R1 as some of the top models these organizations have in the best “normal use” evaluation the AI community has.[3](#footnote-3) These reasoning models are definitely absorbing the other lessons learned in post-training across the AI industry.

Exact returned source excerpt

[DeepSeek-R1](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/deepseek-r1-recipe-for-o1), the new open-weight o1 replication has been showing up at the top of many random benchmarks as top overall, above Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini, and GPT-4o, and alongside o1.

Corpus source-specific finding

Generative verifiers are the proposed route for extending reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards into more domains.

Exact returned source excerpt

Generative verifiers will be crucial to expanding this training to countless domains — they’re easy to use and largely a new development.

Exact returned source excerpt

Reasoning models trained as a judge are a very natural fit because the exact token for a predicted reward or ranking is crucial — CoT is essential.

Corpus source-specific finding

The present RL wave has stronger infrastructure and conditions than the earlier open instruction-tuning period.

Exact returned source excerpt

The difference, compared to the last time RL was at the forefront of the AI world with the fact that reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) was needed to create ChatGPT, is that we have way* better infrastructure than our first time through this.

Exact returned source excerpt

People are already successfully using [TRL](https://github.com/huggingface/trl), [OpenRLHF](https://github.com/OpenRLHF/OpenRLHF), [veRL](https://github.com/volcengine/verl), and of course, [Open Instruct](https://github.com/allenai/open-instruct) (our tools for [Tülu 3](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/tulu-3)/OLMo) to train models like this.

Corpus source-specific finding

RL for reasoning could become a primary driver of language-model development rather than a secondary post-training step.

Exact returned source excerpt

Some of the key points I arrived on: - RLHF was necessary, but not sufficient for ChatGPT.

Exact returned source excerpt

RL training like for reasoning could become the primary driving force of future LM developments.

Corpus source-specific finding

Inference-time compute and self-play are related but not the core of the claimed RL advance.

Exact returned source excerpt

Inference time compute is also orthogonal.

Exact returned source excerpt

With any model, spending more on inference makes it more likely that you will arrive at the correct answer.

Corpus source-specific finding

Reasoning models will improve rapidly but will not dominate every domain or eliminate cost-performance trade-offs.

Exact returned source excerpt

Reasoning models are going to proceed at a rate far, far faster than most expect.

Exact returned source excerpt

These models will not be state-of-the-art on every domain, but probably far more than you expect.
