Corpus framing of this source: Domain experience creates countervailing ability and aversion effects in algorithm-augmented work

Corpus synthesis of this source: The study integrates human-capital and algorithm-aversion research, then tests its framework in a within-subjects field experiment with corporate IT support workers. It argues that experience improves workers’ ability to evaluate algorithmic advice but can also increase rejection of sound advice, producing an inverted U-shaped performance effect. The authors distinguish their experimental performance result from exploratory mechanism evidence, identify accountability as a notable source of expert aversion, qualify the study’s generalisability and recommend experience-specific process design.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors reconcile two previously opposed accounts of experience by modelling ability and aversion as countervailing mechanisms.

Exact returned source excerpt

This line of research typically demonstrates the superior accuracy of even simple algorithms over experts (Dawes 1979, Grove et al.

Yet people—especially experts—tend to exhibit “algorithmic aversion,” and rely more on their own judgment than the advice generated by an algorithm (Dietvorst, Simmons, and Massey 2015, Logg, Minson, and Moore 2019).
