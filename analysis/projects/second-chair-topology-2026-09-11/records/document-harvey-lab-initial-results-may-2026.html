Open topic: Benchmark success: Average scores conceal complete-task failure

Corpus framing of this source: Harvey’s initial LAB results: low end-to-end pass rates, uneven strengths and costly frontier performance

Corpus synthesis of this source: Harvey authors Niko Grupen, Gabe Pereyra and Julio Pereyra report initial Legal Agent Benchmark results in May 2026. They argue that frontier models remain insufficient for complete legal work under a strict all-pass standard, show substantial practice-area variation, and incur meaningful cost and latency. They also advocate examining agent behavior alongside scores.

We also believe strongly that the way an agent works inside a task carries as much information as the score it produces; and that visibility into agent behavior will increase over time in importance in high-stakes, regulated domains like law.

Exact returned source excerpt

The reinforcement-learning and multi-agent systems literature has long studied agents this way, examining agent action sequences and aggregating them into patterns of behavior.

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source evaluates four frontier model families on long-horizon legal-agent work. It argues that performance remains below legal work’s strict standard, varies sharply by practice area and task type, and must be assessed alongside cost and latency. It recommends matching models to tasks and predicts that strong production systems will use multiple models rather than rely on one general winner.
