Native topic findings

  1. The cluster illustrates buyer-led cost control through procurement rules, rather than evidence of a market-wide shift in legal pricing.
  2. The FDIC separates eligibility for work from assignments and retains control over rates, scope and strategy.
  3. Alternative fees operate as documented exceptions within an hourly-rate framework.
  4. Expense rules allocate ordinary operating and technology costs to firms, while restricting subcontracting discretion.
  5. Detailed billing records and retrospective audits make payment conditional on continuing compliance.
  6. Public-sector and cross-border qualifications limit generalisation to other legal buyers.

Outside Counsel Deskbook (revised April 2026)

Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Current wave (2026)

Corpus framing of this source: FDIC outside-counsel rules: controlled referrals, negotiated rates and auditable billing

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Outside Counsel Deskbook (revised April 2026) sets out the FDIC’s contractual and administrative requirements for retaining, supervising and paying domestic and foreign law firms. Its contribution is prescriptive rather than empirical: it establishes negotiated-rate controls, conditional alternatives to hourly fees, approval requirements and audit remedies, not findings about their effectiveness or predictions about the legal market.
