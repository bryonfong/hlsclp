Corpus framing of this source: MillerKing v. DoNotPay: dismissal for failure to plead Article III injury

Corpus synthesis of this source: Chief Judge Nancy J. Rosenstengel held that MillerKing’s allegations about DoNotPay’s purportedly false legal-services claims and unauthorized practice did not establish a concrete, particularized injury to MillerKing. The court dismissed the Lanham Act, Illinois deceptive-trade-practices and unauthorized-practice claims without prejudice, while allowing amendment.

Corpus source-specific finding

Article III required MillerKing to show its own concrete stake in every claim and requested remedy.

Exact returned source excerpt

Article III Standing Article III of the Constitution limits federal courts to resolving “Cases” and “Controversies,” which is understood as restricting the courts to their “constitutionally limited role of adjudicating actual and concrete disputes” in a form that is appropriate for judicial decision and the resolution of which “will have direct consequences on the parties involved.” Nabozny v.

Exact returned source excerpt

“An essential component of the case-or-controversy limitation is the requirement that a plaintiff have standing to sue—that is, a ‘personal stake’ in the outcome of the suit sufficient to engage the jurisdiction of the federal court.” Id.

Corpus source-specific finding

MillerKing alleged false legal-services representations and unauthorized practice, but did not connect them to an injury of its own.

Exact returned source excerpt

MK also avers that its allegation of “literally false” statements by DNP, e.g., that it is a “lawyer” and “attorney,” that it provides “legal services,” and that it has “represented people” in legal disputes, presumptively causes direct competitors like MK harm.

Exact returned source excerpt

MK alleges DNP's representations include descriptions that, among many other things: it provides legal services “behind the scenes,” it will provide the “best advice,” it will “assist with all your legal needs” in “small claims and more,” it can be used to appeal traffic tickets “in any city,” it will “generate ․ court filings,” it will provide a “script to read in court”; it would represent a person in a courtroom; it sent out a subpoena; and it has taken on hundreds of thousands of parking ticket cases.

Corpus source-specific finding

The complaint lacked factual allegations of diverted business, financial loss or reputational harm.

Exact returned source excerpt

As argued by DNP, MK has not alleged any lost revenue or added expenditures as a result of DNP's conduct.

Exact returned source excerpt

Nor has it alleged that any client or prospective client has withheld business, has considered withholding business, or has even heard of DNP.

Corpus source-specific finding

The court rejected MillerKing’s request to infer injury from DoNotPay’s growth or from an asserted direct-competitor relationship.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Seventh Circuit has held, however, that “that a plaintiff's claim of injury in fact cannot be based solely on a defendant's gain; it must be based on a plaintiff's loss.” Silha, 807 F.3d at 174-75.

Exact returned source excerpt

Again, the Court will not infer that MK has suffered harm through lost clients just because DNP has gained them.

Corpus source-specific finding

The court distinguished precedent where detailed allegations made diverted sales and reputational injury plausible.

Exact returned source excerpt

Unlike MK, Static Control not only alleged injury due to diversion of sales and reputational harm, but it also provided the facts necessary to make those allegations plausible.

Exact returned source excerpt

Static Control alleged Lexmark directly targeted its customers and falsely stated that doing business with Static Control was illegal.

Corpus source-specific finding

MillerKing also failed to plead the imminent future harm necessary for injunctive relief.

Exact returned source excerpt

Unlike a claim for damages, the standing requirement for injunctive relief may be satisfied by a risk of future harm, “so long as the risk of harm is sufficiently imminent and substantial.” Id.

Exact returned source excerpt

MK has not sufficiently alleged any imminent harm that will occur if DNP is not enjoined from its conduct.

Corpus source-specific finding

The standing defect defeated all supplied causes of action, but the dismissal initially permitted amendment.

Exact returned source excerpt

In sum, MK has not plausibly alleged that it has suffered a diversion of clients or reputational harm as a result of DNP's actions.

Exact returned source excerpt

Thus, it lacks Article III standing to pursue its Lanham Act claims.
