Native topic findings

  1. The central proposition is continuing human accountability: AI may assist litigation, but it cannot assume responsibility for accurate submissions.
  2. The evidence documents failures of citation integrity, not a general measure of AI reliability or legal reasoning ability.
  3. Responsibility extends through the drafting and supervision chain, including lawyers who did not personally use AI.
  4. Sanctions depend on the governing authority, culpability and procedural context; fabrication does not produce one automatic penalty.
  5. Candor and correction after discovery materially affect courts’ assessments, but remediation does not erase the original harm.
  6. The policy disagreement concerns AI-specific restrictions versus technology-neutral accuracy duties.
  7. Self-represented litigants face the same accuracy obligations but unequal capacity to meet them.
  8. Courts seek deterrence and institutional protection, while critics question whether escalating sanctions address the underlying verification burden.
