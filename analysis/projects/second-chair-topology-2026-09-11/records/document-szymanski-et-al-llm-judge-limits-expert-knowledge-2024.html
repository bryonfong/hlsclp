Corpus framing of this source: Limitations of the LLM-as-a-Judge Approach for Evaluating Expert-Knowledge Outputs

Corpus synthesis of this source: Szymanski and colleagues test whether LLM judges reproduce subject-matter experts’ evaluations of dietetics and mental-health outputs. Their mixed-methods study finds only moderate, domain- and criterion-dependent agreement; shows that experts identify contextual, professional and safety concerns that LLM judges often miss; and recommends combining scalable LLM evaluation with targeted expert oversight and domain-specific tuning.

Corpus source-specific finding

The paper addresses a gap in evaluating open-ended, expert-domain outputs.

Exact returned source excerpt

These metrics become less useful for open-ended tasks where multiple valid responses exist.

Exact returned source excerpt

Other approaches, such as BERTScore, measure semantic overlap in meaning between texts but fail to capture user preferences, domain-specific nuances, or the complexity of reasoning required for tasks that need expert judgement [62].

Corpus source-specific finding

The study compares LLM and expert judgments on matched tasks using quantitative and qualitative methods.

Exact returned source excerpt

To reflect the specialty of the domains, we recruited ten registered dietitians and ten clinical psychologists to complete the evaluation survey tailored to the dietetics and mental health domains, respectively.

Exact returned source excerpt

Using the framework, the LLM judge model was provided the same twenty-five prompt instructions, the set candidate responses, and three ranking questions (one general preference question and two aspect questions) as provided to SMEs in the survey.

Corpus source-specific finding

LLM judges showed substantially lower agreement with experts than experts showed with one another.

By comparison, SMEs agreed with each other 72% in the mental health domain and 75% in the dietetics domain, with an overall agreement of 73% across both domains, establishing a higher baseline for expert agreement.
