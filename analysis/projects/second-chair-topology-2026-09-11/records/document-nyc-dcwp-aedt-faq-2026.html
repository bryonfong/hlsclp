SupportsEmployment screening: Audits reveal bias without remedying it

Open topic: Employment screening: Audits reveal bias without remedying it

Corpus framing of this source: DCWP guidance on NYC automated employment decision tools: scope, audits and notice

Corpus synthesis of this source: The NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection explains Local Law 144’s compliance requirements and limits. Its FAQ supplies regulatory guidance, not empirical findings or forecasts, and distinguishes audit-and-notice enforcement from discrimination enforcement.

Corpus source-specific finding

Covered AEDT use requires a prior bias audit and notice.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Law prohibits employers and employment agencies from using an automated employment decision tool (AEDT) in New York City unless they ensure a bias audit was done and provide required notices.

Exact returned source excerpt

If the Law applies: • A bias audit of the AEDT must be completed before its use.

Corpus source-specific finding

The definition covers substantial assistance as well as replacement of human discretion.

Exact returned source excerpt

An AEDT is a computer-based tool that: • Uses machine learning, statistical modeling, data analytics, or artificial intelligence.

Exact returned source excerpt

AND • Helps employers and employment agencies make employment decisions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Coverage depends on NYC connections and assessment of actual hiring or promotion candidates.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Law applies only to employers and employment agencies that use an AEDT “in the city.” This means: • The job location is an office in NYC, at least part time.

Exact returned source excerpt

OR • The job is fully remote but the location associated with it is an office in NYC.

Corpus source-specific finding

Audits measure demographic disparities but do not themselves trigger prescribed corrective action under this law.

Exact returned source excerpt

A bias audit is an impartial evaluation by an independent auditor.

Exact returned source excerpt

At a minimum, an independent auditor’s evaluation must include calculations of selection or scoring rates and the impact ratio across sex categories, race/ethnicity categories, and intersectional categories.

Corpus source-specific finding

NYC residents must receive advance notice describing the tool’s use and assessed characteristics.

Exact returned source excerpt

Employers and employment agencies must notify employees and job candidates who are residents of New York City that they are using an AEDT and the job qualifications or characteristics the AEDT will assess.

Exact returned source excerpt

Employers and employment agencies must: • Include in the notice instructions to request a reasonable accommodation under other laws.

Corpus source-specific finding

DCWP handles audit-and-notice violations; the NYC Commission on Human Rights handles discrimination claims.

Exact returned source excerpt

To submit a complaint, contact 311 or visit the DCWP website at nyc.gov/dcwp.

Exact returned source excerpt

Complaints should include all of the following: • Details of the job posting or position • Name and type of AEDT • Notice provided, if any • Explanation of suspected violation (for example, AEDT used without notice) 2.

Open topic: Hiring audits: Disclosure cannot guarantee nondiscrimination

Corpus framing of this source: DCWP guidance on New York City’s automated employment decision tool requirements

Corpus synthesis of this source: Automated Employment Decision Tools: Frequently Asked Questions presents DCWP’s compliance guidance for Local Law 144. It explains coverage, independent annual audits, data and disclosure rules, employer responsibility, notice and complaints. The source offers regulatory guidance rather than empirical findings or predictions, and distinguishes audit obligations from separate antidiscrimination duties.

Corpus source-specific finding

Local Law 144 conditions covered AEDT use on prior auditing and notice.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Law prohibits employers and employment agencies from using an automated employment decision tool (AEDT) in New York City unless they ensure a bias audit was done and provide required notices.

Exact returned source excerpt

It took effect on January 1, 2023.

Corpus source-specific finding

Coverage includes intermediate screening but excludes outreach to people who have not applied.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Law applies only to employers and employment agencies that use an AEDT “in the city.” This means: • The job location is an office in NYC, at least part time.

Exact returned source excerpt

OR • The job is fully remote but the location associated with it is an office in NYC.

Corpus source-specific finding

Independent audits must calculate demographic disparities and remain current annually.

Exact returned source excerpt

A bias audit is an impartial evaluation by an independent auditor.

Exact returned source excerpt

At a minimum, an independent auditor’s evaluation must include calculations of selection or scoring rates and the impact ratio across sex categories, race/ethnicity categories, and intersectional categories.

Corpus source-specific finding

The law requires audits without prescribing a response to their findings.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Law requires employers and employment agencies to do a bias audit; however, the Law does not require any specific actions based on the results of a bias audit.

Exact returned source excerpt

Important: Federal, state, and New York City laws prohibit discrimination.

Corpus source-specific finding

Employers must publicly disclose audit results and when they began using the tool.

Exact returned source excerpt

Employers and employment agencies must publish: • A summary of the results of the most recent bias audit.

Exact returned source excerpt

AND • The distribution date of the AEDT.

Corpus source-specific finding

Historical data form the default audit basis, with conditional pooling and no inferred demographics.

Exact returned source excerpt

Historical data of the AEDT must be used to conduct a bias audit.

Exact returned source excerpt

“Historical data” is the data collected during an employer’s or employment agency’s use of an AEDT to assess candidates for employment or employees for promotion.

Corpus source-specific finding

DCWP permits test data while leaving statistical sufficiency and test-data standards flexible.

Exact returned source excerpt

If an independent auditor determines there is insufficient historical data to conduct a statistically significant bias audit, test data may be used to conduct the bias audit.

Exact returned source excerpt

DCWP has not set a specific requirement for statistical significance.

Corpus source-specific finding

Auditor independence depends on impartiality and conflicts, not DCWP approval.

Exact returned source excerpt

An independent auditor is someone who exercises objective and impartial judgment in the performance of a bias audit.

Exact returned source excerpt

Auditors are NOT independent if they: • Work for the employer or employment agency that will use the AEDT or the vendor that developed or distributes the AEDT.
