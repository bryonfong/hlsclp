Workforce planning: Training and retention accompany displacement

Corpus synthesis: Describes automation, augmentation and redeployment together, emphasizing organizational barriers and incomplete worker transitions.

1 distinct stance-bearing works · Supports: 1

Native topic findings

  1. The cluster frames workforce transformation as a skills and organisational challenge, not simply a technology-adoption problem.
  2. Employers plan automation alongside augmentation and redeployment, while still anticipating displacement.
  3. Training anchors the proposed response, but employers expect some workers to miss that transition.
  4. Talent strategies combine development with retention and wider recruitment, rather than relying exclusively on new technical hires.
  5. Sector variation and the survey’s scope limit conclusions about AI’s effects on legal employment.

Future of Jobs Report 2025

Market analysts, consultancies & press · Adoption at scale (2025)

Corpus framing of this source: Employers expect skills gaps to constrain transformation and prioritise training through 2030

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Future of Jobs Report 2025 contributes 2024 employer-survey evidence on transformation barriers, changing task allocation, training needs and workforce plans. Its economy, region and industry profiles show substantial variation around global averages. These are employers’ expectations and intended responses, not demonstrated effects of AI or proof that the proposed interventions work; many supplied profile fragments omit names or interleave table columns.
