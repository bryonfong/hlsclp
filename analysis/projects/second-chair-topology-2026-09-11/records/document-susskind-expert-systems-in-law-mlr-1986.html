Corpus framing of this source: Susskind’s jurisprudential blueprint for legal expert systems (1986)

Corpus synthesis of this source: Richard E. Susskind surveys early legal-AI projects and argues that legal theory should guide the construction of specialised, explainable systems that assist practitioners. His contribution combines a literature-based assessment of technical progress with criticism of weak heuristic knowledge and limited reasoning, qualified predictions about preserving expertise, and an interdisciplinary research agenda.

Corpus framing of this source: Susskind’s jurisprudential blueprint for legal expert systems (1986)

Corpus synthesis of this source: Richard E. Susskind argues that useful legal expert systems require explicit models of law and legal reasoning, specialist human knowledge and meaningful explanations—not merely executable statutory rules. His review combines qualified optimism about technical feasibility with criticism of existing prototypes and an interdisciplinary research agenda.

Corpus framing of this source: A jurisprudential framework for genuine legal expert systems

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source argues that legal expert-system research must model the distinctive structure and reasoning of law rather than merely encode rules in convenient programming forms. It distinguishes knowledge-based systems from databases, critiques contemporary projects against a demanding expert-system standard, applies jurisprudential objections to deductive inference, and proposes research priorities centred on expertise, fact classification, uncertainty, transparency and integration.
