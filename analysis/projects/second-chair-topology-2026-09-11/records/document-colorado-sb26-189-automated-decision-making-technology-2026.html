Corpus framing of this source: Colorado S.B. 26-189: duties and consumer safeguards for consequential automated decisions

Corpus synthesis of this source: Colorado Senate Bill 26-189: Automated Decision-Making Technology sets out a replacement framework for Colorado’s 2024 AI consumer protections. Its substantive contribution is legislative: developer documentation, consumer disclosures and human-review rights, public enforcement, and limits on contractual liability shifting. The supplied material combines bill-summary language with amended legislative excerpts, rather than empirical findings or predictions.

Corpus source-specific finding

The bill replaces the 2024 framework with rules for technology that materially influences consequential decisions.

Exact returned source excerpt

In 2024, the general assembly enacted Senate Bill 24-205, which created consumer protections in interactions with artificial intelligence systems.

Exact returned source excerpt

The bill repeals and reenacts those provisions with new requirements regarding the use of automated decision-making technology SENATE in consequential decisions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Developers must equip deployers to use covered systems appropriately and maintain compliance records.

Exact returned source excerpt

The bill requires the developer of an ADMT (developer) that is used to materially influence a consequential decision (covered ADMT), starting January 1, 2027, to provide a deployer of a covered ADMT (deployer) with technical documentation describing the covered ADMT's intended uses, categories of training data, known limitations, and instructions for appropriate use and human review.

Exact returned source excerpt

Developers must notify deployers of material updates or modifications to the covered ADMT.

Corpus source-specific finding

Consumers receive notice, adverse-outcome explanations, data-correction rights and access to human reconsideration.

Exact returned source excerpt

The bill establishes consumer notice requirements, mandating that deployers provide clear and conspicuous notice to consumers at the point of interaction with a covered ADMT.

Exact returned source excerpt

A deployer is required to provide a consumer with a plain language description of a covered ADMT's role within 30 days after the covered ADMT makes a consequential decision that results in an adverse outcome for the consumer.

Corpus framing of this source: Colorado SB 26-189: notice, human review and responsibility for automated consequential decisions

Corpus synthesis of this source: Colorado Senate Bill 26-189 proposes a statutory framework for automated decision-making technology (ADMT), combining consumer disclosures and review rights with developer documentation, record retention and attorney-general enforcement. It allocates responsibility under existing discrimination law while qualifying coverage through use-based exclusions and sector-specific provisions. The supplied text presents legislative requirements, not empirical findings or predictions about their effectiveness.
