Open topic: Platform governance: External innovation preserves gatekeeper power

Corpus framing of this source: Platforms as infrastructures that organise society and contest public values

Corpus synthesis of this source: In The Platform Society: Public Values in a Connective World (2018), José van Dijck, Thomas Poell and Martijn de Waal develop a political-economic account of platforms as intertwined technical, commercial and governance structures. The supplied excerpts offer conceptual reasoning and illustrative cases, rather than quantified causal findings or forecasts. They distinguish promises of empowerment and disruption from their own argument that platforms reshape existing institutions, concentrate infrastructural power and provoke disputes over collective benefits.

Platforms, in our view, do not cause a revolution; instead, they are gradually infiltrating in, and converging with, the (offline, legacy) institutions and practices through which democratic societies are organized.

Corpus framing of this source: Platform power, public values and democratic governance

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source develops a framework for understanding platform society as an unequal, value-laden ecosystem. It links infrastructural concentration, architecture, data processing, terms of service and business models to the shaping of user conduct and social norms. It argues that public-value conflicts require contextual assessment and shared governance, while assigning governments—especially European governments—a distinctive regulatory responsibility.
