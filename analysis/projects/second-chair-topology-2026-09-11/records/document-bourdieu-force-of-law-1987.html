Corpus framing of this source: Legal authority, professional monopoly and the social production of law

Corpus synthesis of this source: Bourdieu explains law through competition among legal professionals and their relations with wider social power. He treats legal autonomy as relative, links professional authority to exclusion and symbolic legitimacy, and explains how internal struggles can both change law and reproduce social order. The supplied translator’s introduction contextualises this framework; the essay develops it through historical illustrations, national comparisons and cited studies rather than quantified forecasts.

Corpus framing of this source: The juridical field and the symbolic power of legal form

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source develops a sociology of law centred on the relatively autonomous juridical field. It explains legal authority through professional competition, interpretation, formalization, exclusion, state-backed naming and law’s interaction with broader social power, while rejecting both claims of complete legal autonomy and accounts that reduce law to external interests or force.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source treats law as a relatively autonomous social field rather than as either an independent system or a direct instrument of external power.

Exact returned source excerpt

In order to break with the formalist ideology, which assumes the independence of the law and of legal professionals, without simultaneously falling into the contrary instrumentalist conception, it is necessary to realize that these two antagonistic perspectives, one from within, the other from outside the law, together simply ignore the existence of an entire social universe (what I will term the "juridical field"), which is in practice relatively independent of external determinations and pressures.

Exact returned source excerpt

But this universe cannot be neglected if we wish to understand the social significance of the law, for it is within this universe that juridical authority is produced and exercised.
