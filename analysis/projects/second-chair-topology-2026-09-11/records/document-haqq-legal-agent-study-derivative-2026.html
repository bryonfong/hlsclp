Corpus framing of this source: HAQQ proposes matter-level, all-pass testing for legal agents

Corpus synthesis of this source: Stephane Boghossian’s HAQQ company blog argues that legal-agent benchmarks should measure complete, reviewable work on realistic matters, with particular attention to civil-law and MENA practice. It describes a vendor-built study, promotes specialised agents and recommends task-specific scrutiny of delegation. The supplied source is explicitly marked low-reliability and derivative of Harvey LAB; its performance assertions should not serve as established benchmark evidence.

Corpus source-specific finding

Evaluate complete legal assignments rather than isolated legal questions.

Exact returned source excerpt

A long-horizon evaluation measuring whether AI agents can do real legal work end-to-end, not just answer trivia: 1,372 tasks across 24 practice areas graded against ~78,000 atomic, binary pass/fail rubric criteria, with civil-law and MENA coverage by design.

Exact returned source excerpt

## Why short-horizon benchmarks broke Most legal AI evaluations - LegalBench, CUAD, LEXam, even our earlier work - test short-horizon reasoning: read a clause, answer a question, classify a paragraph.

Corpus source-specific finding

Use all-pass grading to expose consequential omissions.

Exact returned source excerpt

A task is marked complete only if every rubric criterion passes — one missed criterion fails the task.

Exact returned source excerpt

The rubric for that single task contains 57 criteria - covering nine planted legal issues, the underlying facts behind each, the severity rating, the financial exposure, and the recommended action.
