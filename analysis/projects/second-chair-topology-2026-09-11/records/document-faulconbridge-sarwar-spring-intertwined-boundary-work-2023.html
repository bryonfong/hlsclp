Corpus framing of this source: How Professionals Adapt to Artificial Intelligence: The Role of Intertwined Boundary Work

Corpus synthesis of this source: Faulconbridge, Sarwar and Spring develop “intertwined boundary work” to explain how accounting and legal professionals respond strategically to AI. Drawing on research conducted from 2018 to 2020, they argue that AI reallocates data-intensive tasks, creates interpretive and advisory work, brings technologists into professional service firms, and changes services, career paths and governance. Their observed outcome is reconfiguration rather than mass professional redundancy, although they qualify this finding as provisional and call for continued empirical study.

Corpus framing of this source: How Professionals Adapt to Artificial Intelligence: The Role of Intertwined Boundary Work

Corpus synthesis of this source: The paper develops and empirically applies the concept of “intertwined boundary work” to explain how mid-sized accounting and law professional service firms in England responded to AI between 2018 and 2020. It argues that professionals neither simply resist AI nor passively undergo replacement: they selectively cede routine tasks, preserve claims over judgement-intensive work, create AI-enabled advisory domains, collaborate with technologists and alter partnership governance. The authors distinguish their findings from replacement predictions and other views they report in the literature.
