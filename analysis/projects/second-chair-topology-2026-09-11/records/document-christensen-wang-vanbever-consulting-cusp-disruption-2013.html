Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Consulting on the Cusp of Disruption

Corpus synthesis of this source: Christensen, Wang and van Bever apply disruption and related business-model theories to consulting, informed by interviews with more than 50 firm leaders, clients, academics and researchers and by comparisons with legal services. They argue that growing client sophistication, service modularization, new providers, analytics and productized intellectual property will reshape consulting, while bespoke work on complex, interdependent problems will preserve a premium segment. They acknowledge uncertainty about timing and outcomes but urge incumbents to build and protect autonomous new businesses rather than rely on established brands and past success.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors present disruption as inevitable but gradual and uncertain, not synonymous with wholesale destruction.

Exact returned source excerpt

If our long study of disruption has led us to any universal conclusion, it is that every industry will eventually face it.

Exact returned source excerpt

No one can say for sure what will happen.
