Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt

It is helpful to conceptually divide the users of AI within law into three groups: the administrators of law (e.g., judges, legislators, police), the practitioners of law (e.g., attorneys), and the users of law (e.g., ordinary citizens and businesses—those who comply with the law).

Exact returned source excerpt

This concep- tual division is useful, because each group plays a different role in the legal system, and each uses AI within law in different ways.

Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

A different but equally useful way to think about “artificial intelligence” is in terms of the underlying technology that enables it.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI has roots in computer science but draws on multiple disciplines.

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

Corpus source-specific finding

Machine learning and knowledge representation and rules-based AI form two broad technical families.

Exact returned source excerpt

The first group of AI technologies is broadly known as “machine learning,” and the second cate- gory is referred to as “knowledge representation and rules-based AI”.

Exact returned source excerpt

Let’s briefly look at each broad group in turn, because each AI approach is used within the administration and practice of law to varying degrees.

Corpus source-specific finding

Machine learning encompasses related techniques that learn patterns from large datasets.

Exact returned source excerpt

Roughly speaking, machine learning refers to a category of AI approaches in which algorithms automatically learn patterns from large amounts of data.

Exact returned source excerpt

These learned pat- terns can then be harnessed to automate tasks such as driving a car, predicting credit card fraud, or recognizing handwriting.
