Corpus framing of this source: Professionalism as an occupational value in organizational workplaces

Corpus synthesis of this source: Julia Evetts (2011) examines how organizational employment reshapes professionalism, including its implications for practitioners, clients and control over work. The supplied abstract presents a conceptual inquiry into both changes and continuities, and treats the benefits of combining professional and organizational logics as possibilities rather than established findings.

Corpus source-specific finding

Evetts adopts an occupational-value account of professionalism.

Exact returned source excerpt

406-422 (2011)* ## Abstract Sociologists interested in professional work and occupations have differentiated professionalism as a distinctive and special way of controlling and organizing work and workers, with real advantages for both practitioners and clients.

The final section considers if there might also be some advantages in the combination of professional and organizational logics for controlling work and workers and whether there are opportunities associated with these changes which might improve both the conduct and practice of professional service work, while being of benefit to practitioners and clients.
