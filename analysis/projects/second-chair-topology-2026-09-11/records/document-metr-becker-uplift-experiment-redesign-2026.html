Corpus framing of this source: AI adoption creates selection bias in a developer productivity experiment

Corpus synthesis of this source: In “We are Changing our Developer Productivity Experiment Design,” the researchers describe a randomized task-level study and argue that growing AI adoption makes recruitment and task submission selective. They infer that these selection effects likely understate AI’s productivity benefits, while qualifying the extent of the bias.

Corpus source-specific finding

Corpus framing of this source: Developer-productivity experiments need redesign as AI adoption creates severe selection and measurement bias

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source argues that growing reliance on AI has undermined its task-level experiment: developers increasingly avoid AI-disallowed work, agentic workflows complicate time measurement, and AI may change task choice, output quality and completion. It presents newer estimates suggestive of speedup but treats them as weak evidence, predicts worsening validity, and proposes shorter intensive experiments, observational analysis, questionnaires and time-use studies.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source identifies nonparticipation by AI-reliant developers as the main selection problem and argues that it likely biases estimated AI speedup downward.

Exact returned source excerpt

The primary reason is that we have observed a significant increase in developers choosing not to participate in the study because they do not wish to work without AI, which likely biases downwards our estimate of AI-assisted speedup.

Exact returned source excerpt

Based on our interviews and surveys we believe the increase in selection is primarily due to higher expectation of AI-driven uplift by our developers.

Corpus source-specific finding

The raw estimates have shifted toward speedup, but the source treats them as weak and inconclusive evidence of how much AI productivity has improved.

Exact returned source excerpt

Based on conversations with study participants, we believe it is likely that developers are more sped up from AI tools now — in early 2026 — compared to our estimates from early 2025.

Exact returned source excerpt

However, because of the selection effects in our experiment, our data is only very weak evidence for the size of this increase.

Corpus source-specific finding

Agentic AI disrupts the experiment’s assumption that elapsed task time is a sufficient and comparable measure of productivity.

Exact returned source excerpt

We additionally believe there have been selection effects due to a lower pay rate (we reduced the pay from $150/hr to $50/hr), and that our measurements of time-spent on each task are unreliable for the fraction of developers who use multiple AI agents concurrently.

Exact returned source excerpt

We also noticed some other problems, though we judged the magnitude of these to be less severe: - Some developers told us the types of tasks they attempted were different with agentic AI, leaning on the strengths of AI.
