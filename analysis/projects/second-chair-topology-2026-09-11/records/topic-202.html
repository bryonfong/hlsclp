Native topic findings

  1. Legal AI vendors are moving from discrete task assistance toward connected, multistep matter work, while retaining lawyer review.
  2. Competition centres on combining models, legal knowledge and workplace integration, rather than model capability alone.
  3. Vendors make verification and confidentiality central to professional trust, but their assurances do not establish accuracy or ethical compliance.
  4. The supplied product evidence chiefly establishes development effort, adoption and perceived usefulness—not comparative legal-work performance.
  5. Task-specific variation qualifies broad claims that an associate-like assistant can improve legal work generally.
  6. Capacity gains may change delegation and access to services, but neither job displacement nor expanded access follows automatically.
