Legal computability: Rules never exhaust legal outcomes

Corpus synthesis: Contrasts formal determinacy with realist prediction and empirical jurimetrics, without rejecting coherent doctrine or limited computability.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Native topic findings

  1. The cluster frames legal computability as a dispute over whether rules determine outcomes, rather than simply whether machines can process legal texts.
  2. Realist critiques separate predicting what courts will do from deriving what rules supposedly require.
  3. Rejecting complete determinacy does not require abandoning regular rules or coherent doctrine.
  4. Jurimetrics calls for empirical investigation rather than replacing one speculative jurisprudence with another.
  5. Predictability and cost-benefit calculation capture only part of the legal function described here.
  6. Legal systematisation also reflects institutions and information tools, not only theories about law.
