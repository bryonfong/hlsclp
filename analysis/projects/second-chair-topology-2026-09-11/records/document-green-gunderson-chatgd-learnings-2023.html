# ChatGD: Learnings (So Far) from our Legal GenAI Experiment *Joe Green — LinkedIn Pulse (Gunderson Dettmer chief innovation officer, first-person) (2023)* In August 2023, we [launched](https://www.gunder.com/news/gunderson-dettmer-launches-chatgd-a-homegrown-generative-ai-chat-app-to-its-lawyers/?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) ChatGD, our homegrown internal generative AI (GenAI) chat application with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), following a successful beta test over the summer.

Exact returned source excerpt

Now, just four months later, ChatGD continues to evolve to keep up with the blistering pace of development in AI.

Corpus source-specific finding

The firm made technical and safe-use training a prerequisite for access.

Exact returned source excerpt

## Upskilling for GenAI As a prerequisite to using ChatGD, we required users to complete an initial training (either live or on demand).

Exact returned source excerpt

We held three live training sessions tailored specifically for our firm’s attorneys, paralegals and business professionals.

Corpus source-specific finding

The rollout sought collective learning rather than prescribing a settled set of workflows.

Exact returned source excerpt

We framed the rollout of ChatGD as a collaborative experiment designed to help everyone move up the learning curve and to crowdsource the most promising use cases and methods for getting the best results out of GenAI-powered tools.

Corpus source-specific finding

The firm distinguished suitable applications from uses that required further advances.

Exact returned source excerpt

We also shared ideal use cases for GenAI (e.g., text manipulation) and areas where we thought the technology was not well suited for use without further advancements (e.g., search engine replacement).

Corpus framing of this source: ChatGD: Early Firmwide Adoption, Usage, Cost and Use Cases

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source reports early results from Gunderson's firmwide ChatGD deployment. It presents participation and usage data, describes adoption of retrieval-augmented generation, projects low annual operating costs and attributes them to specific technical choices, and identifies practical uses by attorneys.

Corpus source-specific finding

Training participation and adoption indicate substantial firmwide interest.

Exact returned source excerpt

More than half of the entire firm attended one of those three trainings in real time, which is a testament to the incredible level of interest in GenAI and the tool we built.

Exact returned source excerpt

Nearly half of the firm has already used ChatGD.

Corpus source-specific finding

Usage and engagement increased during the reported period.

Exact returned source excerpt

Usage has been steadily increasing, with more than nine thousand prompts submitted and completed across several thousand conversation threads.

Corpus source-specific finding

Users adopted retrieval-augmented generation alongside general chat.

Exact returned source excerpt

We have created vector search indices for hundreds of document collections uploaded by our users for RAG, and prompts leveraging the advanced RAG feature accounted for more than 10% of the total volume of interactions with the tool.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source projects an annual out-of-pocket cost below $10,000 and attributes that cost level to model and hosting choices.

Exact returned source excerpt

We project the total annual out-of-pocket cost for providing ChatGD to our entire firm will be less than $10,000—a staggeringly low figure, especially when compared to the alternatives currently available in the enterprise market.

Exact returned source excerpt

We largely attribute the cost-effectiveness of ChatGD to two strategic decisions: (1) self-hosting an open-source model for RAG vector embeddings and (2) leveraging GPT 3.5 Turbo for both pure chat and RAG functionalities instead of using the most expensive models available.

Corpus source-specific finding

Attorneys use ChatGD for document, drafting, ideation and prompting tasks.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our attorneys are using it to retrieve and manipulate or summarize language in legal agreements, draft and change the tone of emails, summarize documents and articles, and brainstorm different examples of legal language or topics for presentations.

Exact returned source excerpt

They have discovered creative ways to have the LLM help teach them to craft better prompts.
