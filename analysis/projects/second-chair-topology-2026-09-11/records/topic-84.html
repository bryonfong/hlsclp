Native topic record

Platform infrastructure: Convenience can deepen private control

Corpus synthesis: Treats platforms as social infrastructure shaped by data, network effects and interconnection power, not neutral marketplaces.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Cross-domain institutional evidence: Innovation and market correction remain conditional on incentives, gatekeeping and measurement · Platform infrastructure: Openness and convenience can entrench private gatekeepers

3 distinct stance-bearing works · Supports: 3

Native topic findings

  1. Platforms organise essential services and social institutions, rather than merely connect buyers and sellers.
  2. Data extraction, network effects and control of interconnection can reinforce concentrated market power.
  3. Modular innovation and user benefits can coexist with dependency and private control.
  4. The main analytical tension concerns whether business incentives sufficiently explain platform conduct.
  5. Private convenience does not establish collective benefit or resolve responsibility for public provision.
  6. Practical governance requires sector-specific scrutiny of access, accountability and competing public values.

Academia & research · Automation debate (2015-2022)

Corpus framing of this source: How platforms become infrastructure—and infrastructures adopt platform logic

Corpus synthesis of this source: Plantin, Lagoze, Edwards and Sandvig combine infrastructure studies and platform studies to explain corporate control over increasingly essential digital services. Their literature synthesis and cases of the Open Web, Facebook and Google connect modular innovation and user benefits with lock-in, data extraction and weakened public-service commitments, while proposing a comparative research agenda.

The Platform Society: Public Values in a Connective World

Academia & research · Automation debate (2015-2022)

Corpus framing of this source: Platforms as institutions that organise social life and contest public values

Corpus synthesis of this source: In The Platform Society: Public Values in a Connective World (2018), José van Dijck, Thomas Poell and Martijn de Waal develop a political-economic account of platform governance, focused on North America and western Europe. The supplied excerpts offer a conceptual framework supported by scholarship and illustrative platform examples, rather than quantified tests or forecasts. They distinguish promises of disintermediation and growth from their own argument that platforms reshape institutions through interconnected technical, commercial and sociolegal arrangements.

Corpus source-specific finding

Platforms reshape existing institutions rather than constitute a separate technological revolution.

Exact returned source excerpt

Some management and business scholars have touted this development the “platform revolution” and focus on the positive economic effects of a technological development they hail as “innovative disruption” (Parker, Van Alstyne, Choudary 2016; Sundararajan 2016).

Exact returned source excerpt

We agree that online platforms are at the core of an important development, but we think of them neither as an exclusive economic phenomenon nor as a technological construct with social corollaries.

Exact returned source excerpt

Now let us first look more closely at the elements that construct a single platform’s anatomy: a platform is fueled by data, automated and organized through algorithms and interfaces, formalized through ownership relations driven by business models, and governed through user agreements.

With each mouse click and cursor movement user data are generated, stored, automatically analyzed, and processed—not just Internet protocol addresses and geolocations but detailed information about interests, preferences, and tastes.
