Corpus framing of this source: AI-assisted performance gains can mask weaker independent learning

Corpus synthesis of this source: Generative AI Without Guardrails Can Harm Learning: Evidence from High School Mathematics distinguishes immediate gains with GPT-4 from subsequent independent performance. The authors report learning harms with unrestricted access, substantial mitigation through tutor safeguards, and recommend deployment designs that protect skill acquisition.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors distinguish productivity gains reported in prior research from the question of skill acquisition.

Exact returned source excerpt

Broadly speaking, these studies have focused on productivity, finding that workers can perform knowledge-intensive tasks significantly more efficiently when given access to generative AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

A key question that remains is how generative AI affects how humans learn novel skills, both in educational settings and through the course of performing their jobs.

Corpus source-specific finding

GPT-4 improves performance while available, but unrestricted access can impair later unaided performance.

Exact returned source excerpt

Consistent with prior work, our results show that having GPT-4 access while solving problems significantly improves performance (48% improvement in grades for GPT Base and 127% for GPT Tutor).

Exact returned source excerpt

However, we additionally find that when access is subsequently taken away, students actually perform worse than those who never had access (17% reduction in grades for GPT Base)—i.e., unfettered access to GPT-4 can harm educational outcomes.

Corpus source-specific finding

Tutor safeguards largely mitigate the negative learning effects.

Exact returned source excerpt

Without guardrails, students attempt to use GPT-4 as a “crutch” during practice problem sessions, and subsequently perform worse on their own.

Exact returned source excerpt

These negative learning effects are largely mitigated by the safeguards in GPT Tutor.

Corpus source-specific finding

Deployment decisions should protect learning, not merely automate current tasks.

Exact returned source excerpt

This process of skill acquisition is critical for safeguarding long-term productivity ( 10 ).

However, many generative AI deployments are designed to automate tasks without consideration for impact on learning.

Corpus framing of this source: AI-assisted practice gains do not establish unassisted learning gains

Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied excerpt from Generative AI Without Guardrails Can Harm Learning: Evidence from High School Mathematics reports mismatches between perceived and actual exam performance, temporary reductions in grade dispersion, limited evidence of differential treatment effects, and robustness checks for treatment noncompliance.

Corpus source-specific finding

Students’ perceptions overstated their exam performance under both AI treatments.

Exact returned source excerpt

While students in the GPT Base arm performed worse on the exam (relative to the control arm), they did not perceive that they performed worse or learned less.

Exact returned source excerpt

Similarly, while students in the GPT Tutor arm did not perform better on the exam (relative to the control arm), they perceived that they performed significantly better.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study finds little statistically significant evidence that treatment effects vary across student characteristics.

Exact returned source excerpt

We look for heterogeneous treatment effects as a function of preregistered variables, capturing students’ ability, resources, and effort.

Exact returned source excerpt

In general, we find limited to no statistically significant support for heterogeneous treatment effects with either treatment, particularly with respect to unassisted exam performance.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI narrowed grade dispersion during assisted practice, but the study detects no corresponding effect after assistance is removed.

Exact returned source excerpt

We examine a measure of dispersion in student performance—the Herfindahl–Hirschman index (HHI).

Exact returned source excerpt

Both GPT Base and GPT Tutor reduced grade dispersion in the assisted practice sessions, matching prior findings that generative AI assistance reduces the “skill gap” by providing the largest benefits for the weakest students ( 7 – 9 ).

Exact returned source excerpt

Builds onWork redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Guardrails determine whether generative-AI assistance supports performance without harming learning

Corpus synthesis of this source: The study reports a field experiment with GPT-4-based mathematics tutors. It finds that both an unrestricted interface and a safeguarded tutor improve assisted practice performance, but unrestricted access reduces subsequent unassisted exam performance. The authors attribute the learning loss mainly to students copying solutions rather than internalising methods, argue that guardrails largely remove the harm but do not produce a learning gain, and recommend more pedagogically active tutor designs, AI literacy, and human-tutor copilots. They limit the findings to short-term mathematics outcomes in one Turkish high school during Fall 2023.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study tests unrestricted and safeguarded GPT-4 tutoring designs.

Exact returned source excerpt

Experimental Design We created a custom math tutoring program based on OpenAI’s GPT-4 ( 1 ); our tutor is designed to help students solve a series of practice problems provided by the teachers (described below).

Exact returned source excerpt

The first variant, “GPT Base,” is a simple chat interface similar to ChatGPT, with a prompt including the current practice problem and indicating that GPT-4 should serve as a tutor and help the student solve the problem.

Corpus source-specific finding

Both tools substantially improve assisted practice performance, especially GPT Tutor.

Exact returned source excerpt

We find that GPT Base and GPT Tutor substantially increased student scores in the GPT-assisted practice sessions by 0.137 and 0.361 (out of 1), respectively, relative to the control arm that only had access to textbooks (mean performance of 0.28).

Exact returned source excerpt

These results imply that GPT Base and GPT Tutor would increase performance on the assisted practice sessions by 48% and 127%, respectively, on average relative to the control arm.

Corpus source-specific finding

Unrestricted GPT assistance harms subsequent unassisted performance, while safeguards remove the measured harm but create no positive learning effect.

Exact returned source excerpt

In stark contrast, in the subsequent unassisted exam, student performance in the GPT Base arm degraded by 0.054 (out of 1) relative to that of the control arm.

Exact returned source excerpt

In other words, GPT Base diminished the average control student’s performance on the unassisted exam by 17%.

Corpus source-specific finding

The evidence points to copying and reduced engagement as the principal mechanism, rather than persistent contamination from model errors.

Exact returned source excerpt

Potential Mechanism: Asking for Solutions In general, students may be adversely affected by GPT Base’s assistance in two ways: 1) errors made by GPT Base mislead students in the subsequent unassisted problems, or 2) using GPT Base as a “crutch” prevents them from fully engaging with or understanding the material prior to attempting the unassisted problems.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our findings suggest that the second explanation (i.e., students using GPT Base as a crutch) is the main mechanism by which GPT Base impedes student learning.

Corpus source-specific finding

GPT Base is unreliable, but its errors alone do not explain the learning loss.

Exact returned source excerpt

For each of the 57 total practice problems, we ask GPT Base for the answer ten times (resetting the system between queries), and then manually categorize any errors in the response as arithmetic (steps followed were correct, but the resulting computation was incorrect) or logical (steps followed were partially or fully incorrect).

Exact returned source excerpt

We find that GPT Base gives a correct answer only 51% of the time on average; it makes logical errors 42% of the time and arithmetic errors 8% of the time.

Corpus source-specific finding

Students misjudge what they have learned, and assisted reductions in the skill gap do not persist without AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

While students in the GPT Base arm performed worse on the exam (relative to the control arm), they did not perceive that they performed worse or learned less.

Exact returned source excerpt

Similarly, while students in the GPT Tutor arm did not perform better on the exam (relative to the control arm), they perceived that they performed significantly better.

Corpus source-specific finding

Robustness analyses support the main findings, and the authors provide replication materials.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our primary specifications use an intention-to-treat analysis—i.e., to preserve randomization, we consider all students in a treatment arm as treated, regardless of whether they actually received that treatment.

Exact returned source excerpt

In SI Appendix , Appendix B.1 , we provide details on noncompliance and perform a regression omitting noncompliers, finding the same insights.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors recommend stronger pedagogical guardrails, active tutor behaviour, AI literacy, and human-in-the-loop deployments.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our findings support both the need for educators to find ways to safeguard student learning in the face of freely available tools such as ChatGPT as well as the need to design more effective guardrails for generative AI tutors.

Exact returned source excerpt

While the guardrails implemented in GPT Tutor appear to largely mitigate these negative effects, substantial work is required to enable generative AI to positively enhance rather than diminish education.

Corpus source-specific finding

The findings have narrow temporal, subject, institutional, and outcome scope.

Exact returned source excerpt

Finally, while our study takes a step toward understanding the potential harms of generative AI on learning, it focuses on a specific deployment context, and substantial work is needed to better understand the nature and scope of these effects.

Exact returned source excerpt

First, our study focuses on two generative AI tutors for a single topic (mathematics) deployed in a single high school in Turkey.
