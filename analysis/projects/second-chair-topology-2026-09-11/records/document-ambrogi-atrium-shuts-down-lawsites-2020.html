Corpus framing of this source: Atrium’s shutdown after a heavily funded dual-entity legal-services experiment

Corpus synthesis of this source: Robert Ambrogi’s 2020 report traces Atrium’s proposed alternative to traditional large law firms, its funding and leadership changes, and its shutdown following an unsuccessful pivot. He distinguishes the company’s closure from the separate law firm’s continued operation and recalls his own earlier doubts based on Clearspire’s similar model.

Corpus source-specific finding

Atrium proposed separating legal practice from technology and operations to compete with traditional large firms.

Exact returned source excerpt

As I explained in [a 2018 post](https://www.lawnext.com/2018/09/innovative-law-firm-tech-company-atrium-raises-65-million.html), the idea behind Atrium was that it would provide corporate clients with a compelling alternative to traditional large firms, with Atrium’s lawyers focusing exclusively on practicing law, while Atrium LTS — the legal technology services company — would handle all operations for the firm, even including marketing, and would develop and operate software to streamline the firm’s workflows.

When it launched, with an initial $10 million round of funding, I questioned in my [Above the Law](https://abovethelaw.com/2017/09/is-revolutionary-law-firm-atrium-a-case-of-clearspire-deja-vu/) column whether it was a case of Clearspire déjà vu, recalling the demise of the strikingly similar dual-entity firm Clearspire, which opened in 2010 and shut down four years later.

12, this blog [broke the news](https://www.lawnext.com/2020/01/reports-say-atrium-letting-go-most-of-its-legal-staff-had-vowed-to-revolutionize-legal-services.html) that [Atrium](https://www.atrium.co/), the company that said it would “revolutionize legal services” through its dual-entity model of a law firm and a technology company, was letting go most of its legal staff and would pivot towards “building a professional services network dedicated to founders.” Today, founder Justin Kan said he has shut down the rest of the company and laid off all its roughly 100 remaining employees, according to an exclusive report by Josh Constine at [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/03/atrium-shuts-down/).

The separate Atrium law firm, which now consists of just a handful of lawyers kept on after the January layoffs to serve existing clients, will continue to operate, Techcrunch reports.
