Open topic: Agent benchmarks: Protocol scores are not competence

Corpus framing of this source: AI Agents That Matter: benchmarking agents for real-world usefulness

Corpus synthesis of this source: AI Agents That Matter argues that agent benchmarks need cost controls, appropriate hold-outs and reproducible evaluation methods rather than direct reuse of model-testing practices. Its experiments and reproduction studies identify misleading comparisons and implementation errors, while its discussion of human feedback cautions that current benchmarks can also underestimate practical usefulness.

Corpus source-specific finding

Agent benchmarking requires methods distinct from language-model evaluation.

Exact returned source excerpt

LLM benchmarks typically consist of input strings and rely on strings as outputs, whereas agents often involve dynamic interactions with environments such as the web or a command line, which are not easily reducible to static inputs and outputs.

Exact returned source excerpt

Without reproducible agent evaluation, it is hard to distinguish genuine improvements in agent designs from artifacts of the differences in evaluation choices.

Builds onWork redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: AI Agents That Matter: Rethinking Agent Benchmarking for Real-World Use

Corpus synthesis of this source: Kapoor, Stroebl, Siegel, Nadgir and Narayanan argue that AI agents differ enough from language models to require redesigned benchmarking. Using reproductions and case studies across HumanEval, HotPotQA, NovelQA and WebArena, they advocate cost-aware comparisons, distinct model and downstream evaluations, appropriate hold-outs, human-in-the-loop testing and standardized, reproducible evaluation practices.
