Corpus framing of this source: ABA Model Rule 5.1: managerial safeguards, supervision and responsibility for misconduct

Corpus synthesis of this source: ABA Model Rule 5.1 and its official comments prescribe reasonable compliance efforts by managing and supervising lawyers, explain when another lawyer’s misconduct creates personal disciplinary responsibility, and qualify that responsibility. The source supplies normative requirements and practical illustrations, rather than empirical findings or predictions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Managers must establish compliance safeguards, and direct supervisors must make reasonable efforts to ensure other lawyers comply.

Exact returned source excerpt

# ABA Model Rule 5.1: Responsibilities of Partners, Managers, and Supervisory Lawyers, with Official Comments *American Bar Association — ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct (2026)* # ABA Model Rule 5.1 ## Rule text ### *Law Firms And Associations*\ \(a\) A partner in a law firm, and a lawyer who individually or together with other lawyers possesses comparable managerial authority in a law firm, shall make reasonable efforts to ensure that the firm has in effect measures giving reasonable assurance that all lawyers in the firm conform to the Rules of Professional Conduct.

Exact returned source excerpt

\(b\) A lawyer having direct supervisory authority over another lawyer shall make reasonable efforts to ensure that the other lawyer conforms to the Rules of Professional Conduct.

Corpus source-specific finding

Compliance measures should reflect the firm’s structure and practice; partners cannot simply assume ethical compliance.

Exact returned source excerpt

[3] Other measures that may be required to fulfill the responsibility prescribed in paragraph (a) can depend on the firm's structure and the nature of its practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

In a small firm of experienced lawyers, informal supervision and periodic review of compliance with the required systems ordinarily will suffice.

Corpus source-specific finding

Responsibility for another lawyer’s violation turns on direction, knowing ratification, or a knowing failure to remedy preventable consequences.

Exact returned source excerpt

\(c\) A lawyer shall be responsible for another lawyer's violation of the Rules of Professional Conduct if: \(1\) the lawyer orders or, with knowledge of the specific conduct, ratifies the conduct involved; or \(2\) the lawyer is a partner or has comparable managerial authority in the law firm in which the other lawyer practices, or has direct supervisory authority over the other lawyer, and knows of the conduct at a time when its consequences can be avoided or mitigated but fails to take reasonable remedial action.

Exact returned source excerpt

Appropriate remedial action by a partner or managing lawyer would depend on the immediacy of that lawyer's involvement and the seriousness of the misconduct.

Corpus source-specific finding

Supervisory responsibility depends on actual circumstances, and inadequate supervision can violate the rule without knowledge of the underlying misconduct.

Corpus source-specific finding

The rule limits derivative disciplinary liability without displacing each lawyer’s own ethical duties.

Exact returned source excerpt

[7] Apart from this Rule and Rule 8.4(a), a lawyer does not have disciplinary liability for the conduct of a partner, associate or subordinate.
