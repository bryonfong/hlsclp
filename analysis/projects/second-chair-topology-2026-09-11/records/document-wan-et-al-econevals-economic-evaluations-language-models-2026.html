Corpus framing of this source: Economic Evaluations of Language Models: Simulation-Based Occupational Exposure

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source introduces a transparent, task-step simulation method for estimating how much worker time current language models could save. It finds broad but lower exposure than rubric-based methods predict, a large gap between capability and observed Claude use, identifiable deployment bottlenecks, and exposure patterns associated with occupational skills and preparation requirements. It releases the underlying simulations to help workers identify practical applications.

Corpus source-specific finding

The source replaces opaque capability rubrics with step-level simulations of workers using language-model chatbots.

Exact returned source excerpt

Economists have developed exposure measures to study this transformation, which measure how much time workers would save given new technologies [Autor et al., 2003, Acemoglu and Autor, 2011, Acemoglu and Restrepo, 2019].

Exact returned source excerpt

We call these blackbox rubric-based exposure measures: the measure yields a task-level exposure score based on a predefined rubric of capabilities with no transparency into how the score was computed.
