Native topic record

Technology competence: Delegation preserves professional responsibility

Corpus synthesis: Extends competence, supervision and confidentiality duties to technology and outsourcing through proportionate preparation and safeguards.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Legal institutions and professional practice: Substantive outcomes depend on context, capacity and judgment · Delegated legal work: responsibility follows the provider chain without becoming strict liability

9 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 1 · Supports: 8

Native topic findings

  1. Technology and outsourcing change how lawyers work, not the underlying requirement for competent, ethical representation.
  2. Competence requires informed use of relevant technology and preparation proportionate to the matter.
  3. Outsourcing requires differentiated selection, direction and monitoring—not a blanket transfer of responsibility.
  4. Confidentiality demands reasonable safeguards, not a guarantee against every breach.
  5. Client involvement and agreed responsibility support delegation but cannot override professional or legal limits.
  6. The unresolved issue is how to translate broad duties into concrete AI compliance standards.

Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Foundations (pre-2015)

Corpus framing of this source: Technology competence and medium-neutral client communication

Corpus synthesis of this source: The ABA Commission on Ethics 20/20 proposes clarifying the Model Rules’ comments to make technology awareness explicit within existing competence duties and to extend responsiveness beyond telephone calls. Its reasoning rests on ethical interpretation and examples of ordinary digital practice, rather than empirical findings.

Corpus source-specific finding

Competent practice requires understanding the basics of relevant technology.

Exact returned source excerpt

1 (Competence) Model Rule 1.1 requires a lawyer to provide competent representation, and Comment [6] specifies that, to remain competent, lawyers need to “keep abreast of changes in the law and its practice.” The Commission concluded that, in order to keep abreast of changes in law practice in a digital age, lawyers necessarily need to understand basic features of relevant technology and that this aspect of competence should be expressed in the Comment.

Exact returned source excerpt

For example, a lawyer would have difficulty providing competent legal services in today’s environment without knowing how to use email or create an electronic document.

Corpus source-specific finding

The proposed competence amendment clarifies an existing duty rather than creating a new obligation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Comment [6] already encompasses an obligation to remain aware of changes in technology that affect law practice, but the Commission concluded that making this explicit, by addition of the phrase “including the benefits and risks associated with relevant technology,” would offer greater clarity in this area and emphasize the importance of technology to modern law practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

The proposed amendment, which appears in a Comment, does not impose any new obligations on lawyers.

Corpus source-specific finding

Lawyers’ duty to respond to clients should apply regardless of communication medium.

Exact returned source excerpt

Model Rule 1.4 (Communication) Model Rule 1.4 describes a lawyer’s duty to communicate with clients, and the last sentence of Comment [4] to Model Rule 1.4 currently instructs lawyers that “[c]lient telephone calls should be promptly returned or acknowledged.” Clients, however, now communicate with lawyers in an increasing number of ways, including by email and other forms of electronic communication, and a lawyer’s obligation to respond should exist regardless of the medium that is used.

Exact returned source excerpt

Accordingly, the Commission proposes to replace the last sentence of Comment [4]
