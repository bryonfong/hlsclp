Corpus framing of this source: ELIS 2025: technology-driven pressure and uneven prospects in language services

Corpus synthesis of this source: The European Language Industry Survey 2025 reports financial insecurity, declining confidence and adaptation plans among independent language professionals. It links these pressures to indiscriminate AI and machine-translation use, while distinguishing respondent perceptions from third-party reports of stronger industry segments and recommending collective support.

Corpus source-specific finding

Respondents blame indiscriminate technology use for reduced human translation work, quality and income.

Exact returned source excerpt

All point the finger to an indiscriminate use of language technology, in particular artificial intelligence and dedicated machine translation, to cut costs and replace or minimise human translation work, even in cases where the technology is producing sub-standard output.

Exact returned source excerpt

Both independent professionals and language companies link the trend directly to indiscriminate use by clients, loss of quality and appreciation, and increased price pressure.

Corpus source-specific finding

Freelancers report substantial insecurity, but intentions to leave do not establish actual departures.

Exact returned source excerpt

Stronger price pressure, especially from large language companies, a drop in work volume and the generalised move from human translation to discounted and less-valued post-editing make 53% of the participants question their future as a freelance professional.

Exact returned source excerpt

It is therefore not surprising that 23% are considering ending their freelance activity.

Corpus source-specific finding

Negative sentiment dominates, although technology attitudes differ across respondent groups.

Exact returned source excerpt

The overall negative trend sentiment is clearly visible in the 52% of respondents not mentioning any positive trend.

Exact returned source excerpt

Figure 39 - Trends Language companies and language department are much more balanced in their opinions regarding language technologies.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report qualifies the downturn as uneven rather than industry-wide.

Exact returned source excerpt

The downturn hits small language companies and independent professionals, who make up the core of the ELIS audience, hard.

Exact returned source excerpt

According to third-party research however, the larger international LSCs are suffering less.

Corpus source-specific finding

Language departments and universities face distinct institutional pressures.

Exact returned source excerpt

Language departments and universities maintain a more positive attitude but are also realistic about the language provider concerns and express their own, especially around shrinking budgets, loss of influence or dwindling student numbers.

Exact returned source excerpt

Language departments Language departments do not have the same existential challenges as language service providers, but are feeling the impact of the changes in the industry nevertheless.

Corpus source-specific finding

Most professionals plan to adapt, while many respondents remain uncertain about how to respond.

Exact returned source excerpt

It is clearly high time for European SME players and independent professionals to decide how to react to the current situation, which three out of four companies consider as a structural change for the industry.

Exact returned source excerpt

On the other hand, a majority of professionals plan to react by adapting to the new situation: by acquiring new skills, developing another main activity or changing focus to different client sectors or services, or new types of clients.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report urges industry associations to support adaptation and policymaking.

Exact returned source excerpt

They can, and should, help their members find a way forward by providing guidance, training and networking opportunities.

Exact returned source excerpt

Moreover, associations are best placed to assist policy makers in their efforts to create the necessary framework for a sustainable European language industry.

Corpus framing of this source: European Language Industry Survey 2025: market contraction, technology disruption and adaptation

Corpus synthesis of this source: ELIS 2025 presents a survey-based account of a European language-services market under structural pressure. It reports declining activity, pricing, investment, staffing and freelance confidence; examines AI and machine translation as both sources of displacement and tools for efficiency; compares language companies, independent professionals, language departments, universities and students; and recommends coordinated adaptation, training and industry support. The report repeatedly qualifies its conclusions because its data capture participant perceptions, concentrate on smaller European providers and under-represent larger companies and voice-related services.

Corpus source-specific finding

The report treats its findings as perception-based and only partially representative.

Exact returned source excerpt

Users of this report need to keep in mind that the data are not objective and verifiable.

Exact returned source excerpt

They reflect the personal opinions and expectations of the participants, which are not only built on reality but also on the general mood of the market.

Corpus source-specific finding

Small language companies and freelancers experienced a marked 2024 downturn and expect little or no recovery in 2025.

Exact returned source excerpt

The reason: a dramatic drop in their own 2024 revenue, resulting in a negative -0.40 score for language companies and -0.31 for individuals.

Exact returned source excerpt

50% language company respondents reported a decrease or strong decrease, on average by 19%.

Corpus source-specific finding

External comparisons qualify the breadth of the downturn.

Exact returned source excerpt

With these results, ELIS 2025 results are more pessimistic than those published in the Slator 2025 Language Service Provider Index (LSPI), which showed flat organic growth among the larger language companies.

Exact returned source excerpt

They are, however, in line with the higher number of revenue decreases that the LSPI records among smaller size boutique agencies.

Corpus source-specific finding

Falling activity is feeding price pressure, weaker investment and sustained staffing reductions.

Exact returned source excerpt
