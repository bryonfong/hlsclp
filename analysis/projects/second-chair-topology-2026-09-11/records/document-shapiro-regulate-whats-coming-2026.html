Corpus framing of this source: AI agents, crypto infrastructure and the emerging accountability gap

Corpus synthesis of this source: Zack Shapiro argues that autonomous AI agents are likely to adopt blockchain infrastructure because its machine-readable, permissionless features suit software better than humans. Their ability to transact, contract, create subordinate agents and interact directly with immutable protocols weakens regulatory chokepoints and strains doctrines built around identifiable human actors, control and legal personhood. He presents current incidents, litigation, legislation and experimental findings as early evidence; identifies unresolved liability, antitrust, contract and securities questions; reports theoretical reform proposals; and urges organizations to establish oversight before courts and regulators set the rules. He qualifies the risk-focused account by stressing permissionless systems’ value to activists under authoritarian rule and rejects any simple regulatory prescription.
