Native topic findings

  1. Frontier-model companies are moving from supplying legal software vendors to competing for lawyers’ everyday workflows.
  2. The main disagreement concerns who controls the interface, not whether competition is increasing.
  3. Partnerships create distribution opportunities while exposing vendors to competition from their own suppliers.
  4. Commentators expect the greatest vendor pressure where products offer easily replicated review, drafting or narrow workflows.
  5. Implementation capacity and sustained investment may determine competitive impact more than model access alone.
  6. Buyers face a choice among packaged specialist tools, direct platform use and proprietary development, with different dependency costs.
  7. Competition extends beyond software, but selling legal work remains distinct from selling tools or deployment services.
  8. The evidence supports changing products and relationships more clearly than improved legal outcomes or inevitable displacement.
