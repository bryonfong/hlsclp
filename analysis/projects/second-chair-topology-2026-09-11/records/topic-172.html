Native topic record

Regional innovation: Network sequence matters more than volume

Corpus synthesis: Links novelty to open anchors, mobility and reciprocal institutional development while separating firm, regional and influence outcomes.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Cross-domain institutional evidence: Innovation and market correction remain conditional on incentives, gatekeeping and measurement · Cross-domain innovation networks: Mobility and open anchors generate new forms without displacing incumbents

7 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 3 · Supports: 4

Native topic findings

  1. Organisational novelty emerges through exchanges across institutional boundaries, not simply through technological replacement.
  2. Regional development depends on how diverse organisations interact, not on resource abundance alone.
  3. Open anchors, mobile careers and multiple independent connections can sustain exploration within a cohesive core.
  4. Network development is sequential and reciprocal, leaving room for disagreement about causation.
  5. Firm survival, regional growth and institutional influence are distinct outcomes.
  6. Institutional flexibility enables hybrid practices but also changes public science.
  7. The practical implications concern institution-building processes, not a transferable recipe for success.
