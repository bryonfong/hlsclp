Open topic: Billable hour: Faster matters need not reduce profits

Corpus framing of this source: Billing reform should protect clients’ share of efficiency gains

Corpus synthesis of this source: The ethicalEsq source questions whether advocates use excessive hourly-billing quotas to justify higher fees without better service. Drawing on its earlier commentary rather than empirical findings, it argues that billing alternatives should preserve competitive price reductions and fiduciary protection while treating lawyers, firms and clients fairly.

Corpus source-specific finding

The author questions whether alternative-billing advocacy serves lawyers’ income rather than clients’ interests.

RedirectsWork redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Open topic: Work redesign: Jagged capability demands selective deployment

Corpus framing of this source: Client-centred critique of alternative billing and technology-driven legal pricing

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source argues that replacing hourly billing is not inherently beneficial if alternative methods merely raise lawyers’ fees without improving service. It recommends assessing billing reforms against fairness to clients, lawyers and firms, while accounting for technology and excess provider capacity that should reduce prices. Reader comments separately advocate technology-enabled affordable service, predict competitive pressure on conventional firms, and recommend niche specialisation and lower-overhead practice.
