Corpus synthesis of this source: The article offers a preliminary institutional account of black underrepresentation in elite corporate law firms. It argues that firms’ responses to costly monitoring—high wages, promotion tournaments, leverage and informal tracking—interact with racial stereotypes and unequal networks to disadvantage black lawyers without necessarily reducing firm productivity. It combines labour-market reasoning, historical and comparative evidence, surveys of black Harvard Law School graduates, law-firm data and case studies, while expressly limiting its empirical claims. It evaluates legal, institutional, educational, demand-side and affirmative-action responses and recommends changing partner incentives and reconsidering firm structure.
