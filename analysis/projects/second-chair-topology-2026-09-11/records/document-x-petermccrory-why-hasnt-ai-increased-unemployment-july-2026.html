Corpus synthesis of this source: Peter McCrory synthesises 18 months of Anthropic economic research into a US-focused framework. He argues that AI adoption is already large enough to affect aggregate outcomes, but has so far operated mainly as a skill-biased, labor-augmenting technology rather than a source of broad displacement. Corpus framing of this source: Peter McCrory: AI currently augments labour more than it displaces jobs

Corpus synthesis of this source: Drawing on Anthropic Economic Research, the Anthropic Economic Index, BLS updates and outside studies, McCrory argues that current evidence does not show material AI-driven unemployment. He attributes this partly to incomplete task automation, persistent human complements and jobs adapting around technology. He acknowledges tentative weakness in hiring for young workers in AI-exposed roles, but stresses major causal confounders. Looking ahead, he expects better models and faster productivity growth, while treating transformative growth as contingent on automating weak links across production and R&D.
