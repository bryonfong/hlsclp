Corpus framing of this source: Institutional isomorphism as an explanation for organizational homogenization

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source develops institutional isomorphism as a field-level explanation for why organizations become alike even without demonstrated efficiency gains. It identifies coercive, mimetic and normative mechanisms; illustrates them with prior studies and organizational examples; proposes testable organizational- and field-level predictors; qualifies the framework’s empirical status and measurement limits; and recommends field-level policy analysis when governments seek organizational pluralism.

Three Mechanisms of Institutional Isomorphic Change We identify three mechanisms through which institutional isomorphic change occurs, each with its own antecedents: 1) coercive isomorphism that stems from political influ- ence and the problem of legitimacy; 2) mimetic isomorphism resulting from standard re- sponses to uncertainty; and 3) normative isomorphism, associated with professionaliza- tion.
