Native topic record

Professional authority: AI enters an already contested field

Corpus synthesis: Frames legal-AI change as competition over expertise, legitimacy and institutional power rather than substitution within a unified profession.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Legal institutions and professional practice: Substantive outcomes depend on context, capacity and judgment · Professional authority: Innovation redistributes expertise within existing power structures

18 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 10 · Supports: 7 · Unclear: 1

Native topic findings

  1. This cluster frames legal-AI change as a struggle over expert work, authority and professional legitimacy—not simply technical substitution.
  2. Historical competition challenges the premise that AI confronts an intact, exclusively lawyer-controlled market.
  3. The central disagreement concerns whether professional protection chiefly sustains privilege or also safeguards socially valuable discretion and service.
  4. Professional autonomy depends on states, employers and clients, and varies within the legal profession.
  5. Institutional approaches explain how professionals shape change; field-based critics ask whose power that change preserves.
  6. Explaining who controls legal work does not fully explain how expert performance becomes possible.
  7. Standardisation can support professional expertise while also making some work accessible to outsiders.
  8. The evidence supports a theoretical and historical framework, not a verified forecast of AI-driven professional change.
