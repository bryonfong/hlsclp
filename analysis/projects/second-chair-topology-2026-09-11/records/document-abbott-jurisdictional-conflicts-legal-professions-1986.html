Corpus framing of this source: Abbott: competition over work shapes legal professions

Corpus synthesis of this source: Andrew Abbott’s 1986 article proposes a work-centred explanation of professional development and applies it to differences between American and English legal professions. The supplied abstract identifies the historical evidence, a principal comparative finding and methodological qualifications, but does not detail the alternative methods proposed.

Corpus source-specific finding

Control of work, rather than professional organisation alone, anchors Abbott’s account of professional development.

Exact returned source excerpt

Earlier studies have generally emphasized professional structure and organization while ignoring work and its control.

Exact returned source excerpt

I argue that control of work is central to professional development.

Corpus source-specific finding

Abbott uses historical conflicts over practice to explain differences between the two legal professions.

Exact returned source excerpt

This paper analyzes the work available to the legal profession, the numbers and types of legal personnel available to do that work, and the various competitors contesting it.

Exact returned source excerpt

Studying in detail complaints of unqualified practice in England (1870–1940) and two American states (1910–50), I locate the types of contested work and the competitors involved, using these to explain important aspects of the two legal prof essions today.

Corpus source-specific finding

The principal competitors differed: corporations in America and the state in Britain.

Exact returned source excerpt

One striking discovery is the contrast in competitors; American lawyers' chief competitors were corporations, while British lawyers' chief competitor was the state.
