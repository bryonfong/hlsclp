Automated evaluation: Stable scoring can preserve bad rubrics

Corpus synthesis: Distinguishes criterion diagnosis, overall quality, calibration and execution, requiring separate tests of standards and judges.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Declared lineage: Evidence for deployed AI: Proxy measures do not establish real-world effectiveness · Legal-AI effectiveness: Complete legal tasks and resolved problems outrank benchmark scores

11 distinct stance-bearing works · Builds on: 5 · Supports: 6

Native topic findings

  1. The central issue is whether automated evaluation measures professional quality, not merely whether judges produce consistent scores.
  2. Expert-grounded, criterion-level benchmarks aim to expose failures that aggregate scores conceal.
  3. The strongest methodological disagreement concerns rubrics as summaries of overall quality versus tools for diagnosis.
  4. Calibration and structured execution improve reported agreement, but address different failure modes.
  5. Rubric execution and rubric quality require separate testing; stabilizing a flawed standard can preserve its defects.
  6. Human validation remains necessary, but reference labels and expert agreement also carry uncertainty.
  7. Practical deployment requires balancing evaluation cost, coverage and safeguards rather than selecting one universally superior scoring method.
