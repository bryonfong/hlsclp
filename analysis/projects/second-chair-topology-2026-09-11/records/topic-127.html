Financialization: Profit sources reshape risk distribution

Corpus synthesis: Defines economy-wide financial accumulation through corporate restructuring and capital allocation, with measurement choices and causal accounts contested.

Native topic findings

  1. Financialization concerns changing sources of profit and forms of capital allocation, not simply a larger financial sector.
  2. Historical US evidence shows increased financial accumulation, but the measurement choices matter.
  3. Accounting, corporate reorganization and offshoring qualify the estimates without fully explaining the reported trend.
  4. The literature offers competing causal accounts, while identifying concrete mechanisms of corporate restructuring.
  5. Financialization shifts risks toward households and links financial returns to unequal outcomes.
  6. Greater financial dependence does not necessarily mean greater control by identifiable banks or a unified corporate elite.
  7. Financial calculation reaches public and social institutions, but national histories shape its effects and possible responses.
  8. The shared methodological implication is to establish mechanisms and variation before making sweeping historical or predictive claims.

The Financialization of the American Economy

Academia & research · Foundations (pre-2015)

Corpus framing of this source: Krippner: Measuring financialization through US corporate accumulation

Corpus synthesis of this source: Greta R. Krippner’s 2005 paper documents a post-1970s shift toward financial sources of US corporate income and profits. It develops complementary measures, tests accounting and organizational objections, and proposes implications for research on corporate control and state autonomy. Krippner distinguishes this descriptive contribution from causal explanations, historical novelty claims and forecasts.

Financialization of the Economy

Academia & research · Automation debate (2015-2022)

Corpus framing of this source: Financialization as a shift from institutions to markets

Corpus synthesis of this source: Davis and Kim’s 2015 sociological review explains how expanding financial markets reshape corporations, households, inequality and political power. Their central contribution distinguishes market-based intermediation from the growing size of financial institutions. They synthesize historical and empirical research, qualify claims of global convergence, and explicitly treat their unifying institutional explanation as partly speculative.

Corpus source-specific finding

The central change concerns how capital reaches borrowers, not simply how large finance becomes.

Exact returned source excerpt

We constrain our review primarily to recent sociological work on the antecedents and effects of the spread of financial markets since the 1970s.

Exact returned source excerpt

The argument that emerges from our review is that how finance is intermediated in an economy—that is, how money is channeled from savers (investors) to borrowers (households, companies, governments)—shapes social institutions in fundamental ways.

Corpus source-specific finding

Theory, technology and political conditions jointly enabled market expansion.

Exact returned source excerpt
