Corpus synthesis of this source: The authors present their British Nationality Act formalisation as a demonstration of logic programming’s promise for implementing rules and regulations. They explain how existing statutory text, a distinction between logical representation and Prolog implementation, and top-down development shaped the experiment. Corpus framing of this source: Logic-programming formalisation of the British Nationality Act

Corpus synthesis of this source: The source reports an experimental translation of substantial portions of the British Nationality Act into extended Horn-clause logic. It uses the project to identify the practical strengths, representation problems and wider software implications of encoding legislation as executable rules.
