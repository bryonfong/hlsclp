Entry routes: Reduced hiring precedes broader displacement

Corpus synthesis: Finds young-worker divergence and automation-oriented associations, qualified by revisions, controls and unresolved education shocks.

Native topic findings

  1. The central debate concerns whether AI is narrowing entry routes into exposed occupations, not whether it has already caused economy-wide job losses.
  2. The ADP evidence shows widening young-worker divergence, but revisions change both the observation period and the headline measure.
  3. Reduced hiring emerges more clearly than increased dismissals, lower pay or unemployment.
  4. Automation-oriented use and seniority-specific employment patterns support a substitution hypothesis, not a general claim that AI disadvantages every junior worker.
  5. Robustness checks leave substantial uncertainty about causation, timing and education-related shocks.
  6. National and cross-country comparisons challenge both the magnitude and the AI attribution of exposed-job declines.
  7. Career-development risks extend beyond employment counts, but the expertise-loss argument remains a hypothesis.
  8. The practical research agenda is to track entry routes and actual deployment without treating exposure, usage or forecasts as measured displacement.
