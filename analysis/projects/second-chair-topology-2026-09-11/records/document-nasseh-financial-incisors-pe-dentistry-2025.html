Corpus framing of this source: Private equity in dentistry: higher charges and a shift toward higher-reimbursement services

Corpus synthesis of this source: Nasseh and colleagues examine practice-level changes after private equity acquisition. They report higher list charges, no statistically significant change in negotiated prices, and shifts in service mix and practice structure. They caution that revenue gains need not benefit patients or providers and recommend policymakers attend to welfare effects.

Corpus source-specific finding

The study uses longitudinal practice and claims data to estimate acquisition effects.

Exact returned source excerpt

**Study Setting and Design.** We utilize a proprietary dental office database linked to administrative dental commercial claims data to estimate the effects of private equity ownership on the financial and operational outcomes of dental offices, employing a staggered difference-in-differences panel design that addresses the nonrandom acquisitions of facilities by private equity firms.

Exact returned source excerpt

**Data Sources.** We rely on private equity transaction data from 2015 to 2021, longitudinal dental office data from the 2015 to 2017 and 2019 to 2021 American Dental Association dental office database, and aggregated commercial dental insurance price and utilization data from 2015 to 2021.

Corpus source-specific finding

Charges rose after acquisition, but negotiated prices did not change significantly.

Exact returned source excerpt

**Principle Findings.** Following acquisition, private equity-owned dental offices increased charges for dental care services by 3.3% (95% CI: 2.3%–4.4%), although allowed prices for these services remained statistically unchanged.

Exact returned source excerpt

**Conclusions.** Allowed or negotiated prices between dentists and payers did not change in dental offices after being acquired by private equity.

Corpus source-specific finding

Acquired offices shifted toward higher-reimbursement procedures and multispecialty practice.

Exact returned source excerpt

Dental offices acquired by private equity firms tended to shift from diagnostic and preventive procedures to generally higher reimbursement restorative, specialty, and surgical procedures.

Exact returned source excerpt

Dental offices were more likely to become multispecialty practices after being acquired by a private equity firm.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors caution that practice revenue gains may not improve patient or provider welfare.

Nevertheless, list prices for dental services increased in private equity-owned practices, meaning higher prices can still be passed on to patients.
