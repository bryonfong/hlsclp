Native topic findings

  1. Regular minimum-wage adjustment can protect low-paid workers during inflation, but average protection does not imply universal gains.
  2. The central policy trade-off concerns predictable protection versus discretion, rather than a simple choice between indexation and inaction.
  3. Adjustment frequency, implementation delays and scope for exceptions shape practical protection.
  4. The OECD judges minimum-wage-driven inflation risks limited, while acknowledging spillovers and modelling uncertainty.
  5. Minimum-wage resilience contrasts with slower and often incomplete protection through collective bargaining.
  6. The comparative evidence supports a bounded assessment of policy responses, not a uniform account of workers’ outcomes.

Corpus framing of this source: Minimum-wage protection during inflation: adjustment mechanisms, trade-offs and evidence

Corpus synthesis of this source: OECD Employment Outlook 2023: Artificial Intelligence and the Labour Market finds that minimum wages broadly kept pace with inflation through May 2023, unlike negotiated wages. It recommends regular adjustments, compares discretionary increases with indexation, and assesses purchasing-power benefits against reduced policy flexibility and wage compression. Its evidence combines wage and price series, a cross-country policy questionnaire and cited research.
