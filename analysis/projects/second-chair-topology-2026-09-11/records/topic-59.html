SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12: Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management

Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Foundations (pre-2015)

Corpus framing of this source: SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12: Model risk management across development, validation and governance

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Federal Reserve and OCC's 2011 supervisory guidance broadens model oversight beyond validation to the full model lifecycle. Drawing on supervisory and industry experience rather than a new empirical study, it recommends proportionate controls, independent and influential challenge, continuing performance assessment, and bank accountability for both internal and vendor models. Its scope concerns quantitative banking models; its warnings describe conditional risks rather than quantified forecasts.

Corpus source-specific finding

Manage model risk across the lifecycle, with rigor proportionate to exposure.

Exact returned source excerpt

The expanding use of models in all aspects of banking reflects the extent to which models can improve business decisions, but models also come with costs.

SupportsSR 26-2: Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management

SR 26-2: Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management

Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Current wave (2026)

Corpus framing of this source: SR 26-2: Risk-based model governance with explicit limits on AI scope

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Federal Reserve, FDIC and OCC present nonbinding guidance grounded in supervisory experience, industry feedback and modeling advances. They recommend proportionate oversight across development, use, validation, monitoring and third-party procurement, while explicitly excluding generative and agentic AI from the guidance’s scope.

Corpus source-specific finding

The agencies recommend tailored risk management rather than enforceable, uniform requirements.

Exact returned source excerpt

PURPOSE AND SCOPE Based on supervisory experience and industry feedback, as well as technological advancements in modeling over the past several years, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, and Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (the “agencies”) are issuing this revised model risk management guidance to clarify model risk management principles, and to set forth a risk-based approach to model risk management, tailored to a banking organization’s model risk profile and the size and complexity of its operations.

Exact returned source excerpt

Continued use of models is essential to maintaining the competitiveness of banking organizations in the financial system.

Corpus source-specific finding

The guidance covers traditional quantitative models and non-generative, non-agentic AI—not generative or agentic AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

For the purposes of this guidance, the term “model” refers to a complex quantitative method, system, or approach that applies statistical, economic, or financial theories to process input data into quantitative estimates.

Exact returned source excerpt

The term “model” in this guidance excludes simple arithmetic calculations, such as those found within spreadsheets, as well as deterministic rule- based processes and software where there are no statistical, economic, or financial theories underpinning their design or use.

Corpus source-specific finding

Risk depends on model assumptions, business significance and actual use—not accuracy alone.

Exact returned source excerpt

A model’s inherent risk reflects several fundamental factors, such as the assumptions made in developing the model, the model’s complexity, the quality of inputs for the model, and data constraints.

Exact returned source excerpt

Model exposure refers to the significance of the model output to a banking organization’s business decisions, which tends to be greater for models affecting larger portfolios or having a larger business impact.

Corpus source-specific finding

Development should start with intended use, incorporate user insights and test proportionately.

Exact returned source excerpt

The 5 ^Ldeveloper’s judgment influences the model’s conceptual design and soundness, inputs, assumptions, and methodology, all of which affect a model’s inherent risk.

Exact returned source excerpt

An effective development process generally begins with a clear statement of purpose to maximize the likelihood that model development is aligned with the intended use.

Corpus source-specific finding

Effective challenge requires expertise, objectivity and influence; validation quality does not depend on a particular organizational structure.

Exact returned source excerpt

Sound model risk management also involves “effective challenge,” which refers to the critical analysis conducted by objective experts who evaluate model risk and effect appropriate changes throughout the model lifecycle, from model development to ongoing monitoring.

Exact returned source excerpt

Effective challenge is performed by individuals with the appropriate expertise to conduct a critical and objective challenge, sufficient independence to maintain objectivity, as well as the organizational standing and influence to effect any change.

Corpus source-specific finding

Validation combines conceptual review with real-world outcomes analysis and continuing monitoring.

Exact returned source excerpt

Components of Model Validation Conceptual Soundness Validating conceptual soundness involves assessing and documenting model design (including key modeling choices, assumptions, qualitative judgments, and data selection), construction, and developmental testing.

Exact returned source excerpt

While evaluating theoretical construction may be important for some models, other assessments—such as interpretability measures or benchmarking to other models—may be more practical for other models.

Corpus source-specific finding

Governance should establish accountability, manage conflicts and preserve institutional knowledge.

Exact returned source excerpt

GOVERNANCE AND CONTROLS All components of the model lifecycle benefit from model governance that is supported by clear policies and effective controls.

Exact returned source excerpt

Roles and Responsibilities Model risk management benefits from clear roles and responsibilities with well-defined accountability, including with respect to potential conflicts of interest (e.g., misalignment of incentives between different reporting lines, such as model development and validation groups).

Proprietary vendor models remain subject to model-risk principles despite information constraints.

Exact returned source excerpt
