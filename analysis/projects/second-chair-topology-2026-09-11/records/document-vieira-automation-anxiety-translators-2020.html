Corpus framing of this source: Automation anxiety and translators: technology, pay and professional roles

Corpus synthesis of this source: Lucas Nunes Vieira combines translators’ online discourse with employment research, wage statistics and professional surveys to argue that machine translation’s economic effects depend heavily on business practices and work organisation. His predominantly 2010–2017 evidence identifies concerns about quality and commercial use rather than widespread immediate displacement anxiety. He recommends preserving translators’ broad advisory role across technical and creative domains.

Corpus framing of this source: Automation anxiety and translators

Corpus synthesis of this source: Lucas Nunes Vieira contextualizes translators’ attitudes to machine translation within employment trends, occupational-automation research, industry data and translators’ own discourse. He argues that machine translation poses no short- to medium-term danger to the profession as a whole; translators’ immediate concerns centre more on technology’s limitations and associated market practices. He cautions against treating human translators and machines as substitutes, interpreting uncertain automation estimates literally, or pushing qualified translators out of technical fields, and recommends integrated business models that preserve translators’ broad advisory role.
