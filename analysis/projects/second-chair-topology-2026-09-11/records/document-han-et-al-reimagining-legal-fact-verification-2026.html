It finds that plausible errors, confidentiality risks and mandatory human review can offset speed gains, positioning GenAI as an aid that itself requires verification rather than an autonomous verifier. It recommends auditable, transparent systems that preserve human oversight and professional learning.

Corpus framing of this source: Reimagining Legal Fact Verification with GenAI: Toward Effective Human-AI Collaboration

Corpus synthesis of this source: Based on semi-structured interviews with 18 non-litigation lawyers, Han and colleagues characterise legal fact verification as iterative professional sensemaking rather than a discrete checking step. They find that lawyers currently use GenAI mainly for orientation, retrieval support, risk spotting and routine drafting, while retaining manual verification and responsibility. The authors propose auditable, transparent and legally specialised systems that automate preparatory work without displacing professional judgment, subject to confidentiality, provenance and accountability constraints.
