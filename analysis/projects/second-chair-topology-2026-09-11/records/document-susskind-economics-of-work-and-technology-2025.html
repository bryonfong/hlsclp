Corpus framing of this source: From skill-biased technological change to tasks, substitution and complementarity

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Economics of Work and Technology (Gresham College, The Future of Work series, Lecture 2) traces why economists reconsidered a benign account of technological progress. The lecturer contrasts its education-policy implications with difficulties faced by middle- and lower-skilled workers, then endorses task-level analysis and the distinction between technology substituting for and complementing workers.

Corpus framing of this source: Technology reshapes tasks more often than it eliminates occupations

Corpus synthesis of this source: The lecture argues that analysis should focus on tasks rather than job titles and distinguish technology’s substituting effect from its complementing effect. It presents the historical predominance of complementarity as a qualified general pattern, uses radiology as a contemporary illustration, and tentatively questions whether increasingly capable AI could strengthen substitution while shrinking the unautomated work available to people.
