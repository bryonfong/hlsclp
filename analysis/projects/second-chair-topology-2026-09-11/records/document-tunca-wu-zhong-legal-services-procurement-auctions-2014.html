Corpus framing of this source: Open-ended legal procurement auctions: price savings, quality and apparent incumbency advantages

Corpus synthesis of this source: An Empirical Analysis of Price, Quality, and Incumbency in Procurement Auctions combines auction theory with GECF legal-services procurement data. The authors argue that discretionary award rules and new bidders can pressure high-quality incumbents to reduce prices, while quality preferences—not incumbency alone—can explain contract awards. They distinguish direct savings from indirect competitive benefits and qualify their recommendations by market conditions and concentration risks.

Corpus framing of this source: Open-ended procurement auctions can reduce legal-service costs without sacrificing quality

Corpus synthesis of this source: Tunca, Wu and Zhong combine an auction model with before-and-after evidence from GE Commercial Finance’s legal-services procurement to explain how nonbinding scoring rules, hidden quality preferences and nonincumbent participation affect prices, supplier quality, incumbency and buyer utility. They find that open-ended awards can induce aggressive bidding by incumbents and nonincumbents, preserve average quality, and improve buyer utility, while qualifying the results with modelling simplifications, possible endogeneity and unobserved supplier costs.
