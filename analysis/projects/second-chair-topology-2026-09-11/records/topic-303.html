Native topic findings

  1. Insurance connects AI governance to financial accountability, but does not replace professional duties or legal compliance.
  2. Sources disagree about the adequacy of existing coverage, with important differences by jurisdiction, policy line and wording.
  3. Verification failures create liability exposure, but their effect on coverage remains contested rather than settled.
  4. Reported malpractice claims are emerging, but sanctions, litigation counts and insurer concerns do not establish loss prevalence or causation.
  5. Immature loss data constrain pricing, while commercial competition can restrain tighter underwriting.
  6. Specialist products transfer different risks; an AI insurance label does not establish comprehensive protection.
  7. Practical recommendations converge on documented controls and portfolio-wide coverage review, although underwriting requirements vary.
  8. Insurance-backed assurance offers a proposed governance mechanism, but procedural compliance may not establish substantive safety.
  9. Existing law supplies causes of action, but allocating responsibility across the AI supply chain remains unresolved.
