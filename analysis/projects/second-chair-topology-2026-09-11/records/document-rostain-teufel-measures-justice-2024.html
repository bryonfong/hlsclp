Exact returned source excerpt

Objectives-based evaluation compares the number of services provided or people helped to a program’s goals, the total comparable needs of the community targeted by the intervention, or the number of services provided or people helped by comparable programs, if they exist, to determine whether people find a service useful.96 Another basic way to assess these programs — a staple of service assessment, except in law – is to ask people about their experiences using the services by asking whether they are satisfied with the service.97 A third simple assessment involves using net promoter scores, which assess how likely a person would be to refer the service to a friend or family member.98 As noted above, research shows that a significant barrier people face in obtaining a just resolution of their problems is a lack of capacity to recognize those problems as legal and specifically to understand how the law connects to the specific issues they face.

Corpus synthesis of this source: The article proposes a people-centred framework for evaluating lay legal assistance across quality control, individual effectiveness, social impact, sustainability and scalability.
