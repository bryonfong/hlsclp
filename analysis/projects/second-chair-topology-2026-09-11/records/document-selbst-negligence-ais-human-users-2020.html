Open topic: Human oversight: Accountability is not effective control

Corpus framing of this source: Negligence and AI’s Human Users: limits of oversight and compensation

Corpus synthesis of this source: Andrew D. Selbst’s 2020 article examines whether negligence can preserve meaningful duties of care and compensation when people use AI decision assistance. Through doctrinal reasoning, reported research and illustrative hypotheticals, he identifies problems of foreseeability, human capacity, operational security and unequal injuries. He recommends interventions to support adaptation but leaves their effectiveness uncertain.

Corpus framing of this source: Negligence and AI’s Human Users — Andrew D. Selbst (2020)

Corpus synthesis of this source: Selbst shifts tort analysis from autonomous systems and manufacturer liability to human use of decision-assistance AI. He argues that AI can weaken negligence law’s capacity to connect reasonable care, fault and individual injury, identifies four mechanisms producing that difficulty, and proposes transparency, documentation and alternative compensation or regulation while acknowledging substantial uncertainty about adaptation and remedies.

Corpus source-specific finding

Reframes AI tort analysis around users of decision-assistance tools

Exact returned source excerpt

Though AI often conjures images of autonomous robots, especially autonomous vehicles, most existing AI technologies are not autonomous.

Exact returned source excerpt
