SupportsAssisted inequality: Productivity gains need not improve careers

Open topic: Assisted inequality: Productivity gains need not improve careers

Corpus framing of this source: AI narrows education-based task-performance gaps without eliminating human-capital differences

Corpus synthesis of this source: Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps? Evidence from a Randomized Experiment reports a preregistered Argentine experiment showing that GPT-4.1 assistance closes roughly three-quarters of an education-based performance gap. The authors distinguish improved task execution from underlying skills, find limited immediate carryover after AI removal, and explicitly avoid predicting wage or aggregate inequality effects.

Corpus source-specific finding

The experiment isolates between-education differences that workplace studies often cannot capture.

Exact returned source excerpt

In our preregistered online experiment, 1,174 individuals aged 25 to 45 from Ar- gentina were recruited to complete a workplace-relevant task with randomized access to an AI assistant.

Exact returned source excerpt

2 Experimental design 2.1 Recruitment strategy We conducted an online experiment in Argentina between September and November of 2025, in which participants were required to complete an incentivized task on a personal computer.

Corpus source-specific finding

The education comparison and task deliberately target general cognitive performance.

Exact returned source excerpt

We define low-education individuals as those who only have a high school diploma or have completed less than half of a postsecondary de- gree (university or non-university tertiary programs), and high-education individuals as those who have completed more than half of such a program.2 This education classification was preregistered, and our results are robust to alternative definitions of the threshold for high education.

Exact returned source excerpt

The task involves solving a realistic hypothetical business scenario: participants receive an email from their boss, and to answer it they must analyze several sources of information.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI improves both groups’ scores but benefits lower-education participants more.

Exact returned source excerpt

Measured in standard deviations (SD) relative to the low-education control group, AI access

Exact returned source excerpt

increases the overall task score (our main outcome of interest) by 1.242 SD for low- education individuals and by 0.834 SD for high-education individuals.

Corpus source-specific finding

Immediate unassisted results reject a purely temporary-delegation account, not establish general skill formation.

Exact returned source excerpt

Immediately after submitting the task, all participants com- pleted an incentivized follow-up module without access to the assistant, including an open-ended question asking them to articulate the root cause of the business problem and some recall questions about the task content.

Exact returned source excerpt

The results do not support a pure delegation interpretation: treated participants do not perform worse than controls once AI is removed.

Corpus source-specific finding

Carryover associates with sustained engagement, while effective tool use still varies by education.

Exact returned source excerpt

Consistent with this interpretation, when we divide treated participants into four mutually exclusive groups based on levels of AI assistance and engagement with the task, we find that intensive AI assistance predicts strong task performance even when engagement is low, but follow-up performance is substantially higher only when intensive AI assistance is combined with high engagement with the task.

Exact returned source excerpt

The chat-log analysis is consistent with this interpretation: both groups obtain substantial assistance from the tool, but higher-education participants use it somewhat more effectively across margins such as prompt detail and workflow structure.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors interpret AI as reducing task-execution constraints rather than equalizing fundamental abilities.

Exact returned source excerpt

By assist- ing participants in formulating their responses and producing the final written output, AI substitutes for these scarcer inputs and leads to larger productivity gains for this group, even though higher-education participants also benefit.

Exact returned source excerpt

Instead, it relaxes constraints related to problem structuring, organization, and written communication that are more binding for some individuals than for others.

Corpus source-specific finding

The literature review presents equalization as common but dependent on setting and task.

Exact returned source excerpt

Taken together, the emerging evidence suggests that generative AI often raises performance and compresses the productivity distribution within relatively homo- geneous groups of workers, though heterogeneous effects can vary across settings and tasks.

Exact returned source excerpt

In an online experiment with 444 college-educated professionals, Noy and Zhang (2023) find that access to ChatGPT increases output quality by about 0.45 SD and reduces completion time by about 0.83 SD, with larger gains for lower baseline performers.

Corpus source-specific finding

The findings do not directly predict wages or economy-wide inequality.

Exact returned source excerpt

By conducting the experiment outside firms, we recover the between-education-group variation that organizational data cannot cleanly identify, and isolate a task-level capa- bility effect under equal access and basic onboarding.

Exact returned source excerpt

Our results are specific to a self-contained workplace-style task that combines reading comprehension, problem diagnosis, and written communication.

Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied excerpts from Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps? Evidence from a Randomized Experiment describe the authors’ grading methodology, not findings about productivity gaps. They explain task-specific AI evaluation, mechanical scoring safeguards, follow-up assessment and publication of grading materials; they do not establish grading accuracy or report predictions.

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors use task-specific prompts and examples to structure AI-assisted grading.

Exact returned source excerpt

In each call, the model receives two inputs: (1) the participant’s response and (2) the relevant task-specific rubric, which includes the full task context and the scoring guidelines (including the illustrative examples).

Exact returned source excerpt

The model is invoked with a fixed system prompt (in Spanish, matching the language of the tasks and answers) that instructs it to behave as an extremely rigorous grader, to avoid inferring missing information, and to return only a valid JSON object in a fixed schema.

Corpus source-specific finding

The solution rubric evaluates proposed actions separately and rewards root-cause relevance.

Exact returned source excerpt

The solution rubric explicitly allows participants to propose one or more concrete actions.

Exact returned source excerpt

Each action is evaluated separately under three criteria: whether it addresses the root cause (B1), how specific it is (B2), and whether it is realistic (B3).

Corpus source-specific finding

The authors separate content and writing scores and apply explicit safeguards against undeserved credit.

Exact returned source excerpt

The system prompt also enforces an “anti-hallucination” rule: if a content element is not present in the participant’s response, it must be marked as absent and assigned 0 points.
