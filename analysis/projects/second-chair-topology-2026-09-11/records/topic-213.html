Filing duties: AI use never displaces inquiry

Corpus synthesis: Applies existing certification and verification duties across participants, with sanctions depending on authority and contested mental-state standards.

This record retains the provider’s full available topic findings and source perspectives. It does not claim a new independent analytical review. Positions refer to this topic’s proposition: “Redirects” is not simple opposition, and “Unclear” is not support.

Native topic findings

  1. The central proposition is that AI-assisted filings remain subject to existing duties of reasonable inquiry and human verification.
  2. Responsibility follows participation and certification, not simply who operated the AI tool.
  3. Good intentions, plausible output and correct legal conclusions do not necessarily excuse fabricated supporting authorities.
  4. The principal doctrinal disagreement concerns the mental-state threshold for court-initiated sanctions.
  5. Courts must identify an applicable sanctions mechanism rather than assume Rule 11 governs every fabricated citation.
  6. Sanctions remain discretionary, proportionate and procedurally constrained even after a violation is established.
  7. Correction, candor and preventive safeguards affect sanctions, while repetition and continued advocacy increase exposure.
  8. The policy debate asks whether existing accountability rules suffice or need AI-specific reinforcement.
