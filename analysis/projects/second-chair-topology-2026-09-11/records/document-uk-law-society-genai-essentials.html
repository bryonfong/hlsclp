Corpus framing of this source: Generative AI: opportunities, limits and solicitors’ continuing responsibilities

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Law Society of England and Wales’s 2025 primer presents legal AI applications alongside uncertain long-term effects, accuracy risks and practical safeguards. Aimed particularly at small and medium-sized firms, with relevance to in-house solicitors, it stresses that AI assistance does not displace professional duties. It offers guidance rather than legal advice.

Corpus source-specific finding

Adoption offers potential service improvements, but its long-term effects remain uncertain.

Exact returned source excerpt

While it is uncertain how generative AI may impact the legal profession in the long-term, many solicitors and law firms are using and investing in tools as part of their practice, with the aims of improving service provision, reducing costs and meeting new client demands.

Exact returned source excerpt

These include: - analysing contracts - drafting or summarising documentation - facilitating e-discovery - powering client chatbots - enhancing internal knowledge databases - predicting case outcomes As generative AI is an emerging market, how and to what extent these technologies are used within the legal profession as well as what tools become available is likely to rapidly change.

Corpus source-specific finding

Survey respondents expect substantial impact while expressing mixed feelings.

Exact returned source excerpt
