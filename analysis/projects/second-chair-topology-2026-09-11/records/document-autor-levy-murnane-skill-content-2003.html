Corpus synthesis of this source: Autor, Levy and Murnane’s 2003 study develops and tests a task-based explanation of technological change using US employment and occupational data for 1960–1998. It links computerization to declining routine labor input and rising nonroutine cognitive work, including changes within education groups and occupations. Corpus framing of this source: Computerization reallocates work from routine to nonroutine tasks

Corpus synthesis of this source: The study develops and tests a task-based account of technological change. It argues that falling computer costs substitute for routine cognitive and manual work while complementing nonroutine analytic and interactive work, and it links this mechanism to observed US task shifts between 1960 and 1998. It also identifies important measurement, scope and inference limitations.
