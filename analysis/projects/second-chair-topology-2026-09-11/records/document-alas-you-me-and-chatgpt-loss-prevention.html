Open topic: AI privilege: Legal subject matter is insufficient

Corpus framing of this source: Managing confidentiality and liability risks from clients’ GenAI use

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Loss Prevention Takeaways source argues that clients’ independent use of GenAI creates confidentiality, privilege and filing risks that lawyers must address. It recommends documented client warnings, restrictions on AI recording and transcription, human verification of client-added content, and personnel training. It supports its advice with professional-conduct obligations, reported transcription problems and accounts of judicial sanctions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Clients’ GenAI use may engage lawyers’ confidentiality obligations.

Exact returned source excerpt

Notably, ABA Model Rule of Professional Conduct 1.6 requires attorneys to make reasonable efforts to prevent the inadvertent or unauthorized disclosure of, or access to, confidential information.

Exact returned source excerpt

This obligation may be triggered if a lawyer is aware that a client is using, or intends to use, a GenAI platform to supplement the firm's legal advice or otherwise participate in the representation.

Corpus source-specific finding

Restrict AI recording and transcription, with safeguards when clients insist.

Exact returned source excerpt

As noted in our June 16, 2025 Loss Prevention Minute, AI transcription tools often produce errors or incomplete transcripts.

Exact returned source excerpt

When hosting a meeting, lawyers should disable AI transcription tools.

Corpus source-specific finding

Lawyers must verify AI-assisted client edits and check tribunal requirements.

Exact returned source excerpt

Because these tools are prone to hallucinations—producing false or fabricated information—there is a real risk that clients could insert inaccurate, misleading, and fundamentally flawed outputs into court filings.

Exact returned source excerpt

If a lawyer fails to thoroughly verify the accuracy of these outputs and files documents containing hallucinations, the lawyer may face judicial reprimand, sanction, and other professional discipline.

Corpus source-specific finding

Document client education through prominent warnings and targeted alerts.

Exact returned source excerpt

Below are practical ways to document advice to clients regarding these risks: - **Add a notice to transmittal emails and correspondence containing privileged information.** When transmitting confidential or privileged communications, including written documentation such as briefs, memorandums, etc., consider including a clear and prominent notice advising clients not to input or upload the information to a GenAI tool.

Exact returned source excerpt

- **Distribute a client alert.** Consider sending a client alert or other transmittal identifying the risks of inputting confidential or privileged information and legal documents into GenAI technology.

Corpus source-specific finding

Train personnel to support client warnings and verify client-added material.

Exact returned source excerpt

- **Educate firm personnel.** Ensure that firm personnel are aware of the firm's efforts to highlight the risks of clients uploading confidential or privileged information to GenAI platforms.

Exact returned source excerpt

Firms should also remind lawyers of their duty to confirm the accuracy of any client-added content in documents, particularly those intended for submission to a tribunal or third party, to ensure the information is accurate and authentic.

Indeed, in one "embarrassing" incident, a client, who was also an attorney, faced sanctions after providing his counsel with fake citations generated by AI.[^1] That attorney was fortunate, as this has not been the case in other situations.

Corpus framing of this source: ALAS on managing client use of GenAI in legal matters

Corpus synthesis of this source: ALAS identifies clients’ undisclosed use of GenAI as an emerging loss-prevention risk for law firms. It argues that uploading legal material can expose confidential or privileged information, while AI-assisted transcription and document editing can introduce errors, hallucinations, tribunal-rule violations and professional liability. It recommends proactive client warnings, verification of client-added content, restrictions and protocols for AI recording, and internal education. Its approximately 230-opinion figure is presented as evidence from an external hallucination-cases database, while several client-use examples are illustrative scenarios rather than reported incidents.
