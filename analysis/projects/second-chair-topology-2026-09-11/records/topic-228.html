Native topic findings

  1. Coding-assistant productivity depends on the human workflow around generation, not generation speed alone.
  2. Verification, editing and prompting create overhead that conventional interaction metrics undercount.
  3. Randomized experiments report uneven gains, with stronger benefits for less-experienced developers.
  4. Perceived usefulness, measured speed and code quality are distinct outcomes.
  5. Study design, uptake and tool evolution limit generalization.
  6. Practical proposals focus on reducing checking burdens and adapting assistance to user intent.
