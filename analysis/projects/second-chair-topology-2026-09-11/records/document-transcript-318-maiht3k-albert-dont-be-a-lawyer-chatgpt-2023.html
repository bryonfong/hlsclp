Corpus framing of this source: Legal-AI hype mistakes text performance for legal competence

Corpus synthesis of this source: Emily M. Bender, Alex Hanna and Kendra Albert argue that demonstrations such as passing bar-exam questions or generating legal-looking documents do not establish an ability to practise law. They emphasise the relational, investigative, evaluative and ethical dimensions of legal work; identify risks involving confidentiality, fabricated content and unequal users’ ability to assess outputs; reject generative AI as a default answer to the justice gap; and question claims that legal work reduces to manipulating a complete documentary corpus. They also use non-legal examples to illustrate how indiscriminate generative-AI use can displace human communication and burden access-enabling institutions.

Corpus source-specific finding

Albert treats bar-exam performance as a weak proxy for legal competence, while Bender explains the result as a construct-validity failure rather than evidence that a model is a lawyer.
