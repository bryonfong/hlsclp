Bandla v Solicitors Regulation Authority — High Court Strikes Out Appeal Grounds on Its Own Motion as Abuse of Process for Citation of 25 Non-Existent Authorities

Regulators, courts & professional bodies · Adoption at scale (2025)

Corpus framing of this source: Bandla v SRA: refusal of a substantially late disciplinary appeal

Corpus synthesis of this source: The supplied judgment extracts explain why the court refused an extension of time: it found valid sending of the SDT judgment, unreliable accounts of receipt, insufficient medical evidence to explain the delay, and weak underlying appeal grounds. They flag a separate concern about cited case law but do not supply the later ruling on that issue.

Corpus source-specific finding

The court refused an extension after a delay exceeding seven years.

Exact returned source excerpt

The basis on which the Appellant was struck off was that he had no insurance for 2015/16; that he had misleadingly and dishonestly stated on his 6 October 2015 renewal application that insurance was held; and that he had abandoned his firm without taking the appropriate steps in relation to the SRA as regulator and in relation to his clients.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Appellant’s appeal notice in this Court was lodged on 10 October 2024.

Corpus source-specific finding

Sending the written reasons in June 2017 started the appeal period; personal service under the Hague Convention was unnecessary.

Exact returned source excerpt

PD52D §3.3A provides as follows: ***Where a statement of reasons for a decision is given later than the notice of that decision, the period for filing the appellant’s notice is calculated from the date on which the statement is sent to the appellant.*** 14.

Exact returned source excerpt

So far as the “sending” of the order and judgment are concerned, I have already explained the SDT’s order striking off the Appellant was “sent to him” by letter on 10 May 2017 and the judgment was “sent to him” on 22 June 2017.

Corpus source-specific finding

Contradictory accounts undermined Bandla’s denial of receiving the judgment.

Exact returned source excerpt

It is quite right that the envelope handed over to him in person on 29 March 2017 did not contain the judgment.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Court has been given no convincing explanation at all as to why, in his admittedly healthy state of mind, the Appellant would have put forward to this Court in his Grounds of Appeal a clear description of receipt of “the Tribunal Judgment” in an envelope, if the truth were that he never received the judgment.

Corpus source-specific finding

Evidence of mental illness did not establish inability to appeal throughout the relevant period.

Exact returned source excerpt

His position is that this description of an inability to “understand” or “communicate”, due to delusional problems arising from schizophrenia, continued from 1 August 2015 right through to July 2017 and through to February 2024.

Exact returned source excerpt

There is some documentary evidence which supports the fact that the Appellant was undergoing mental ill-health when he travelled to India from the United Kingdom in November 2015.

Corpus source-specific finding

The court accepted evidence of seeking lawyers in 2024 without treating it as sufficient justification for an extension.

Exact returned source excerpt

During that period (February 2024 to 10 October 2024) his explanation of the delay is that he was, with all due diligence, seeking the assistance of legal representatives; but he was unable to afford their fees to act on his behalf.

Exact returned source excerpt

He told me that he began engaging with potential legal representatives in May 2024.

Corpus source-specific finding

The proposed procedural challenges did not establish a strong underlying appeal.

Exact returned source excerpt

The various SRA reports – forensic investigation and intervention – set out in detail, supported by documentary evidence, the diligent attempts that were undertaken.

Exact returned source excerpt

That report recommended the intervention action that was subsequently taken, to assume control of the firm and protect the interests of any clients.

Corpus source-specific finding

The substantive challenge to the disciplinary findings and sanction also lacked evidential strength.

Exact returned source excerpt

So far as his abandonment of his firm is concerned, there is of course the clear evidence that he did leave an office, that he did travel to India, and that he never communicated at any stage with his regulator the SRA; nor took any steps regarding the firm or its clients.

Exact returned source excerpt

The Appellant – with no evidential support – has sought to turn an evidenced case in which he is said to have misled his regulator about the firm’s insurance into an unevidenced case in which he asserts that he was himself the victim of a fraud by the broker.

Corpus source-specific finding

The extracts flag separate case-law and CV concerns without providing their eventual resolution.

Exact returned source excerpt

The SRA’s solicitors have, as I have explained, also prepared a bundle containing the 53 cases cited by the Appellant which exist.

Exact returned source excerpt

As to cases “which exist”, there is in this case a specific and very serious concern about caselaw which the Appellant cited to the court in grounds of appeal which he has maintained.
