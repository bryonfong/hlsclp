# In-house lawyers warn they're swapping firms for AI (RollOnFriday survey write-up + anonymous comment thread) *RollOnFriday; anonymous in-house lawyer survey respondents and commenters — RollOnFriday (reporting free-text responses to its In-House Lawyer Survey 2026, with comment thread) (2026)* A majority of respondents in [The RollOnFriday In-House Lawyer Survey 2026 ](https://www.rollonfriday.com/form/rollonfriday-s-in-house-lawyer-s)have predicted they will send fewer instructions to law firms over the next two years due to their increased use of AI.

Exact returned source excerpt

"There is such a big push on AI," said an in-house lawyer in finance, while a GC in banking said "we will use external counsel for fewer and fewer things because modern tools (hint: AI) allow us to do self-service at a fraction of the time and cost."   "As AI tools become more and more reliable our need for external advice will shrink," predicted an in-house lawyer in private equity.

Corpus source-specific finding

Respondents distinguish routine substitution from continuing demand for major matters and professional indemnity cover.

Exact returned source excerpt

AI won't completely replace private practice, they said, as external advisors "will remain important for larger transactions or disputes".

Exact returned source excerpt

But routine work may increasingly stay in-house "in a world where passable redlines for more basic work can be produced at the click of a button".

Corpus source-specific finding

Business growth and staffing constraints can offset AI-driven reductions in outsourcing or hiring.

Exact returned source excerpt

Company growth coupled with hiring freezes would in some cases mean the volume of instructions sent to external lawyers would increase, said other respondents.

Exact returned source excerpt

An in-house lawyer in finance, with a "growing team" commented: "We are always hiring but it is hard to find the lawyers who want to come in-house for in-house prices - particularly for the LevFin/Funds/Private Credit roles." "Our small team is already harnessing AI but the scope of worldwide growth means additional lawyers will be taken on," said an in-house lawyer in the insurance sector.

Corpus source-specific finding

Automation advocates predict particular pressure on junior document work and commodity practices.

Exact returned source excerpt

Why pay some overworked averagely able associate £600 an hour to churn out bundles and chronologies when AI can do a better job in about 10 seconds?

Exact returned source excerpt

But juniors, trainees, paralegals esp in big firms, simply AI is going to replicate a lot of what you do.

Corpus source-specific finding

Several commenters locate lawyers' enduring value in framing problems, verification and client judgment.

Corpus framing of this source: In-house AI adoption could displace routine external work, but accuracy, verification and business growth complicate the outlook

Corpus synthesis of this source: The RollOnFriday write-up and anonymous comment thread present no single unified position. Survey respondents and commenters variously report productivity gains, predict reduced outsourcing and junior work, defend the continuing value of senior judgement and human support, recount costly AI errors, and argue that growth or constrained in-house hiring may increase external instructions. Most forecasts turn on whether AI becomes reliable enough for legal outputs to be trusted.

Corpus source-specific finding

AI could internalise routine, high-volume legal work and reduce demand for lower-value external advice.

Exact returned source excerpt

If tools exist to reliably turn volume stuff around, then great you aren't going to outsource grindy high volume work any more.

Exact returned source excerpt

--- **[anonymous commenter | +29 / -17]** A lot of work solicitors do isn’t complex legal analysis and advice especially juniors.

Complex, high-risk work is expected to retain a substantial role for firms and senior lawyers.

Exact returned source excerpt

What AI can’t yet do is to provide the complex legal analysis in a client or court or opponent ready manner and it needs senior lawyers to check it and make decisions on strategy.

Exact returned source excerpt

Plus clients want humans to share the process with and to support them especially in high risk matters.
