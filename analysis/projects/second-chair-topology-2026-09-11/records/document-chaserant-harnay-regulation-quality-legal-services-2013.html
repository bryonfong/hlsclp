Corpus synthesis of this source: Chaserant and Harnay argue that regulatory protection should reflect differences among legal services and information gaps between regulators and lawyers. Their 2013 article contrasts public-interest and private-interest theories, reviews mixed empirical findings, and treats neither blanket regulation nor blanket deregulation as conclusively supported.

Corpus framing of this source: Service-specific regulation for heterogeneous legal services

Corpus synthesis of this source: Chaserant and Harnay reject a universal choice between regulation and deregulation. They argue that legal services differ in their search, experience and credence characteristics, so policymakers should compare market mechanisms, self-regulation and external regulation service by service. They treat the public-interest and private-interest accounts as competing theories rather than adopting either wholesale, and find the empirical record too mixed to resolve their disagreement.
