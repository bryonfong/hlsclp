Corpus framing of this source: Flaherty and Boehm’s case for abolishing the Legal Services Corporation

Corpus synthesis of this source: In their 1995 Heritage Foundation report, Peter Flaherty and Kenneth Boehm argue that the Legal Services Corporation diverts civil legal-aid resources toward ideological advocacy and resists effective oversight. They recommend abolition, drawing on funding figures, litigation examples and investigations of its political activities, while reporting supporters’ rebuttal and a qualification concerning criminal liability.

The authors recommend abolition rather than reform.

Corpus framing of this source: Why the Legal Services Corporation Must be Abolished

Corpus synthesis of this source: The Heritage Foundation article argues that the Legal Services Corporation’s structure, funding arrangements and confidentiality practices prevent meaningful oversight, permit grantees to evade restrictions and entrench political advocacy. It supports this case with governance details, monitoring figures and controversial litigation examples, then concludes that the LSC cannot be reformed while acknowledging the principal categories of legal need among poor people.

Corpus source-specific finding

The article portrays the LSC’s grant structure and legal status as barriers to governmental control and accountability.

Exact returned source excerpt

The LSC is an independent, private, nonprofit corporation which makes grants to separately incorporated, private, nonprofit grantees, some of which make subgrants to other groups and to each other.

Exact returned source excerpt

An eleven-member board is appointed by the President, subject to Senate confirmation,[12](#12) but has little actual influence over grantees and how they spend their grant money.

Corpus source-specific finding

It argues that grantees use multiple funding sources to evade restrictions on taxpayer-supported activity.

Exact returned source excerpt

Since most grantees receive at least some funding from IOLTA funds and from state and local governments, they can claim that any restricted activities are not supported by LSC funds.

Exact returned source excerpt

The result: Restrictions on LSC involvement in abortion, congressional redistricting, politics, lobbying, and advocacy are ignored or circumvented.

Corpus source-specific finding

The article contends that missing time records and closed case files make resource use and effectiveness impossible to verify.

Exact returned source excerpt

There is no way to confirm resource allocation claims because LSC attorneys do not keep time sheets.

Exact returned source excerpt

Attorney time is the most valuable asset in any law office, and timekeeping is absolutely *de riguer* at any private firm.

Corpus source-specific finding

It characterises on-site monitoring as the only available grant-tracking mechanism and presents evidence that it was sparse and then sharply reduced.

Exact returned source excerpt

At present, the only way the LSC can track how grants are used is through a monitoring program involving on-site visits to programs by Washington-based staff.

Exact returned source excerpt

During the Bush Administration, about 125 visits took place annually, so the average program could expect a visit only once every three years.
