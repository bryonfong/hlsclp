Corpus synthesis of this source: In The Chinese Legal Profession in the Age of Globalization, Liu draws on experience at Microsoft and GE to describe stronger Chinese law firms and rising general counsel influence. Liu links that influence to global business, government directives and professional organising, while stressing persistent governance constraints and uncertain implementation.

Corpus source-specific finding

Exact returned source excerpt

Exact returned source excerpt

That is a huge change, because in the past years many GCs of Chinese companies and SOEs in particular would report to a general (supporting) function, like finance.

Corpus framing of this source: The Chinese Legal Profession in the Age of Globalization

Corpus synthesis of this source: Fengming Liu argues that stronger domestic law firms, regulatory reform, rule-based corporate governance and outbound globalization are elevating Chinese lawyers and general counsel. He supports this account with professional experience, the SASAC directive, GC forums and compliance work, while stressing variation among company types and continuing cultural constraints. He recommends stronger professional standards, ethics, international capabilities, technological literacy and business acumen. David B. Wilkins frames the interview questions but does not advance the principal substantive claims summarized here.
