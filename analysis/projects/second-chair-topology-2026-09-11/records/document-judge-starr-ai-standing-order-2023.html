Corpus framing of this source: Starr’s AI certification order: human verification and continuing responsibility

Corpus synthesis of this source: Judge Brantley Starr’s standing order requires certification of human checking for AI-drafted filings and preserves Rule 11 responsibility. It grounds these safeguards in concerns about fabrication, bias and AI’s lack of professional duties, while recognising other legal uses and permitting applications for leave. The supplied material also reproduces distinct disclosure and verification requirements from Judges Baylson and Kang.

Corpus source-specific finding

Starr requires attorneys to certify either no generative-AI drafting or human verification of AI-drafted language.

Exact returned source excerpt

All attorneys appearing before the Court must file on the docket a certificate attesting either that no portion of the filing was drafted by generative artificial intelligence (such as ChatGPT, Harvey.AI, or Google Bard) or that any language drafted by generative artificial intelligence was checked for accuracy, using print reporters or traditional legal databases, by a human being.

Corpus source-specific finding

Starr retains human responsibility for filings and enforces certification by striking noncompliant filings.

Exact returned source excerpt

Accordingly, the Court will strike any filing from an attorney who fails to file a certificate on the docket attesting that the attorney has read the Court’s judge-specific requirements and understands that he or she will be held responsible under Rule 11 for the contents of any filing that he or she signs and submits to the Court, regardless of whether generative artificial intelligence drafted any portion of that filing.
