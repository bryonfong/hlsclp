Second Chair is this project’s evidence base: 600 works on AI and the legal profession, 1953–2026 — scholarship, industry evidence, court materials and practitioner interviews — each read end to end. The Analysis section applies two published analytical lenses: topic mapping and actor mapping, with driver and dynamics mapping and scenario mapping in progress.

01
Support against feasibility

Wanting something and being able to do it are different things

The grid's central claim, drawn as two bars per intervention. The left bar is support, and it carries two facts at once: how far the track runs is how many of the eight kinds of source addressed the intervention at all, and how far it fills is how warmly they took it. A short, full stub therefore means one source spoke and liked it — not that the field agrees. The right bar is feasibility: whether a named body has the authority to act, whether the mechanism has been tried anywhere, and how many parties have to sign up. The two are scored on different evidence and never combined. Where they come apart is where a response portfolio goes wrong.

02
Ahead of the commentary

Where things are being decided faster than they are being discussed

A fast-moving field produces decisions before it produces literature about them. These are four places where that gap is widest in this corpus — insurers writing exclusions, buyers restructuring panels, courts improvising rules — and they are worth reading as pointers to where the assurance standard is actually being set, well ahead of anyone writing it down.

03
What each one is aimed at

The problems this portfolio does and does not address

Each intervention names the self-reinforcing dynamic it is trying to interrupt — the review backlog, the sanction loop, the draining training commons. An intervention that cannot name what it acts on is a preference rather than a proposal. Read the list for the gaps: a problem with nothing aimed at it is one this portfolio leaves running.

04
Limits

What this portfolio cannot claim

The honest boundary of the artefact. Some of these are properties of the corpus — 600 chosen works assembled to track a field while it is still forming, not a survey of the profession — and some are properties of the analysis. Read together they set what can and cannot be concluded from the grid above.
