What this shows+

Every topic cluster on one side, every source that took a position on the other, and a line wherever a source holds a stance — nothing joins a topic to a topic or a source to a source. The second reading is time: every stance is dated, so the map is an agenda in motion rather than a snapshot. Capability questions spike when generative models arrive and then recede, deployment questions climb from a seventh of the agenda to over half, and a thirty-year argument about junior lawyers gets renamed three times without being resolved.

What it is built from+

Second Chair is this project’s evidence base: 600 works on AI and the legal profession, 1953–2026 — scholarship, industry evidence, court materials and practitioner interviews — each read end to end. The Analysis section applies two published analytical lenses: topic mapping and actor mapping, with driver and dynamics mapping and scenario mapping in progress.

01
The agenda shift

From what it can do to whether the firm can absorb it

Every one of the 1,654 stances carries a year, so the corpus can be read as an agenda in motion. Each row is an era; each column is the kind of question being asked, as a share of all stances in that era. Read down a column, not across a row. Capability spikes to 20% when generative models arrive and falls back to 8% once the answer stops being interesting. Practice and workflow questions climb from 14% to 52% and never give ground. The profession's own questions about expertise and formation fall from nearly half the agenda to 7%.

02
Six eras

What each period was actually arguing about

Era is the corpus's one ordinal axis, and the eras are very unevenly sized — 81 stances before 2015 against 567 in 2026 — so these read as periods with distinct preoccupations rather than as a trend line. The sociology-of-professions era gives way to the Frey–Osborne automation debate, then to reliability, then to business models.

03
Rising and cooling

What is arriving, and what the field has stopped arguing about

Change in each cluster's share of the agenda between the generative shock (2023–24) and now. A cooling cluster is losing share of a growing conversation, not necessarily shrinking — the Capability Frontier holds 39 stances then and 41 now, still the largest cluster in the corpus, but a smaller slice of a much bigger argument. Four clusters have dropped out of the record altogether since 2022; two arrive for the first time in 2026.
